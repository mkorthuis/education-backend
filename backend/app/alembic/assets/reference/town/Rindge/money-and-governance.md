# Rindge: Money, Taxes and Governance in the Jaffrey-Rindge Cooperative

Part of the Rindge research (town/Rindge). Gathered November 21, 2026.

## Summary

**What's covered:** 8 topics, 43 facts and 3 number tables on Rindge's side of the Jaffrey-Rindge Cooperative School District's money and governance. Jaffrey's research (`town/Jaffrey/money-and-governance.md`) already covers the district: the budget and its history, the 2026 default budget and cuts, special education spending, the support staff contract, central office costs, budget votes and board seats. This file cites those facts and adds what Rindge residents asked that they didn't cover:

- **Rindge's share over time:** the district's apportionment history for 2018-19 to 2025-26 and its estimate for 2026-27 (table rindge_cooperative_share_history). Rindge's cost share moved between 50.1% and 53.0%, and its share of the two towns' local school tax between 55.6% and 59.5%. What grew was the total: the local school tax both towns raised rose 41.5% (**derived**). For 2026-27 the district estimated Rindge's local school tax at $13,433,509, up $1.18 million (9.6%), and its rate at about $9.16, up $0.80. Rindge's 2026 rate hadn't been set on November 21, 2026, and second-half bills were delayed.
- **Changing the split:** the 50/50 formula has been in place since 1970. In March 2018 a school-board-backed amendment moving to 100% pupils failed, 47.82% against the two-thirds needed (Rindge voted 1,298–131 for it, Jaffrey 48–1,338 against). A 2019 dissolution study recommended against separating. A 2025 Rindge petition for a new withdrawal study failed, 803–854.
- **The tax bill:** the Town of Rindge's charts put schools at 67.7% of the 2025 rate (60.6% local, 7.1% state) and 66% in 2020. The 2025 revaluation raised average residential values 89.7%. Rindge's elderly exemption amounts are given too.
- **Special education:** by the district's own statements, net special education cost was 84.0% of special education spending in 2024-25 and 77.4% in 2023-24 (**derived**).
- **Staff, substitutes and covering classes:** substitute days rose from 1,530 to 2,002 between FY23 and FY25. A board member said 5–10 para jobs are usually open, and more paras now come through agencies. Exit interviews are offered but most staff decline them. Local wages (OEWS, May 2025): fast food workers' median was $14.65 an hour, retail $17.19 and childcare workers $17.23. Teaching assistants' annual median was $37,580.
- **Rindge Memorial's own budget:** $3,434,645 adopted for 2025-26, plus $2,538,697 of special education budgeted under Student Services.
- **Little Orioles:** it opened in fall 2023, partly grant-funded. The superintendent says it has no tax impact, and it was made fully tuition-funded in 2026.
- **Charter schools:** the district's lawyer said "The State pays for charter schools." Charter students are left out of a town's adequacy aid, and the district keeps paying for their special education.
- **Enrollment and public comment:** the district's October 1, 2026 count was 948 (K–12), with 777 projected by 2036-37. The board's public comment rules are included.

**Main sources:** the Town of Rindge's 2019, 2020 and 2025 annual reports, its 2026 warrant, voters guide and election results, its tax collector, revaluation and exemptions pages; SAU 47's FY2027 and FY2028 budget pages (estimated revenues and assessments, Rindge Memorial and Student Services line items, substitute analysis, enrollment report, NESDEC projection of November 18, 2026); the district's 2019 annual report (the March 2018 votes); the Articles of Agreement; the clerk's March 2026 results; school board minutes (November 2025 to July 2026; the scanned PDFs' text, with key passages checked against page images); policies BEDH and BEA; NH Housing's 2024 rental survey; BLS OEWS (May 2025); two Monadnock Ledger-Transcript articles where news was the only source; and the site's database.

## district-sau-governance: Sharing an SAU: Seats, Votes and Costs

- kind: local
- question_type: governance_money
- description: How a district shares an SAU: board seats and votes, how SAU costs are divided, and what the local board decides versus the SAU

1.
