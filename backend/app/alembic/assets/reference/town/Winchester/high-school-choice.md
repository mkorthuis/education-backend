# High School Choice From Winchester

Part of the Winchester research (town/Winchester). Gathered November 22, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers Winchester's side of high school: how Keene ended the old all-or-nothing contract and closed its door to new Winchester students, the 20 percent choice beside Hinsdale High (the anchor school) and its blind lottery, what families pay and drive when they choose Monadnock Regional, Pioneer Valley or Franklin County Technical School, career and technical education for Winchester students (the Windham Regional Career Center through Hinsdale, or Franklin County Technical School as a choice), the Keene and Hinsdale calendars and school days side by side, the 2026-27 buses to both Keene and Hinsdale and what officials have said about later years, special education under the Hinsdale agreement, the transition activities that bring Winchester middle schoolers to Hinsdale, football, Winchester's Senior Night scholarships, and a state rule on parents paying tuition differences.

## school-residency-rules: Parents Paying a Tuition Difference, and Enrolling Outside the District

- kind: rule
- question_type: rules
- description: Which district is legally responsible for a student under New Hampshire law: domicile, guardians, divided homes and parenting plans, moves between districts, attending a district where a student doesn't live, and who decides disputes (added from the November 13, 2026 towns)

1. New Hampshire law lets a school board in a district with no public school for a child's grade contract with other public schools or approved private tuition schools, let parents choose among them, and require a parent to pay the difference when the chosen school's tuition is above the district's set amount, as long as at least one option is a public school or academy that requires no extra payment from the parent.
   - citation: RSA 193:3, VI
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XV/193/193-3.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-22
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text: "If there is no public school for the child's grade in the resident district, the school board may contract with another public school in another school district or with any private school that has been approved as a school tuition program by the school board ... The district may either assign all children to schools that have been approved as a school tuition program, or allow each child's parent to choose a school from among schools that have been approved as a school tuition program. To enroll a child in a tuition school approved by the local school board whose tuition cost is above the district's established tuition cost per pupil as determined and approved by the board, the local board may require the parent to pay the tuition cost difference as long as at least one option is a public school or public academy that does not require additional tuition payment from the parent." The Hinsdale–Winchester agreement also refers to Winchester's ability "to recoup excess tuition costs from parents/custodians/adult students (RSA 194:27)" (section 4(c)). Tuition duties of districts without a high school: library gov-tuition-towns 1–7. Raw: raw/high/law/rsa-193-3.txt (.html); raw/high/law/rsa-194-27.txt (.html)
2. New Hampshire law lets a parent apply to enroll a child in a public school or academy outside the home district; if that school agrees, it may charge tuition to the parent or agree with the home district on payment, it may not refuse the child because of a disability, and the home district keeps all responsibility for the child's special education.
   - citation: RSA 193:3, IV(a)–(d)
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XV/193/193-3.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-22
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text: "(a) Any person having custody of a child may apply to enroll that child in a public school or public academy outside the school district in which the person and child reside. If the non-resident school district or public academy agrees to enroll the child it may charge tuition to the parent or may enter into an agreement for payment of tuition with the school district in which the child resides"; "(c) ... the district in which the child resides shall retain all responsibility for the provision of special education and related services pursuant to RSA 186-C"; "(d) The decision ... to deny enrollment of a non-resident pupil shall not be based, in whole or in part, on whether such pupil is a child with a disability."
