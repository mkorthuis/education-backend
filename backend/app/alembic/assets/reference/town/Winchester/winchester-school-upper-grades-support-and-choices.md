# Winchester School's Upper Grades, Support and Choices

Part of the Winchester research (town/Winchester). Gathered 2026-11-22.

## Summary

This file covers support and choices at Winchester School (preschool to grade 8, 85 Parker Street) for its families: who to contact for a special education or speech evaluation, the referral form, who provides special education and related services (on staff or contracted), the state's special education rating of the district, paraprofessionals' contract, pay and staffing, the school's exit from Comprehensive Support and Improvement (CSI) status, reading and math help (MTSS, the daily WIN block, interventionists and the new Title I tutoring program), federal grants, counseling, social work and behavior support, discipline, the bullying policy and behavior data, the phone policy, middle school sports, access for home-educated and EFA students and the posted home education policy, gifted and advanced learners, volunteering and gifts, ACCESS's food pantry, medication at school, and New Hampshire's rules on medication. Facts about the district and school use `district Winchester`; ACCESS's pantry uses `town Winchester`; state rules have no place.

## school-medication-rules: Medication at School: New Hampshire Rules

- kind: rule
- question_type: rules
- description: New Hampshire's rules for giving students medication during the school day and at school activities: who may give it, the prescriber's and parent's paperwork, how medicine reaches school and is stored, field trips, and students carrying their own inhalers and epinephrine (proposed from the Winchester research)

1. New Hampshire's school rule on medication defines the "school day" as any time in the day, afternoon or evening when a child is attending school or another school-related activity, and requires that a student who must take a prescribed medicine during the school day be given it, or helped to take it, by the school nurse, or by a registered or practical nurse under the school nurse's direction; the school nurse may delegate giving medicine to others only as the state's nursing law and rules allow.
   - citation: N.H. Code Admin. R. Ed 311.02(a)–(d)
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rules/state_agencies/ed300.html
   - as_of: 2026-11-22
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text: "(a) For the purpose of this rule, 'school day' means any time during the day, afternoon, or evening when a child is attending school or other school related activity. (b) Any student, who is required during the school day to take a medication prescribed by a licensed physician, advanced registered nurse practitioner, licensed physician's assistant, or dentist, shall have a school nurse either assist the student to take the medication or administer the medication. (c) This duty may also be undertaken by a registered nurse (RN) or licensed practical nurse (LPN), under the direction of the school nurse. (d) The school nurse shall only delegate the administration of medications, if appropriate, to others pursuant to RSA 326-B and Nur 404." Current text: "ss by #14197, eff 2-21-25". The rule does not mention school buses. Statewide, so no place. Raw: raw/middle/state/ed-311-02-medication-during-school-day.txt

Under the same rule, students may carry and use their own asthma inhalers and epinephrine auto-injectors as state law allows, and when the school nurse is not available the principal or a designee may help a student take required medicine by making it available, watching the student take it or not, and recording whether it was taken.
   - citation: N.H. Code Admin. R. Ed 311.02(e)–(f)
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rules/state_agencies/ed300.html
   - as_of: 2026-11-22
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
