# Winchester School's Early Grades and Families

Part of the Winchester research (town/Winchester). Gathered November 22, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers Winchester School's youngest grades and the questions young families asked: pre-K, after-school care, bus stops, free meals, English learners and translation, the parent portal, a missed bus, help with forms and fees, the calendar, snow days, recess, enrolling mid-year, preschool screening, child care subsidies, the kindergarten cutoff and class sizes.

**Sources.** The district's website, www.wnhsd.org, refuses automated requests with a bot check, so its pages were not read. The main session listed the site's document library through its document API (`raw/browser/wnhsd-documents-index.tsv`), and the documents themselves were downloaded directly from the district's file host: the 2025-26 Student/Parent Handbook, the 2026-27 and 2025-26 calendars, the 2024-25 ("SY25") bus routes, the registration documents, the cafeteria policy, the special education procedures plan and referral form, Section E, I and J policies, and School Board minutes from 2023 to October 2026. Earlier sources: ACCESS's 2024 federal grant agreement (G&C agenda), FRAC's community eligibility fact sheet, NCES, state rules and federal meal rules.

**Main findings:**
- **Preschool:** Winchester School's preschool keeps a waitlist; for 2026-27 it has separate classes for 3- and 4-year-olds, each started with 8–9 children so children needing special education can join when they turn 3. The board stopped busing general-education preschoolers for 2026-27 (a 5-0 vote on November 20, 2025 to drop $130,000 in bus monitors). Day length, a lottery and tuition are not stated in any document read.
- **After school:** ACCESS runs 2:00–5:30 p.m. on school days for grades K–8, with scholarships, a waiting list, a new agreement with the district for 2026-27, and a five-week summer camp at the school for over 100 children in 2026.
- **Buses:** First Student runs them (two-year contract from 2025). The only posted route sheet is for 2024-25. On it, the Route 10 stops on Route A were picked up 7:07–7:14 and reached school at 7:23. For 2026-27 some stops were consolidated into central walk-to stops.
- **Calendar:** 2026-27 has 3 early release days, used for teacher professional development: October 8, December 23 and January 22. Closings go out by "Robo Call", on WMUR-TV and radio, and on the website and Facebook.
- **Recess:** children go outside whenever possible and stay in below 20 degrees.
- **Meals:** every student gets free breakfast and lunch under community eligibility, approved through June 2027. The school still asks families to fill out a meal form, because it affects state adequacy aid, and offers help with it.
- **Kindergarten:** a child must turn 5 before September 30 (policy JEB). Kindergarten screening is done in August.
- **Class size limits (policy IIB, October 2025):** 18 in kindergarten and grade 1, 20 in grades 2–3 and 25 in grades 4–8.
- **Registration:** a birth certificate, immunization and physical records, and one proof of residency (a tax bill, water bill, signed lease or mortgage) are required.

## early-supports-to-preschool-special-ed: Leaving Early Supports at Age 3, and Preschool Special Education

- kind: rule
- question_type: rules
- description: The move from Family Centered Early Supports and Services (FCESS, NH's early intervention for children under 3) to school district preschool special education, and that it is free.

1. New Hampshire's special education rules require every school district to find, identify and evaluate each child aged 2½ to under 22 living in the district who may have a disability ("child find"), so that an eligible child has an IEP by age 3, and any person may refer a child, for reasons including "inability to progress or participate in developmentally appropriate preschool activities."
   - citation: N.H. Admin. Rules Ed 1105.01(b)–(c) and Ed 1105.02(a)–(b)
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rules/state_agencies/ed1100.html
   - as_of: 2026-11-22
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text: "any child 2.5 years of age up to 22 years of age residing within its jurisdiction is referred to the IEP team"; "For children from 2.5 years of age, the LEA shall use the special education process ... to find, identify, and evaluate all children who are potentially children with disabilities ... thereby ensuring that an IEP will be developed and implemented for any child who is eligible for special education by age 3"; "Any person may refer a child under the age of 22 years to the IEP team for reasons including ... (5) Inability to progress or participate in developmentally appropriate preschool activities; and (6) Receiving service from family centered early support and services."
