# Jaffrey: Money, Taxes and Governance of the Jaffrey-Rindge Cooperative School District

Part of the Jaffrey research (town/Jaffrey). Gathered November 21, 2026.

## Summary

**What's covered:** 6 topics, 62 facts and 5 number tables on how the Jaffrey-Rindge Cooperative School District (SAU 47) is paid for and governed, and what that means for Jaffrey taxpayers. District-wide facts are placed with the district so Rindge's research can cite them; Jaffrey-only facts (tax bill, exemptions, home sales) are placed with the town.

- **The tax bill:** the Town of Jaffrey's own 2025 chart puts 49% of the $18.95 rate to the local school tax and 6% to the state education tax. The 2025 town-wide revaluation raised Jaffrey's valuation 83%, which is why every rate fell; in "2024 terms" the school rate rose $0.89. The 2026 rate had not been posted on November 21, 2026.
- **The cost split:** the 1970 Articles of Agreement split all costs 50% by resident pupils (ADM) and 50% by equalized property value, then credit each town with its own state aid. For 2025-26 Jaffrey had 51.6% of the pupils and 43.2% of the value, so 47.44% of the cost; after its larger adequacy grant it paid 42.8% of the local school tax: about $13,500 per pupil against Rindge's $19,200 (**derived**).
- **Budget votes:** the operating budget failed in March 2022 and March 2026 (defaults), and passed in 2021, 2023, 2024 and 2025. In 2025 voters chose a budget cut by $3 million at the deliberative session over a higher default. In 2026 Jaffrey's voters approved the $33.5 million budget 688–421 and Rindge's rejected it 608–943, so the $31,991,442 default took effect.
- **Why athletics and pre-K are exposed:** after the 2025 cut, the board paid for athletics, co-curriculars and full-day 4-year-old pre-K from retained fund balance. A default budget starts from the prior year's adopted budget, so those one-time-funded programs were outside the 2026-27 default. The district's FY27 sheets list them under "Our Choices" ($589,384 for activities, $479,892 for pre-K). Under the default the board cut an athletic trainer, four elementary teachers, a high school math teacher and half of full-day pre-K, and moved middle school sports toward intramurals.
- **Central office:** SAU 47 serves one district, so its office is in the district budget: 13.5 of 247.82 staff (October 2025), and about 4.1% of 2026-27 appropriations for the superintendent's office, business office and board (**derived**). The superintendent says the district is under HB 1300's 6% SAU cap. There's been no curriculum director for 8–9 years, the facilities director post was cut in 2025, and the CFO works part time.
- **Special education and "mandates":** special education spending rose from $7.5 million (2021-22) to $9.1 million (2024-25). In the district's own FY27 breakdown, $1.21 million of the $2.72 million increase was "non-discretionary" (contract steps, health insurance, placements, transportation the law requires, vocational tuition and two positions tied to the new Ed 306 rules), and $1.51 million was the board's choices.
- **Pay:** the 2026–2029 support staff contract (passed 53.24%) starts paraprofessionals at $17.25 an hour in 2026-27, $17.75 in 2027-28 and $18.50 in 2028-29.

**Main sources:** SAU 47's budget pages for FY2022–FY2028 and their documents: warrants, voter's guides, clerk's vote reports for 2021–2026, MS-22, budget and default overviews, apportionment, budget and tax-rate histories, staffing and the deliberative session presentation. Also the district's annual reports for February 2024, 2025 and 2026 (with the 2025 deliberative minutes, MD&A and the RSA 32:11-a special education reports), the Articles of Agreement, school board minutes (March 16, March 30, April 6, October 5 and November 2, 2026), the draft FY28 budget calendar, the Town of Jaffrey's 2025 tax distribution, its exemptions sheet and tax collector page, NH REALTORS® October 2026 market updates, and the site's database.
