# Families and Community in Bristol and the Newfound Area School District

Part of the Bristol research (town/Bristol). Gathered November 22, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers families and community for questions P5.1–P5.3, P7.1, P7.3, P8.1, P8.3, P17.3, P18.2, P18.3, P19.1, P19.3 and P20.3: free and low-cost summer programs (the Tapply-Thompson Community Center's camp and its scholarships, Copper Cannon Camp and summer meals); free and reduced-price meals (prices, the 2026-27 application rules and what happens when a family's income or hours change); messages in other languages; the parent portal and other ways to reach the school without a home computer; storm closings, delays, early release, the inclement weather plan and bus routes; Education Freedom Account (EFA) enrollment at Newfound and the district's home education policy; moving between the district's towns; summer tutoring; community use of school fields and gyms; background checks for volunteers and coaches; and how residents, including retired people, can volunteer.

**Counts:** 13 topics, 76 facts, 0 number tables.

**Main sources:** SAU 4's website (volunteer, food services, closings and delays, Infinite Campus, resources, bus routes, home education, prospective families and facilities pages); Newfound Area School Board policies IICC, GBCD, EBCD, EEA, EEAB, EFB, JFAA, JCA, IHBG, KA, KG, KG-R and KG-a (policy index pages on sau4.org); the 2026-27 free and reduced-price meal letter and LINQ app guide; the district's Inclement Weather Plan (updated 11/12/24) and School Day Schedule image; the EFA Student Enrollment Procedure; the ESOL Compliance Plan (2014); the 2026-27 elementary and NMMS student handbooks; the Tapply-Thompson Community Center (TTCC) summer camp, scholarship, forms, basketball and volunteer pages; Copper Cannon Camp's pages; the Minot-Sleeper Library's FAQ and policies; the NMMS Athletics partnership page; RSA 189:13-a (copy from the Franklin research).

| P8.3 | Homeschoolers must test every year | **Not under state law since July 1, 2026.** The library shows the annual evaluation section was repealed. Newfound's policy IHBG (adopted March 2023) and its Home Education page still describe an annual evaluation and a notice to a "participating agency." EFA students are different: the library says they must keep a yearly record (district-homeschool-access 1–4). |
| P18.2 | A 3rd grader can get free tutoring | **The state's free tutoring starts at grade 4.** Tutor.com's New Hampshire program covers grades 4–12. No district summer tutoring or summer school for elementary students was found; Project Promise runs after school during the school year (district-school-day-and-calendar 1; library statewide-online-tutoring in Hampstead's file). |
| P18.3 | Moving between Alexandria, New Hampton and Bristol means a school switch | **Only the elementary school can change.** All of these towns are in the same district. Alexandria, Bristol and Danbury families use Newfound Elementary at Bristol; New Hampton families use Newfound Elementary at New Hampton. Grades 5–8 and 9–12 are at Bristol for everyone. A parent may ask in writing for another in-district school (district-attendance-zones-and-transfers 1–3). |
| P19.1 | Middle school fields and gym aren't open to community leagues | **Policy opens them to youth groups, subject to approval.** Non-profit youth groups pay no rental fee (custodial fees may apply), and the TTCC schedules time in the NMMS gym. Student activities have priority, and an application, insurance and approval are required. The sources don't say why use is limited (district-facility-use 1–9). |
| P19.3 | Background checks may not clear in time | **No timing is published.** The district's volunteers go through a fingerprint appointment (cost $21.25, reimbursable); the TTCC requires a check for every coach it chooses. NH DOS's scheduling page was blocked (district-volunteers-and-partners 1–9). |
| P17.3 | Seniors may audit classes or use the track | **No audit rule and no public-track rule found.** Adults may volunteer after approval and a background check if they work with students regularly; community groups may apply to use fields (district-volunteers-and-partners 1–5, 10; district-facility-use 1–4). |
| P8.1 | EFAs are open to everyone | **Correct since June 10, 2025,** within the state's enrollment cap (library efa-eligibility-and-cap 1–2). A Newfound-resident EFA student cannot also attend Newfound full time, and the district's procedure for using its classes is in district-homeschool-access 5–7. |
