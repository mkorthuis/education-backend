# Bristol: Money, Taxes, Governance and the Newfound Area School District

Part of the Bristol research (town/Bristol). Gathered November 22, 2026.

## Summary

**What's covered:** 12 topics, 78 facts and 7 number tables on how the Newfound Area School District (district id 388) is paid for and run, as it affects Bristol (town id 67): why Bristol's school tax moves, how the four member towns split the cost, the district's two budget failures in a row, its 2% local tax cap, repairs, energy projects and bonds, test results against the state, where and when Bristol votes, the deliberative session, how a year-round resident gets a say, busing and administration costs, teacher turnover and Bristol's elderly exemption.

- **The 2025 rate drop was a revaluation, not a cut.** Bristol's 2025 total rate was $13.16 per $1,000 against $23.62 in 2024 because the town's net assessed valuation went from $549,100,592 to $1,033,562,950 after its 2025 revaluation. The total tax raised rose 4.6% and the local school tax 9.9% (**derived**). Since 2014 Bristol's local school tax is up 58.8% while its resident students fell 5.8% (**derived**; table bristol_school_tax_and_resident_students).
- **The cost split.** The four towns now share the district's local assessment (Bristol's estimated 2026-27 share is $6,531,240 of $16,563,232, or 39.4%, **derived**). The district's slides say the split follows each town's average daily membership (ADM), but the dollars per ADM differ by town; the cooperative's articles of agreement were not found online.
- **Two default budgets in a row.** The operating budget failed in March 2025 (after a recount, 683 to 701) and again on March 10, 2026 (857 to 890), so the default budget applies both years. The district also holds a 2% local tax cap, adopted in 2012, which twice survived a vote to drop or loosen it. A 60% vote was needed for items over the cap; the teachers' contract (57.9% yes) and the $6.16 million high school heating and ventilation lease (51.5% yes) fell short. The 2026 annual meeting was legalized at special meetings on June 29 and October 26, 2026 to fix filing defects.
- **Repairs and bonds.** No bond is on the table as of November 22, 2026. The heating lease (not a bond) did not reach 60%, Bristol Elementary's structural and asbestos repairs (about $350,000) are paid from the Maintenance Trust Fund, and the board voted on August 10, 2026 to stop spending on the school consolidation plan. Danbury Elementary closed after the March 2026 vote.
- **Votes and voices.** Bristol voted on the school budget at Newfound Regional High School's gym on March 10, 2026 and at the Old Town Hall in 2025; the deliberative session is always at the high school auditorium. Seats on the board and budget committee are elected at large; a new year-round resident can register up to the polls on Election Day.

**Main sources:** the district's 2026 warrant, MS-27 proposed budget, default budget (MS-DSB), January to March 2026 budget presentations, 2026 election results, 2024-25 annual report (with the 2025 and 2026 deliberative session minutes), mandatory report to voters, teacher salary comparison, the board's minutes from September 2025 to November 2026 and its energy audit presentation; the Town of Bristol's 2025 annual report, 2026 voter's guide, tax rate, exemption, voter registration and election pages; RSA 195:7; the site's database (DRA tax data, enrollment).

## cooperative-district-apportionment-rules: How New Hampshire Cooperative School Districts Divide Costs Among Member Towns

- kind: rule
- question_type: rules
- description: How New Hampshire cooperative school districts divide capital and operating costs among their member towns under RSA 195:7: the equalized valuation and average daily membership formulas, other formulas a district may adopt, and how home-educated pupils are counted (proposed from the Bristol research)

1. Under RSA 195:7, a cooperative school district formed before July 1, 1963 divides its capital and operating costs among its original towns by equalized valuation, or half by equalized valuation and half by the previous year's average daily membership, or by another formula that its board offers, the district adopts and the State Board of Education approves, and home-education pupils who get no services from the district are left out of the membership counts.
   - citation: RSA 195:7, I–II
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XV/195/195-7.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-22
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text: "during the first 5 years after the formation of a cooperative school district each preexisting district shall pay its share ... in accordance with either one of the following formulas as determined by a majority vote of the cooperative district meeting: (a) ... equalized valuation ... (b) One-half ... equalized valuation ... and 1/2 ... average daily membership for the preceding year. (c) Some other formula offered by the cooperative school board ... adopted by the cooperative school district and approved by the state board of education. II. Home education pupils who do not receive services from the cooperative school district shall not be included in the average daily membership." The statute speaks of the first five years; what formula a district uses afterward is set in its articles of agreement, which for Newfound were not found.
