# Newfound Memorial Middle School: Support, Phones, Buses and Choices

Part of the Bristol research (town/Bristol). Gathered 2026-11-22.

## Summary

This file covers Newfound Memorial Middle School (NMMS, grades 5 to 8) in the Newfound Area School District (SAU 4): how a foster child enrolls, the counseling and mental health staff in the building, bullying that happens on phones, how a family starts an evaluation for special education or speech, how the move from the elementary schools works for a child with an IEP (this is the first year grade 5 is at NMMS), special education staffing and out-of-district placement, homeschooled and Education Freedom Account (EFA) students in middle school sports and band, how long the bus rides are, the phone policy and whether there is a practice bus.

**Counts:** 11 topics, 68 facts, 3 number tables. Raw copies are in `raw/middle/` (about 8 MB): the 2026-27 NMMS student handbook, 14 SAU 4 board policies, the 2021 special education procedures plan, the 2026-27 athletic handbook, 2026 school board minutes and packets (cleaned excerpts), the SAU 4 Student Services pages and the 2026-27 bus route times (a derived CSV with street names only).

**Main sources:** the NMMS 2026-27 handbook (bell schedules transcribed from image); SAU 4 policies JFABE, JFAA, JFABD, JICJ, JICK, JICL, JJJ, JJJ-R, JJIC, EEA, EEAB, IHBA, JLD and JLDBB; the Newfound Special Education Procedures Plan (approved August 9, 2021); the Transportation Committee's September 3, 2026 minutes; the 2026-27 morning and afternoon bus route sheets (scanned images, read by text recognition and checked against the pages); School Board minutes and packets from January to November 2026; the Project Promise and NMMS Athletics pages; the SAU 4 Student Services pages.

**Main gaps:**
- No source says how many students with IEPs NMMS serves or how many aides each has. Aides follow each IEP, and the sources give only the staff directory and the staffing changes in the board minutes.
- No source says when speech services are scheduled, or how the NMMS case managers meet incoming grade 5 families.
- No middle-school practice or activity bus is described anywhere. The district ended a "late bus" in February 2026, and the sources don't say which schools it served.
- NMMS sports fees were not found. Policy JJJ says non-enrolled students pay the same fees as enrolled students.
- Ride times are scheduled times on route sheets, not what each child rides. The sheets don't show which stops serve which school.
- NH DOE's page for the state's special education determination (the "Needs Intervention" rating) refused automated access.

## district-conduct-policies: Bullying, Cyberbullying and Conduct at NMMS

- kind: local
- question_type: local_practice
- description: A district's bullying policy and its rules for student conduct, including on school buses

1. Newfound's bullying policy JICK applies to bullying and cyberbullying that happens off school property or outside a school activity "if the conduct interferes with a student's educational opportunities or substantially disrupts the orderly operations of the school."
   - citation: SAU 4 School Board Policy JICK, section A.1 (RSA 193-F:4, II(a))
   - source: https://www.sau4.org/school-board/school-board-policies/section-j-students
   - as_of: 2026-11-22
   - review_by: 2027-11-22
   - place: district Newfound Area
   - note: Policy text: "The prohibition against bullying and cyberbullying, also applies to conduct that occurs on, or is delivered to, school property or a school-sponsored activity or event on or off school property, or occurs off school property or outside of a school-sponsored activity or event, if the conduct interferes with a student's educational opportunities or substantially disrupts the orderly operations of the school". Adopted November 9, 2015; revised January 27, 2026 and August 24, 2026 (second reading waived). Raw copy: raw/middle/policy-JICK-bullying.txt
2. Under JICK, examples of cyberbullying include sharing a student's private information ("doxxing"), repeatedly contacting a student who asked not to be contacted, fake accounts ("catfishing") and sharing altered images or audio ("deepfakes"), and the policy's definition of electronic devices includes phones and text, image and video messages.
   - citation: SAU 4 School Board Policy JICK, section B.2-B.3
   - source: https://www.sau4.org/school-board/school-board-policies/section-j-students
   - as_of: 2026-11-22
   - review_by: 2027-11-22
   - place: district Newfound Area
   - note: Policy text: "Additional examples of cyberbullying conduct include ... “Doxxing” ... “Cyberstalking” ... “Catfishing” ... Sharing of “deepfakes”"; "“Electronic devices” includes, but is not limited to, telephones, cellular or smartphones, computers, pagers". Raw copy: raw/middle/policy-JICK-bullying.txt
3. A student can report bullying "to the Principal, or to a school district employee or volunteer that the student feels more comfortable making the report" to, and parents and other adults can report to the principal; anonymous reports are allowed, but discipline can't rest only on an anonymous report.
   - citation: SAU 4 School Board Policy JICK, section D
   - source: https://www.sau4.org/school-board/school-board-policies/section-j-students
   - as_of: 2026-11-22
   - review_by: 2027-11-22
   - place: district Newfound Area
   - note: Policy text: "Any student who believes he or she has been the victim of bullying/cyberbullying, retaliation, or false accusations should report the alleged acts immediately to the Principal, or to a school district employee or volunteer that the student feels more comfortable making the report"; "formal disciplinary action may not be based solely on an anonymous report". Raw copy: raw/middle/policy-JICK-bullying.txt

   - citation: SAU 4 School Board Policy JICK, sections E.3, F.1 and F.7 (RSA 193-F:4)
