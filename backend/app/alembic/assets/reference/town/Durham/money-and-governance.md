# Money and Governance in Durham and the Oyster River Cooperative

Part of the Durham research (town/Durham). Gathered November 21, 2026.

## Summary

**What's covered.** Durham's 2026 tax rate ($20.81, with the local school rate up $1.15 to $11.94) and how the Oyster River Cooperative School District's budget is split among Durham, Lee and Madbury; the 2025 revaluation; Durham's elderly exemption, tax deferral and other relief; the 2016 Durham–UNH agreements under which UNH pays the town for children living in its tax-exempt housing and for half of fire department costs; the FY27 budget, the 77% share for salaries and benefits, the six staff cuts and the $519,000 the deliberative session added back; the March 10, 2026 ballot (the $9,827,000 elementary bond, contracts, open enrollment, and four petitioned articles including the performance audit and a local tax cap); the December 2024 space audit of Mast Way and Moharimet and what the district has done since the bond failed; the FY28 draft budget (8.6% over FY27, with $1.745 million for the high school track and turf); the superintendent's departure, the interim superintendent and the 2026 search; the draft 2026–2031 strategic plan; and how and when residents can give input on the FY28 budget.

**Counts.** 8 topics (7 shared local topics with district or town places, and 1 shared state-rule topic), 80 facts, 2 number tables.

**Main sources.** ORCSD's March 10, 2026 official results, 2026 annual report (ballot, tax impact), signed 2026 warrant, January 14 hearing and February 3 deliberative session slides and minutes, FY27 budget FAQ, school board minutes (January–November 2026), the FY28 proposed budget workshop materials and minutes (October 28, 2026), the FY28 budget calendar, the November 18, 2026 board packet, the December 2024 facilities assessment, and district news posts; the Town of Durham's assessing pages, code chapter 132, 2025 revaluation FAQ, 2027 proposed revenue report and its 2016 school and fire agreements with UNH; RSA 32:10.

**Main gaps.** ORCSD's articles of agreement and the current apportionment formula weights were not found (the district says the formula uses equalized valuation and enrollment; a 2016 sample used 50/50). How many acres UNH owns and the value of exempt property in Durham were not retrieved (NH DRA and NH DOE pages refused automated requests; the old town site's master plan links are dead). No source describes elementary classes being held in hallways or closets. The 2025 deliberative session minutes were not posted. The board had not decided on the superintendent (deliberating after November 18, 2026) or adopted its FY28 budget (due December 2, 2026), and no elementary bond for March 2027 had been proposed in the materials posted through November 18, 2026.

## school-budget-overruns-and-reserves: When School Costs Exceed the Budget

- kind: rule
- question_type: rules
- description: What a New Hampshire school district can do when costs exceed its budget during the year: special meetings, emergency overexpenditures, retained fund balance, contingency funds and special education reserves

1. Under RSA 32:10, I(e), if a New Hampshire town or school district meeting does not approve an appropriation in a separate warrant article (or deletes a purpose or cuts it to zero), that purpose is treated as one for which no money was appropriated, and no amount may be transferred to it or spent on it.
   - citation: RSA 32:10, I(e)
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/III/32/32-10.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-21
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text: "if the meeting deletes a purpose, or reduces the amount appropriated for that purpose to zero or does not approve an appropriation contained in a separate article, that purpose or article shall be deemed one for which no appropriation is made, and no amount shall be transferred to or expended for such purpose." This applies within the budget year; a later year's budget is voted on anew. "Purpose" here has the meaning in RSA 32:3, V (RSA 32:10, II). Raw: raw/money/rsa-32-10.txt
2. Under RSA 32:10, a New Hampshire school board may move unspent money between budget lines during the year as needs change, as long as total spending stays within the total voted, it keeps records any citizen can see under the Right-to-Know law, and money voted in a special warrant article or put into a capital reserve fund is used only for that article's purpose.
   - citation: RSA 32:10, I(a), (b) and (d)
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/III/32/32-10.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-21
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text: "(a) The total amount spent shall not exceed the total amount appropriated at the town or district meeting. (b) Records shall be kept by the governing body, such that the budget committee, if any, or any citizen requesting such records pursuant to RSA 91-A:4, may ascertain the purposes of appropriations to which, and from which, amounts have been transferred ... (d) Any amount appropriated at the meeting under a special warrant article, or to a capital reserve fund pursuant to RSA 35:5, may be used only for the purpose specified in that article and shall not be transferred."
