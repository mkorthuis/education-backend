# Oyster River Middle and High School

Part of the Durham research (town/Durham). Gathered November 21, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers Oyster River Middle School (grades 5–8) and Oyster River High School (grades 9–12), the Oyster River Cooperative School District's schools for Durham, Lee and Madbury, on the questions assigned to agent 2: algebra and geometry in middle school and high school credit for it; UNH and other college courses during high school and who pays; how the district sets its calendar and how it lines up with UNH's; the high school turf field and its trust fund; away-game buses, the attendance rule for athletes and the 8:15 start; concussions and return to learn; career and technical education (CTE) at the Dover, Somersworth and Rochester centers, and the bus; credit for paid work and apprenticeships through extended learning opportunities (ELOs); the phone policy, school laptops and screen time; the bullying policy; who coordinates internships; school counselors and AP; start times; how competency-based grading shows up on transcripts; and environmental and outdoor electives.

- **13 fact topics, 117 facts and no number tables.** Twelve topics are shared `local` topics with place `district Oyster River Coop` (one fact, UNH's own calendar, has no place); one (`school-sports-rules`, 4 facts, no place) is a **proposed new shared topic** for New Hampshire's head injury law for school sports (RSA 200:49–52), which no existing topic covers.
- **Raw copies** are in `raw/high/` (about 49 MB): the high school's 2026-27 Program of Studies and school profile, the 2026-27 high school, athletics and middle school handbooks, the Class of 2030 Program of Studies, the middle school's competencies and grading scale, the high school concussion protocol, the 2026-27 district calendar, UNH's 2026-27 academic calendar, 12 district policies (IMBD, IHCD, ICA, IHBH, IK, ILBAA, JICJ, JICK, JLCJ, IKB, IGAD, IHCA) and the policy index page, 27 sets of school board and finance committee minutes (`raw/high/minutes/`), the March 2026 ballot, the November 18, 2026 board packet (with an image of the trust fund page), the 2024 UNH Survey Center report on middle school competency grading, the 2025-26 Tri-City CTE guide, the transportation, IT, laptop-insurance, sustainability and middle school home pages, an NHPR story on the 2016 start-time change, and RSA 200:49–52.

## school-sports-rules: School Sports Rules: Concussions, Medical Coverage and Cooperative Teams

- kind: rule
- question_type: rules
- description: New Hampshire law and NHIAA rules for school sports: concussion removal, return to play and return to learning, medical coverage at practices and games, physicals, cooperative teams and their no-cut rule

1. New Hampshire law (RSA 200:49) requires every school board to develop guidelines and forms to educate coaches, student-athletes and parents about the nature and risk of concussion and head injury, including continuing to play after one, and requires schools to give all student-athletes a concussion and head injury information sheet every year.
   - citation: RSA 200:49, Head Injury Policies for Student Sports
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XV/200/200-49.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-21
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Statute text: "The school board of each school district shall develop guidelines and other pertinent information and forms for student sports to inform and educate coaches, student-athletes, and student-athletes' parents or guardians of the nature and risk of concussion and head injury including continuing to play after concussion or head injury. On an annual basis, a school district or school shall distribute a concussion and head injury information sheet to all student-athletes." Raw copy: raw/high/rsa-200-49.txt
2. New Hampshire law (RSA 200:50) says a coach, official, athletic trainer or health care provider who suspects a student-athlete has a concussion or head injury in a practice or game must remove the student from play immediately, and the student may not return that day or until a health care provider gives written clearance and a parent gives written permission.
   - citation: RSA 200:50, Removal of Student-Athlete
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XV/200/200-50.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-21
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Statute text: "A student-athlete who has been removed from play shall not return to play on the same day or until he or she is evaluated by a health care provider and receives medical clearance and written authorization from that health care provider to return to play. The student-athlete shall also present written permission from a parent or guardian to return to play." Raw copy: raw/high/rsa-200-50.txt
3. New Hampshire's head injury law covers intramural sports outside the school day and competitive sports between schools in grades 4–12, and defines a "health care provider" as someone licensed to treat patients and trained in evaluating and managing concussions; its four sections (RSA 200:49–52) deal with return to play and say nothing about schoolwork or "return to learn."
   - citation: RSA 200:52, Definitions; RSA 200:49–51
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XV/200/200-52.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-21
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Statute text: "'Student-athlete' means a student involved in any intramural sports program conducted outside the regular teaching day or competitive student sports program between schools in grades 4-12." "'Health care provider' means a person who is licensed, certified, or otherwise statutorily authorized by the state to provide medical treatment and is trained in the evaluation and management of concussions." The subdivision "Head Injury Policies for Student Sports" has sections 200:49 to 200:52 (checked in the chapter listing, https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XV/200/200-mrg.htm). Academic supports after a concussion come from local policy (such as Oyster River's JLCJ) and Section 504. New Hampshire law (RSA 200:51) protects school employees, volunteers, parents and others from civil liability for good-faith conduct about a student-athlete's injury when they follow the head injury law and local concussion policies, and says a school may share its concussion guidelines with outside groups using school property but need not enforce them.
