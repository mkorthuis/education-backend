# Student Support and School Choices in Durham (Oyster River)

Part of the Durham research (town/Durham). Gathered November 21, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers the support and choice questions for Durham residents in the Oyster River Cooperative School District (SAU 5): paraeducators after the 2026 ORPaSS contract, who handles IEPs and 504 plans when a child moves from Mast Way or Moharimet to Oyster River Middle School, what evaluations and extra help cost families, EFAs, Seacoast Charter School and home education, St. Thomas Aquinas students in Oyster River classes and sports, returning to ORHS from home education, the 2026 open enrollment article (Article 9) and who pays for out-of-district students, drop-off traffic on Coe Drive, the middle school's solar panels, and cafeteria waste.

- **11 topics, 60 facts, no tables.** Ten are shared slugs from `shared-topics.md`: `district-classrooms-and-staff`, `district-special-education-services`, `district-curriculum-and-assessment`, `school-programs-after-grade-6`, `district-homeschool-access`, `town-school-options-nearby`, `district-attendance-zones-and-transfers`, `town-streets-and-safe-routes`, `district-facilities-and-capital` and `district-meals`. Raw copies are in `raw/support/` (about 68 MB, most of it the 33 MB scanned 2021 Sustainability Management Plan).
- **Places used:** `district Oyster River Coop` for the district, its schools, policies, staff, budget lines and board; `district Diocese of Manchester` for St. Thomas Aquinas High School (as in the Dover research); `town Durham` for drive times from Durham; no place for the federal evaluation rule.
- **Main findings:**
  - **Paraeducator contract:** the ORPaSS agreement (paraeducators, aides and school nutrition workers) runs July 1, 2026 to June 30, 2029 and was approved by voters as Article 5 on March 10, 2026 ($543,972 for 2026-27). Paraeducators earn $21.92 to $29.87 an hour in 2026-27. On November 21, 2026 the job board listed 15 openings, including full-time special education paraeducators, a speech and language assistant at ORHS and part-time IEP program support at ORMS. No source measures whether pull-out services are missed.
  - **Middle school move:** ORMS's handbook sends special education questions to the Middle School Assistant Director of Student Services; ORMS staff visit both elementary schools in spring, and grade 4 has a Step Up Day and parent night in June.
  - **Evaluations:** policy IHB covers evaluations by district staff, by contractors the district hires and independent evaluations parents ask the district to fund. Federal rules give parents who disagree with a district evaluation the right to one independent evaluation at public expense.
  - **Article 9 (open enrollment) did not open Oyster River to other towns.** It appropriated $0 for the tuition of Oyster River students who attend open enrollment schools elsewhere, so the district would not pay the 80% the law sets. No 2026 article designated an Oyster River school an open enrollment school. Students from Barrington already attend ORHS under a tuition agreement (182 enrolled in September 2026); under policy JFAB the student's home district keeps responsibility for special education.
  - **Home education and outside students:** policy JJJ (2023) opens classes, activities and sports to home-educated, charter and private school residents by written request to the principal; it does not mention EFA students. Policy IHBG (2023) still describes the annual home education evaluation that state law repealed July 1, 2026.
  - **Solar:** ORMS's 1,450 panels (roof and parking canopy) came with the new building in 2022; the district projected it would produce 30% more energy than it used and earn almost $300,000 in Eversource rebates. No source reports actual utility savings. The FY2027 budget still carries $220,600 for ORMS electricity.
  - **Cafeterias:** composting has been district-wide since 2014, and the Sustainability Committee's November 2025 notes say all three levels "have moved away from plastics in cafeterias." No source names the kind of trays used.
  - **Coe Drive:** ORMS (1 Coe Drive) and ORHS (55 Coe Drive) share the road; ORMS's 2026-27 handbook reserves the Coe Drive loop for buses and sends cars to Dennison Road. No traffic study was found.
- **Main gaps:** any measure of missed IEP or pull-out minutes; measured energy production or savings at ORMS (the public monitoring page needs a browser); what cafeteria trays are made of; official 2026 vote counts for Articles 5 and 9; a town traffic study or 2026 Traffic Safety Committee action on Coe Drive; whether the board adopted policy JF (enrollment capacities) after its May 20, 2026 first read.

## special-ed-evaluation-request: Asking for a Special Education Evaluation, and the Timelines

- kind: rule
- question_type: rules
- description: How a parent asks the school to evaluate a child for special education, and the deadlines New Hampshire sets.

1. Under federal special education rules, a parent who disagrees with a school district's evaluation of a child has the right to an independent educational evaluation, by a qualified examiner not employed by the district, at public expense, meaning the district pays the full cost or ensures it is at no cost to the parent; the district must, without unnecessary delay, either provide it or file for a due process hearing to show its own evaluation is appropriate.
   - citation: 34 CFR 300.502(a)(3), (b)(1)–(2)
   - source: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-300.502
   - as_of: 2026-11-21
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Regulation text: "Public expense means that the public agency either pays for the full cost of the evaluation or ensures that the evaluation is otherwise provided at no cost to the parent"; "A parent has the right to an independent educational evaluation at public expense if the parent disagrees with an evaluation obtained by the public agency"; "the public agency must, without unnecessary delay, either— (i) File a due process complaint to request a hearing to show that its evaluation is appropriate; or (ii) Ensure that an independent educational evaluation is provided at public expense". If the hearing finds the district's evaluation appropriate, the parent may still get one, but not at public expense (300.502(b)(3)).
