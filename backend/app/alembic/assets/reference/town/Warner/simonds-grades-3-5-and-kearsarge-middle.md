# Simonds Grades 3–5 and Kearsarge Middle School, From Warner: The Bus to North Sutton, Free Lunch, Absences, IEP Help, Challenge and Reading Help at Simonds, the Move to Grade 6, Clubs, Military Families and Screens

Part of the Warner research (town/Warner). Gathered 2026-11-22.

## Summary

Covers the upper grades and support questions of Warner families in the Kearsarge Regional School District (SAU 65): challenge and reading help at Simonds Elementary School (kindergarten to grade 5, Warner) and what the school and district publish about enrichment, class sizes and staff; Warner's 2026-27 middle school buses (Buses 71 and 72, the Market Basket transfer and the late run) and a model drive; free and reduced-price meals; attendance, tardiness and truancy at Kearsarge Regional Middle School (KRMS, grades 6–8, North Sutton) and in the elementary handbooks; special education timelines, preschool busing and who helps parents; the move from Simonds to KRMS; welcoming families; bullying, the bus and conduct rules; clubs and programs in Warner for children who aren't in sports; military children, deployment absences and the state compact; reading specialists; and Chromebooks, books and outdoor time.

17 topics (16 shared, 1 library topic), 66 facts and 1 number table. The facts are Warner's side only: New London's `kearsarge-middle-support-and-choices.md` and `kres-new-london-and-families.md` already hold the KRMS day, phones, policies, clubs, transition events, ESOL, Child Find, Parent Partnership Group and meal prices, and are cited rather than repeated. Main sources: the district's 2026-27 route sheets, Bus Transfer Guide and late-run flyer; the KRMS 2026-27 and KRES-NL 2026-27 handbooks; Simonds' 2026-27 family handbook (updated August 2026); the 59th Annual Report (March 2026) and its October 2025 enrollment tables; the federal Civil Rights Data Collection 2023-24 and NCES Common Core of Data; the district's 2026 newsletters; the enrichment coordinator's Google Site; the Pillsbury Free Library's pages; and RSA 110-D.

**Findings that change or fill the picture:**
- Warner's middle schoolers ride Buses 71 and 72. The first morning pickups are at 6:29 a.m. (Bus 72, which reaches KRMS at 7:25 for a 7:35 start) and 6:31 a.m. (Bus 71), and the Warner late bus leaves KRMS at 4:35 p.m. with stops at the Warner Park & Ride (4:58) and Warner Town Hall (5:05).
- New Hampshire's military compact (RSA 110-D:6, V) lets a superintendent grant "additional excused absences" for a child whose parent is on duty, on leave from or just back from deployment to a combat zone or combat support posting. The library's `military-children-compact` topic lacked it. It covers National Guard members only on active duty orders.
- Simonds' own 2026-27 handbook and the district enrichment coordinator's site describe a gifted individual action plan process, a Kearsarge Math League for grades 2–5 and an enrichment coordinator, yet Simonds told the 2023-24 federal survey it had no gifted and talented program.
- Warner's preschool is the Kearsarge Regional Preschool at Bradford (the old "James House" name survives on older pages). An older FAQ says the preschool provides no transportation, but the district's IEP page lists transportation as a service.

**Main gaps:** no Simonds newsletter or school web page was readable (kearsarge.org's pages show a bot check; the 2026-27 handbook is a downloadable file and was read); no published wait time for evaluations or speech therapy; no published figure for time on Chromebooks, reading or recess; no Kearsarge military-family program, Purple Star list entry or deployment attendance rule beyond the state compact; the texts of policies JH, JICK, JICC and EEAEC weren't readable; no robotics club in Warner was found; Warner's Bus 73 and the morning Market Basket transfer times aren't printed.

## military-children-compact: Excused Absences for Deployment Under the State's Military Compact

- kind: rule
- question_type: rules
- description: What the Interstate Compact on Educational Opportunity for Military Children (RSA 110-D) and NH residency law provide for military-connected students.

1. Under New Hampshire's military children's compact, a student whose parent or guardian is an active duty member of the uniformed services and has been called to duty for, is on leave from, or has just returned from deployment to a combat zone or combat support posting "shall be granted additional excused absences at the discretion of the local education agency superintendent" to visit the parent or guardian about that leave or deployment.
   - citation: RSA 110-D:6, V (Article V of the compact, "Absence as related to deployment activities")
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/VIII/110-D/110-D-6.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-22
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text: "V. Absence as related to deployment activities. A student whose parent or legal guardian is an active duty member of the uniformed services, as defined by the compact, and has been called to duty for, is on leave from, or immediately returned from deployment to a combat zone or combat support posting, shall be granted additional excused absences at the discretion of the local education agency superintendent to visit with his or her parent or legal guardian relative to such leave or deployment of the parent or guardian." Who is covered is in RSA 110-D:4: members of the National Guard and Reserve only "on active duty orders pursuant to 10 U.S.C. chapters 1209 and 1211," and not "inactive members of the national guard and military reserves" (library military-children-compact 1–2). The statute sets no number of days and says nothing about drill weekends or family vacations. The library's summary of the compact (military-children-compact 1–9) doesn't include this paragraph.
