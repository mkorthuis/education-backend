# New Boston Central School and Young Families

Part of the New Boston research (town/New Boston). Gathered November 21, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers New Boston Central School (NBCS, preschool through grade 6) and the questions young families ask: the new full-day kindergarten and how big its classes are planned to be, the Readiness class, after-school care (the town Recreation program and its waitlist, the YMCA bus from NBCS to Goffstown, and the walk-over care at Roots and Wings School), the school day and drop-off rules, snow days and the calendar, reading help, test results against Bedford and Amherst, how the school reaches families, recess, phones and smartwatches, the preschool, meals and their privacy rules, allergies and emergency care, supervision at recess and dismissal, and classroom placement of twins.

- **16 topics, 79 facts and 5 number tables.** Places: `district New Boston` (most facts), `town New Boston` (Recreation, child care, fire station, plowing). No new state rules were written; the state rules on recess, allergies, twins, snow days, meal privacy, phones and dyslexia screening already exist in the library or earlier towns' files and are cited in the question table.
- **New data concepts:** the district's kindergarten section-size planning table, projected 2027-28 class sizes by grade, Readiness and first-grade class sizes since 2010-11, kindergarten and first-grade June enrollments, a derived grade-by-grade 2026 test comparison with Bedford, Amherst and Goffstown, the after-school options that reach NBCS with their posted prices, the 2026-27 meal prices, and a derived drive distance from the fire station to the school.
- **Main sources:** the NBCS 2026-27 student handbook (with the 2026-27 calendar), New Boston School Board minutes from June 2025 to October 2026 and the FY28 requested budget packet (November 18, 2026), the October 28, 2026 Readiness presentation, New Boston School District policies (JG, JICJ, IMAH, JLCF, EBCD, IC, JLCE, JLCD, EF-R, JHC, IHBC), the Title I parent handbook, the Town of New Boston's Recreation and Fire Department pages, the Granite YMCA's school-age care page and family handbook, Roots and Wings School's registration page, and the site's own test-score data.
- **Main findings:**
  - Full-day kindergarten began in fall 2026 after voters approved Article 3 in March 2026. The district's planning range is 15 to 18 children a class; 33 children were registered in April 2026. No 2026-27 kindergarten section count has been published; the FY28 budget packet projects 3 kindergarten classes averaging 12 children in 2027-28 and adds 2 kindergarten support paraeducators.
  - The Readiness class (a year between kindergarten and first grade, run since 1976) continues in 2026-27 with 10 children; first grade averages 19. In October 2026 the principal said he would place "significant value" on smaller primary classes for all students, and the board has not yet decided Readiness's future.
  - The town Recreation after-school program (White Buildings beside NBCS, until 6:00 p.m., $275 a month) listed "over 55 children" on its waitlist as of May 2025, the latest figure posted. The YMCA Allard Center in Goffstown buses children from NBCS (2:00–6:00 p.m., $128 or $137 a week), and Roots and Wings School in New Boston walks children from NBCS to its after-care ($20–$22 a day, pickup by 5:30 p.m.) and offers before-care from 7:00 a.m. with a walk to NBCS at 7:45.
  - NBCS opens at 7:45 a.m., with no supervision before then; school starts at 8:00 and dismisses walkers at 2:10 and buses at 2:15. The 2026-27 calendar has one early release day, the last day (June 16, 2027, at noon).
  - In 2026, NBCS's grades 3–6 were above the state in reading in every grade and in math in grades 3, 4 and 6. Bedford was higher than NBCS in every grade and subject; Amherst was higher in math in grades 4–6 and in grade 3 reading.
  - School meals rose to $2.50 (breakfast) and $4.00 (lunch) for 2026-27. Every student pays through the same ID-number account, and policy says a child who asks for a meal gets one even with no money in the account.
- **Main gaps:** the actual 2026-27 kindergarten section count and class sizes (NH DOE's school enrollment report was not requested because it blocks automated requests); a current Recreation waitlist count; how the Recreation program handles no-school and early release days; NBCS's food allergy management plan (state law requires one to be public; none was found on the SAU 19 site); recess supervision ratios; any count of snow or mud-season closings; how ambulance service is staffed in New Boston; any Weare child care program that picks up at NBCS; NBCS preschool eligibility and tuition for "role model" seats.
