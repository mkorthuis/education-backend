# Pembroke: Money, Taxes, Tuition and Governance

Part of the Pembroke research (town/Pembroke). Gathered November 21, 2026.

## Summary

**What's covered:** 9 topics, 88 facts and 2 number tables on the Pembroke School District's money and governance.
- **Budgets:** the FY27 (2026-27) budget the March 7, 2026 meeting adopted, and why it rose (a 30.3% health insurance increase, special education, contracts). The FY26 budget voters amended upward in 2025, and the $3,000,000 cut at the 2024 meeting with the layoffs that followed. The FY28 proposal of November 17, 2026 ($36,881,595, +3.9%). Where the line-item budget is posted.
- **Taxes and state aid:** tax year 2026's final rate ($22.74; local school $14.23, set November 4, 2026) and the 2025 calculation. The Budget Committee's March 2026 projection. The 2024 revaluation. The district's own breakdown of its adequacy aid losses and its revenue mix (tuition 44%, state aid 38%). A **derived** estimate of the next hold-harmless step. The Question 2 recount that confirmed the school tax cap (2,114 to 1,231).
- **Tuition contracts:** Pembroke Academy's 2026-27 rate ($18,434.70). The sending districts' budgeted students (Allenstown 139, Chichester 62, Epsom 172). The Deerfield and Hooksett agreements, policy JFAB, and the open enrollment vote that allows up to 204 more nonresident students at the Academy.
- **SAU 53:** each district's share of SAU costs (Pembroke 37.6% for FY27, 38.1% for FY28), how the superintendent's pay was split, the board structure, and the separate SAU warrant article and 6% SAU cap.
- **Buildings and history:** no bonded debt, the energy lease, $2.4 million in school trust funds (December 2025), the FY28 capital plan (a $300,000 roof request), the paused Hill School project ($39 million option), and the 2018–2019 closing and sale of the Pembroke Village School.
- **Town:** 2024 revaluation home prices, proposed 2026 elderly exemption amounts, and how tax bills are timed.

**Main sources:** the Pembroke School District pages and Google Drive folders on sau.sau53.org: warrants, the DRA MS-27 form, line-item budgets, budget summaries, the Budget Committee's tax impact sheet, school district meeting and board minutes from 2018 to 2026, agreements and policies. Also the Town of Pembroke's 2025 annual report, its 2026 DRA tax rate sheet, revaluation release and capital improvement plan; the sending districts' 2026 budget packets (gathered November 8 in `additional-data/raw/districts/batch-7/`); the Hooksett–Pembroke agreement (from the Hooksett research); WMUR's Question 2 tally; and the site's database, read only.

| P4.3 | Voting down the budget at Town Meeting brings a "default budget" | Pembroke has no default budget. The school budget is decided at a traditional annual school district meeting, not SB2. Voters can amend the figure up or down on the floor, up to 10% above the Budget Committee's total ($39,052,830 in 2026). In 2024 the adopted budget was $3,000,000 below the board's proposal; in 2025 voters raised the Budget Committee's figure by $400,000. |
| P10.1 | Pembroke Academy takes high schoolers from Allenstown, Epsom, Chichester "and even some from Hooksett" | True; Deerfield also sends a few. Hooksett's agreement allows up to 20 a grade. | P10.2 | Out-of-town students make classes and hallways overcrowded | Not shown. Pembroke Academy's enrollment fell from 832 (2015-16) to 744 (2024-25) (site data, **derived**). No building capacity figure was found. The district offered 10 open enrollment seats a grade at the Academy for 2026-27. |
| P10.3 | "Pembroke runs the elementary and middle schools on its own" | Pembroke runs all three of its schools, Pembroke Academy included. SAU 53 provides the superintendent, business office and other central services to Pembroke and four other districts. |
| P13.1 | "Fewer kids in town than 20 years ago", yet the budget goes up every year | The site's figures go back only to 2013-14. Resident students fell from 1,155 (2013-14) to 967 (2025-26), and the FY21-to-FY26 operating budget rose 25.2% (both **derived**). Voters set each budget at the annual meeting; in 2025 they raised the Budget Committee's figure by $400,000. |
| P13.3 | "About to get hit with a huge bond for a new roof or heating system?" | No bond is pending: the district has no bonded debt, and no bond was on the 2025 or 2026 warrants. The FY28 capital plan asks for $300,000 for roofs from an existing reserve. A $39–45 million Hill School project was planned for a March 2025 bond vote but was not put on that warrant. |
| P15.2 | The old Pembroke Village School | It closed in June 2019, when its students moved to Pembroke Hill. The board sold it in September 2019 (a broker reported $151,000), and it is being converted to apartments. All K–4 students attend Pembroke Hill. |
| P15.3 | Taxes may "jump another $1,000 just from school costs" | The 2026 local school rate rose $0.48, about $220 on a home assessed at the 2024 median sale price of $458,500 (**derived**). A $1,000 school-only rise on that home would take about $2.18 per $1,000 (**derived**). The voter-adopted tax cap limits the school levy's growth from FY2028 unless a 3/5 vote overrides it. |
| P19.1 | Central office costs "split between" the five towns | True. FY27 shares are Pembroke 37.6%, Epsom 17.4%, Allenstown 13.6% and Chichester 10.0%; Deerfield's share wasn't found in its budget papers. SAU 53's written formula was not found. |
| P19.2 | The state "keeps phasing out those Hold Harmless grants" | True: the phase-down is scheduled in law, at 60% of FY2025 in FY2028. For Pembroke that is about $269,811 less, roughly $0.21 per $1,000 if nothing else changes (**derived**). |
| P13.2 | Losing teachers to Concord or Hooksett over pay | Starting pay (BA, step 1) in 2025-26: Pembroke $43,983, Hooksett $44,800, Concord $52,695 (site data and the town report). No source counts teachers leaving Pembroke, or where they go. |
