# Pembroke Academy and Other High Schools From Epsom

Part of the Epsom research (town/Epsom). Gathered November 21, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers Epsom's side of high school: how Epsom students get to Pembroke Academy (PA) and whether a bus exists, other high schools an Epsom family might choose (Coe-Brown Northwood Academy, Prospect Mountain) and the routes the district names (manifest hardship, best interest, open enrollment and the pending Supreme Court appeal), the move from Epsom Central's small 8th grade to PA, what PA's own reports to the Epsom board and the AREA board say about AP, dual enrollment, the CRTC and graduation, special education at PA, PA's safety measures, attendance and sports rules that bear on travel teams, the NHIAA's rule on outside teams, personal finance and work skills, and local scholarships open to Epsom graduates. Pembroke Academy's courses, CRTC rules, counselors, buses and outcomes are already in `town/Pembroke/pembroke-academy.md`; this file cites those facts and adds only what they lack.

- **12 fact topics, 45 facts and 2 number tables.** Raw copies are in `raw/high/` (about 5 MB): twelve Epsom School Board minutes (March 2020 to November 10, 2026), the 2026 deliberative session budget packet (text), PA's October 22, 2025 AREA presentation, Epsom Central's 2026-27 bus routes, PA's handbook (OCR of the scanned PDF saved in the Pembroke research), PA's athletic policy, Exceptional Services and registration pages, PA's 2026 scholarship handout, the NHIAA's 2026-27 eligibility by-laws, a derived CRDC comparison and derived drive times.
- **Places:** 19 facts use `district Epsom`, 20 `district Pembroke` (PA itself), 2 `district Coe-Brown Northwood Academy`, 1 `district Concord` (the CRTC), 1 `town Epsom`, and 2 have no place (the NHIAA rule).
- **New data concepts:** Epsom's missing high school bus and its history; the routes besides PA that Epsom's superintendent names, and the manifest hardship case now at the New Hampshire Supreme Court (described without naming the family); Epsom's estimated split of high school students between PA and Prospect Mountain; Coe-Brown's private-tuition rule for towns without an agreement; Epsom students' counts in PA's dual enrollment courses; PA's own AP pass claim and graduation figures given to the Epsom board; PA's digital hall passes and camera rule; the NHIAA non-school competition rule; and Epsom-named scholarships.

## school-sports-rules: The NHIAA's Rule on Outside Teams

- kind: rule
- question_type: rules
- description: New Hampshire law and NHIAA rules for school sports: concussion removal, return to play and return to learning, medical coverage at practices and games, physicals, cooperative teams and their no-cut rule

1. Under the NHIAA's 2026-27 by-laws, a member of a New Hampshire high school team may not miss a school practice or game to play with an out-of-school team (including tournaments, showcases and combines) unless the principal and athletic director grant a waiver, which "shall only be granted in extraordinary circumstances"; a first violation costs the next four events or three weeks of the season, whichever is greater, and a second makes the student ineligible for the rest of the school year.
   - citation: NHIAA Handbook 2026-27, By-Law Article II (Eligibility), Sect. 7: Non-School Competition
   - source: https://assets-rst7.rschooltoday.com/rst7files/uploads/sites/586/2026/09/21142244/4HB-26-27-II-Eligibility.pdf
   - as_of: 2026-11-21

   - note: Text: "Bona fide members ... of a school team are prevented from missing a high school practice or competition to compete with an out-of-school team, practice or competition to include tournaments, showcases, combines or other athletic events. Whenever a conflict arises between the high school team practice/competition and an out-of-school practice/competition on the same day, the high school team practice/competition must be honored ... It is expressly understood that waivers shall not be granted on a regular basis and shall only be granted in extraordinary circumstances"; "for the first time shall be declared ineligible for the next four (4) consecutive interscholastic events or three (3) weeks of a season ... whichever is greater"; "a second or subsequent time shall be declared ineligible ... for the balance of the school year."
