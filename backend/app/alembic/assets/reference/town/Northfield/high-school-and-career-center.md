# Winnisquam Regional High School and the Career Center, From Northfield

Part of the Northfield research (town/Northfield). Gathered November 21, 2026.

## Summary

Covers Winnisquam Regional High School (WRHS, grades 9–12, in Tilton) and the Winnisquam Career Center (WiCC) next door, as seen from Northfield, for the questions assigned to agent 3. The Tilton research (`town/Tilton/high-school-and-wicc.md`) already covers WRHS and WiCC in depth: WiCC's five programs, credits and enrollment; the Huot Center's bus and schedule; AP, honors and Early College; ELOs, credit recovery, the Diploma Academy and HiSET; counseling, FAFSA help and graduates' plans. Those facts are cited, not repeated. This file adds Northfield's side and what Tilton's file lacked:
- how far Northfield is from WRHS, the Huot Center, Concord's career center, the community colleges and the nearby charter schools, and that Concord's center doesn't serve Winnisquam;
- WiCC's daily class time, the credentials its pages name (and the lack of named college credit), the state's cosmetology license hours, and Winnisquam students by WiCC program in 2025-26;
- ApprenticeshipNH's listing of WRHS as a high school partner, and what the trades WiCC and Huot point to pay in Central New Hampshire;
- WRHS's outdoor PE course, where WRHS appears in the NHIAA's 2026-27 sport alignments (no football or girls' soccer listed), and the hockey and football co-op budget decisions;
- WRHS's arts and music courses, its arts credit requirement, the middle school's Unified Arts, and the one arts-related cut found (a 0.4 "UA" position);
- the attendance and truancy policy (JH) and WRHS's absence rules, with a **derived** comparison of truancy counts at nearby high schools;
- North Country Charter Academy's distance and supported districts, the district's reassignment policy (JCA) and its transfer-credit rule (IK).

**Counts:** 10 topics, 31 facts and 5 number tables.

**Main sources:** the WRHS 2026-27 Comprehensive Guide (re-exported November 21, 2026); WiCC's website; district policies JH, JCA and IK; school board minutes (December 2025 to the November 16, 2026 draft) and the October 19, 2026 packet, copied from the Tilton research's same-day downloads; the NHIAA Handbook 2026-27 sport articles; the Concord Regional Technical Center's sending-schools page; North Country Charter Academy's website; ApprenticeshipNH's East-Central hub page; RSA 313-A:11 and 313-A:24; the BLS OEWS API (May 2025); OpenStreetMap Nominatim and OSRM routes; and the site's database (truancy counts, enrollment).

**Main gaps:** how much WiCC class time is shop or lab work, and how many cosmetology hours WiCC credits; WRHS's FAFSA completion rate (the federal site didn't respond); a chronic absenteeism rate for WRHS; any survey of how residents or graduates rate WRHS's preparation for work; any named WRHS or WiCC apprenticeship placement; how many WiCC students come from Northfield; arts staffing and course enrollment over time.

**Topic note:** no new topics proposed. `occupational-licensing-rules` gets two state rules (cosmetology); `local-occupation-wages` gets a Northfield-area table.

**Questions answered wholly by earlier files:** 4 of 13 (P4.3, P15.1, P15.2, P15.3). For P15.2 this file adds only Northfield's drive distances to LRCC and NHTI.

## occupational-licensing-rules: Cosmetology Licenses and Apprentice Certificates in New Hampshire

- kind: rule
- question_type: rules
- description: New Hampshire licensing rules for trades and health careers that career programs prepare students for: plumbing and fuel gas licenses, apprentice and trainee licenses, required training and work hours, exams and minimum ages, and nursing assistant (LNA) licensing

1. Under New Hampshire law, a cosmetologist's license requires good professional character, a high school diploma or its equivalent, at least 1,200 hours of training in a board-approved cosmetology school or at least 3,000 hours spread over at least 18 months under a cosmetologist licensed and practicing in the state for at least 2 years, a passing exam and a fee.
   - citation: RSA 313-A:11, I
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XXX/313-A/313-A-11.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-21
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text: "(c) Have received training of: (1) A minimum of 1,200 hours of training in a school of cosmetology approved by the board; or (2) A minimum of 3,000 hours distributed over a period of at least 18 months under a licensed cosmetologist who has engaged in the practice of cosmetology within the state for at least 2 years; (d) Pass an examination." Source note: "2024, 327:20, eff. July 1, 2024. 2025, 91:1, eff. Aug. 9, 2025." Paragraph II lets an applicant who lacks the education or hours petition for an exemption. WiCC doesn't say how many hours its Cosmetology I and II count toward this (cte-center-programs 6). Raw: raw/high/other/rsa-313-A-11.txt
2. Under New Hampshire law, no one may start a barbering, cosmetology or esthetics apprenticeship or enroll in such a school without first registering as an apprentice and getting an apprentice certificate, which requires being at least 16, paying a fee and good character, and a salon or barbershop may have no more than one apprentice per licensed professional (barbers may have two).

## district-attendance-zones-and-transfers: Leaving Winnisquam for Another School or Program, and Coming Back

- kind: local
- question_type: local_practice
- description: How a district assigns students to schools by home address, its feeder pattern from elementary to middle to high school, redistricting history and plans, proof of residency, and requests to attend a different school (added from the Manchester research)

1. Under Winnisquam Regional's policy JCA, a parent who thinks another school would serve a child better, including a public school or academy in another district, sends a written request to the superintendent, who meets with the parent within 10 days and decides in writing within five school days; if approved, the district sets the tuition with the receiving school and the board approves it, the parent is responsible for transportation, and the reassignment lasts through the next school year before review, while a denied request can go to a school board hearing on "manifest educational hardship."
   - citation: WRSD Policy JCA, Change of Class of School or Assignment, Best Interests and Manifest Hardship (approved December 18, 2023)
   - source: https://drive.google.com/file/d/1Ekg9tuUEMHho37TymzZ5xnZBB8BnXnic/view

   - place: district Winnisquam Regional
   - note: Text: "the parent/guardian shall submit to the Superintendent a written request stating why and/or how the child's best interests warrant reassignment"; "the Superintendent shall schedule a meeting ... to be held within 10 days of receiving the request"; "Within five school days of the reassignment meeting, the Superintendent shall deliver to the parent/guardian a written determination"; "the Superintendent shall work with the Superintendent or administrator of the receiving school district/approved school to establish a tuition rate"; "Transportation for a student reassigned to a school in another district under this Section A (best interest) shall be the responsibility of the parent/guardian"; "A reassignment on the basis of best interest of the student shall be limited to no longer than the end of the ensuing school year."
