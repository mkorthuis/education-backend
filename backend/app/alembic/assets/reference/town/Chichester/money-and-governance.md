# Chichester: Money, Taxes, Tuition, Buildings and Governance

Part of the Chichester research (town/Chichester). Gathered November 22, 2026.

## Summary

**What's covered:** 13 topics, 62 facts and 8 number tables on the Chichester School District's money and governance and the town's side of taxes and home values. One fact adds a statewide rule (RSA 32:10) to the library topic `budget-traditional-district-meeting`.
- **Budget and cost per pupil:** the district's own posting ($25,916 for 2024-25) and the site's NH DOE figure ($25,210) are the same series 2.8% apart; Central School's enrollment fell to 188 in 2023-24 but was 203 in October 2025 and 238 in August 2026. FY2627 passed 57–24 at $8,504,806 (the district's "2.87%" compares against a budget that included $180,000 of trust transfers; like-for-like it is 5.2%, **derived**). The FY2728 draft is $9,134,065 (+5.4%), with high school tuition up $465,150 (62 to 76 students at an estimated $21,159.22). Special education spending and special education transportation are the other large drivers.
- **Tuition against the building:** Pembroke Academy tuition is 13.4% of the FY2627 operating budget (about $1.69 per $1,000 of assessed value, **derived**) and 17.6% of the FY2728 draft. Its 2026-27 rate ($18,434.70) is 12.4% below Concord High and 14.7% below Coe-Brown; no Pittsfield High School tuition rate was found. The AREA agreement's text isn't posted; the library's AREA rules and Epsom's file explain how leaving works; Chichester's board records only that opening the agreement "has been reviewed and explored several times."
- **Taxes and the state cap:** the school portion of the rate was $15.61, $11.12, $11.35 and $11.57 in 2022 to 2025 (the 2023 drop followed the revaluation); the estimate for 2026 is $12.54 (+$0.97, or $96.65 per $100,000 assessed). Chichester voted 834–560 (59.8%) on Question 2, three votes short of 60% in the unofficial count; a recount was set for November 18. The 2026 rate isn't set yet.
- **Governance and information:** traditional March meeting (no deliberative session); floor amendments to cut the budget failed in 2025 (a 9% cut, 55–84) and none was offered in 2026; the cuts that were made came earlier, in the board's winter reviews. An advisory budget committee is hard to fill (none in 2025 and none formed as of October 27, 2026 for the FY2728 budget); the $7,000 recording article failed.
- **Buildings:** a $9.5 to $10 million five-year capital plan (roof replacement first) is planned for the March 2027 warrant, with cash, lease-purchase or bond still open; the district carries no debt and has $1.05 million in trust funds.
- **SAU 53 and sharing:** Chichester's SAU share is $253,158 (10.0%), 3.0% of its operating budget; Epsom is already in the same SAU, and Pittsfield buys superintendent services from Concord for $260,000. The town and school share a few services but no crews.
- **Home values:** Chichester's typical value ($543,166, ZIP 03258) is below Bow's and Hopkinton's but rose about as fast as theirs since 2023.

**Main sources:** the Chichester board and budget pages on SAU 53's site and their Drive folders (FY2627 and FY2728 budget packets and handouts, hearing presentations, the March 2026 warrant and meeting minutes, the March 2025 certified meeting minutes, the RSA 189:76 posting, board minutes July 2025 to October 2026, agendas), the Town of Chichester's website (Select Board minutes, the advisory committee guidelines, tax rate and highway pages), NH Revenue Administration's RSA 32:10 text, Zillow's ZIP code home value file, the site's database, and earlier files for Pembroke, Epsom, Allenstown, Deerfield, Pittsfield and the library.

## budget-traditional-district-meeting: The Traditional School District Meeting

- kind: how_it_works
- question_type: governance_money
- description: How a school budget is adopted at a traditional annual school district meeting.

1. Under RSA 32:10, a governing body may move an unspent balance from one appropriation to another during the year, but the total spent may not exceed the total the district meeting appropriated; an amount voted under a special warrant article or for a capital reserve fund may be used only for its stated purpose; and if the meeting votes separately on purposes within a budget, that does not remove the governing body's power to transfer, except that a purpose the meeting deletes or reduces to zero counts as one for which no appropriation is made.
   - citation: RSA 32:10, I(a), I(d), I(e) and II
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/III/32/32-10.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-22
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Statute text: "The total amount spent shall not exceed the total amount appropriated at the town or district meeting."; "Any amount appropriated at the meeting under a special warrant article, or to a capital reserve fund pursuant to RSA 35:5, may be used only for the purpose specified in that article and shall not be transferred."; "The town or district meeting may vote separately on individual purposes of appropriation contained within any warrant article or budget, but such a separate vote shall not affect the governing body's legal authority to transfer appropriations, provided, however, that if the meeting deletes a purpose, or reduces the amount appropriated for that purpose to zero ... that purpose or article shall be deemed one for which no appropriation is made." Which school districts the chapter reaches and how the budget law applies to a district without a budget committee were not checked; at Chichester's March 8, 2025 meeting the district's attorney told voters the purpose of a warrant article cannot be changed and the board decides where the money goes (school-district-meeting-minutes-2025-03-08-clean.txt, p. 7).
