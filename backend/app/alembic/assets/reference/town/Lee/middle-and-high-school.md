# Oyster River Middle and High School From Lee

Part of the Lee research (town/Lee). Gathered November 21, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers Oyster River Middle School (grades 5–8) and Oyster River High School (grades 9–12), both on Coe Drive in Durham, from a Lee family's point of view: UNH courses, advanced math at the middle school, career and technical education (CTE) and its buses, support for the trades, the move from Mast Way to the middle school for a child with an IEP, AP courses and results, Barrington's tuition students, college counseling and scholarships, competency grading, 5th-grade class sizes, clubs and sports at the middle school, late buses, work readiness and business partnerships, practical life-skills courses, and night classes and certificates near Lee. Much of this was already gathered in `town/Durham/middle-and-high-school.md` and `town/Durham/support-and-choices.md`; the question table cites those facts by topic and number, and this file adds only what they lack.

- **12 fact topics, 63 facts and 4 number tables.** All 12 are shared topics from `shared-topics.md`. Places: `district Oyster River Coop` for the district and its schools (56 facts), `town Lee` for adult learning near Lee (7 facts, with drive times from Lee Town Hall). No new topic is proposed.
- **Tables:** `oyster_river_ms_hs_enrollment_oct2026` (middle and high school enrollment by grade, with tuition students, October 1, 2026), `oyster_river_5_12_residents_by_town_oct2026` (Durham, Lee and Madbury students in grades 5–12), `orhs_to_cte_centers_drive_times` and `lee_to_adult_learning_drive_times` (derived with OSRM).
- **Raw copies** are in `raw/high/` (about 20 MB): the high school's 2026-27 Program of Studies, profile and handbook; the middle school's handbook, athletic handbook, athletics, clubs and late-bus pages and staff directory; the district's 2026 annual report; the October 7, 2026 board back-up (with a rendered image of the enrollment page); five sets of board minutes (`raw/high/minutes/`); the counseling department's seniors, scholarships, resources and college-fair pages, its posted annual calendar and the 2026 local scholarship packet; policies IFA and IIB; Great Bay Community College and UNH continuing education pages (`raw/high/adult/`); and Nominatim and OSRM results (`raw/high/geo/`, requested as `nhfacts-research`).
- **Main findings:**
  - **Advanced math:** besides Grade 8 Algebra (taken by some 7th graders), some middle schoolers take high school math at ORHS; the district runs two shuttles between the schools every other day for it, and the middle school keeps its blue/white schedule partly for these students.
  - **The move to the middle school:** the district told the board in November 2026 that it sees a recurring dip in performance in grades 4–6 and moved 5th grade to Reveal Math; its special education report names the 4th-to-5th move a priority. ORMS had 155 5th graders on October 1, 2026 (62 from Lee), and the principal's plan raised 5th-grade teachers from 6 to 8 (about 19 students per teacher, derived).
  - **CTE:** ORHS students start CTE in grade 10. Welding and electrical are at Dover's center; Rochester has neither. The district runs 12 CTE shuttles a day. ORHS's CTE count rose from 10 (2015-16) to 101 (2024-25), with 123 enrolled in September 2026.
  - **Late buses:** the high school's late bus leaves at 4:30 p.m., Monday to Thursday; the middle school's club page times activities to end about 4:10–4:15 "in time to catch the late bus" and says a club ending at 4:30 has no late bus.
  - **Life skills:** ORHS offers a half-credit Personal Finance course (budgeting, saving, investing, credit), and from the Class of 2030 a personal finance half credit and a Capstone Experience are required (25 credits). No course description mentions taxes or car maintenance; automotive programs are at the CTE centers.
- **Main gaps:** middle school practice end times and late-bus routes and stops in Lee; how many middle schoolers take high school math; how CTE students' days are split; any measure of whether Barrington's students affect class sizes or which programs their tuition supports; results of the counseling department's senior exit survey; how often the UNH course is used; Oyster River's yearly gifted-and-talented report to NH DOE.
