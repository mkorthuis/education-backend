# Student Support and School Choices for Lee Families (Oyster River)

Part of the Lee research (town/Lee). Gathered November 21, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers support and choice questions from Lee families in the Oyster River Cooperative School District (SAU 5), with Mast Way School in Lee as the starting point: getting an IEP changed and who to ask at Mast Way, Mast Way's special education staff and counts, inclusion and pull-out (and what isn't published), grandparents and paper or phone contact, counseling for family changes, tutoring and the new math, homeschooled children in middle school sports and high school band, the home education paperwork premise, placement and credits on entering Oyster River High, the district's nondiscrimination complaint steps, mid-year enrollment and records, military families, English learners and translation, and the race and ethnicity of Lee's children and Oyster River's students.

- **Places used:** `district Oyster River Coop` for Mast Way, the other schools, policies, staff and the board; `town Lee` for the 2020 Census figures; no place for the NHIAA by-law.
- **Main findings:**
  - **IEP changes:** a parent may ask for an IEP meeting at any time; a written request must lead to a meeting, or a written refusal, within 21 days (state rule, in Belmont's file; the guide Oyster River links says the same). Mast Way sends learning concerns to the teacher or the principal; Mast Way lists three special education teachers, an "Extended Resource Room," a speech-language pathologist, a psychologist and two behavior analysts. On October 1, 2025, 38 Mast Way students received special education (about 10.3%, derived); district-wide, 16.2%.
  - **Inclusion:** the district's annual report commits to "the least restrictive environment" and lists a state monitoring visit with classroom observations and family feedback this year. No public source says how much time Mast Way children spend outside regular classes or describes the Extended Resource Room; NH DOE's district pages refused automated requests.
  - **Homeschooling:** the premise of yearly paperwork is out of date. Since July 1, 2026 New Hampshire requires no annual filing; a declaration is needed only to use district programs or when leaving a public school (library). Starting spring 2027, ORMS teams follow NHIAA Middle School League rules, which send home-educated athletes to the by-law's Section 13. ORHS band is a half-credit course, so it goes through policy JJJ's course request. In February 2026 the board leaned toward letting EFA students take part for a fee; no such policy was found by November 21, 2026.
  - **9th grade and transfers:** every ORHS 9th grader takes Essentials of English. Students who entered 9th grade in 2026-27 or later need 25 credits and a capstone, against 22 credits before. Credits from accredited schools transfer at face value (Durham).
  - **Military families:** no Oyster River buddy or transition program for military children was found. The interstate compact covers Guard members only on Title 10 active-duty orders (library). The site's 2026 test data counts 30–35 military-connected students tested district-wide.
  - **Language:** the 2026-27 new-student form asks for a family's preferred language for automated emails, and policy IJO commits to "reasonable efforts" in a family's primary language. No source says progress reports are translated.
  - **Diversity:** NCES counted Mast Way at 83.3% White in 2024-25 (derived), near New Hampshire's 82.9% (fall 2022). The 2020 Census counted 83.0% of Lee's 1,024 children as non-Hispanic White, against 79.8% statewide (derived).
- **Main gaps:** time children with IEPs spend outside regular classes and the meaning of "Extended Resource Room"; NH DOE's district special education data (HTTP 403); any after-school tutoring at Mast Way or the Lee Public Library (the town site returned 403); translated report cards; a current Purple Star school list; any student climate survey results; how lower-income families or students of color feel at school, which no source measures.
