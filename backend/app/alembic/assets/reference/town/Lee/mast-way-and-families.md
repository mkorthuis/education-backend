# Mast Way and Young Families in Lee

Part of the Lee research (town/Lee). Gathered November 21, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers Lee's side of the Oyster River Cooperative School District's elementary years: drop-off and pick-up at Mast Way, the bus stops and times on Lee's roads (for Mast Way, Moharimet and the middle and high school), which Lee neighborhoods are on Moharimet's buses, delays, days off and the one early release of 2026-27, class sizes by grade at Mast Way, reading screening in kindergarten, outdoor and farm learning, screen time, reaching teachers and the counselor, help with food and other costs, summer camps and youth sports, the nearest Head Start, and Lee's resident-owned manufactured home community. Much of Mast Way's ground is already covered in `town/Durham/elementary-and-early-years.md` and the other Durham files; the question table points to their facts rather than repeating them.

- **13 topics, 34 facts, 2 number tables.** All topics use shared slugs from `town/shared-topics.md`. No new topic is proposed. Raw copies are in `raw/elementary/` (about 11 MB): the 2026-27 Mast Way handbook and parent drop-off and pick-up maps, the district's closing and delay page, the 2026-27 calendar, Mast Way and district news posts (`news/`), three board minutes (`board/`), the Seven Rivers and R.E.A.C.H. guides, the Health Office resource guide, ORYA's winter programs page (`orya/`), the NH Community Loan Fund's list of resident-owned communities, 197 bus lookups along Lee's roads (192 location searches and 5 address searches) with a CSV of the 291 stop times they returned (`infofinder/`), and OpenStreetMap routing and place lookups (`drive/`).
- **Sources:** orcsd.org pages and posts; the Mast Way 2026-27 handbook; School Board minutes of June 3, August 5 and November 4, 2026; the district's bus lookup (Infofinder i), queried at points along every named road in Lee; Durham Parks and Recreation's Seven Rivers guide; the Oyster River Youth Association's site; the NH Community Loan Fund; OSRM and Nominatim (under `nhfacts-research`); and the site's own database (class size, child care) to check premises.
- **Main gaps:** the Town of Lee's website (leenh.org) refused automated requests, so Lee recreation, Lee Public Library summer programs, the town's general assistance page and its housing chapter were not read; Live and Learn's own terms (whether its Mast Way program runs on delays, early releases and workshop days, and what "Rising Hawk" is) were not found; the early-release time for December 23; how many Lee children attend Moharimet; minutes of screen time a day; and whether ORYA or the summer camps offer fee help.
- **Spot-checked against raw copies:** the delay table's "Mast Way ... 10:50 a.m." and "* When school is delayed, there is no morning PEP." (`school-closing-delay.txt`); "In grades K-6, we intentionally limit screen time during the school day and keep laptops at school unless they are needed for a specific assignment." (`news/or-connection-2026-09-24.txt`); and the lookup's "Hayes Rd @ Chestnut Way","stopTime":"08:27 AM" on trip "AM MO 10" for MOH (`infofinder/loc-chestnut-way.json`).

| P6.1 | Mast Way may be overcrowded | Mixed. On October 1, 2026, Mast Way's grades came to about 18–19 children per classroom teacher, within the board's guidelines of 20–22 for grades 1–4 and a little over its 18 for kindergarten. The site's data gives 2025-26 averages of 18.0 (grades 1–2) and 18.8 (grades 3–4). The 2024 space audit found enough general classrooms but a cafeteria at code capacity and overbooked small rooms; the March 2026 expansion bond failed. |
| P6.2 | Lee may have no public pre-K | Lee has no public preschool of its own. The district's preschool is at the high school in Durham, mainly for children with disabilities, with some community children. | P6.3 | School may be assigned strictly by street | No. Policy IIB-A puts balancing grade sizes between the schools ahead of street zones. In the bus lookup, most Lee stops are on Mast Way routes, but Lee's northeast corner north of Route 4 (Old Mill Road, Pinkham Road at Daniels Drive, Hayes Road at Chestnut Way) is on a Moharimet route. |
| P7.1 | There may be regular early-release days | Not in 2026-27. The adopted calendar has no early releases. Its five teacher workshop days are full days off. The board added one early release, December 23. On a two-hour delay, Mast Way starts at 10:50 and still dismisses at 3:25. |
| P9.1 | Rides from the far side of Lee may be long | The lookup shows middle and high school pickups from 6:53 to 7:04 a.m. on Lee's southern and western roads, for an 8:15 start, and drop-offs as late as 4:01 p.m. (61 minutes after the 3:00 dismissal) on Harvey Mill and Old Nottingham Roads. The district's stated goal is a ride of no more than 40 minutes. |
| P9.2 | Everything may be on laptops now | No. The district says it limits screen time in grades K–6 and keeps laptops at school. Mast Way planted a courtyard food forest in 2026, a naturalist leads a fourth-grade class on a walk each week, third graders did field science with UNH, and Moharimet's 2026-27 theme is a "Year of Farming." |
| P9.3 | There may be a morning late bus | None was found. Drivers "cannot wait," and a bus does not return for a child who misses it. The district's "late run" activity buses are afternoon buses for the middle and high school. No district carpool program was found. |
| P11.3 | Elementary children may use school iPads or Chromebooks | Partly right. The district's 1:1 program issues laptops, and iPads to some younger students; Mast Way has a digital learning class. No source gives minutes of use a day. |
| P14.1 | Manufactured home communities may get different bus or after-school service | No source shows a difference. Bus eligibility under Policy EEA depends only on distance from school, and after-school programs are open to all Oyster River students for a fee. The data can't tell how families experience it. Lee's one resident-owned manufactured home community on the Loan Fund's list is Sandy Pines Cooperative (12 homes). |
| P14.2 | There may be free summer camps through the school or town | The district's R.E.A.C.H. summer camps charge $130 to $145 a week. Free summer help found is limited to invited students (the Title I "academic boost" and "Brain Boost") and special education's extended school year. The town's own programs could not be read. |
