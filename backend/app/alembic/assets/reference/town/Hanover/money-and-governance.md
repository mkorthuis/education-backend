# Hanover: Money, Taxes, Staffing and Governance (Hanover and Dresden School Districts, SAU 70)

Part of the Hanover research (town/Hanover). Gathered November 21, 2026.

## Summary

**What's covered:** 11 topics, 92 facts and 6 number tables on the Hanover School District (Ray School, K–5) and the interstate Dresden School District (Richmond Middle and Hanover High), SAU 70, the Hanover–Norwich cost split, tuition from sending towns, buildings and debt, Hanover's tax relief programs, community use of buildings, and housing costs.

- **How Hanover's school tax is built.** A Hanover property owner pays for two school districts: the Hanover School District's net assessment and Hanover's share of the Dresden assessment. For 2026-27 these came to $14,506,251 and $21,110,262, a total of $35,616,513 (+7.71%). SAU 70 projected a school tax rate of $9.09 per $1,000 ($7.94 local and $1.15 state), $0.63 above 2025-26.
- **The cost split.** Under the New Hampshire–Vermont Interstate School Compact (RSA 200-B), Dresden splits what is left after its own revenue between the two towns. It uses each town's average daily membership (ADM), counted two years earlier. Hanover's ADM includes its grade 6 (since 2022-23); Norwich's covers grades 7–12. For 2026-27 Hanover pays 69.838% and Norwich 30.162%. Per ADM pupil the two towns pay almost exactly the same, about $33,384 and $33,363 (**derived**).
- **Why the Dresden assessment rose.** Hanover's Dresden assessment rose every year from FY2022 to FY2027, but by very different amounts. The rise was 20.97% in FY2023, when Hanover's 6th graders moved into Dresden's count (Hanover's own assessment fell at the same time). It was 6.53% in FY2024, 0.63% in FY2025 (a large construction loan had been paid off in August 2023), 3.32% in FY2026 and 7.83% in FY2027. For FY2027 the causes were a 25.8% rise in SchoolCare health premiums, a surprise retroactive SchoolCare bill, the end of a $400,000 fund balance offset, and a small rise in Hanover's ADM share. That share shift accounts for about $116,051 of the $1,532,535 increase (**derived**). Dresden's ADM rose only from 873.8 to 905.4 between 2019-20 and 2024-25.
- **The 6.7 FTE cut.** The Ray School budget for 2026-27 drops 6.74 FTE, from 102.20 to 95.46. Most of the drop is 5.04 FTE of regular and special education aide positions that had been unfilled for a couple of years. The rest is 1.48 FTE of tutors and a 1.0 English learner specialist, partly offset by 0.78 FTE added. Classroom teacher FTEs stayed at 27.00. Richmond cut 1.6 FTE of educational assistant and elective positions.
- **Tuition.** For 2024-25 Dresden budgeted 72 Lyme tuition FTE at $23,420, or $1,686,242. Its tuition rate formula adds a per-pupil share of debt to a base that rises with the high school operating budget. Tuition revenue is budgeted at $3,316,317 for 2026-27, about 9.8% of the Dresden budget (**derived**), and it lowers the amount split between the towns. The 2026-27 private tuition rate is $26,061 at Hanover High.
- **Buildings.** Neither district's March 2026 warrant had a bond article. Hanover has one remaining loan (2014 Series B, $3,520,414 including interest, last payment 2034-35). SAU 70 formed a Capital Improvement Plan Committee, and a long-range capital plan is a 2026-27 goal. No building condition study was found.
- **Taxes and relief.** Hanover's 2025 revaluation raised assessments by an average of 44%. Hanover's elderly exemption takes $145,000, $205,000 or $285,000 off assessed value by age, with income limits of $46,000 (single) and $65,000 (married).
