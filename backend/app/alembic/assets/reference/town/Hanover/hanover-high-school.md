# Hanover High School

Part of the Hanover research (town/Hanover). Gathered November 21, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers Hanover High School (HHS), the Dresden School District's high school for Hanover and Norwich, Vermont, students: where its students take career and technical education (the Hartford Area Career and Technology Center, HACTC, in White River Junction, Vermont) and the hands-on courses HHS teaches itself; counseling for college and for other paths; fees and fee help; the ninth-grade program, course levels and placement; Dartmouth's Community High School Program and dual enrollment; homework, assessment and absence rules that bear on athletes' late away games; parking; what new students from other schools go through; world languages and the arts; extended learning opportunities (ELOs), independent study and March Intensive; the rules on generative AI; and student well-being, including the 2025 Vermont Youth Risk Behavior Survey results for Hanover High.

- **14 fact topics, 111 facts and 4 tables.** All topics are shared town topics; none is proposed. Raw copies are in `raw/high/` (about 16 MB, 96 files): HHS's program of studies pages, departments, counseling and college counseling pages, the student and family FAQs, the handbook's academics, behavior and school-information sections, the 2026-27 school profile, the Class of 2027 college guide, the 2025 NEASC visiting team report, the ELO and March Intensive pages, the parking page, the winter 2026-27 athletics letter, the SAU 70 enrollment page, Dartmouth's Community High School Program page, HACTC's home and about pages and 2026-27 student handbook, the Dresden FY2024-25 budget book, the Vermont Department of Health's 2023 and 2025 YRBS reports for SAU 70, SAU 70's transportation and technology pages, and the Town of Hanover's parking page.
- **Places:** `district Dresden` for Hanover High School, its handbook, policies and budget, and what HHS says about HACTC and Dartmouth. `town Hanover` for the town's parking. No place for general rules (none were needed; existing library and earlier-town rule topics are cited instead).
- **New data concepts:** a high school with no AP courses and unlimited honors; a free college-course program at a neighboring university (Dartmouth's CHSP) with its eligibility rules; an out-of-state career center attended under the sending school's arrangement, with the district's tuition and transport budget lines; a school rule letting athletes arrive late after a late return from away games; homework rules (weekend limits, a "code of quiet," three-assessments-in-a-day relief); parking permits limited to hardship cases and a senior lottery; a written generative AI rule built into every course's standards; a Vermont YRBS report for an interstate New Hampshire high school.
- **Main gaps:** how many HHS students attend HACTC each year; how many apply to Dartmouth's CHSP or are placed; any HHS guidance written for apprenticeships or the trades; waitlist or course-cap data for languages and arts; the Dresden Board's written policies (JLIE parking, IKF, IHBH, IMBC, any AI policy), which Simbli loads by script and BoardDocs blocked; the number of student parking spaces; the HACTC tuition rate charged per student.

| P4.1 | Kids "have to get bused over to Hartford or Lebanon" for trades or career-tech | Partly accurate. Career and technical programs (automotive, building trades, welding, cosmetology, culinary, health and more) are at HACTC in White River Junction, Vermont, as half-day programs for juniors and seniors (and up to five sophomores in an exploration program); each sending school provides the bus. No fee was found for HHS's shop and technology courses, and art course materials are provided (district-fees 1–7). |
| P7.1 | A student who finishes HHS math and computer science can "walk over and take a class at Dartmouth" | Accurate, with limits. Dartmouth's Community High School Program is tuition-free for juniors (winter and spring) and seniors at schools within 50 miles, one course a term and four in all, after a student has exhausted the high school's courses in that field; HHS counselors recommend students with a 3.8 GPA (or 3.8 in the subject and 3.6 overall), and Dartmouth students get seats first (school-programs-after-grade-6 9–13). |
| P7.2 | HHS "pushes every kid into a rigid freshman schedule" | Partly accurate. HHS says most ninth graders are scheduled into "a structured program" with "a rather limited number of choices," but ninth graders are placed at one of five math levels up to Algebra 2 Honors (higher with course-specific exams), can take Honors Methods in Science, and may take placement tests; policy allows credit for "demonstrated mastery." The principal surveyed families on the schedule in November 2026 (school-programs-after-grade-6 1–5, 16; district-school-day-and-calendar 1, 6). |
| P7.3 | A "pressure cooker" | Can't be measured directly. In the 2025 Vermont YRBS, Hanover High students reported anxiety (21%), sadness or hopelessness (15%) and poor mental health (18%) less often than Vermont students overall (29%, 23%, 25%), and more often said they feel close to people at school (75% vs. 60%). HHS limits weekend and vacation homework and lets students move a third major assessment on one day; NEASC's 2025 team described a plan to manage "increasing mental health needs" and improved student well-being (district-student-support 10–13; district-curriculum-and-assessment 1–4; table hanover_hs_yrbs_2025). |
| P12.1 | A January move risks "losing credits" | Can't be settled for one student. HHS awards credit by semester (the second quarter ends January 15, 2027); the counselor reviews the student's records and may require placement tests; state rules say the principal reviews a transfer student's transcript toward HHS's competencies. Dresden's written policy on transfer credit could not be read (school-programs-after-grade-6 17–18; district-school-day-and-calendar 5). |
| P13.1 | Whether teachers are "actually flexible" for late away games | Written rules exist: a student back from school travel between 11 p.m. and midnight may miss the first 1 hour 25 minutes of classes, and after midnight the first three hours; planned absences use a "goldenrod" form; missed work gets as many days as were missed. How individual teachers apply them can't be measured (district-school-day-and-calendar 2–4; district-curriculum-and-assessment 1–3). |
| P13.3 | Parking "impossible to get" | Largely accurate for a junior. HHS calls parking "very limited" and "an acute problem"; permits go first to hardship cases (sports, activities and carpooling don't count) and then to seniors by lottery if spaces remain (district-transportation 1–3). |
| P18.1 | HHS is "a much bigger public high school" | HHS had 707 students in 2026-27 (a Class of 2027 of 177). About 130 students a year come as tuition students from other towns or private arrangements, so newcomers in grade 9 are common (district-classrooms-and-staff 1–2). |
| P18.2 | College counseling is "mostly on the parents" | Not as stated. HHS's counselors run a grade-by-grade process (a 10th-grade meeting, junior-year classroom sessions, senior workshops, a counselor-written recommendation) and host more than 100 college admissions officers each fall; HHS also says it is "the student's responsibility to begin the process in a timely fashion" (district-student-support 3–7). |
| P18.3 | Freshmen "getting waitlisted" for languages and arts | No waitlist data were found. Four languages begin at level 1; Design (the art prerequisite) is open to ninth graders; several music ensembles need no audition. The program of studies warns that some course enrollments may be capped and that not every request can be met (district-enrichment-and-advanced-learners 1–4; school-programs-after-grade-6 6). |
| P19.1 | Whether seniors can still do independent study, or school is "way more test-focused" | Independent work continues: ELOs (0.5–1.0 credit, pass/withdrawal), an Independent Study course, Senior Bridges for second-semester seniors, and the required March Intensive week; NEASC (2025) noted a new part-time ELO coordinator. HHS offers no AP courses; the 11th-grade SAT is the state test (school-programs-after-grade-6 20–25; district-fees 4). |
| P19.2 | Schools are "banning" AI or "teaching kids how to use it" | Neither, as written. HHS's handbook says students may use generative AI only when a teacher explicitly allows it, must say how they used it and cite it, and that misuse falls under the Academic Integrity Policy; every course must state its own generative AI policy. No HHS course teaching AI use was found; HACTC's IT program discusses AI weekly (district-technology-and-phones 1–5). |
| P4.2 | Counselors help students "line up apprenticeships or community college" | Partly found. HHS's counselors cover "academic, social/emotional, and career planning," use YouScience and SCOIR career inventories, and the ELO program offers internships, a Career Fair and "Career Chats"; HACTC places students in paid co-ops. No HHS page on apprenticeships was found; HHS's published materials focus on college (district-student-support 1–2, 8; school-programs-after-grade-6 20–22; cte-center-programs 8). |
