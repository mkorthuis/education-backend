# Plymouth Regional High School and the Career Center, From Campton

Part of the Campton research (town/Campton). Gathered November 22, 2026.

## Summary

Covers what Campton families' questions need to know about Plymouth Regional High School (PRHS, run by the Pemi-Baker Regional School District) and its career and technical center: the two "Campton / Thornton" morning bus runs and their times; the center's real name and whether welding is offered (it isn't); nursing assistant (LNA) training in Health Science; college classes at Plymouth State and Lakes Region Community College and what PRHS says about apprenticeships; PRHS's math and science courses above the regular level set beside Holderness School's; Unified Basketball and Unified Soccer, the Unified classes and club, and Campton Elementary's own Unified Basketball; the sports PRHS lists, how many students play, its NHIAA division and what is and isn't posted about tryouts; the size of the move from Campton Elementary (265 students) to PRHS (651); PRHS's freshman supports; its English, writing and money-related courses; the state's Math Learning Communities program; how Campton's grade 8 and PRHS's grade 11 compare with the state; and PRHS's counselor load and what it posts for students not headed to a four-year school. It cites Plymouth's file (`town/Plymouth/prhs-and-career-center.md`) for what is already gathered and adds only Campton's side.

**Counts:** 7 topics (1 proposed new), 46 facts and 4 number tables.

**Main sources:** PRHS's 2026-27 bus route PDF (a scan, read from images), student handbook, Program of Studies, 9th Grade Program of Studies, co-curricular handbook, Parent Night slides, School Profile and Health Science syllabi, all through Plymouth's saved copies or fresh copies; Pemi-Baker school board minutes (July 2025 to November 2026, marked unofficial); the SAU 48 counseling site's 8th Grade Transition page; Special Olympics New Hampshire's Unified Sports pages and articles; the NHIAA's 2026-27 classification by-law (a copy saved by Amherst's research); Campton Elementary's athletics page; CCSNH's program list, Math Learning Communities pages and January 2025 report; RSA 193-I; Lakes Region Community College's high school page; the College Board's SAT benchmarks page; Holderness School's 2026-27 Curriculum Guide and AP page; and the NH Education Facts database. Road distances are from OpenStreetMap and OSRM routing between school map points only.

**Main gaps:** The NHIAA's Unified Basketball pages blocked automated requests (HTTP 403), so its roster rules for "athletes" and "partners" were not read. No PRHS tryout or cut policy, bus eligibility policy or afternoon bus was found. No source reports PRHS results, college writing or math placement, or CTE enrollment by home town. No NHIAA division for PRHS's soccer or ski teams was read. Whether PRHS runs the Math Learning Communities courses is unconfirmed.

**Proposed new shared topic:** `math-learning-communities`: Math Learning Communities (kind programs, question type help). Description: "New Hampshire's state-funded program in which community colleges partner with public high schools on two math courses for students who need a stronger math foundation for college and careers: the law, the courses and how they count toward college." | P5.2 | Comparing PRHS AP and honors with Holderness School | **Holderness School is a private school, not a PRHS sender; course lists differ.** PRHS lists AP Pre-Calculus, AP Calculus, AP Biology and AP Physics; Holderness lists AP Calculus AB and BC, AP Statistics, AP Computer Science A, AP Biology, AP Environmental Science and two AP Physics C courses (school-programs-after-grade-6 1-2). |

| P14.2 | A "250-kid K-8" school in Campton | **About right.** Campton Elementary (preschool to grade 8) had 265 students in 2024-25, 34 of them in grade 8; PRHS had 651 (school-programs-after-grade-6 3). |
| P14.3 | Soccer and skiing teams at PRHS | **Offered.** PRHS lists boys' and girls' soccer, alpine and Nordic skiing and ski jumping, and 65.7% of students took part in athletics in 2024-25; no cut policy is posted (district-pe-outdoor-and-athletics 7-9). |
| P18.2 | Many graduates leave with gaps in college writing or budgeting | **Can't be checked.** No source measures it. PRHS's courses and requirements for writing and money are listed (school-programs-after-grade-6 11-13); its juniors' 2026 math SAT average (496) was below the College Board benchmark (530) and its reading and writing average (528) above its benchmark (480) (high-school-outcomes 3). |
