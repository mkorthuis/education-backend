# Campton Elementary's Learning, Support and School Choices

Part of the Campton research (town/Campton). Gathered 2026-11-22.

## Summary

This file covers Campton Elementary School (CES, preschool to grade 8): how big its classes and grades are, who its staff and paraprofessionals are, reading help and special education contacts, enrichment and advanced learning, arts and music, shop and life skills, cursive, civics and New Hampshire history, counseling and student support, tutoring for middle schoolers, bullying rules, concussions and return to play, sports eligibility and schedules, access for homeschooled, charter and EFA students, private high school applications, and how CES compares with Thornton Central, Holderness Central and Plymouth Elementary.

**Counts:** 14 topics, 102 facts and 2 number tables. Raw copies are in `raw/learning/`: pages from CES's Google Sites, the CES 2026-27 handbook, 7 board policies, 16 sets of school board minutes and hearing notes (names removed), the 2026-27 Title I plan, the SAU 48 social studies maps, team schedules, and the private schools' application pages. Two derived CSVs hold the site-data queries.

**Main sources:** the CES website and its team, Student Services, Student Assistance Program (SAP), music, art and counseling sites; the CES Parent/Student Handbook 2026-2027; Campton School Board policies JLCJ (concussions, updated October 13, 2026), JJJ (access for non-enrolled students, September 2026), JICK (bullying, September 8, 2026), JJ, JFAB, JLCJA and JLCK; Campton School Board minutes from August 2024 to October 2026 and the February 4, 2026 budget hearing; the CES Title I Schoolwide Plan 2026-27; SAU 48's social studies curriculum maps; Mountain Village Charter School's FAQ; Holderness School's and New Hampton School's application pages; the Pemi Youth Center; Tutor.com's New Hampshire page; and the site's own data (test scores, class size, enrollment and meals) for derived comparisons.

**Main gaps:** no source says whether a 1-on-1 aide is present every day or what CES does when one is absent. No CES or SAU 48 page names cursive, and none describes a gifted or enrichment program for advanced readers beyond the middle school intervention period. The gifted and talented report CES files with the state, and a 2022 state approval letter for Campton, are on NH DOE's site, which blocked automated requests. No page describes how CES helps families apply to private high schools, how it schedules unified arts classes, or what its "Skills" class teaches. CES's sports schedules are shared documents that coaches edit; no source says why they change. The PE page and the middle school band page could not be read. No social worker appears on any staff list.
