# Campton: Money, Taxes, Governance, Staff Pay and Who Attends

Part of the Campton research (town/Campton). Gathered November 22, 2026.

## Summary

**What's covered:** 20 topics (3 of them proposed new rule topics), 72 facts and 11 number tables on how Campton School District (Campton Elementary School, CES) is paid for and run inside SAU 48, how its money divides between CES, the Pemi-Baker Regional School District (Plymouth Regional High) and the SAU, what voters decide and when, the school buildings and bond, special education and bus costs, jobs and pay at CES and the SAU, local wages and rents, short-term rentals and second homes, the 2026 Question 2 school tax cap vote, combining with Thornton or Waterville Valley, who the students are, and who stays in the public schools.

- **The 2026-27 budget:** the adopted sheet is $9,554,611, with $7,781,053 to be raised from taxpayers (up $461,695, 6.31%, $0.54 per $1,000 of the 2024 valuation); the operating budget alone rose 0.91% and the rest came from the new support staff contract, a lower use of one-time money and the loss of special education aid. The whole $9.55 million is broken out by area in table `campton_2026_27_appropriation_by_area` (the lines add to the printed total within $1).
- **Where the money goes:** about 73% of the two school levies goes to CES and about 27% to Pemi-Baker's high school district (**derived**, 2026-27); SAU 48's office takes $309,655 (3.2% of Campton's budget, down $22,645). Campton's local school tax dollars rose 44.2% from tax year 2019 to 2025 while its resident students fell 9.4% (**derived**; table `campton_local_school_tax_and_resident_students`).
- **Question 2 (school tax cap), November 2026:** Campton voted 56.5% yes (951 to 732), Pemi-Baker's seven towns 51.6% and SAU 48's eight towns 51.2%, all unofficial and all under the 60% needed, so no cap for any of the three; a separate March 2026 town 2.5% tax cap petition, which the town attorney said would affect only the town's own budget, was reported defeated (NHPR).
- **Buildings and bond:** CES is one school building on NH Route 175; the 2026 warrant took $85,110 from the capital reserve for roof and door repairs with nothing from taxes; the district is in year 6 of a 20-year bond ($72,480 of principal and interest in 2026-27, balance $705,000 after the next payment); no new bond was on the 2026 warrant; the ten-year plan lists $1,448,795 including $648,000 to add air conditioning wing by wing from 2027-28.
- **Staff pay:** the support staff contract (July 2026 to June 2029) starts custodians and paraeducators at $17.00 an hour and aides at $16.00 (2027-28 scale) with health insurance for 30 or more hours a week; no Campton openings were posted October 2, 2026.
- **Who attends:** 242 of CES's 265 students in 2024-25 were White (91.3%), 12 Hispanic, 6 two or more races, 5 Asian; Plymouth Elementary 89.0% and Plymouth Regional 90.9% (NCES, **derived** shares); about 130 Campton students a year are counted at Plymouth Regional, which matches the 129 Campton residents in grades 9-12 (**derived**).

**Main sources:** Campton's adopted 2026-27 budget sheet and draft 2026 warrant (scanned; transcribed from images); 15 sets of Campton School Board minutes, August 2025 to October 2026 (student, resident and individual employee names removed from the saved copies); the support staff contract for 2026-2029 (scanned); the CES 10-year capital plan; Campton's town pages, 2026 zoning ordinance and Board of Selectmen minutes (excerpts); SAU 48's pages, 2027-28 budget sheets and 2027 meeting calendar; Pemi-Baker's 2025 annual report (page 12 image); NH DOE/NCES school data; 2020 Census redistricting tables; BLS OEWS wage estimates; NH Housing's 2024 rental survey; RSAs 72:6, 75:1, 78-A and 279:21; 34 CFR 300.34; the site's database for derived comparisons.

## short-term-rental-taxes: Taxes on Short-Term Rentals

- kind: rule
- question_type: rules
- description: How New Hampshire taxes short-term rentals: the state's 8.5 percent rooms and meals tax on rental occupancy (what counts as a short-term rental, who collects it and where the money goes) and how rental property is valued for the property tax (proposed from the Campton research)

1. New Hampshire law defines a "short-term rental" as "the rental of one or more rooms in a residential unit for occupancy for tourist or transient use for less than 185 consecutive days," and defines a "hotel" broadly enough to include "cottages, camps, chalets ... and apartments" that hold themselves out for rent.
   - citation: RSA 78-A:3, VII and XXIII
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/V/78-A/78-A-3.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-22
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text: "XXIII. "Short-term rental" means the rental of one or more rooms in a residential unit for occupancy for tourist or transient use for less than 185 consecutive days." and "VII. "Hotel" means an establishment which holds itself out to the public by offering sleeping accommodations for rent ... The term includes ... tourist homes and cabins, ski dormitories, ski lodges ... cottages, camps, chalets ... and apartments." Also "XV. "Permanent resident" means any occupant who has occupied any room in a hotel for at least 185 consecutive days." Raw: raw/money/data/rsa-78-A-78-A-3.txt
2. New Hampshire charges a tax of 8.5 percent of the rent on each occupancy of a hotel room or short-term rental, which the operator (including a rental or room facilitator) collects and pays to the state.
   - citation: RSA 78-A:6, I and III; RSA 78-A:3, XIII
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/V/78-A/78-A-6.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-22
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text: "I. A tax of 8.5 percent of the rent is imposed upon each occupancy." and "III. The operator shall collect the taxes imposed by this section and shall pay them over to the state as provided in this chapter." The definition of operator includes "a rental facilitator and a room facilitator." Raw: raw/money/data/rsa-78-A-78-A-6.txt, raw/money/data/rsa-78-A-78-A-3.txt
3. The money from the rooms and meals tax goes to the state treasurer, first for bond payments and then to the state's general fund (not the education trust fund), and 30 percent of the net income is shared with towns and cities by population, not with school districts; only the separate tax on motor vehicle rentals is deposited in the education trust fund.
   - citation: RSA 78-A:26, I, II, III and IV; RSA 78-A:6, II-a
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/V/78-A/78-A-26.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-22
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text: "(b) The remainder to the general fund." and "30 percent of the net income ... shall be deposited into the meals and rooms municipal revenue fund for distribution to the unincorporated towns, unorganized places, towns, and cities. The amount to be distributed to each such town, place, or city shall be determined by multiplying the total amount to be distributed by a fraction, the numerator of which shall be the population of the ... town, place, or city and the denominator of which shall be the population of the state." and "II. ... all revenue collected pursuant to RSA 78-A:6, II-a [the 8.5 percent tax on motor vehicle rental receipts] ... for deposit in the education trust fund". Towns' shares are spent as each town decides. Raw: raw/money/data/rsa-78-A-78-A-26.txt, raw/money/data/rsa-78-A-78-A-6.txt
4. For the property tax, New Hampshire selectmen appraise taxable property other than special classes "at its market value," meaning "the property's full and true value as the same would be appraised in payment of a just debt due from a solvent debtor," and "all real estate, whether improved or unimproved, shall be taxed except as otherwise provided," so a rental, a second home and a primary home are taxed on value at the same rate per $1,000.
   - citation: RSA 75:1; RSA 72:6
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/V/75/75-1.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-22
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text (75:1): "... and all other taxable property at its market value. Market value means the property's full and true value as the same would be appraised in payment of a just debt due from a solvent debtor."; (72:6) "All real estate, whether improved or unimproved, shall be taxed except as otherwise provided." The statute has no separate rate for rentals or second homes; the special classes in 75:1 include open space, farm structures and certain historic buildings.
