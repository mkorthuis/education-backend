# Dunbarton: Money, Taxes, the Bow AREA Agreement and Governance

Part of the Dunbarton research (town/Dunbarton). Gathered November 22, 2026.

## Summary

**What's covered:** 10 topics, 51 facts and 7 number tables on Dunbarton's school taxes and budget, what it pays Bow, the AREA agreement's terms and exits, the school district meeting and absentee voting, Dunbarton Elementary's building, enrollment and new housing, and teacher pay and turnover.
- **Taxes and state aid (11 facts):** the tax year 2026 rate DRA set on October 16, 2026 ($16.99; local education $11.46), the education share of the rate (73.5% in 2026, 71.0% in 2025), the 2025 revaluation and why a falling rate came with a rising school levy, the levy against enrollment since 2020, how the 2026 levy was built, Dunbarton's adoption of the HB 1300 school tax cap (1,093 to 639) and the district's own estimate of what it would have cut in 2026-27 ($293,342), and a full-value comparison with Bow.
- **Budget and pay (12 facts):** the $11,608,626 2026-27 operating budget (passed 147 to 59), what drove its 8.08% rise (benefits +19.19%, special-needs transportation +80%), health insurance and the van, the Special Education Fund, an article-by-article list of the March 7, 2026 warrant, cost per pupil, the 2025–2028 teachers' contract and how its salary schedules compare with Bow's, and teacher stability.
- **Bow and the AREA agreement (7 + 2 facts):** what Dunbarton pays Bow ($4,550,290 budgeted for 2026-27), Bow's per-student rates, Dunbarton's say, the 15-year term and the 128 to 8 renewal vote, how the agreement can be changed or ended (with the statute's steps), how the formula limits and passes through Bow's costs, and Bow High's upkeep in the tuition.
- **Meetings and voting (8 facts):** the Saturday school district meeting (March 7, 2026; March 6, 2027 at 9 a.m.), attendance, the separate March 10 officer ballot, and what the sources show about absentee ballots.
- **Building and growth (11 facts):** the November 2026 Capital Needs Assessment of Dunbarton Elementary, its heating, safety systems, history, capital budget and bond, DES enrollment since 2012, a New Hampshire Housing study with Dunbarton-specific numbers, new-home permits, and home values.

**Main sources:** the Dunbarton board pages on sau67nh.gov and their Drive files (2026 and 2025 budget hearing packets, the posted 2026 warrant, the teachers' contract, board minutes and packets September 2025 to November 2026, the HB 1300 information session minutes, the Capital Needs Assessment), the Town of Dunbarton's site (DRA's 2026 rate sheets, the five-year rate table, election pages and results, annual reports 2021–2025), the Bow–Dunbarton AREA agreement, RSA 195-A and the election statutes, NH Housing's 2024 housing and enrollment study, a Zillow file, one Concord Monitor article (March 8, 2026), and the site's database.

## gov-area-agreements: AREA Schools (Authorized Regional Enrollment Areas)

- kind: term
- question_type: governance_money
- description: What an AREA agreement is and how it differs from a cooperative.

1. Under RSA 195-A:14, a sending or receiving district may vote at an annual or special meeting, after the third anniversary of the area school's date of operating responsibility, to study withdrawing from the AREA; a committee of two school board members from each district in the AREA, the superintendent (without a vote) and two members of the town or city governing body of the district asking for the study reports to the State Board of Education within 180 days; the State Board reviews any withdrawal plan, which must say how every district's pupils will be educated, and recommends for or against it; and a majority of the voters present and voting in the withdrawing district decides, with withdrawal taking effect July 1 at least 2 years after the vote unless the plan sets an earlier date.
   - citation: RSA 195-A:14, III, IV, VII and VIII
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XV/195-A/195-A-14.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-22
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text: "After the third anniversary of the date of operating responsibility a sending or receiving school district, at an annual or special school district meeting, may vote to undertake a study of the feasibility and suitability of a withdrawal from the area. The study shall be conducted by a committee composed of 2 school board members from each district of the area, the superintendent of schools as a non-voting member, and 2 members of the town or city governing body from the school district requesting the study. Within 180 days after the date of its formation, the committee shall submit to the state board of education either a report that withdrawal is not feasible or suitable or a report that includes a withdrawal plan"; the plan must include "The manner in which the withdrawing district or districts shall provide for the education of all pupils in the withdrawing district or districts and a plan for the education of the pupils in the remaining sending and/or receiving districts"; the warrant question reads "Shall the school district accept the provisions of RSA 195-A:14, as amended, providing for the withdrawal of the sending (or receiving) district of ___ from the ___ area"; "If a majority of the voters present and voting shall vote in the affirmative ... The vote to withdraw from an area shall take effect on July 1 of the calendar year which shall be at least 2 years after the date on which the withdrawal vote is adopted. The plan may provide for an earlier date." The library has the short version (gov-area-agreements fact 5); this adds who sits on the study committee, the 180 days and the State Board's review. The statute lets a receiving district such as Bow withdraw by the same route. A withdrawing sending district also stays liable for any rental charge, for bonds, and for lost school building aid (library fact 6). ## voting-in-local-elections: Who May Vote in Local Elections, Absentee Voting and Who Turns Out

- kind: how_it_works
- question_type: governance_money
- description: Who may vote in New Hampshire town and school district elections (voting domicile, students), absentee voting for local elections, and who turns out by age (proposed from the Hampton research)

1. New Hampshire law builds absentee voting into the official-ballot (SB2) form of school district meeting, whose second session follows "all requirements pertaining to absentee voting, polling place, and polling hours" (RSA 40:13, XII), and defines a "school district election" as one "to choose a school district officer" (RSA 652:9), while a district that keeps the traditional meeting holds it between March 1 and March 25 to vote money and other business and, where officers are not chosen at town meeting, to choose them (RSA 197:1); the statutes read give no absentee ballot for the business vote at a traditional meeting.
