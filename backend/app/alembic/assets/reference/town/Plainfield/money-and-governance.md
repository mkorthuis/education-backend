# Plainfield: Money, Taxes, High School Tuition, the Shared SAU and Governance

Part of the Plainfield research (town/Plainfield). Gathered November 22, 2026.

## Summary

**What's covered:** 14 topics, 77 facts and 7 number tables on why Plainfield's school tax rises while enrollment falls, what Plainfield pays Lebanon and its say in Lebanon's budget, HB 1300 and Question 2, the SAU 32 office and how it is shared with Cornish (costs, the superintendent's contract), the Plainfield-Cornish cooperative study, Kimball Union Academy's tax-exempt property, the school building and its repairs, teacher turnover, special education budget lines and trust funds, whether Plainfield is locked into the AREA agreement, and enrollment and housing.
- **Taxes and the budget:** the 2026-27 budget ($8,761,921, passed 209 to 66 on March 14, 2026) is almost flat (+0.08%), but the local property tax need rises $441,538 (7.24%) to $6,541,393 because less surplus and less Cornish revenue offset it; health insurance premiums rose 25.4%; 6.5 staff positions were cut; enrollment fell from 220 (2020-21) to 164 (November 2026). The district's own January 2026 rate estimate is $12.88 per $1,000 (local $11.86, state $1.02); the final 2026 rate was not found.
- **HB 1300:** Plainfield voted 41.7% yes on Question 2 (unofficial), below the 60% needed, so no cap binds the school district or SAU 32 and no override is needed in March 2027.
- **Lebanon tuition:** 81 students at $19,344 each, $1,566,864 for 2026-27 (17.9% of the budget); Plainfield has no vote on Lebanon's budget but sits on the AREA Committee; the agreement runs to at least July 2031.
- **SAU 32 and Cornish:** Cornish is in its own SAU 100 and buys central office services from Plainfield's SAU 32 (since 2017); the three shared positions are split 55/45 or 60/40; the superintendent's contract was extended one year "at the same salary and benefits" on November 9, 2026; the draft 2027-28 SAU budget is $723,219.
- **Cornish and the cooperative:** Cornish voters defeated a middle-school tuition deal 91 to 25 on March 7, 2026; the Cooperative Committee now recommends one K-8 school at Plainfield's building, with a first vote in March 2027; a joint middle school basketball team could not be formed in fall 2026.
- **KUA:** Kimball Union Academy's educational property ($71.3 million) is exempt and the town lists its payment on taxable property at over $296,000; an illustration shows what the exempt value would mean at the school rate.
- **Building:** a $1.5 million middle school wing opened for 2026-27 ($1 million donation, $500,000 five-year note); five electrical panels were replaced in 2025; building reserves are small; the building's age, roof and boiler were not documented.

**Main sources:** the Town of Plainfield 2025 Annual Report (school section, scanned and transcribed), Plainfield School Board minutes and reports (October 2025 to November 2026), the November 2026 draft SAU budget, the joint Plainfield-Cornish board minutes and the Cooperative Committee's Phase I report and draft Articles of Agreement, the AREA agreement and AREA Committee minutes, Cornish School District's website, Valley News reports (for vote counts), RSA 72:23 and the site's database (read-only).

## tax-exempt-property-rules: Tax-Exempt Property: Schools, Charities and Churches

- kind: rule
- question_type: rules
- description: Which property New Hampshire law exempts from local property tax (schools and colleges, charities, churches), the limit on exempt dormitories and dining halls, and the filing rules for charities

1. New Hampshire law exempts the buildings and structures of schools, academies, colleges and universities, with their land, athletic fields and gymnasiums, when the school owns, uses and occupies them directly for its educational purpose, except that the value of dormitories, dining rooms and kitchens above $150,000 is taxable and a town may vote at its annual meeting to raise that amount.
   - citation: RSA 72:23, IV
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/V/72/72-23.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-22
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text: "provided further that if the value of the dormitories, dining rooms and kitchens shall exceed $150,000, the value thereof in excess of said sum shall be taxable. A town at an annual town meeting or the governing body of a city may vote to increase the amount of the exemption upon dormitories, dining rooms and kitchens." The exemption is for property "not including lands and buildings not used and occupied directly for the purposes for which they are organized or incorporated". Raw: raw/money/rsa-72-23.txt
2. New Hampshire law also exempts the buildings, lands and personal property of charitable organizations used directly for their purposes, and requires a charity (other than religious and educational organizations whose property is exempt under paragraphs III and IV) to file a statement of its financial condition with the town each year before June 1.
   - citation: RSA 72:23, V and VI
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/V/72/72-23.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-22
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text: "The buildings, lands and personal property of charitable organizations and societies ... owned, used and occupied by them directly for the purposes for which they are established" and "shall annually before June 1, file with the municipality in which the property is located ... a statement of its financial condition".
