# Lancaster: Money, Governance, Taxes, Buildings, Buying and Safety in the White Mountains Regional School District (SAU 36)

Part of the Lancaster research (town/Lancaster). Gathered November 22, 2026. Written for district-wide use: Whitefield's research will cite these facts.

## Summary

**What's covered:** 14 topics, 95 facts and 14 number tables on how the White Mountains Regional School District (SAU 36; Carroll, Dalton, Jefferson, Lancaster and Whitefield) is paid for and run: its budget, how school taxes are split among the five towns and the state, the Question 2 tax cap vote, the central office and board meetings, teacher pay and mentoring, bidding, buildings and reserve funds, safety plans, which school each town's children attend, and voting on the school budget.
- **Why Lancaster's school taxes rise as students fall.** Lancaster's school taxes (local plus state education) rose 32.8% from tax year 2018 to 2025 ($3.34 million to $4.43 million) while its resident students fell 5.5% (421 to 398), so school tax per student went from $7,922 to $11,126 (**derived**, site data). The district's own figures: operating budget up 4.38% for 2026-27, health insurance up $609,241, state aid down $286,696, and 40% of each town's share follows property value (Lancaster's rose 17.95% against 13.35% for the district). Lancaster's 2026-27 school tax rate estimate is $11.13 per $1,000 (from $9.82); the final tax year 2026 rate was not found.
- **Who pays what.** The five towns' shares of the district's net cost are set 60% by students and 40% by equalized value. For 2026-27 Lancaster's share is $8,647,453 (36.7%), from 45.4% of students and 23.7% of value; after $3,629,933 of state aid Lancaster's taxpayers owe $5,017,520 ($4,492,760 local, $524,760 state education tax). The state education tax is collected by the town and goes to the district, not the state. The district's budget is one pool; the lines coded to Lancaster Elementary are 22.5% of it (**derived**). The formula has been under review since 2022 (Carroll study committee) and again in late 2025, when a 65/35 proposal did not reach the 2026 warrant and the question was tabled.
- **Tax cap.** Question 2 passed in the district 68.8% yes (Lancaster 64.3%), above the 60% needed (WMUR, unofficial). The caps start with the FY2028 budget (March 2027). The district held a hearing October 29, 2026; it has said health insurance will rise 24.2% (about $1.1 million) and FY2028 state aid will fall about $785,000, but published no cap calculation or list of cuts.
- **Central office and meetings.** The central office is at 135 Regional Road, Whitefield, beside the high school. All 32 board agendas that name a place (July 2025 to November 2026) are in Whitefield (31 at the high school, 1 at the central office); public comment is in person only, with live stream. The assistant superintendent position was cut for FY27 (salary line $122,011 to $0); District Administration fell 12.6% (**derived**).
- **Pay and mentoring.** The teachers' agreement (to June 30, 2027) pays step 1 $42,300 and step 15 up to $65,000 in 2026-27; mentors get $750 per teacher mentored. The site's average salary ranks 153rd of 198 (**derived**). The board approves 23 to 24 mentors a year; mentor budget lines fall from $24,750 to $16,500 (**derived**). No turnover figure was found.
- **Buying.** Policy DJF lets in-district suppliers bid up to 5% (max $5,000) above others; bids are posted on the website and a Drive folder; the bus contract (Notchview Transport, five years) was a closed bid. The board has waived bidding when few vendors exist. No plowing or paving bid notices were found.
- **Buildings and safety.** Lancaster Elementary (built 1995) got a $903,180 boiler by lease-purchase (no bond); a 2025-26 condition assessment of four buildings flagged electrical, heating, roofing, plumbing and emergency lighting needs (report not posted). Board policy requires each school to have an emergency plan, reviewed yearly (the plans aren't posted); Lancaster Elementary does 10 drills a year; a $312,434 state grant is paying for entry upgrades.
- **Which school.** By the district's draft 2026-27 bus routes, Lancaster children go to Lancaster Elementary (pre-K to 6), Whitefield, Carroll and Dalton children to Whitefield School, Jefferson to either; all grades 7 to 12 go to the middle-high school in Whitefield.

## voting-in-local-elections: Who May Vote in Local Elections, Absentee Voting and Who Turns Out

- kind: how_it_works
- question_type: governance_money
- description: Who may vote in New Hampshire town and school district elections (voting domicile, students), absentee voting for local elections, and who turns out by age (proposed from the Hampton research)

1. A person who is at least 18 on election day, a United States citizen and domiciled in the town may register to vote there under RSA 654:7, and the statute asks about where a person lives, not whether the person owns or rents a home; a renter who makes the town his or her one voting home can therefore vote on the school district budget.
   - citation: RSA 654:7, I and RSA 654:1, I
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/LXIII/654/654-7.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-22
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: RSA 654:7, I: "Any person registering to vote shall be: (a) At least 18 years of age on the day of the next election; and (b) A United States citizen; and (c) Domiciled in the town or city in which the applicant is registering to vote." RSA 654:1, I defines domicile as "that one place where a person, more than any other place, has established a physical presence and manifests an intent to maintain a single continuous presence for domestic, social, and civil purposes." Neither section mentions property ownership; Hampton's file has the same domicile fact (voting-in-local-elections fact 1). Raw: raw/money/state/rsa-654-7.txt; raw/money/state/rsa-654-1.txt
2. A qualified voter whose name is not on the checklist can register at the polling place on election day and vote at that election, bringing proof of qualifications, under RSA 654:7-a.
   - citation: RSA 654:7-a, Registering at the Polling Place; Election Day Registration
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/LXIII/654/654-7-a.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-22
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
