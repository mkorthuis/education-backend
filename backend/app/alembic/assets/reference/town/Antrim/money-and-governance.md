# Antrim: Money, Taxes and Governance of the ConVal School District

Part of the Antrim research (town/Antrim). Gathered November 22, 2026.

## Summary

**What's covered:** 9 topics, 54 facts and 6 number tables on what drives Antrim's school taxes, how the Contoocook Valley (ConVal) School District (SAU 1) divides its costs, the small-school and middle-school consolidation questions, the cap votes and the state funding case, teacher pay and the union, exemptions, and how Antrim residents can follow and be heard. Facts about Antrim's own bill, votes, share and seats are placed `town Antrim`; ConVal-wide facts are placed `district Contoocook Valley`. Peterborough's money file (`town/Peterborough/money-and-governance.md`) already holds the district-wide facts (the Articles of Agreement, the 2024 closing article, Francestown's withdrawal, board votes, the budget, bonds and the cap results); this file cites it and adds what is Antrim's.

- **The bill:** in 2025 Antrim's tax commitment was $10,005,029, up $997,606 (11.1%) from 2024. School taxes (local education $5,638,007 plus state education $492,114) were 61.3% of it (**derived**), but only 42.1% of the year's increase; the town's own tax caused 47.2% and the county's 11.8% (**derived**). The 2025 rates the question quotes ($23.11 total, $12.89 local school, $1.19 state education) match the site's data. The state education tax is not an extra charge on top of ConVal's bill: it is credited against Antrim's $8,241,728 share, which the state education tax covered 6.0%, the state's adequacy grant 25.6% and the local education tax 68.4% (**derived**).
- **Why it keeps rising:** ConVal splits costs 50% by pupils and 50% by equalized property value. Antrim's share rose from 13.64% (2023 bills) to 14.97% (2025) because its pupil count in the formula rose (299.93 to 332.49) while the district's total stayed near 2,000, and its property value rose faster than the district's. For 2026-27 ConVal projects Antrim's local school tax up $831,942 (14.76%) against 2.10% for the nine towns together. NH DOE's FY2027 estimate cuts Antrim's adequacy grant by $450,047 (21.3%, **derived**); $392,243 of that is the extraordinary need grant, which fell from $392,674 to $430 because Antrim's property value per free or reduced-price meal pupil crossed the formula's $7,003,972 limit (**derived**).
- **Caps and the lawsuit:** Antrim voted 38% yes on the petitioned 2025 ConVal cap and 59.5% yes (unofficial) on Question 2 in November 2026, just under the 60% needed; across ConVal 52.90% voted yes, so no cap applies. ConVal's own figures show no new state money from its lawsuit; Antrim's estimated adequacy grant fell from $2,145,014 (FY2025) to $1,661,522 (FY2027).
- **Schools and consolidation:** the 1967 vote that formed ConVal was 1,278 to 450 (all nine towns yes); the district began operating July 1, 1968. Antrim Elementary was one of four elementary schools the 2024 article would have named to keep; Antrim voted 49% yes on it (212 to 219). Great Brook School, not Antrim Elementary, is the Antrim school with a closure history (petitions in 2013 and 2015 failed with 37% and 40%) and is one of two middle schools in a 2026 facilities review whose options include one new consolidated school; no decision has been made.
- **Teachers and the union:** the CVEA agreement runs to June 30, 2027; the minutes read record no impasse in 2025-26, and bargaining with the CVEA and the administrators' association was under way in October 2026. No turnover rate is published; in the federal 2023-24 data Antrim Elementary kept 8 of 12 teachers (66.7%) and Great Brook 28 of 29 (96.6%) (Peterborough's table).
- **Administration against shop spending:** in ConVal's 2024-25 reports to NH DOE, general and school administration cost $7,822,692 (13.9% of spending on instruction, support and facilities) against $1,278,163 (2.3%) for vocational programs (**derived**); the lines are not like for like.
- **Gaps:** Antrim's own site, its annual report and the state's exemption tables blocked automated requests, so Antrim's senior exemption amounts, its warrant and its town budget were not retrieved; ConVal's 2026 apportionment by town, the Prismatic report and ConVal's board policy on public participation were not found.

**Main sources:** ConVal School Board's Financial Articles (December 2023, December 2024, February 2026), Articles of Agreement, FAQs, board roster, March 2026 vote results, the October 2026 Question 2 report and slides, the Mandatory Report to Voters, the FY27 budget, the 2026 warrant, the teachers' agreement (2025-2027) and the December 2023 addendum, the Francestown withdrawal study (October 2025) and its appendices, board, committee and Selectmen's Advisory Committee minutes (January 2025 to November 2026); the site's NH DRA, NH DOE and DOE-25 data; and, where nothing else exists, the Monadnock Ledger-Transcript.
