# Greenland: Money, Governance, Portsmouth Tuition and SAU 50

Part of the Greenland research (town/Greenland). Gathered November 21, 2026.

## Summary

**What's covered:** 10 topics, 74 facts and 6 number tables on where Greenland's school money comes from and how the local school tax fits with it, the 2027-28 (FY2028) budget from the October draft to the board's November 12 approval, what drives it, how Portsmouth High School tuition is budgeted and paid, the tuition trust fund, Question 2 (the school tax cap and the SAU cap) in Greenland and across SAU 50, Greenland Central School's building and capital plan, the default budget and the budget calendar, where budget documents are posted, a say at Portsmouth, enrollment at Greenland Central, Portsmouth's conditional open enrollment policy, paraeducator and substitute openings, staffing changes, and SAU 50 itself: its schools and staff, the joint board, how its cost is split, the districts' 2026-27 shares, the new separate SAU budget article, the 2026 superintendent search and what the new superintendent has changed, and the SAU strategic plan.

- **The budget.** The October 2026 draft raised Greenland's general fund 10.35% ($1,603,340); on November 12, 2026 the board approved a budget with $971,607.50 in net cuts, up 4.09% ($633,340) including the SAU share. The largest drafted increases were special education contract services, salaries, Portsmouth High tuition, building repairs, health insurance (+12.8%) and contracted behavior services. Article 2 (operating) is $15,800,801.86 and Article 1 (SAU 50 share) $691,607.
- **Portsmouth tuition.** Tuition is a per-student rate times the number of Greenland students: 166 enrolled in 2026-27 against 162.5 budgeted, leaving the line $159,064 over budget at Portsmouth's $25,529 rate. It is about a quarter of Greenland's budget. A $371,211 tuition trust fund backs the board's choice to budget 10 fewer students for 2027-28.
- **Taxes and the cap.** 81% of Greenland's 2026-27 school revenue is local property tax; the amount raised rose 8.8% from 2025-26. Question 2 failed in Greenland (55.0% yes) and across SAU 50 (51.8%), so neither cap applies to 2027-28.
- **The building.** Greenland Central (built 1920, with a 2004 section) has no renovation or bond planned; the capital plan runs $15,000–$128,470 a year. The cafeteria's flat roof and make-up air unit need replacing; the board took both ($186,910) out of the budget to look at the maintenance trust fund or a separate article.
- **SAU 50.** Costs are split half by students and half by property value; 2026-27 shares were Greenland $681,658, New Castle $231,659 and Newington $208,364 and Rye $1,033,994. From March 2027 the SAU budget is a separate article decided by the four towns' combined vote. Dr. Ellen Kaschuluk became superintendent after a spring 2026 search.

**Main sources:** the Greenland School Board's FY27 meeting folders (the 144-page FY28 proposed budget book of October 2026, the FY28 budget summary and cut list, the HB 1300 and August financial memos, the FY27 revenue chart, the November 11 financial report, the capital improvement plan, the facilities, principal's and superintendent's reports, and minutes of August 10, September 21, October 19, October 27, November 12 and November 16, 2026); SAU 50 Joint Board agendas (February–November 2026) and September 30, 2026 minutes; the SAU 50 website, policies, collective bargaining page and job board; New Castle's and Newington's November 2026 financial reports; the Town of Rye Budget Committee's December 2025 questions with SAU answers; the Greenland Central budget process flyer for 2026-27; Portsmouth City Council packet (May 2026) and June 8, 2026 draft minutes; the library's WMUR Question 2 tally; and the site's database for derived comparisons.
