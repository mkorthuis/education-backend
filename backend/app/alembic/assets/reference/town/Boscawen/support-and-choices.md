# Student Support, the Middle School and School Choices for Boscawen Families

Part of the Boscawen research (town/Boscawen). Gathered November 21, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers Boscawen's side of student support and school choice in the Merrimack Valley School District (SAU 46): the middle and high school buses from Boscawen, including the Gerrish end of town, and the same buses' second trip for Boscawen Elementary School (BES); transportation to outside special education programs; homeschooled and EFA students' access to district classes, band and sports, and what the board charges EFA students; homeschoolers at the Concord Regional Technical Center (CRTC); fights, vaping and drugs at Merrimack Valley Middle School (MVMS), and its cameras and drills; busing for private school students, Bishop Brady included; Boscawen's fifth graders joining MVMS; private middle schools near Boscawen and what an EFA covers; the 2027 bill requests to restore an EFA income limit; and the early start of the middle and high school day. It builds on the Loudon research (`town/Loudon/`), which already covers the district-wide homeschool access rules at MVMS, the phone, bullying and nondiscrimination policies, changing schools within the district, counseling and the Learning Center, and on Concord's Penacook bus facts (`town/Concord/getting-to-school-and-safety.md`). Sources: the district's 2026-27 route sheets (Buses 9, 11, 12, 13, 18 and 19, and the four BES routes), transportation page and policies EEA, JICG and JICH; the June 1, October 5 and November 2, 2026 board packets and minutes; the 2026 annual report; the BES, MVMS and MVHS 2026-27 handbooks; the CRTC's 2026-27 Program of Studies and sending-schools page; St. John Regional School's tuition and aid pages; the EFA marketplace feed; the Town of Boscawen's Parks & Recreation page; RSA 189:9; the General Court's 2027 LSR list; OpenStreetMap place and street lookups; and U.S. Naval Observatory sun tables. Staff, driver and student names are left out of the statements.

**Counts:** 14 topics (12 shared slugs from `shared-topics.md`; `school-transportation-rules` and `efa-eligibility-and-cap` are existing library topics, used with their own kind, question type and description), 38 facts and 2 number tables (scheduled minutes from Boscawen bus stops, and the board's charges for EFA students).

**New data concepts:**
- Scheduled ride times from Boscawen's own stops, including the Gerrish area, on six middle and high school routes, with the riders each route is assigned.
- The district's two-tier bus day in Boscawen: the same driver and bus run the middle and high school trip, then the BES trip.
- The board's June 2026 "Cost Sheet for EFA Students": per-class, activity and per-sport charges.
- Special education transportation spending, including the out-of-district line.
- The 2027 legislative service requests on EFA income eligibility.

**Main findings:**
- From the Gerrish area (Daniel Webster Highway and Forest Lane), Bus 13 picks up from 6:51 to 7:02 a.m. and reaches MVMS at 7:25, a scheduled 23–34 minutes; afternoon drop-offs there are 26–44 minutes after MVMS lets out. Every Boscawen stop checked is under the district's one-hour limit.
- Boscawen's middle and high school runs are assigned 45 to 69 riders each; the district's newest full-size buses seat 81. In October 2026 the board's transportation committee reported many driver vacancies and voted to propose seeking bids to contract out busing.
- On June 1, 2026 the board accepted charges for EFA students (about $2,800 per class; $100–$509 per middle school sport), while the MVMS 2026-27 handbook says homeschool and EFA students pay no fees. The two conflict, and neither mentions homeschoolers who don't use an EFA.
- The CRTC says its programs are free to home-schooled students in its region, with no transportation cost, and lists Boscawen as a participating town. District policy EEA buses "all students who reside in the District" to a career center.
- District buses carry private school students only within the district, on the regular routes. No 2026-27 route stops at a private school; Bishop Brady, in Concord, runs its own morning bus ($875 a year).
- Three 2027 bill requests would add household income limits to EFAs. Their text isn't public yet.
- Policy JICG counts e-cigarettes and vapor pens as tobacco, which the MVMS handbook lists as grounds for suspension; MVMS cameras cover hallways but not bathrooms.

**Main gaps:** no count of late buses, crowded runs or each route's bus size; no district explanation of why middle and high school start at 7:45; no MVMS-specific vaping or fight counts beyond the site's discipline data; no Boscawen count of students placed out of district; tuition at private middle schools other than St. John Regional; the outcome of the NHSBA delegate vote on EFA resolutions.
