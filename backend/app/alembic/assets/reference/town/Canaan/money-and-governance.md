# Canaan: Money, Taxes and Governance in the Mascoma Valley Regional School District

Part of the Canaan research (town/Canaan). Gathered November 22, 2026.

## Summary

**What's covered:** 8 topics, 40 facts and 7 number tables on Canaan's side of Mascoma Valley Regional School District (SAU 62) money: why Canaan's school taxes move as they do, what share of its bill is for schools, how the five towns' shares of district cost are set and compare per student, pay for paraeducators and bus drivers against Lebanon, where aide money comes from, the line-by-line budget, buildings and staff as enrollment falls, Education Freedom Accounts and state aid, lake towns' state aid, the high school renovation bond, how long the deliberative session ran and how to vote without it, and the known inputs to the next few years' school tax. Enfield's money file (`town/Enfield/money-and-governance.md`) already covers the district's budget, default budget, board seats and posted documents; this file cites it and adds Canaan's side.
- **Taxes:** Canaan's local school tax dollars rose 47.4% from tax year 2015 to 2025 ($6,201,581 to $9,138,549) while its resident students fell 18.1% (470 to 385); its assessed value rose by the same 47.4%, so the school rate barely moved ($19.22 to $19.36 per $1,000) (**derived**). Canaan's 2026 rate was still not certified on November 18, 2026. Of its 2025 rate of $31.16, $20.85 (66.9%) is for schools (**derived**). Its assessments fell to 61.09% of market value by 2025 (94.30% in 2021), so its posted rate overstates what the town pays per $1,000 of market value ($11.73, the highest of the five towns).
- **State aid:** Canaan's adequacy grant fell $634,844 (24.3%) from FY2025 to FY2027 (an estimate), because its aid-counted pupils fell 9.6% and temporary hold-harmless grants are shrinking; the state's schedule steps Canaan's $743,930 hold-harmless grant down to zero by FY2034 (**derived**).
- **Cost split:** each town's share of the district's net school appropriation was rebuilt from the site's data and matches the state's rate sheets for Canaan exactly: Canaan paid 41.0%, 40.6%, 40.1% and 39.4% for tax years 2022 to 2025, in line with its share of pupils (about 39%) and well above its share of property value (about 31%). Per resident student, Canaan's share ($31,065) is within 1.2% of Enfield's and Grafton's; after state aid its local school tax per student ($23,736) is below Enfield's ($25,404) and above Grafton's ($21,772) (**derived**).
- **Pay:** Mascoma's first-step paraeducator rates for 2026-27 ($18.05 and $19.88 an hour) are 7.3% and 6.0% below Lebanon's ($19.48 and $21.14) (**derived**); Butler's Bus Service, which lists Mascoma among its service areas, advertised $18 to $30 an hour for bus drivers in early 2026, and Lebanon's part-time drivers were advertised at $26.56 (a November 2024 notice). Hartford, Vermont's pay wasn't found.
- **Bond and meetings:** the $21.5 million high school renovation was approved March 11, 2014 and opened in 2016; its payments are a level $1,358,455 a year through at least 2026-27, so it is not paid off, and no document read gives its last year. The January 31, 2026 deliberative session ran 1 hour 52 minutes (9:05 to 10:57 a.m., **derived**), and the final vote is by ballot, with absentee ballots from Canaan's town clerk.

**Main sources:** the Town of Canaan's website (tax notice, assessing, voting pages, state tax rate sheets for 2023 and 2024), Mascoma's 2026 annual report, FY28 superintendent's proposed budget, support staff agreement for 2025-2028, superintendent's November 17, 2026 report, deliberative session minutes, Lebanon's support staff agreement (2026-2029) and bus driver notice, NH Senate news release on HB 1758, Valley News (January 7 and November 4, 2026), NH Legislative Budget Assistant's school building aid report, and the site's database (tax, adequacy aid, DOE-25, staff, EFA and Question 2 tables, read-only).

## property-tax-bills-and-escrow: How Canaan's Tax Bills Are Set and Why the Second Is Higher

- kind: how_it_works
- question_type: governance_money
- description: How and when property tax rates are set and bills issued, and how mortgage escrow payments adjust when taxes change

1. The Town of Canaan says its first property tax bill is an estimate based on last year's budget and tax base because the county, state and school amounts aren't known until November, so the second bill must catch up on all of the increases in six months and is "twice the increase as the annual tax increase"; bills are mailed twice a year, due in July and December, and the tax year runs April 1 to March 31.
   - citation: Town of Canaan, Assessing page and Tax Collector page
   - source: https://www.canaannh.gov/202/Assessing
   - as_of: 2026-11-22

   - place: town Canaan
   - note: Assessing page: "People often ask why the second tax bill is so much higher than the first bill. The first tax bill is an estimate based on last year's budget and tax base. The actual rate is not set until November of every year. The Town doesn't receive the amount to be raised by taxes for the County, State, and the school until November. Your first bill is usually lower because of that timing. The second bill has to catch up on all of the increases in six months and is therefore twice the increase as the annual tax increase."
