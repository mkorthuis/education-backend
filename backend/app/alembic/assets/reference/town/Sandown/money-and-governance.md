# Sandown: Money, Taxes and Governance

Part of the Sandown research (town/Sandown). Agent 4. Gathered 2026-11-21.

## Summary

**What's covered:** Sandown's side of the Timberlane Regional School District's money and governance, reusing `town/timberlane/timberlane-money-and-governance.md` (cited below as "Timberlane money file", by topic and fact number) for the district-wide budget, the four-town formula, the fund balance, the lease and the 2015 study. 8 topics, 57 facts and 4 number tables.
- **Sandown's tax bill.** The 2026 rate ($20.89; local school $15.50, up $3.07) from the town's own rate history, which the site's data (through 2025) doesn't have yet; the school share of the bill; the 2013, 2018 and 2023 revaluations; the failed 2026 town budget; Sandown's share of Timberlane's costs from FY2018 to FY2026 and of the four towns' equalized value (about 22%, against about 29% of the students); the elderly exemption, which voters widened in March 2026, veterans' and blind credits and the state's relief.
- **Votes.** The district-wide results for March 2023–2026 by town, found on Sandown's site (the Timberlane research had not found the 2026 totals): every 2026 article, including the 25% tax cap (failed 1,939 to 3,220) and full-day kindergarten (failed); how often Sandown's majority differed from the district result (20 of 53 articles, **derived**); the 2023 lease vote (55.5% yes; Sandown 273 to 377 against); the 2025 advisory lease petitions.
- **Leaving Timberlane.** The 2015 minority plan (Pinkerton Academy for high school, Sandown North for grades 4–8, the buyout figures) and the March 2016 town vote against starting withdrawal (1,311 against, about 80% by speakers' accounts).
- **Buildings, meetings and outlook.** The Sandown North sprinkler ($2.1 million) and expansion plan deferrals, the oil tank, Sandown Central's kitchen, how the capital plan works and why work waits; where and when the board meets, the 2026 tax cap hearing at Sandown North, the September 2026 recess and the Right-to-Know disputes; NESDEC's Sandown projections (867 to 846 students over ten years); Sandown home prices.
- **A state rule:** RSA 33:7-e, which lets a district approve a lease-purchase with a nonappropriation clause by simple majority, not as debt.

**Main gaps:** no Timberlane document splits costs by school, so the elementary and secondary parts of a Sandown tax bill can't be separated; the official result of Sandown's March 2016 withdrawal article (only speakers' statements in draft selectmen's minutes were found); the June 19, 2023 special district meeting that, by a 2025 board resolution, ratified the lease; Sandown's equalization ratio for 2026; whether lease payments count as "bonded capital costs" under the Question 2 cap; turnover or "stability" measures; the town's Tax Assistance page (HTTP 403).

**Topic proposal:** `lease-purchase-financing-rules` (rule, rules): "How New Hampshire towns and school districts may finance equipment and related building improvements with lease-purchase agreements: the simple majority vote, nonappropriation clauses, and how leases differ from bonds." No shared topic covers lease financing; the library's `budget-school-bonds` covers only bonds.

**Questions answered wholly by earlier files:** 2 of 18 (P8.1 from the Timberlane money file and the site's DOE-25 data; P12.3 from the site's cost per pupil data and the library's measure-cost-per-pupil). The rest add Sandown's own facts.

| P6.1, P6.3 | "Withdrawal talks" | Correct as history. No later study was found. |
| P12.1 | The Budget Committee "cuts $2 million to hit a tax target" | No $2 million Budget Committee cut was found. For FY2027 the Budget Committee's reductions were $3,878,407; for FY2026 the $6.6 million cut was made by voters at the deliberative session; for FY2028 the district's planning shows a $3.8 million Budget Committee reduction and about $2.1 million more to fit the Question 2 cap. |
| P12.2 | A "$25 million lease-purchase agreement without a regular bond vote" | Partly. Voters approved it on the March 2023 ballot (Article 3), 1,658 to 1,327 (55.5%; Sandown 273 to 377 against). A lease-purchase with a nonappropriation clause needs only a simple majority and isn't treated as debt under RSA 33:7-e; a bond needs 60%. A 2025 advisory vote to rescind it passed, but it was advisory; payments of about $1.0 million a year run to 2044. |
| P8.3 | Meetings "always turning into shouting matches" | Not supported as "always." Most minutes record orderly votes. One recess, on September 17, 2026, followed what a board member called disorderly discussion; the disputes in the record are over the budget's size, the fund balance, tax caps, the lease, and conduct around the 2026 deliberative session and Right-to-Know requests. |
| P20.3 | "SAU meetings in Plaistow" | Correct. Timberlane is its own SAU 106; regular School Board and Budget Committee meetings are in the SAU boardroom at 30 Greenough Road, Plaistow, streamed on Vimeo. Voting is at Sandown Town Hall, and some hearings are held in each town (Sandown North, February 20, 2026). |

## lease-purchase-financing-rules: Lease-Purchase Financing for Town and School Buildings

- kind: rule
- question_type: rules
- description: How New Hampshire towns and school districts may finance equipment and related building improvements with lease-purchase agreements: the simple majority vote, nonappropriation clauses, and how leases differ from bonds

1. Under RSA 33:7-e a New Hampshire town or school district's governing body may enter into leases of equipment, and appropriations for lease agreements that have nonappropriation clauses may be approved by a simple majority of the legislative body and are not treated as debt, unlike bonds and notes, which need a three-fifths ballot vote.
   - citation: RSA 33:7-e; RSA 33:1, I; RSA 33:8 (library: budget-school-bonds 1)
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/III/33/33-7-e.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-21
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Statewide rule, so no place. Text: "The governing body may enter into leases of equipment as required by the municipality. Appropriations to fund lease agreements with nonappropriation clauses may be approved by a simple majority vote of the legislative body. Lease agreements with nonappropriation clauses shall not be treated as debt under RSA 33:4-a." RSA 33:1, I: "'Municipality' or 'municipal corporation,' town, city, school district or village district". A nonappropriation clause generally lets the borrower end the lease if voters don't fund a year's payment (the statute doesn't define it). Raw: raw/money/rsa-33-7-e.txt; raw/money/rsa-33-1.txt.
2. Under RSA 33:7-e "lease" includes a lease-purchase, sale and lease-back, installment sale or similar agreement to acquire use or ownership of equipment, and building or facility improvements related to the installation, purpose or operation of such equipment count as equipment, so their cost may also be financed by such a lease.
   - citation: RSA 33:7-e (as amended 2014, 60:1)
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/III/33/33-7-e.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-21
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Statewide rule, so no place. Text: "For the purposes of this section, 'lease' shall include lease-purchase, sale and lease back, installment sale, or other similar agreement to acquire use or ownership of such equipment ... For purposes of this section and RSA 382-A, building or facility improvements related to the installation, purpose, or operation of such equipment shall be deemed to constitute equipment and the costs of such improvements may be financed through lease agreements under this section." "Source. 1999, 35:1, eff. July 10, 1999. 2014, 60:1, eff. July 26, 2014." Hooksett's school board chair described the same difference (50% for leases, 60% for bonds) in 2026 (Hooksett money-and-governance.md district-facilities-and-capital 2).
