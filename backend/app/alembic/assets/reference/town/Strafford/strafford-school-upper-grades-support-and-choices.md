# Strafford School: Upper Grades, Support and Choices

Part of the Strafford research (town/Strafford). Gathered November 22, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers Strafford School's middle grades and the supports and choices around them: what the district says about its state test results, how students who fall behind get help (MTSS, WIN Time, Title I tutoring, the Spartan Camp summer school), special education staff and who decides services, who to contact at SAU 105, middle school sports and clubs and what else Strafford offers young teens, homeschooled and EFA students' access, the school counselors, STEM and technology, world languages, and enrichment for advanced learners. It also sets out which of these programs are on the draft list of reductions the district prepared after Strafford voters adopted the HB 1300 tax cap on November 3, 2026. Sources are the Strafford School and SAU 105 websites, the published staff directory, the 2026–27 PreK–5 and grades 6–8 student handbooks, the K–8 curriculum guide, MTSS guidebook and assessment guide, the November 18, 2026 board packet (minutes of October 14, October 28 and November 10, 2026, the superintendent's report and the stipend summary), other 2026 board packets and minutes (June 10, June 24, August 12, September 9, September 23, October 14 and November 10), the FY27 spend-to-date and FY28 budget snapshots, the December 2 special budget hearing flyer, the June 2026 year-end data summary, the 2025 town report, Strafford Recreational Sports and Hill Library pages, the library's EFA marketplace list, and three district policies on home-educated pupils (JJJ, IHBG, IHBG-R) that the main session opened in Chrome on November 22.

**Counts:** 10 topics (all shared slugs), 100 facts and 2 number tables (support and activity budget lines, FY27 and FY28 proposed; i-Ready math placement in grades 5–8, 2025–26).

**New data concepts:**
- A district's own explanation of its test results: opt-outs in grades 7 and 8, local growth measures (NWEA, i-Ready placement shifts) beside state proficiency.
- A draft "what-if" reduction list under a new tax cap, matched against the programs residents asked about (tutoring, summer school, athletics, a special education case manager, the student support center, tech education).
- How a school's summer school is funded (Title I stipends) and staffed, separately from special education's Extended School Year.
- A receiving high school's survey of a sending town's freshmen, used by the sending board.

**Main findings:**
- Fewer than half of Strafford School's students were proficient in 2025–26: 45% in math (state 44%) and 48% in reading (state 55%), site data. The district's own data summary says "a large portion" of grade 7 and 8 students opted out of the spring test, which "likely had a significant impact on data"; site data show grade 8 participation of 62% in math and 54% in reading. Math growth (NWEA) was above the national norm; reading growth was below it.
- The draft reduction list (November 10, 2026) includes "regular ed summer school," "all tutoring," "all co-curricular programs including athletics," "the student support center," "the tech education program" and "a special education teacher/case manager." The flyer for the December 2 hearing says "FIRST DRAFT ONLY, NOTHING DECIDED." Special education's Extended School Year is not on the list.
- Strafford School has three school counselors totaling 2 full-time equivalents (one full-time, two part-time). Two of them cover grades 6–8 five days a week; one covers K–5.
- Strafford School teaches no world language. 30% of the 35 Strafford freshmen CBNA surveyed in September 2026 named this as a barrier, and the board approved an after-school Spanish Club for grades 6–8 on November 10, 2026.
- Home-educated children can join Strafford School courses, sports, band and chorus under regulation IHBG-R: the parent writes to the principal, and the child follows the same conduct and eligibility rules. Related services such as speech or counseling are "generally" denied. Policy IHBG still describes the home education notice, evaluation and portfolio rules New Hampshire dropped on July 1, 2026, and none of the three policies names EFA students.
- Middle school teams: soccer, volleyball and cross country in fall, basketball in winter, baseball and softball in spring. Strafford School has no track team; the Strafford Rec Sports site points to the NH Thunder track program at Coe-Brown's track.

**Main gaps:**
- No caseload numbers for special education case managers or speech and occupational therapists were found. No public source says whether any child has missed IEP services.
- Strafford's home education policies (JJJ, IHBG, IHBG-R, opened in Chrome by the main session on November 22) don't mention EFA students, and IHBG still describes the home education rules from before July 1, 2026. The athletics site loads by script, and NH DOE's home education page returned HTTP 403.
- Whether the Title I–funded tutor and Spartan Camp, which are paid from federal grants, count among the draft list's "all tutoring" and "regular ed summer school" isn't stated.
- Strafford School's gifted-and-talented report to NH DOE wasn't retrieved. No computer science course is named in the handbook or curriculum guide.
- No web search was used. Everything came from district, town and organization sites and their linked Google Drive files.
