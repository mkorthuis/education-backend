# Litchfield: Money and Governance

Part of the Litchfield research (town/Litchfield). Gathered November 21, 2026.

## Summary

**What's covered:** 5 topics, 62 facts and 8 number tables on how the Litchfield School District (SAU 27) builds, votes on and pays for its budget, what happened when it ran on a default budget, what it spends on special education, its teachers' contract and pay against Hudson, the school tax rate, the Griffin Memorial School (GMS) building project going to voters in March 2027, the capital plan for buildings, gyms and fields, and how the School Board and Budget Committee meet, stream and take public input.

- **The budget.** The FY2028 (2027-28) budget stood at $28,073,982 on November 10, 2026, 2.69% over the prior year, after the board cut health insurance, attrition and special education lines; it now goes to the Budget Committee (vote expected December 3). The FY2027 budget ($28,411,138) passed 755–730 in March 2026; the FY2026 budget failed in March 2025 and the district ran on the default budget, cutting the late bus, some repairs and six-period class funding but not staff beyond 10 paraprofessional positions already planned. The district says it has cut more than 20 positions over three years and moved to a self-insured health plan.
- **Special education.** $5,367,067 spent in FY2025 (up from $4,777,845), about 21% of general and special revenue fund spending (**derived**); 208 students with disabilities in December 2025, 14 placed out of district; a special education trust fund of about $377,000.
- **Teachers.** The 2026–2028 LEA contract (approved 814–665) starts teachers at $47,848 in 2026-27, $3,152 below Hudson's $51,000 (**derived**); the district said it adjusted first-year pay to compete with Hudson. No turnover rate is published; vacancies produced a $1.3 million FY2026 surplus, while "lower turnover" in 2026 limited FY2028 attrition savings.
- **Taxes.** The local education rate was $9.73 in both 2025 and 2026, though the school levy rose $549,829 (2.8%, **derived**) as valuations rose. The GMS project's district estimate is about $297 in year one and about $755 a year after that on a $618,000 home (about $0.48 and $1.22 per $1,000, **derived**).
- **Buildings.** GMS (1930, additions to 1983, portables) is in "fair" condition; the 2024 new-school bond ($44.29 million) failed with 43.9% yes. The board approved a $38.9 million renovation and addition (25-year bond) for the March 2027 ballot. Campbell's roof is planned for 2031 from a reserve the district is building up. The capital plan shows no large field projects soon; gym floor and lighting work at Campbell is listed for 2031–2032 and the track for 2043.
- **Governance.** Five board members, three-year terms (two seats end March 2027); meetings on the first and third Wednesday at 5:30 p.m. at Town Hall, streamed and posted on LitchfieldTV's YouTube channel, as are Budget Committee meetings.

**Main sources:** the district's Finance & Operations, District Information, School Board, Agendas, Minutes, Capital Plan and Contracts pages; the 2025 Annual Town and School Report (school section: 2025 and 2026 deliberative session minutes, warrants, MS-27, special education statement, enrollment projections); 2024 and 2026 official election results; 2025 warrant; School Board minutes (March 2025 to October 2026) and the board's one-page meeting summaries ("snapshots") of October 7, November 4 and November 10, 2026; the LEA tentative agreement (October 2025); policies BEA and BEDH; the Griffin Memorial School Project site and On-Site Insight's capital needs assessments (December 2024); the district's Capital Improvement Plan workbook; the town's 2026 tax rate sheet, Budget Committee agenda, LCTV page and YouTube feed; the site's database (DOE-25, salary data, tax rates).

Athletics: the board discussed a pay-to-participate model ($50 a sport, $100 a year maximum, with scholarships) and reported 152 fall, 124 spring and 113 winter athletes (same snapshot) (P10.1); a cooperative girls' lacrosse team with Alvirne was debated in June 2026 (raw/money/minutes/minutes-2026-06-30.txt). The default budget left robotics with $400; voters added $9,600 at the 2026 deliberative session (district-budget-and-cost 9). Board members said Campbell's state ranking rose from 78th (2023) to 19th (minutes August 19, 2026; raw/money/minutes/minutes-2026-08-19.txt) (P12.3, also agent 3).
