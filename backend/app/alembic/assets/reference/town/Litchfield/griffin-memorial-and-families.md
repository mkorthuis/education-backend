# Griffin Memorial School and Young Families

Part of the Litchfield research (town/Litchfield). Gathered November 21, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers Griffin Memorial School (GMS, preschool to grade 4) from a young family's side: full-day kindergarten and what families pay, the 2:45–2:50 dismissal and the before- and after-school care inside the building, the kindergarten birthday cutoff and the integrated preschool, bus changes to a babysitter's or child care address, free and reduced-price meals, special education and speech services, IEPs that move in from another district, aides and class sizes, the reading program, Chromebooks and screens, closing alerts and pickup by grandparents, mid-year registration and buses, help for advanced and struggling students, after-school clubs, and how Griffin's test results compare with the state's.

- **14 topics, 57 facts and 2 number tables.** Raw copies are in `raw/elementary/` (about 39 MB): the GMS 2025-26 handbook (the version linked from the school's page), 29 district and school pages, six board policies (JEB, JG, JQ, IIB, EFC, EEA-R), the alternative transportation form, the special education procedures (Section 8) and program descriptions, the Title I plan, Child Find notice, 2026-27 calendar, meal FAQ and Chromebook fee notice, the ELA curriculum framework, six GMS newsletters (with private family details removed), ten sets of School Board minutes (`raw/elementary/minutes/`), the Nashua Adult Learning Center's School Age Adventures page and 2026-27 Litchfield enrollment packet (scanned; pages 1 and 5 saved as images and transcribed in the notes), UFLI's program page, and two read-only queries of the site's database.
- **Topics:** twelve shared slugs (`district-preschool`, `district-school-day-and-calendar`, `district-transportation`, `district-fees`, `district-meals`, `district-special-education-services`, `district-classrooms-and-staff`, `district-curriculum-and-assessment`, `district-enrichment-and-advanced-learners`, `district-technology-and-phones`, `district-family-communication`, `district-school-results-compared`) and two library topics with their own kind and description (`kindergarten-day-length`, `kindergarten-entry-cutoff`). | Question | Premise | What the sources show |

| P1.1 | Full-day kindergarten at Griffin Memorial is "free" | Correct as far as the sources go. Kindergarten is full day (8:25 to 2:50 on the kindergarten page) and no tuition is posted anywhere, including the 2025-26 handbook. Families do pay for some things: items on the supply list, meals ($2.75 breakfast, $3.75 lunch unless the child qualifies), and optional extras. The $5 Chromebook usage fee is for grades 1–4 only. No kindergarten "enrichment" fee was found. |
| P1.2 | School lets out at 2:50 | Roughly right; sources differ by five minutes. The GMS office and kindergarten pages say 8:25–2:50; the 2025-26 handbook says 2:45. | P1.3 | A late-September birthday may "miss" the kindergarten cutoff | Probably not. Policy JEB lets a child start kindergarten if he or she turns five on or before September 30, so a birthday up to September 30 qualifies. The policy found is from 2008 and sits in an archive folder; no early-entrance exception is written. The GMS integrated preschool takes children age 4 and 5, on a space-available, tuition basis for children without special needs. |
| P5.1 | First Student runs the buses and might drop a first grader at a babysitter's on work days | First Student is correct. A different drop-off address is possible only as "Alternative Transportation" for child care, approved by First Student, on a long-term basis (at least two weeks), for every school day, morning and afternoon, to an existing stop, and only if the bus has room. The form says requests are considered only "on a FIVE day per week basis," so drop-offs only on the days a parent works are not offered. |
| P15.1 | About 60% of Griffin students are proficient in math | Out of date. Griffin's math proficiency (grades 3 and 4 together) was 60% in 2024-25 but 71% in 2025-26, in the site's data. The 60% matches the 2024-25 figure on SchoolDigger. |
| P15.2 | There might be a "before-school band" | No before-school band, STEM club or gifted program was found. Band starts in grade 5 at Litchfield Middle School. GMS has after-school chorus (grades 3–4), Art Club (3–4), drama (2–4) and Girls on the Run (3–4). |
| P11.1 | Teachers may still use "older balanced-literacy" methods | The district reports using UFLI Foundations, which UFLI describes as an explicit and systematic program for foundational reading skills (phonemic awareness, phonics), in kindergarten and as a grade 3 pilot in 2025-26. The Title I reading tutors' plan (revised 2022) names Fountas and Pinnell Leveled Literacy Intervention. Whether any classroom still uses other methods is not recorded. |
| P8.2 | Litchfield may "re-test everything from scratch" for an IEP from Nashua | Not what the district's procedures say. For a child moving from another New Hampshire district, the disability identification "will be honored without delay" and comparable services start while the team adopts the old IEP or writes a new one. Extra evaluation is described only for children from another state. |

   - place: district Litchfield
   - note: Policy text: "A student may enter Kindergarten if his/her chronological age will be five on or before September 30 of the year of entering school. A certified birth certificate must be presented upon registration as proof of the date of birth ... When there is a change of residence, the Litchfield School District will honor the 'age of entrance' policy of the sending school district." - question_type: local_practice
- description: Devices students use, screen time, technology rules, and phone and personal device policies

1. Griffin Memorial's handbook says no student uses the internet during the school day without adult supervision, Chromebooks are district devices loaned as learning tools with an internet filter, and parents must acknowledge the district's acceptable use policy each year in PowerSchool.
   - citation: GMS Student/Parent Handbook 2025-2026, section 7 and 7.1
   - source: https://docs.google.com/document/d/1SnnwuwBONAf0IzsBz0gQSPdUk-MQoUSq4NBnc2d9Phs/edit
   - as_of: 2026-11-21
   - review_by: 2027-11-21
   - place: district Litchfield
   - note: Handbook: "Griffin Memorial has access to technology and the internet. During the school day no student will have access to the internet without adult supervision ... Chromebooks are Litchfield School District issued devices and are loaned to students as an educational tool ... An internet filtering system is used on all district issued Chromebooks ... The Litchfield School District Digital Technology/Electronic Media Acceptable Use policy is one of the forms that parents must review and acknowledge yearly in PowerSchool." The district technology page (undated) says Chromebooks "are placed with students in grades 5-12" in its 1:1 program; the GMS pages link learning sites such as ABCmouse, Starfall, XtraMath and IXL. No source gives how many minutes a day K–4 students use screens. Raw: raw/elementary/gms-handbook-2025-26.txt; raw/elementary/sd-technology.txt; raw/elementary/gms-teams.txt

2. On bad-weather remote learning days, Griffin Memorial students in grades 1 to 4 stay home and use a Chromebook or other computer to join their teacher and classmates through Google Classroom and Google Meet and to work on their own, taking a school Chromebook home the night before if the family paid the usage fee, while kindergartners get paper materials instead.
   - citation: GMS Student/Parent Handbook 2025-2026, section 2.2
   - source: https://docs.google.com/document/d/1SnnwuwBONAf0IzsBz0gQSPdUk-MQoUSq4NBnc2d9Phs/edit
   - as_of: 2026-11-21
   - review_by: 2027-11-21
   - place: district Litchfield
   - note: Handbook: "Remote learning days may be called for students in grades 1-4 during inclement weather. On these days students stay at home and use a Chromebook or other computer to log into Google Classroom to meet with their teacher and classmates through Google Meet, as well as to work independently on assignments ... Students that pay the Chromebook usage fee (See Section 7.4) will have a school issued Chromebook sent home with them the night before ... Students in kindergarten do not use a Chromebook on remote learning days." State rules on remote days: library remote-learning-and-snow-day-rules. Raw: raw/elementary/gms-handbook-2025-26.txt

3. Griffin Memorial students who bring a phone or smartwatch must keep it turned off and in their backpack all day, may not make or receive calls or texts, and may not photograph or record other students or staff at school or on buses; parents who need to reach a child call the office.

Students are not allowed to make or receive calls or texts during the school day, and parents should not be calling or texting students during the course of the school day ... may not use that device to take pictures or video of any student or staff member while at GMS (this includes the playground and on buses)." State phone law: library climate-phone-free-school-day. Raw: raw/elementary/gms-handbook-2025-26.txt

Mr. O'Connell emphasized that technology should be used intentionally to support instruction. ## district-family-communication: How Schools Reach Families

- kind: local
- question_type: local_practice
- description: How schools reach families: parent portals, grade tracking, emergency notices, and translation and interpreting

1. Litchfield posts closings, delays and remote learning days, usually decided around 5:00 a.m., on the district's Facebook page, the district website and WMUR Channel 9, and the Superintendent's office sends a phone call and email to families after 5:30 a.m.; parents can also opt into district text messages by texting 67587, and the handbook says the district uses only these channels.
   - citation: GMS Student/Parent Handbook 2025-2026, section 2.2, "No School/Delayed Opening/Remote Learning Day"
   - source: https://docs.google.com/document/d/1SnnwuwBONAf0IzsBz0gQSPdUk-MQoUSq4NBnc2d9Phs/edit
   - as_of: 2026-11-21
   - review_by: 2027-11-21
   - place: district Litchfield
   - note: Handbook: "As soon as the decision to delay/cancel school or have a remote learning day is made (usually around 5:00 am), it will be posted to the district Facebook page (Litchfield School District, Litchfield NH), the district website and WMUR Channel 9. Also, a phone call and an email from the Superintendent will be scheduled to go out to families after 5:30 am. The school district will provide only these 5 methods of notification ... Parents can opt into Litchfield School District text messaging at 67587 if they would like to receive district information, including school delays/closures, via text." The Facebook page, website and WMUR are open to anyone, including grandparents. The handbook doesn't say whether calls, emails or texts can go to someone other than a parent. Raw: raw/elementary/gms-handbook-2025-26.txt

2. Griffin Memorial keeps each student's address, phone numbers and emergency contacts in PowerSchool, which parents update through the Parent Portal and must confirm every year in the back-to-school forms (due September 30), and the district sends phone and email messages about closings, early dismissals and school events through PowerSchool Messenger.
   - citation: GMS Student/Parent Handbook 2025-2026, sections 2.3 and 2.4; Back-to-School Forms notice
   - source: https://docs.google.com/document/d/1SnnwuwBONAf0IzsBz0gQSPdUk-MQoUSq4NBnc2d9Phs/edit
   - as_of: 2026-11-21
   - review_by: 2027-11-21
   - place: district Litchfield
   - note: Handbook 2.3: "On a very rare occasion it may be necessary to dismiss students early due to an emergency (i.e., power failure, severe storm). Parents need to ensure that the address, phone numbers, emergency contacts and other pertinent information is accurately reflected in PowerSchool ... PowerSchool Messenger is a communication product that utilizes telephone and e-mail systems. It allows us to inform parents about school closures as well as important school events." Notice: "These forms must be completed by September 30 – they contain information that is critical for updating contact information". Absences are reported through SafeArrival (SchoolMessenger app, go.schoolmessenger.com or 1-833-516-0370). Raw: raw/elementary/gms-handbook-2025-26.txt; raw/elementary/back-to-school-tech-news.txt; raw/elementary/gms-office.txt

3. For anyone other than a parent to pick up a Griffin Memorial child, the parent sends a written note with the child's full name, the date and the full name of the adult picking up; that adult must show a picture ID, the child is signed out to the adult, and early dismissals should happen before 2:30 p.m.
   - citation: GMS Student/Parent Handbook 2025-2026, section 4.5, "Dismissal"; GMS Office page
   - source: https://docs.google.com/document/d/1SnnwuwBONAf0IzsBz0gQSPdUk-MQoUSq4NBnc2d9Phs/edit
   - as_of: 2026-11-21
   - review_by: 2027-11-21
   - place: district Litchfield
   - note: Handbook: "We ask that early dismissals take place before 2:30 ... Without a note the student will be sent home his/her usual way. You must present a picture ID when coming to dismiss any child. The GMS staff member dismissing your child may not know you. If you are intending to pick up a child other than your own, the Main Office MUST have a NOTE from the parent of the child that includes the child's full name, date of dismissal, and the full name of the adult who will be dismissing the child. Without a note, the child will not be allowed to go with you. Students must be signed out to the custody of an adult." The office page links a sample dismissal note. No standing list of authorized adults is described. Raw: raw/elementary/gms-handbook-2025-26.txt; raw/elementary/gms-office.txt

   - citation: Griffin Memorial School News ("Griffin Times"), November 20, 2026; GMS home page ("Weekly GMS News-From Principal Mitchell")

## district-school-results-compared: Comparing a District's Schools: Scores, Growth and Student Needs

- kind: local
- question_type: comparison
- description: How a district's schools compare on state test proficiency and growth, the share of low-income students and English learners at each, state ratings and improvement designations, and what the comparisons can and can't show (added from the Manchester research)

1. From 2014-15 through 2025-26 (11 test years in the site's data), Griffin Memorial's grade 3 students were at or above New Hampshire's grade 3 proficiency rate in both reading and math every year, while its grade 4 students were above the state in math in 6 of the 11 years and in reading in 9 of the 11; in 2025-26 both grades were 16 to 21 points above the state in both subjects.
   - citation: NH Education Facts database (NH DOE state assessment results by school, grade and state), queried read-only
   - source: https://nhfacts.com
   - as_of: 2026-11-21
   - review_by: 2027-11-21
   - place: district Litchfield
   - note: Derived by comparing Griffin's percent proficient or above (all students) with the state's for the same grade and year; see tables litchfield_griffin_math_grades_3_4_vs_state and litchfield_griffin_reading_grades_3_4_vs_state. Grade 3 reading in 2016-17 was 54% against the state's 53.92%, and grade 3 math in 2021-22 52% against 51%. Grade 4 fell below the state in math in 2014-15, 2020-21, 2021-22, 2022-23 and 2024-25 (51% against 52%), and in reading in 2017-18 (54% against 55%) and 2021-22 (39% against 48%). Each grade had about 60 to 105 tested students, so one student is roughly 1 to 1.7 percentage points (derived); the site's data has no 2019-20 results. Across grades 3 and 4 together, Griffin's math proficiency was 60% in 2024-25 and 71% in 2025-26 (site data). Comparing small groups: Tamworth high-school.md, comparing-small-schools facts 1–2; rating sites: library school-rating-sites. Raw: raw/elementary/site-data-griffin-scores-vs-state.txt
