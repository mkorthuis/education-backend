# New Ipswich: Money and Governance

Part of the New Ipswich research (town/New Ipswich). Gathered November 22, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers the Mascenic Regional School District's money and governance as New Ipswich residents meet them: why the school tax has risen, how costs are split between New Ipswich and Greenville, the proposed and default budgets and three straight years on a default, the March 2026 ballot town by town, the teachers' and support staff contracts and what a contract adds to the tax rate, paraprofessional pay and the failed support staff contract, substitutes and their rates, the capital reserve funds and current building repairs, the Highbridge Hill bond ending in FY27, the FY28 (2027-28) draft budget, board meetings and how to watch them, the deliberative session, the HB 1300 tax cap vote, the building consolidation question, the youth population and enrollment, and the schools' place on Turnpike Road.

**Counts:** 10 topics, 84 facts, 11 number tables.

**Main sources:** Mascenic's own budget documents (the FY27 warrant, default budget form MS-DSB, voted appropriations MS-22, signed 2026 election results, deliberative session presentation and minutes, budget FAQ, tax impact and revenue sheets, voting mailer, FY28 Draft 1 and its summary, FY28 timeline, HB 1300 FAQ, Advisory Budget Committee orientation), the MEA (teachers) 2025-2028 and MESSA (support staff) 2023-2026 contracts, policy GCGA (substitutes), and the school board's 2025-26 and 2026 minutes, memos and capital reserve requests from the district's public Google Drive folder; the Town of New Ipswich Master Plan Update 2026; the 2010 and 2020 Census; and Monadnock Ledger-Transcript articles for the 2024 and 2025 votes. Two Ledger-Transcript articles on the March 2026 vote print district totals as New Ipswich's figures; the facts use the signed results.

**Main findings:**
- **Default budgets:** the operating budget failed in 2024, 2025 and 2026. In March 2026 the proposed $23,899,107 lost 623 to 981 (New Ipswich 488–889, Greenville 135–92), putting the $23,431,429 default in place. To meet it the board cut $580,048 (two Boynton teachers, a Highbridge third grade teacher and preschool teacher, Boynton co-curriculars and more).
- **Split:** local school costs are apportioned 75% by each town's resident pupils (ADM) and 25% by property value. New Ipswich's 2025 share was 76.0492%. Each town keeps its own state aid, and Greenville gets far more aid per pupil (an extraordinary needs grant New Ipswich does not get), so New Ipswich makes about 83% of the monthly payments to the district (derived).
- **Contracts:** the teachers' 2025-2028 contract passed in 2025 (about $287,085 in year one). The support staff contract failed in March 2026 (669–921), leaving paras on the 2025-26 scale ($16.15 to $20.30 an hour for paraprofessionals). The board added a $2,000 retention bonus and said its scale is $4 to $5 an hour below neighbors that passed contracts.
- **Capital reserve:** the building repair and maintenance reserve held $241,623 on October 31, 2026, after a $183,500 Highbridge heating replacement and voters' $150,000 addition. Current requests are sprinklers ($17,346) and the Highbridge septic controls.
- **FY28:** the Highbridge Hill bond is paid off in FY27, removing about $1.5 million of debt service. Draft 1 for 2027-28 is $23,234,798 (about 2% less), but health insurance (+15% estimated) and special education (+$707,474) take much of the savings. The SAU budget goes on its own warrant article for the first time. The deliberative session is February 3, 2027.
- **Enrollment and youth:** district enrollment fell from 887 to 833 between October 2025 and October 2026, mostly from a smaller kindergarten (49). New Ipswich's under-18 population fell 8.1% between the 2010 and 2020 Censuses. A 2026 enrollment study expects enrollment to stabilize and slowly rise.

**Main gaps:** NH DRA's cooperative apportionment worksheet (refused automated requests), so the exact ADM and valuation inputs behind 76.0492% are unconfirmed; no traffic study or pickup-and-drop-off plan for Turnpike Road; no published substitute fill rate or para vacancy count; the new support staff scale under negotiation; the FY28 default budget, not set until January 2027; and the board's November 16, 2026 minutes, not yet posted.

## district-sau-governance: Sharing an SAU: Seats, Votes and Costs

- kind: local
- question_type: governance_money
- description: How a district shares an SAU: board seats and votes, how SAU costs are divided, and what the local board decides versus the SAU

1.
