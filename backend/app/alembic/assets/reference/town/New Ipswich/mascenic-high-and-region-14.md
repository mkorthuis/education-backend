# Mascenic Regional High School and Region 14 Career Programs

Part of the New Ipswich research (town/New Ipswich). Gathered November 22, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers Mascenic Regional High School (MRHS, grades 9–12, in New Ipswich) for New Ipswich families: classes for homeschooled and EFA students; the automotive program and the building trades program that restarted in fall 2026; Mascenic's side of the Region 14 career and technical education (CTE) agreement with ConVal and Jaffrey-Rindge, and how Mascenic gets students to other centers; AP, honors, Running Start and VLACS; Viking Block; class rank and GPA weighting; SAT and PSAT results and what the district says it is doing about math; graduates' plans; counseling, college applications, the military and records; courses cut, kept and added; sports and co-ops; and local businesses' part in the trades programs.

- **12 fact topics, 66 facts and 2 number tables.** Raw copies are in `raw/high/` (about 13 MB): eight district policies (JJJ, IHCD, IMBA, IKFG, IHBH, IGDA, JRA, IKF); the 2026-27 MRHS Program of Studies (approved February 2, 2026) with its change notes, and the 2025-26 summary of changes; the 2026-27 Student/Family Handbook; the August 2025 course-enrollment memo and the August 2026 low-enrollment list; the amended Region 14 agreement (December 2025); eleven MRHS principal's reports (September 2025 to November 2026); 14 sets of board, budget-hearing and deliberative-session minutes (December 2024 to October 2026) and a January 2025 academic update; the FY2027 MS-22, the FY2026 default-budget adjustments, the FY2028 first budget version and the district's March 2026 newspaper column; the automotive truck donation request; three NHIAA 2026-27 handbook sections; Region 14's application page; 20 U.S.C. 7908; and the routing files for the derived drive times. Mascenic's own website (mascenic.org) answered automated requests with a "Client Challenge" page, so everything here comes from the district's Google Drive (policies, board folders), which allows direct requests.
- **Places:** 64 facts use `district Mascenic Regional`; 2 federal rules have no place. Two facts (district-transportation 3, high-school-outcomes 1) and table mascenic_graduates_plans_compared are **derived**, and cte-center-programs 7 includes a derived check of the March 2026 vote.
- **New data concepts:** the Region 14 agreement's seat split (75/25), CTE access program tuition (80% of cost per pupil) and calendar rule; Mascenic's 2026 building trades warrant article and its outcome; Mascenic's in-house CTE vans and CTE tuition line; courses the board approved with fewer than nine students; Viking Block; GPA weighting, class rank and valedictorian rules; SAT School Day and PSAT averages against the state; graduates working or enlisting compared with the state; the federal military-recruiter access rule.
- **Proposed new shared topic:** `military-recruiter-access-rules` (rule, rules): "Federal rules on military recruiters' access to high school students and their contact information, and how parents opt out." No existing topic covers it.
- **Main gaps:** how many Mascenic students attend Region 14 programs at ConVal and the van times there; credentials offered in the new building trades program; FAFSA nights and scholarship help (the MRHS counseling pages are on the blocked website); how former students request transcripts; VLACS's own course list (its catalog loads through scripts); a Mascenic school profile for colleges; any football or hockey co-op discussion (none found).

## district-homeschool-access: Homeschooled and EFA Students Taking Classes at Mascenic High

- kind: local
- question_type: local_practice
- description: How homeschooled and EFA students can take part in a district's classes, activities and sports, and the district's homeschool notice and evaluation process

1. Mascenic Regional School District policy JJJ, adopted October 5, 2026, says resident students who are home educated, in the Education Freedom Account (EFA) program, or attending a chartered public or nonpublic school have the same access as enrolled students to the district's courses and co- and extracurricular programs, and must meet the same eligibility rules, such as registration deadlines, prerequisites, parental permission and health requirements.
   - citation: Mascenic Regional School District Policy JJJ, Access to Public School Programs by Non-Public, Charter School, and Home Educated Pupils (adopted 10.5.2026), section A
   - source: https://drive.google.com/file/d/1xQXm1rq9A8fJ-wO-ickDUILDBA-8r2L0/view
   - as_of: 2026-11-22
   - review_by: 2027-11-22
   - place: district Mascenic Regional
   - note: Text: "All non-enrolled resident students will have the same access as do enrolled students to the District's courses and programs and shall not be subject to any policies, procedures or standards ... that are more restrictive than those governing the District's enrolled students"; "provided they meet the eligibility requirements for participation (e.g., deadlines for registration, academic progress/performance, parental permission, third party (e.g., NHIAA) requirements, physical exams/health requirements, etc.)." Families whose home education ended when the student entered the EFA program must tell the district the student is now an EFA student. Under Mascenic's policy JJJ, a parent asks the building principal in writing for a homeschooled or EFA student to take a course; the principal decides whether the student meets the course's prerequisites the same way as for transfer students, may consider course descriptions, syllabi or other information the parent offers, and may require assessment testing.
   - citation: Mascenic Policy JJJ, section B, Participation in Curricular Courses
   - source: https://drive.google.com/file/d/1xQXm1rq9A8fJ-wO-ickDUILDBA-8r2L0/view

The Principal may also require assessment testing." "The building Principal will provide eligibility criteria to parents or guardians of non-enrolled resident students upon request." The MRHS main number is 603-878-1113 (2026-27 handbook). Raw copy: raw/high/policies/policy-jjj.txt

## cte-center-programs: Career and Technical Education at Mascenic: Automotive, Building Trades and Region 14

- kind: local
- question_type: local_practice
- description: The programs at a regional career and technical center, which schools send students, credentials and college credit

1. Under the Region 14 career and technical education agreement amended in December 2025, the Contoocook Valley (ConVal) and Mascenic Regional districts are both "receiving" districts that host programs and "sending" districts, while Jaffrey-Rindge only sends students; Mascenic's board approved the revised agreement 4-0 on December 15, 2025, and it runs through June 30, 2029.
   - citation: Region 14 Regional Career and Technical Education Agreement, December 2024, amended December 2025, pp. 1, 4 and 10; Mascenic School Board minutes, December 15, 2025, item 5a, and December 16, 2024, item b
   - source: https://drive.google.com/file/d/1tCZrkI-kHKerRu16C969Y3Y_2IQf81Ah/view
   - as_of: 2026-11-22
   - review_by: 2027-11-22
   - place: district Mascenic Regional
   - note: Text: "Contoocook Valley and Mascenic Regional School Districts are both Receiving and Sending Districts, and Jaffrey-Rindge School District is only a Sending District."; "This RCTEA is effective from the last dated signature through June 30th, 2029"; "The Contoocook Valley School District, at its own expense, shall employ a full-time Director of CTE". December 15, 2025 minutes (https://drive.google.com/file/d/1i6GzZzabfjrYnhroAMijC9SIqezLbYRy/view): "Dr. Pogorzelski reviewed the revisions to the agreement ... it has to be approved by January 1st. 2. The Region 14 agreement says students are placed in CTE programs through an application platform using a rubric and an interview, and that generally 75% of a program's seats go to the host district's students and 25% to the sending districts', reviewed each year against high school enrollments and shifted when seats are open.
   - citation: Region 14 Regional Career and Technical Education Agreement (amended December 2025), section IV.4
   - source: https://drive.google.com/file/d/1tCZrkI-kHKerRu16C969Y3Y_2IQf81Ah/view
   - as_of: 2026-11-22
   - review_by: 2027-11-22
   - place: district Mascenic Regional
   - note: Text: "Region 14 will use an application platform to place students based on a rubric aligned with student success metrics and an interview."; "the formula for participation ... will be 75% of the available spaces designated for the receiving district and 25% of the available spaces for the sending districts, to be reviewed based on the total high school enrollments annually. This may be shifted based on the availability of seats in any program." So a Mascenic student applying to a ConVal program competes for the sending districts' share, and a ConVal or Conant student applying to Mascenic's programs does the same. Raw copy: raw/high/other/region14-rctea-amended-2025-12.txt
3. Under the Region 14 agreement's "CTE access program," a student from another Region 14 district who takes a CTE class at Mascenic or ConVal may also take regular classes there; the student's home district pays tuition of at least 80% of its own average cost per pupil (prorated by credits for students taking fewer than three regular classes) and provides the transportation.
   - citation: Region 14 Regional Career and Technical Education Agreement (amended December 2025), section V
   - source: https://drive.google.com/file/d/1tCZrkI-kHKerRu16C969Y3Y_2IQf81Ah/view
   - as_of: 2026-11-22
   - review_by: 2027-11-22
   - place: district Mascenic Regional
   - note: Text: "Each of the Receiving Districts shall allow students from Sending Districts to enroll either part-time or full-time in non-CTE courses ... the Sending District student must be enrolled in and attending one or more CTE classes at the receiving school."; "(80% of ACPP/5) x # of credits"; "not less than 80% of the sending district's average cost per pupil"; "The Sending Districts of part-time and full-time program students shall be responsible for providing transportation to and from the Receiving District." When a home district's high school doesn't offer the CTE course, it pays at least 25% of the CTE tuition and the state up to 75% (section VIII.4). Raw copy: raw/high/other/region14-rctea-amended-2025-12.txt

   - citation: Region 14 Regional Career and Technical Education Agreement (amended December 2025), sections VI and IX.3; Mascenic School Board minutes, February 16, 2026, item 5b

## military-recruiter-access-rules: Military Recruiters' Access to High School Students

- kind: rule
- question_type: rules
- description: Federal rules on military recruiters' access to high school students and their contact information, and how parents opt out

1. Federal law requires every school district that receives funds under the Elementary and Secondary Education Act to give military recruiters, on request, the names, addresses and phone numbers of its high school students, and to give recruiters the same access to students that it gives colleges or prospective employers.
   - citation: 20 U.S.C. 7908(a)(1) and (a)(3) (ESEA section 8528)
   - source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/20/7908
   - as_of: 2026-11-22
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text: "each local educational agency receiving assistance under this chapter shall provide, upon a request made by a military recruiter or an institution of higher education, access to the name, address, and telephone listing of each secondary school student served by the local educational agency, unless the parent of such student has submitted the prior consent request under paragraph (2)."; "(3) Same access to students Each local educational agency receiving assistance under this chapter shall provide military recruiters the same access to secondary school students as is provided to institutions of higher education or to prospective employers of those students." The exception is a private school with a verifiable religious objection to military service (subsection (c)). Raw copy: raw/high/law/20-usc-7908-cornell.txt (.html)
2. Under federal law a parent of a high school student may ask the district in writing not to release the student's name, address and phone number to military recruiters without the parent's written consent, the district must tell parents of this option, it may not use an "opt-in" process instead, and once a student turns 18 the choice is the student's.
   - citation: 20 U.S.C. 7908(a)(2), (a)(4) and (a)(5)
   - source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/20/7908
   - as_of: 2026-11-22
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text: "A parent of a secondary school student may submit a written request, to the local educational agency, that the student's name, address, and telephone listing not be released for purposes of paragraph (1) without prior written consent of the parent."; "Each local educational agency shall notify the parents of the students served by the agency of the option"; "Nothing in this subsection shall be construed to allow a local educational agency to withhold access ... by implementing an opt-in process"; "whenever a student has attained 18 years of age, the permission or consent required of and the rights accorded to the parents of the student shall only be required of and accorded to the student." Mascenic's handbook describes its general directory-information opt-out (district-student-support 5) but not this recruiter-specific notice. Raw copy: raw/high/law/20-usc-7908-cornell.txt
