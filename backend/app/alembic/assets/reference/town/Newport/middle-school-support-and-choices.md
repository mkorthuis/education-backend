# Newport Middle School, Student Support and School Choices

Part of the Newport research (town/Newport). Gathered November 22, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers Newport Middle School (NMS, grades 6–8, 245 North Main Street, in the same building as Newport High School) and the supports and choices around it for Newport families in SAU 43: special education staffing and the open paraeducator posts, who families contact about services, the SOAR program and the BASE alternative program, counseling and behavioral health support, bullying and behavior rules, the phone rule, homeschooled and EFA students in district programs, returning from homeschool, registration, finishing the year after moving out of town, volunteer background checks, advanced learners and grade 7–8 high school credit, middle school math benchmarks, and the private and charter schools near Newport (Newport Montessori School, Mount Royal Academy in Sunapee, Cornerstone Chartered Public School and Granite Hill School).

- **Main findings:**
  - **Paraeducators (P6.1):** confirmed. 6 special education para posts at NMS (including a self-contained classroom post requiring an LNA license) and an instructional math para were open on November 22, 2026, among 17 para openings district-wide (**derived**); the director told the board in September that para positions had been posted as special education grew to 207 students. NMS had 53 students with IEPs (33%) of 160 in October 2026, and case managers carry about 15 students each at the middle school.
  - **One building, one principal:** NMS and the high school share 245 North Main Street, and for 2026-27 the middle school principal, music and office posts were cut, with one "High School & Middle School Principal" and a middle school assistant principal. The two schools' lunches still don't overlap in 2026-27 (**derived**), but their students share the cafeteria and gym before the first bells.
  - **Support for students who struggle:** the board voted 5–0 on April 23, 2026 to close SOAR Middle School and expand the voluntary BASE program for grades 6–12 inside the building, and approved a grant-funded Behavioral Health & Wellness Coordinator. The schools also use Cartwheel telehealth (virtual therapy, free with Medicaid), Effective School Solutions at the middle school, DBT with Dartmouth Health, the Student Assistance Program and the Student Support Team, which anyone may refer a student to.
  - **Phones (P14.3):** policy JICJ (July 24, 2025) bans use from first bell to dismissal, including lunch; middle schoolers keep phones off in their lockers.
  - **Bullying (P14.2):** the board adopted a revised JICK on August 13, 2026 with a 20-school-day investigation limit, and an HB 131 revision (48-hour parent notice, written rights for the alleged victim, appeals through policy ACA) was at first reading on November 19, 2026. A January 2023 district survey found 17% of responding middle schoolers rarely felt safe and 34% said adults ignore bullying.
  - **Homeschool access (P11.1, P11.2):** policy JJJ (re-approved November 19, 2026) and the 2026-27 handbook give home-educated and EFA students equal access on the same terms and fees. Re-enrolling students are placed after an evaluation with the principal. Middle school band now depends on one shared high and middle school music teacher.
  - **Advanced learners (P18.2):** policy IK (August 13, 2026) lets 7th and 8th graders take advanced courses for high school credit, but no middle school Algebra I course or gifted program was found, and no gifted policy is posted.
  - **Moving out mid-year (P16.3):** policy JFAB (approved September 25, 2025) lets students who move out within 90 school days of year's end finish it tuition free with the superintendent's approval, without district busing.
  - **Volunteers (P17.2):** only designated volunteers need a fingerprint check, with no yearly recheck in GBCD or IJOC; the 2026-27 handbook now allows parents to chaperone some field trips after the check (the 2024-25 NMS handbook didn't).
  - **Choices:** Newport Montessori (preschool–grade 8) publishes no tuition and asks families to use EFA money first; Mount Royal is about 7.6 miles away (**derived**); Cornerstone Chartered Public School (K–3) had eight Newport students in September 2026, and Newport buses them. Newport voters approved, 363–206, an open enrollment article that lets no Newport student attend another open enrollment school.
