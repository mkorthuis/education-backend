# Barrington: Money, Tuition Contracts and Governance

Part of the Barrington research (town/Barrington). Gathered November 21, 2026.

## Summary

Covers the Barrington School District's (SAU 74) budget and what drives it, its three high school tuition arrangements (Dover and Oyster River contracts, the Coe-Brown memorandum of understanding), tuition rates and spending, the teachers' and paraprofessionals' contracts, school taxes and state aid as the district explains them, a derived tax comparison with Durham and Lee, enrollment and the effect of new housing, capital reserves and building projects, the 2026 and 2027 budget votes, and proof of residency.

- **Counts:** 8 topics, 60 facts, 5 number tables. Five facts cite draft 2027-28 budget documents and say so.
- **Sources:** SAU 74's own documents, nearly all scanned PDFs on Google Drive (contracts, board packets and minutes, deliberative session slides and minutes, budget drafts, the NESDEC enrollment report, policies JFA, JFAA and JFABA-R6, the residency affidavit). They were read by rendering the pages to images and running on-device OCR, so notes say "transcribed from image". The site's database was queried read-only for a derived tax comparison.
- **Main gaps:** The town website (barrington.nh.gov) and NH DRA refused automated requests (HTTP 403), so the town report, the official March 2026 ballot results for each article and the tax year 2026 rate weren't retrieved. No source measures teacher turnover before and after the 2025 contract.

## district-attendance-zones-and-transfers: Proof of Residency in Barrington

- kind: local
- question_type: local_practice
- description: How a district assigns students to schools by home address, its feeder pattern from elementary to middle to high school, redistricting history and plans, proof of residency, and requests to attend a different school (added from the Manchester research)

1. Barrington's policy JFA requires parents to verify residency when a student enrolls, before the end of 4th grade, before the end of 8th grade and every year of high school, and lets the district verify residence at other times from preschool through grade 12.
   - citation: Barrington School District Policy JFA, Residency (reviewed December 5, 2023)
   - source: https://drive.google.com/file/d/1rQQlglYNrcBRHI-gorypOl6cjN6W623s/view

The district also reserves the right to periodically verify residence of students in grades preschool to Grade 12". Legal residence follows RSA 193:12 (library and Chatham `money-and-governance.md`, school-residency-rules 1–3). Raw: raw/money/policy-jfa-residency.pdf and .txt
2. Barrington's residency affidavit asks for one housing document, such as mortgage papers, a certificate of occupancy for a new home, a signed and dated lease, a notarized letter from the landlord, or a notarized letter from the property owner explaining the living arrangement, plus one document showing the address, such as a gas, electric, oil or water bill, a cable or credit card bill, a public aid card, or a homeowners, renters or auto insurance policy.
   - citation: Barrington School District, SAU 74, Residency Affidavit (posted as "2027 Residency Affadavit")
   - source: https://drive.google.com/file/d/15rOobnHLl6he175ZOFbmpTEIOskrhJd1/view
   - as_of: 2026-11-21
   - review_by: 2027-11-21
   - place: district Barrington
   - note: Transcribed from image: "Category I – (one document required) Homeowners/Renters: Homeowners: Mortgage papers; Certificate of Occupancy on a newly built home. Renter: Signed and dated lease. Other: Notarized letter from landlord; Notarized letter with explanation of living arrangements from the resident owner of property containing contact information for verification by the SAU. Category II – (one documents showing proper address are required): Most recent cable television and/or credit card bill; Current public aid card; Current homeowners/renters insurance policy; Auto insurance policy; Most recent gas, electric, oil and/or water bill." So a family whose utilities are in the landlord's name can use, for example, a lease or landlord letter with a renters or auto insurance policy or a credit card bill.
