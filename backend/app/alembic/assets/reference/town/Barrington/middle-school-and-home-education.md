# Barrington Middle School and Home Education

Part of the Barrington research (town/Barrington). Gathered November 21, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers Barrington Middle School (BMS, grades 5–8, 51 Haley Drive) and home education in the Barrington School District (SAU 74): hands-on Tech Ed and computer classes, the move from Barrington Elementary to BMS in grade 5, IEPs, algebra and the lack of a gifted program, high school credit in grade 8, Spanish, the phone and bullying rules, grades and work habits, sports eligibility and costs, field trips and band, and what home-educated and EFA students may join and what paperwork SAU 74 asks for.

- **12 topics, 83 facts, no tables.** Eleven topics are shared district topics (`district-enrichment-and-advanced-learners`, `district-curriculum-and-assessment`, `district-technology-and-phones`, `district-conduct-policies`, `district-classrooms-and-staff`, `district-transportation`, `district-special-education-services`, `district-pe-outdoor-and-athletics`, `district-fees`, `district-homeschool-access`, `high-school-tuition-options`), all `place: district Barrington`. One is the shared general topic `school-sports-rules` (no place), with one new NHIAA fact. No new topic is proposed. Raw copies are in `raw/middle/` (about 24 MB). Board packets are scanned; only the cited pages were kept, machine-read (OCR) and checked against the page images. Several notes say "draft" because the October 28 and November 4, 2026 minutes are marked draft in the packets; no fact is pending.
- **Main findings:**
  - **Tech Ed is hands-on and required in grades 7 and 8.** Grade 7 builds and races CAD-designed CO2 cars; grade 8 builds a working hydraulic or pneumatic arm, models a framed building and makes an independent woodworking project. Every grade 3–8 student has a school Chromebook. In 2026-27 BMS added a full-time Computer Science teacher.
  - **BMS has an Algebra class but no gifted program.** The grade 8 team lists an "Algebra" syllabus beside "8th Grade Math", and BMS reported one Algebra I class (19 students in grades 7 and 8, all passing) to the federal Civil Rights Data Collection for 2023-24. All three Barrington schools reported no gifted and talented program in 2021-22 and 2023-24. Policy IMBD allows high school credit for grade 7–8 courses only if the high school principal approves first.
  - **Phones are banned bell to bell,** lunch and passing time included, under policy JICJ (August 2025). A 2011 policy encouraging personal devices "for academic purposes" is still posted.
  - **Home education paperwork:** SAU 74's own policy IHBG (March 2026) already said no annual notice is needed, but it still describes the yearly evaluation that state law repealed on July 1, 2026. Policy JJJ (May 2025) opens courses, sports and band to home-educated, charter and private school residents by written request; it does not yet name EFA students, whom state law added on August 18, 2026.
  - **Sports eligibility:** BMS athletes need a 2 or better in every class; under NHIAA rules, students entering grade 9 for the first time don't have to meet the grade rule in their first fall marking period, so BMS grades don't decide high school eligibility.
- **Main gaps:** no posted fee amounts for sports, band rental or field trips; no BMS course catalog (who qualifies for Algebra, whether any science is accelerated); the Algebra and grade 8 math syllabi need a Google sign-in; no written procedure for moving IEPs from BES to BMS; no source says whether Dover, Oyster River or Coe-Brown grant credit for BMS Algebra or Spanish; behavior figures read from a chart, not a table.

## high-school-tuition-options: Choosing a High School After Barrington Middle School or Home Education

- kind: local
- question_type: local_practice
- description: For districts that do not run a high school: which high schools their students attend or may choose, and the arrangement the district describes.

   - citation: NHIAA Handbook 2026-27, By-Law Article II (Eligibility), Preface
   - source: https://assets-rst7.rschooltoday.com/rst7files/uploads/sites/586/2026/09/21142244/4HB-26-27-II-Eligibility.pdf

   - note: Text: "Students entering grade nine in the fall for the first time do not have to meet the Scholastic Standing requirements of By-Law Article II, Sect. 2. All other standards for eligibility must be met"; "The sole responsibility for determining athletic eligibility rests with the principal of the high school."
