# Bow Elementary School, the Preschool and Young Families

Part of the Bow research (town/Bow). Gathered November 21, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers Bow Elementary School (BES, kindergarten to grade 4) and the Bow School District's preschool, and the town programs young families use: the kindergarten day, the Bow Rec Center's before- and after-school program (enrollment, waitlist, fees, snow days and delays, pick-up rules, children with IEPs), the town's Celebrating Children Preschool, the morning and afternoon bus rides, special education staffing and aides, how parents see IEP progress and service minutes, pick-up paperwork at BES, school meal prices and accounts, the preschool's move back to BES and the move from preschool to kindergarten, early reading instruction, and a cross-state comparison for families coming from Massachusetts.

- **14 topics, 56 facts and 3 number tables.** Raw copies are in `raw/elementary/` (about 21 MB): the Rec Center's program page, 2026-27 fee schedule and policy booklet, the Parks & Recreation FAQ, the Celebrating Children page and 2026-27 letter; extracts of the Town of Bow 2025 annual report (Parks & Recreation, the School Board chair and BES reports); the 2026-27 BES Family-Student Handbook; SAU 67 pages (special education, food services, MySchoolBucks, meal charging, cancellation procedures, transportation, the Bow routes page), BES's Parking & Traffic page, the BHS home page, the 2026-27 calendar and the SAU 67 job board; the 13 Bow bus route sheets (August 2026); ten sets of Bow School Board minutes (October 2025 to November 2026) and page extracts from thirteen board packets; the Educational Opportunity Project's 2025 reports for Bow, New Hampshire and Massachusetts; RSA 170-E:3, 28 CFR 35.104 and 35.130, and 34 CFR 300.320 and 300.323; a site-database export of Bow's licensed child care programs and a derived bus ride table.
- **Topics:** shared slugs `district-classrooms-and-staff`, `district-special-education-services`, `district-preschool`, `district-curriculum-and-assessment`, `district-transportation`, `district-school-day-and-calendar`, `district-meals`, `district-school-results-compared`, `town-recreation-and-summer`, `town-child-care`; the library topics `kindergarten-day-length`, `kindergarten-entry-cutoff` and `special-ed-iep-basics` (same kind, question type and description as the library's); and **one proposed new shared topic**, `after-school-program-rules` (rule, rules): "Which before- and after-school programs New Hampshire licenses as child care and which are exempt, and what federal disability law requires of programs run by towns and other public bodies."
- **Places:** `district Bow` for BES, the preschool and Bow's buses; `sau 67` for SAU-wide pages and reports (special education referral and services, meal prices and accounts, storm announcements, the superintendent's and student services director's staffing reports); `town Bow` for the Bow Rec Center and Celebrating Children (Town of Bow Parks & Recreation); no place for the state licensing law and the federal rules.
- **Main gaps:** no source says how long the Rec Center's waitlist is or how many children are on it; no page uses the words "full-day kindergarten" (the hours show a full school day); no named phonics or foundational reading program for kindergarten to grade 2 was found, and no district report gives results for struggling readers beyond the state test data the site already has; the number of open paraprofessional positions at BES isn't posted; no source says whether missed IEP minutes have occurred or how they are made up; the preschool's peer-model tuition amount and preschool busing rules aren't posted; Bow School Board policies on Simbli refused automated requests.

## district-special-education-services: Special Education Services in the District

- kind: local
- question_type: local_practice
- description: Who provides special education and related services in a district (on staff or contracted), reading specialists, 504 practice, IEP moves between schools, and out-of-district placements

## after-school-program-rules: Child Care Licensing Exemptions and Disability Access in After-School Programs

- kind: rule
- question_type: rules
- description: Which before- and after-school programs New Hampshire licenses as child care and which are exempt, and what federal disability law requires of programs run by towns and other public bodies.

1. New Hampshire's child care licensing law exempts municipal recreation programs, including after-school and summer recreation programs, as well as kindergartens and other daytime programs run by a school system, and it requires an exempt municipal or recreational program to tell a new child's parent or guardian that the program is not licensed and operates as a legally license-exempt program.
   - citation: RSA 170-E:3, I(a), I(f) and III
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XII/170-E/170-E-3.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-21
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Statute text: "I. The definitions in RSA 170-E:2, IV shall not apply to the following: (a) Kindergartens, nursery schools, or any other daytime programs operated by a public or private elementary or secondary school system ... (f) Municipal recreation programs, including after-school and summer recreation programs."; "III. Whenever a child day care that is license exempt under subparagraphs I(c), (e), (f), or (g) accepts a new child into the program, the provider shall inform the child's parent or legal guardian that the program is not licensed and is operating as a legally license exempt program." Exempt programs remain subject to RSA 170-E:4, II (paragraph II). Raw: raw/elementary/law/rsa-170-E-3.txt (.html)

2. Under the federal Americans with Disabilities Act rules for state and local governments, a public entity, which includes any town and its departments, may not exclude a qualified person with a disability from its programs and must make reasonable changes to its policies, practices or procedures when needed to avoid disability discrimination, unless it can show the change would fundamentally alter the program.
   - citation: 28 CFR 35.104 (definition of "public entity"); 28 CFR 35.130(a) and (b)(7)(i)
   - source: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-28/section-35.130
   - as_of: 2026-11-21
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Rule text: "(a) No qualified individual with a disability shall, on the basis of disability, be excluded from participation in or be denied the benefits of the services, programs, or activities of a public entity"; "(7)(i) A public entity shall make reasonable modifications in policies, practices, or procedures when the modifications are necessary to avoid discrimination on the basis of disability, unless the public entity can demonstrate that making the modifications would fundamentally alter the nature of the service, program, or activity." 35.104: "Public entity means— (1) Any State or local government; (2) Any department, agency, special purpose district, or other instrumentality of a State or States or local government". A child's IEP is the school district's plan and doesn't bind a town program; the rules don't say what counts as reasonable for any particular program. ## special-ed-iep-basics: Special Education and IEPs: What They Are and Who Qualifies

- kind: rule
- question_type: rules
- description: What an IEP and special education are, which children qualify in New Hampshire, and that services are free.
