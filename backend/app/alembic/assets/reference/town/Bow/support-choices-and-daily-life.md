# Student Support, School Choices and Daily Life in Bow

Part of the Bow research (town/Bow). Gathered November 21, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers phones at Bow Memorial School (BMS), whether Bow's middle and high school teams cut players, how home-educated and EFA students use Bow's schools, school hours at BMS and Bow Elementary (BES), buses after school and to private schools, bullying rules and follow-up with parents, Bishop Brady High School as an alternative to Bow High (BHS), the Bow Rec Center on early-release and teacher workshop days, how families hear about weather delays and buses, and the school calendar.

- **11 topics, 60 facts, no tables.** All 11 are shared slugs from `shared-topics.md`: `district-technology-and-phones`, `district-pe-outdoor-and-athletics`, `district-homeschool-access`, `district-school-day-and-calendar`, `district-transportation`, `district-family-communication`, `district-conduct-policies`, `district-student-support`, `school-programs-after-grade-6`, `town-school-options-nearby`, `town-recreation-and-summer`.
- **Places used:** `district Bow` (the Bow School District, its schools and board), `sau 67` (the shared SAU 67 calendar and the SAU-wide ParentSquare system), `town Bow` (the Bow Recreation Center, and Bishop Brady High School as a school option near Bow). No general rules were written; state rules are cited from the library by slug.
- **Main findings:**
  - **Phones:** BMS has run a "bell to bell" ban since August 2025. Phones and smartwatches must be silenced and stored all school day, including lunch, with "Phone Homes" in every homeroom. The board adopted policy JICJ on October 2, 2025. About 20 days into 2025-26 BMS reported 4 violations, all first offenses.
  - **No-cut teams:** not found. BMS has a written cut policy (8th graders preferred over equally skilled 7th graders) and BHS coaches may hold tryouts and cut players; BHS seniors may not play on sub-varsity teams.
  - **Late buses:** none. The 2026-27 route sheets show 53 runs on 12 buses, all regular morning and afternoon runs; no late or activity run. Most BMS clubs meet before school (7:55–8:30 a.m.), and BMS students may ride the earlier elementary bus with a teacher's pass.
  - **Calendars:** one SAU 67 calendar covers BES, BMS, BHS and Dunbarton. The SAU 67 Board adopted it 9-0 on December 8, 2025, built to mirror Concord's calendar for career-center classes. It has two early-release days in 2026-27 (November 23 and June 16), the same at every school. Daily hours differ: BES 8:00 a.m.–2:15 p.m., BMS 8:30 a.m.–3:10 p.m.
  - **Rec Center:** teacher workshop days, vacations and storm days need a separate sign-up through an emailed link, at an extra fee. On a storm early dismissal, staff collect regularly enrolled children from the schools and families pay for the extra hours.
  - **Homeschool and EFA:** in March 2026 the superintendent said Bow must let home-educated students take part but was not required to admit EFA students (HB 1817 changed that from August 18, 2026), and that Bow's practice is to charge a participation fee. SAU 67 itself is listed in the EFA marketplace.
  - **Bishop Brady:** 2026-27 tuition is $15,950; it is in the EFA marketplace and served 100 EFA students in 2025 (CSF draft list). Bow buses make no stops at Concord private schools; Brady charges $875 a year for its own morning bus.
- **Main gaps:** Bow's policy manual (Simbli) blocked automated access, so the texts of JICJ (phones), Bow's homeschool and nonpublic access policy, its transportation policies and the final JICK were not read from the manual (JICK's draft text was read in the October 2026 board packet). Also not found: the size of the homeschool or EFA participation fee, Bishop Brady's average class size and bus routes, and any notice system for a single late bus.
- **Proposed shared topic:** none.
