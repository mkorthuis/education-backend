# Money and Governance: The Deficit, the Bank Loan, Bluff, the Funding Cases, SAU 6 and Taxes

Part of the Claremont research (town/Claremont). Gathered 2026-11-21.

## Summary

This file covers the Claremont School District's 2025 financial crisis and recovery, the $4 million bank loan, the state loan bills, the FY2027 budget and vote, school taxes, the Bluff and Masonic Temple properties, Unity's and Cornish's exits from SAU 6, tuition agreements, audits and accountability, class sizes and staffing after the cuts, the money side of the tech center, and what the Claremont school funding decisions held. Main sources: Claremont School Board and finance subcommittee minutes (June 2025 to November 2026, from SAU 6's public Drive folders), superintendent reports and board packets (fall 2026), SAU 6 news posts, the 2025 and 2026 warrants, the FY2022 draft audit report and corrective action plan, the September 2026 recovery plan draft, the Region 10 CTE agreement, HB 292 and HB 1399 bill records, the State's Rand brief (library raw copy), City of Claremont pages and November 2026 results, Census 2010 and 2020 counts, and NH Housing's 2024 rent survey.

Counts: 13 topics, 104 facts, 2 number tables.

Main gaps: the tax year 2026 tax rate (DRA pages blocked; not found elsewhere); audited figures for FY2023 to FY2026, so the final size of the deficit is still unverified; official March 10, 2026 school ballot results (news is the only source found); the October 1, 2025 board minutes and slides; no source tracks why families move.

## money-rand-appeal-2026: The Rand School Funding Appeal at the NH Supreme Court (2026)

- kind: how_it_works
- question_type: governance_money
- description: The State's appeal of the Rand school funding ruling (No. 2026-0150): the request to overturn Claremont, the recusal motions, status and legal costs.

1. In Claremont School District v. Governor (1993), "Claremont I," the New Hampshire Supreme Court held that Part II, Article 83 of the State Constitution "imposes a duty on the State to provide a constitutionally adequate education to every educable child in the public schools of New Hampshire and to guarantee adequate funding," and declared a free public education "at the very least an important, substantive right."
   - citation: Claremont School District v. Governor, 138 N.H. 183, 184, 192 (1993), as quoted in the Brief for the State of New Hampshire, Case No. 2026-0150, pp. 43–44
   - source: https://www.courts.nh.gov/sites/g/files/ehbemt471/files/documents/2026-08/8.14.26-state-s-brief.pdf
   - as_of: 2026-11-21
   - review_by: 2027-11-21
   - note: The decision itself was not read; these are the brief's quotations of it. Raw (library): additional-data/raw/state-issues/money-rand-appeal-2026/8.14.26-state-s-brief.txt, lines 1209–1235
2. In Claremont School District v. Governor (1997), "Claremont II," the court struck down the school funding system under Part II, Article 5, holding that the property tax levied to fund an adequate education is a state tax and that, used for that purpose, it "must be administered in a manner that is equal in valuation and uniform in rate throughout the State"; it also called a state-funded adequate education a fundamental right.
   - citation: Claremont School District v. Governor, 142 N.H. 462, 466, 471, 473 (1997), as quoted in the Brief for the State of New Hampshire, Case No. 2026-0150, pp. 45–46
   - source: https://www.courts.nh.gov/sites/g/files/ehbemt471/files/documents/2026-08/8.14.26-state-s-brief.pdf
   - as_of: 2026-11-21
   - review_by: 2027-11-21
   - note: The decision itself was not read. Brief text: "Claremont II held that 'the property tax levied to fund education is, by virtue of the State's duty to provide a constitutionally adequate public education,' a state tax" and "'To the extent that the property tax is used in the future to fund the provision of an adequate education, the tax must be administered in a manner that is equal in valuation and uniform in rate throughout the State.'" The statewide education property tax followed from this ruling (library money-statewide-education-property-tax). Raw (library): 8.14.26-state-s-brief.txt, lines 1244–1278
3. The State's August 2026 brief in the Rand appeal gives its reasons for asking the court to overrule Claremont I and II: it argues Claremont I "finds no basis in the text" of the Constitution, Claremont II is "fundamentally incompatible with the text and history" of it, the decisions fail the test for keeping precedent, and they have led to "the impractical and unworkable state of affairs that exists today in our education funding law"; it says the State, including towns and school districts, shares the duty to educate.
   - citation: Brief for the State of New Hampshire, Case No. 2026-0150 (filed August 14, 2026), Table of Contents (Argument I.A–D) and Preliminary Statement, pp. 12–13
