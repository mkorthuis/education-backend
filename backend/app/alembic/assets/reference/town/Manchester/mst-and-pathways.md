# MST, Career Pathways and Adult Education

Part of the Manchester research (town/Manchester). Gathered November 15, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers the Manchester School of Technology (MST): its full-time high school and its regional career and technical education (CTE) center, admission, schedule, grading, credentials and college credit, sports, private and homeschool students, dual enrollment for the other high schools, work-based learning and employer partnerships, and adult diploma, HiSET and evening trade classes in the city.

## adult-diploma-and-equivalency-rules: Adult High School Diplomas and HiSET Testing in New Hampshire

- kind: rule
- question_type: rules
- description: New Hampshire rules for adult high school diploma programs and high school equivalency (HiSET) testing: ages, required credits, sources of credit, moving from a day high school, and testing before 18

1. Under New Hampshire's rules, a student must be at least 16 to enroll in an adult high school program, which a local school department runs as a regular adult evening program or an adult high school approved by the Department of Education, leading to a high school diploma or an adult high school diploma.
   - citation: Ed 702.01, Ed 702.04
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rules/state_agencies/ed700.html
   - as_of: 2026-11-15
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Rule text: "A secondary school educational program shall be offered in regular adult evening programs or adult high schools conducted under the auspices of a local school department and approved by the department leading to a high school diploma or an adult high school diploma."; "Ed 702.04 Eligibility. The student shall be at least 16 years of age to enroll in an adult high school program." Raw copy: raw/pathways/law/ed700.txt
2. Under New Hampshire's rules, an adult high school diploma takes at least 20 credits, including 3 in social science (U.S. history 1, economics ½, civics ½), 4 in English, 2 in math, 2 in science and ½ in computer literacy, and each 1-credit class meets at least 45 hours.
   - citation: Ed 702.05, Table 702-1
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rules/state_agencies/ed700.html
   - as_of: 2026-11-15
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Rule text: "Each student shall successfully complete a minimum program of 20 units for graduation including 11 ½ units as outlined in Table 702-1: ... Social Science Including U.S. History 1 unit Economics ½ unit Civics ½ unit 3 units English 4 units Mathematics 2 units Science 2 units Computer Literacy ½ unit"; "each one credit subject offered in this program shall meet for not less than 45 hours." The state's regular day-school minimums are in library graduation-requirements 3–4. Raw copy: raw/pathways/law/ed700.txt
3. Under New Hampshire's rules, adult high school credit can come from earlier high school or college work, adult high school classes, apprenticeships the program accepts, up to 2 credits of practicum in business or industry, up to 2 credits of community service, and an unlimited number of credits from accredited institutions, licensed trade schools, colleges, online courses and extended learning opportunities.
   - citation: Ed 702.06(a)
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rules/state_agencies/ed700.html
   - as_of: 2026-11-15
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Rule text: "(1) Previous high school work completed for which credit was granted by an approved high school and/or accredited post-secondary institution; (2) Course work successfully completed in the adult high school program; and (3) Work experiences and non-high-school educational experience ... a. Credits for apprenticeship programs that meet the requirements of local adult high schools shall be given upon review ...; b. A maximum of 2 credits may be earned by observation/practicum experiences in business, industry and/or other institutions ...; g. Unlimited numbers of credits may be earned from accredited institutions, licensed trade schools, business schools, colleges, extension courses, distance learning, web-based courses, correspondence courses and extended learning opportunities (ELO) ...; h. A maximum of 2 credits may be earned by a student's supervised direct involvement in a voluntary community service activity". The rule also allows credit for work in the home and homemaking skills. Raw copy: raw/pathways/law/ed700.txt
4. Under New Hampshire's rules, a student in a regular high school may transfer to an adult high school program with the principal's written recommendation (and a parent's written consent if under 18), and some students still in a day high school may earn credits through adult high school programs.
   - citation: Ed 702.03
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rules/state_agencies/ed700.html
   - as_of: 2026-11-15
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Rule text: "(a) A student presently enrolled in a regular high school program may transfer to the adult high school program with written recommendation of the student's principal, as well as written consent of the student's parent or guardian if the student has not attained the age of majority. (b) Not withstanding (a) above certain in-school youth may earn credits via the adult high school programs." Separately, a district must educate a student at its expense until a diploma or age 21 (library special-ed-transition-and-age-limit 6), so turning 18 does not end the right to attend regular high school. Raw copy: raw/pathways/law/ed700.txt
5. Under New Hampshire's rules, an adult high school student must complete at least one adult high school class before receiving a diploma, which comes from the school system where the requirements were finished.
   - citation: Ed 702.08
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rules/state_agencies/ed700.html
   - as_of: 2026-11-15
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Rule text: "(a) A student shall successfully complete at least one adult high school class before the student is eligible to receive a high school diploma or an adult high school diploma. (b) The adult high school graduate shall receive his or her high school diploma or adult high school diploma from the school system where the student completed the requirements". Raw copy: raw/pathways/law/ed700.txt
6. Under New Hampshire's rules, a person must be 18 to take the high school equivalency test, or 16 or 17 with permission from the superintendent of the home school district after passing the certified practice test; home-schooled 16- and 17-year-olds may register through a participating agent's attestation instead.
   - citation: Ed 704.02
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rules/state_agencies/ed700.html

(1) Be at least 16 years of age; (2) Demonstrate that he or she has passed the certified practice test ...; (3) Meets any additional qualifications for testing set by the test publisher."; "(f) Home schooled students age 16 or 17 may register ... if a participating agent ... attests that the student has completed the home schooling program".
