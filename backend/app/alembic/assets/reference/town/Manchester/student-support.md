# Student Support in Manchester: Special Education, English Learners, Counseling and Family Communication

Part of the Manchester research (town/Manchester). Gathered November 15, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers support for Manchester School District students: how special education evaluations and IEPs work in the district, special education teachers, paraprofessionals and psychologists by school, what the FY2026 and FY2027 budgets show about those positions, English learner identification and services, the newcomer programs for students new to the country (including students with interrupted schooling), grade placement, tutoring, the papers a guardian needs at registration and to see grades, school counselors, social workers and community mental health staff by school, interpreters and translation, foster care contacts, and religious holidays and PE excuses.

- **6 topics, 76 facts and 3 number tables.** All topics are existing shared slugs (`district-special-education-services`, `district-classrooms-and-staff`, `district-student-support`, `district-family-communication`, `district-school-day-and-calendar`, `district-pe-outdoor-and-athletics`), all with `place: district Manchester`. No new topic is proposed. State and federal rules are left to the library slugs named in the question table (`special-ed-evaluation-request`, `special-ed-moving-with-iep`, `special-ed-iep-meetings-and-consent`, `special-ed-who-is-parent`, `student-records-ferpa`, `english-learner-rights`, `interpreters-and-translation-for-parents`, `prayer-and-religion-in-school`, `school-staffing-and-class-size`) and Conway's `foster-care-school-stability` (in `town/Conway/daily-life.md`).
- **New data concepts:** counselor, social worker, psychologist and paraprofessional positions by school, their funding (general fund or special revenue funds) and vacancies, counted from the district's scanned FY2027 preliminary budget roster (February 11, 2026) by text recognition and checked against the page images; derived students per counselor; the district's English language development minutes and newcomer program rules by grade level; home language counts; the district's interpreter and translation turnaround times.
- **Main sources:** Manchester School District policies 503, 509, 510, 546, 553, 707, 714, 717, 761, 765 and 1033; the Student Services, Child Find, Multilingual Learner Education, Language Access, Parent Language Line, Welcome Center, registration, tutoring and behavioral health pages; the district's Guide to Providing Language Access; its elementary, middle and high school English language development program overviews; the M-Power Manchester site; the FY2027 budget presentation and preliminary budget data (February 11, 2026); the 2026-27 calendar; NH DOE's 2024-25 foster care contact list (saved by the Conway research); and the site's own enrollment data.
- **Main findings:**
  - Grade 5 is already middle school in Manchester: from 2024-25 every 5th grader was in one of the four middle schools (grades 5–8), so a district 5th grader is already in middle school, not about to move up to one.
  - The FY2027 roster lists 67 paraprofessional and behavior-support positions in the middle schools (8 vacant) and 16 vacant such positions district-wide; the FY2026 budget's cuts through attrition fell on administration, teachers, principals, support staff and athletics, not on the paraprofessional line, which rose to $8,410,326 in the FY2027 initial budget.
  - Every school has at least one counselor (4 at each middle school, 4 to 6 at the comprehensive high schools); elementary social workers are paid from special revenue funds, middle school social workers from the general fund. Five of the six psychologists are split between two schools, and three district psychologist positions were vacant.
  - The district's staff guide asks for 48 hours to arrange an interpreter and 5 business days to translate IEPs and evaluations over 5 pages, bars using children or relatives as interpreters, and parents can call a phone interpreter line themselves. The multilingual liaison team covers Arabic, French, Spanish, Portuguese, Lingala, Swahili and Kinyarwanda, but not Somali or Maay.
  - Newcomers are screened in English (WIDA), math and their first language; high schools run an Integrated Newcomer Academy, and Hillside and Parkside run a separate Designated Newcomer Academy for middle schoolers, including students with interrupted schooling.
- **Main gaps:** any figure on how quickly Manchester completes special education evaluations (NH DOE's Indicator 11 reports were blocked); how much time students with IEPs spend outside regular classes; the January 27, 2026 board presentation on special education and the 2025 paraprofessional presentations (news coverage blocked; board materials not found); the final adopted FY2027 budget's effect on paraprofessionals (see the money file); a current Manchester foster care contact (the state list is from 2024-25); any district guidance on students who fast.
