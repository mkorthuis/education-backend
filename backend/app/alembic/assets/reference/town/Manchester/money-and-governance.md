# Manchester: Money and Governance

Part of the Manchester research (town/Manchester). Gathered November 15, 2026.

## Summary

**What's covered:** 7 topics, 81 facts and 6 number tables on how Manchester's school budget is set and paid for.
- **The 2026-27 (FY2027) budget from start to finish.** The district's three February budgets (tax-cap $235.5 million, baseline $251.4 million, fully funded $265.3 million); what drove costs up; the aldermen's failed June 2 override vote and the June 9 override; the $238,492,749 the city appropriated; and how the Board of School Committee closed the $12.9 million gap. Sources: the superintendent's memos, the Board of Mayor and Aldermen's own minutes, and the final line-item budget.
- **The city charter tax cap** (Section 6.15): its formula, the 10-of-14 override, its 2011 adoption, the yearly cap rates and the budget calendar. Also how Question 2 and the 2026 SAU budget law do or don't reach a city with no annual meeting.
- **Where the money goes:** salaries and benefits (69.6% of the FY2027 all-funds plan); a **derived** DOE-25 split by function against the state; administrators' raises tied to the tax cap; the ESSER positions moved onto the budget; out-of-district special education tuition; trust fund balances.
- **Spending per pupil:** **derived** comparisons with Bedford, Goffstown and the state, Manchester's rank (4th lowest of 160), the median by district size, and roughly what closing the gap to the state average would cost.
- **Pay:** teachers' tuition reimbursement, endorsement and loan repayment terms (2022–24 contract); paraeducator ($18.04 and $18.57 an hour) and substitute bus driver ($23.00) rates; Manchester-area wages for retail, warehouse and bus jobs (BLS); staff retention.
- **Enrollment** since 2005-06 (NCES): 17,511 then, 11,980 in 2023-24, with teacher FTEs.
- **Buildings:** 20 buildings averaging 70 years old; Hallsville and Henry Wilson closed; the $306 million Priority One project; air-conditioned modular classrooms; the June 2021 heat closure.
- **Public meetings:** the Board of School Committee's makeup, meeting times and committees; Manchester Public TV Channel 22 and on-demand video; the city's agenda portal; budget hearings; the 2019–2020 school charter commission.
- **The state funding fight:** the ConVal case's timeline (filed March 2019, decided July 2025, no payment ordered). Manchester was a plaintiff. The district's own account of its state aid.

**Main sources:** the Manchester School District's FY2027 budget presentation (February 11, 2026), superintendent's memos of February 11 and June 16, 2026, the final line-item budget, the written answers for the March 25, 2026 joint BMA–BOSC meeting, and Committee on Joint School Buildings minutes. Also the City of Manchester's charter Section 6.15, BMA minutes of June 2 and 9, 2026 (CivicClerk), the BMA and Manchester Public TV pages, the 2020 School District Charter Commission report (with the charter as then in force), the 2022–2024 MEA contract (NCTQ copy), SchoolSpring job postings, the BLS OEWS API, NCES CCD (Urban Institute API), the ConVal opinion and the site's own database.

| P36.2 | Manchester spends "so much less per student than smaller NH towns" | Correct as a pattern. In FY2025 the median cost per pupil was $27,237 in districts under 500 pupils and $19,752 in districts of 2,500–4,999. Manchester's $18,020 ranked 4th lowest of 160 (**derived**). |
| P36.3 | "The new state law letting voters weigh in on administrator pay and tax caps" | Partly. HB 1300's Question 2 asked voters for a school tax cap and a cap on SAU central office spending (not pay as such). Manchester's 59.9% "yes" (unofficial) fell short of 60%, so neither cap applies. The 2026 SAU budget ballot vote applies only to districts with an annual meeting, which Manchester lacks. |

## research-schools-and-home-values: What Research Says About Schools and Home Prices

- kind: research
- question_type: research
- description: What studies find about how school spending, school buildings and school results relate to home prices

1. A study of California school bond elections that passed or failed by narrow margins found that passing a bond for school buildings raised home prices in the district by about 6% within a few years, an effect that lasted at least a decade, implying buyers valued each $1 of facility spending at $1.50 or more; test scores rose only slightly, and the authors concluded parents value things test scores don't capture, such as safety.
   - citation: Cellini, S. R., Ferreira, F., & Rothstein, J. (2008). The Value of School Facilities: Evidence from a Dynamic Regression Discontinuity Design. NBER Working Paper 14516 (published 2010 in the Quarterly Journal of Economics, 125(1))
   - source: https://www.nber.org/papers/w14516
   - as_of: 2026-11-15
   - review_by: 2027-11-15
   - note: Abstract: "passing a referendum causes immediate, sizable increases in home prices, implying a willingness-to-pay on the part of marginal homebuyers of $1.50 or more for each $1 of facility spending ... parents value improvements in other dimensions of school output (e.g., safety) that may be not captured by test scores." Introduction (p. 2): "passage of a bond measure causes housing prices in the district to rise by about six percent. This effect persists for at least a decade." Working paper version read; not peer reviewed in this version. It studies California building bonds, not cuts to operating budgets, so it does not predict what budget cuts would do to Manchester home values. ## For another file

- **Elementary and enrollment agents (P4, P22, P23 premises):** the district's January 2026 facilities slides place Hallsville's closing in 2021–2022 and Henry Wilson's in 2022–2024 and count 20 buildings, down from 22 (district-facilities-and-capital facts 1–2; raw/money/msd-fy27-board-budget-memo-revised.pdf pp. 43–44).
- **Main session (brief figures):** the site's school enrollment table has no rows for Hallsville or Henry Wilson in any year, so the district totals for years they were open undercount. The brief's "13,526 in 2015-16" excludes both schools. NCES counted 14,396 for fall 2015.
- **Secondary agent (P9.2, P16.1):** projected 2026 enrollment and capacity by high school (Central 1,213/1,990; Memorial 1,361/1,530; West 737/1,636). The district reported "No classes over 25" at Central and Memorial and "15 classes over 25" at West (memo pp. 44–46).
- **MST agent:** MST's projected enrollment is 239 CTE and 355 high school students. Capacity is given as 878 (p. 44) and 800 (p. 46). Under the Perkins seat allocation, 50.67% of MST's CTE seats are for Manchester students (2024 data, memo p. 49).
- **Support agent (P6.2, P6.3):** IEP counts (3,097 students, 23%; 157 in charters, 124 out of district) and out-of-district tuition overruns (district-budget-and-cost facts 26–27). Schools now complete "a formal reflection process before submitting paraprofessional requests" (memo p. 52). The fully funded budget's counselor request is fact 23.
- **Daily agent (P2.3, P10.2, P16.3, P27.1):** 164 routes, 53 vehicles, 46 drivers, about 3,000 riders. The 1.4-mile walking distance was kept for 2026-27. Options considered and rejected included 1.1, 1.5 and 2.0 miles and a fee to ride for high school; changing bell times "would require an agreement with the unions." FY2025 general fund transportation was $16,220,278 (memo pp. 5, 9; joint-meeting answers p. 4).
- **Results agent (P10.1, P29.1):** the district's March 2026 answers gave districtwide proficiency as ELA 28%, math 18% and science 14, with no elementary math coaches and four middle school math coaches (raw/money/msd-fy27-joint-meeting-bosc-bma-2026-03-25.txt p. 5).
- **Library, district-practices.md, budget-form-and-vote-dates fact 79:** its note's news report that FY2027 budgets were "finalized June 2026 after a tax cap override" is confirmed by the BMA's own minutes: override 11–3 on June 9, 2026; school appropriation $238,492,749.
- **Library, state-issues.md, budget-question-2-2026-results fact 3:** still unofficial. The City Clerk's Election Results page posts scanned ward return forms for November 3, 2026, which could be used to check Manchester's Question 2 totals.
