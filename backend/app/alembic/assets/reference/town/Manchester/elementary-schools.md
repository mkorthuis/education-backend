# Manchester Elementary Schools and the Early Years

Part of the Manchester research (town/Manchester). Gathered November 15, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers Manchester's elementary schools (kindergarten to grade 4) and the years before them: the Spanish and French dual-language immersion programs, gifted and enrichment programs, music and the arts, class sizes and kindergarten, recess, food allergies, homework and screens, sensory supports, reading help and curriculum training, the district's preschool, free programs for babies and toddlers, and the kindergarten day and calendar.

- **10 topics, 43 facts, no number tables.** Raw copies are in `raw/elementary/` (about 21 MB, 63 files). Seven topics use shared slugs from `town/shared-topics.md`, one adds a Manchester fact to the library's own `kindergarten-day-length` topic, and two are **proposed new shared topics** (no place):
  - `recess-activity-and-homework-rules` (kind rule, question type rules): "Recess, Daily Physical Activity and Homework: What NH Rules Require": the local policies New Hampshire's school approval rules require on daily physical activity, recess and homework, and how recess counts toward instructional time.
  - `school-allergy-rules` (kind rule, question type rules): "Food Allergies and Epinephrine at School": New Hampshire's rules for students with life-threatening allergies: district allergy management plans, staff training, school supplies of epinephrine and students carrying their own.
- **The main obstacle: the district's own website refused automated requests.** Every `*.mansd.org` page (the district site and all school sites) returned a "Client Challenge" bot check on November 15, 2026. So did Manchester Ink Link and its media server (HTTP 403, which also blocked the FY2027 budget PDF), education.nh.gov and nh.gov (HTTP 403). The facts here come from the district's own pages saved by the library research on November 8, 2026 (whose collapsed FAQ answers were recovered from the saved HTML), the district's newsletter on Smore, the 2022–2024 teachers' contract (posted by NCTQ), state law and rules, the site's database, NHPR, Union Leader articles syndicated on Yahoo, WMUR, WZID, and community organizations' own pages. News is cited only where it was the only source reached, and each such note says so.
- **Main gaps:** the dual-language application rules (lottery or not, transportation for families outside the zone) and the middle school that will host grades 5–6; any current gifted or pull-out program; the elementary fine arts page; the district's homework, device and allergy policies and its student code of conduct (recess); Jewett's kindergarten class count and aides; the reading programs and interventions by name; the 2026-27 calendar's early-release days; the current (2024–2027) teachers' contract text.

## school-allergy-rules: Food Allergies and Epinephrine at School

- kind: rule
- question_type: rules
- description: New Hampshire's rules for students with life-threatening allergies: district allergy management plans, staff training, school supplies of epinephrine and students carrying their own

1. Under New Hampshire law, each school district, under the direction of a school nurse, must carry out a plan for managing students with life-threatening allergies, based on NH Department of Education guidelines, and make the plan available to the public; the guidelines cover staff training, emergency response, individual health care and allergy action plans for every student with a known life-threatening allergy, and protocols to prevent exposure to allergens.
   - citation: RSA 200:44-a, II
   - source: https://www.gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XV/200/200-44-a.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-15
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text: "the development of individualized health care plans and allergy action plans for every student with a known life-threatening allergy; and protocols to prevent exposure to allergens. Not later than September 1, 2017, each school district, under the direction of a school nurse, shall implement a plan based on the guidelines ... and make such plan available to the public." Manchester's plan was not reached (its policy "Students 145.1, Special Physical Health Needs of Students" is on mansd.org, which refused automated requests). A food allergy can also qualify a student for a Section 504 plan (library section-504-plans). Raw: raw/elementary/rsa-200-44-a.txt.

2. New Hampshire law requires school staff designated by the school nurse, with the principal, to complete anaphylaxis training before giving epinephrine and at least every two years after, so that as many hours of the school day as possible are covered by at least one trained person.
   - citation: RSA 200:44-a, I
   - source: https://www.gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XV/200/200-44-a.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-15
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text: "Designated assistive personnel shall complete an anaphylaxis training program prior to providing or administering epinephrine at least every 2 years ... Designated assistive personnel shall be identified by the school nurse, in consultation with the principal, to ensure as many hours as possible of the school day are covered by at least one assistive person." Raw: raw/elementary/rsa-200-44-a.txt.

3. A New Hampshire school district that employs or contracts a nurse must adopt a policy and keep a supply of epinephrine for allergic emergencies, with the nurse deciding the amount and type; the nurse must order a replacement within 5 business days of a use, and schools report their supply and use to the Department of Education every year.
   - citation: RSA 200:44-b
   - source: https://www.gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XV/200/200-44-b.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-15
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text: "A school district shall adopt a policy to and maintain a supply of epinephrine for use in an allergic emergency when a nurse is employed or contracted by the district. The nurse shall determine the quantity and type of medication the school should maintain." and "the school nurse shall order a replacement within 5 business days." Source: 2024, 12:1; 2025, 204:2. Raw: raw/elementary/rsa-200-44-b.txt.

4. Under New Hampshire law, a student with a severe, potentially life-threatening allergy may carry and use their own epinephrine at school and school events with written approval from their health care provider and, for a minor, a parent, plus the provider's confirmation that the student can do so safely; after using it, the student must report to the nurse's or principal's office.
   - citation: RSA 200:42 and RSA 200:43
   - source: https://www.gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XV/200/200-42.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-15
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: RSA 200:42, I and III–IV: "The pupil has the written approval of the pupil's health care provider and, if the pupil is a minor, the written approval of the parent or guardian ... The pupil's parent or guardian shall submit written verification from the health care provider confirming that the pupil has the knowledge and skills to safely possess and use epinephrine in a school setting ... the pupil may possess and use the epinephrine at school or at any school-sponsored activity". RSA 200:43 (https://www.gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XV/200/200-43.htm): "Immediately after using epinephrine during the school day, the pupil shall report to the nurse's office or principal's office".
