# Plymouth Regional High School and Its Career and Technical Center

Part of the Plymouth research (town/Plymouth). Gathered November 21, 2026.

## Summary

Covers Plymouth Regional High School (PRHS, run by the Pemi-Baker Regional School District): its seven career and technical education (CTE) programs (Automotive Technology, Culinary Arts, Drafting & Design, Health Science, Marketing, Outdoor Recreation Leadership, TV & Media), their courses, credits, credentials and college credit; the temporary TV & Media teacher posted for spring 2027; graduation credits, the math rule that lets some CTE courses count, and the four graduation pathways; AP and honors courses, AP exam payment and grade weighting; Early College and Plymouth State University courses and what they cost; extended learning opportunities (ELOs), volunteer credit and how employers take part; counseling, college and financial aid events; special education transition services; graduates' plans, SAT and AP results; the phone rule; and what the posted bus information says about getting home after activities. Adds two federal special education rules (to the library's `special-ed-iep-basics`), what ASE automotive certifications require, and May 2025 wages for the Northern New Hampshire area that includes Grafton County.

**Counts:** 11 topics, 67 facts and 4 number tables.

**Main sources:** PRHS's 2026-27 Program of Studies, 9th Grade Program of Studies and Student Handbook; the 2026-27 School Profile; the PRHS CTE website and career-technical page; the School Counseling and Student Services sites; the 2026-27 list of scheduled events; Pemi-Baker school board minutes (July 2025 to November 17, 2026); the district's 2025 annual report (a scanned PDF, read by OCR); EdJobsNH's posting for the TV & Media teacher; Plymouth State University's Accelerated High School Program and 2026-27 tuition pages; ASE's own pages; eCFR; and the BLS wage data API.

**Main gaps:** No late or activity bus is described anywhere PRHS posts bus information. No source says how many graduates stay in Grafton County, or how many CTE graduates local shops hire. No fire or EMT ride-along program was found; one "Fire Fighter" ELO is named among 2024-25 ELOs. The cost of the Plymouth State courses taught through PRHS's CTE programs is not posted. PRHS's documents disagree on the math credits needed (3, 3.5 or 4), noted where cited.

**Proposed new shared topic:** `industry-credentials-explained`: What Career Credentials Earned in High School Mean (kind how_it_works, question_type after_high_school). Description: "What the industry credentials that high school career programs offer actually certify, and what more a full credential needs, such as ASE entry-level versus professional automotive certification." Two facts here; Dover, Exeter, Keene and Laconia's career center files also name ASE student certification.

## special-ed-iep-basics: Special Education and IEPs: What They Are and Who Qualifies

- kind: rule
- question_type: rules
- description: What an IEP and special education are, which children qualify in New Hampshire, and that services are free.

1. Under the federal IDEA regulations, special education includes vocational education (organized programs that prepare people for paid or unpaid work or for careers not requiring a bachelor's degree) when it meets the definition of specially designed instruction, and it must be provided at no cost to parents apart from incidental fees charged to all students.
   - citation: 34 CFR 300.39(a)(2)(iii), (b)(1) and (b)(5)
   - source: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/subtitle-B/chapter-III/part-300/section-300.39
   - as_of: 2026-11-21
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text: "(2) Special education includes each of the following, if the services otherwise meet the requirements of paragraph (a)(1) of this section— ... (iii) Vocational education"; "(1) At no cost means that all specially-designed instruction is provided without charge, but does not preclude incidental fees that are normally charged to nondisabled students"; "(5) Vocational education means organized educational programs that are directly related to the preparation of individuals for paid or unpaid employment, or for additional preparation for a career not requiring a baccalaureate or advanced degree." Fetched from the eCFR API (version dated 2026-11-01). Raw: raw/high/ecfr-34-300.39.txt
2. Under the federal IDEA regulations, a student's IEP must state the special education, related services, supplementary aids and program changes the school will provide so the student can take part and progress in the general education curriculum and "participate in extracurricular and other nonacademic activities" alongside students without disabilities.
   - citation: 34 CFR 300.320(a)(4)
   - source: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/subtitle-B/chapter-III/part-300/section-300.320
   - as_of: 2026-11-21
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text: "(4) A statement of the special education and related services and supplementary aids and services ... and a statement of the program modifications or supports for school personnel that will be provided to enable the child— (i) To advance appropriately toward attaining the annual goals; (ii) To be involved in and make progress in the general education curriculum ... and to participate in extracurricular and other nonacademic activities; and (iii) To be educated and participate with other children with disabilities and nondisabled children". The regulation doesn't name CTE classes; an IEP covers the student's school program, which includes CTE courses taken at the high school. CCSNH says IEPs and 504 plans aren't applied to the college-credit part of Early College courses (library `early-college-at-your-high-school` 8). Raw: raw/high/ecfr-34-300.320.txt
