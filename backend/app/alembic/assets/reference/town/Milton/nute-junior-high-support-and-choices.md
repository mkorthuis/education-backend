# Nute Junior High: Support and Choices

Part of the Milton research (town/Milton). Gathered November 22, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers Nute Middle School (Nute Junior High, grades 6–8) and the support and choices around it: math results and what the district is doing about them, special education staffing and how services are delivered, who to call about an IEP, homeschooled and EFA students' access to Nute's sports, band and classes, the open enrollment article and other schools, returning mid-year, sports grade checks, 6th graders in a grades 6–12 building, bullying and phone rules, extra math help in 6th grade, private schools within a drive, absences and the Nute Library. Sources are SAU 64's website (www.sau64.org) and the files it links: Milton School Board policies, the Nute family handbook (2025–26, still the one linked), the bell schedule, the student code of conduct, the transportation handbook, the strategic plan, school board minutes and agendas (July 2025 to November 2026), the December 2025 special education review and January 2026 MTSS review summaries, and the March 10, 2026 election results; plus RSA 194-D, the library's EFA marketplace list, and OpenStreetMap/OSRM for drive times.

**Counts:** 12 topics (all shared slugs), 72 facts and 2 number tables (drive times from Nute to private schools; special education students by disability, Milton and New Hampshire).

**New data concepts:**
- Milton's open enrollment program as voted (10 students in to grades 6–12 at Nute, 1% of residents out), the vote (422–395) and the board's implementation talks.
- The district's own outside reviews of special education and its multi-tiered system of supports (MTSS), and the contracted special education staff count (6 case managers and 16 paraprofessionals in November 2025).
- The FY2027 staffing cuts that touch support staff (paraprofessional days and contracted hours).
- Grades 6–12 in one building: the 6th grade's own lunch period and teachers.

**Main findings:**
- Milton voters adopted open enrollment on March 10, 2026 (422–395): Nute may take 10 students from outside the district in grades 6–12, and 1% of resident students (the superintendent said about 5) may go to another district's open enrollment school. As of June 24, 2026 the district had one outbound request and no written procedure; counsel advised first-come, first-served. 2026–27 minutes aren't posted.
- The December 2025 special education review found "substantial turnover" over 5–7 years among paraprofessionals, special education teachers, related service providers and the director, plus vacancies filled by contracted staff; in November 2025 the district had 6 contracted case managers and 16 contracted paraprofessionals (K–12). The January 2026 MTSS review says "Inclusive and push-in service models are strongly valued" but "implementation has outpaced readiness."
- FY2027 cuts include four teaching positions (among them "secondary elective and student support programming"), paraprofessional contract days from 188 to 186, and contracted paraprofessional hours from 7.5 to 6.5 a day.
- The 23% math figure is what the district printed on the 2026 ballot (district-wide, 2024–25 tests). Nute's middle school uses shared "skills" time with i-Ready, interventionists and Title I reading and math support for grades 6–8.
- Milton rewrote its bullying policy JICK and its access policy JJJ (which now names EFA students) on November 4, 2026. The phone policy JICJ (August 2025) bans personal devices bell to bell, lunch and passing time included.
- The Nute Library is both the school's library and a public library; students may use it after school for library work.

**Main gaps:**
- No Nute document describes how 6th graders are kept apart from high schoolers in halls, on buses or at arrival; only the lunch schedule, teacher assignments and events are separate.
- No current count of vacant special education or paraprofessional positions; Milton posts jobs on AppliTrack, which wasn't read.
- 2026–27 school board minutes (July–November 2026) aren't posted, so the open enrollment procedure and the outcome of the November 12, 2026 "Discussion of reductions" are unknown.
- The Nute Library's public hours and card rules: the town website refused automated requests (HTTP 403).
- No source shows whether Rochester or Wakefield accepts open enrollment students (Wakefield is being researched now; see For another file).
- Web search: the session's search budget ran out after one search, so everything else came from pages reached directly.
