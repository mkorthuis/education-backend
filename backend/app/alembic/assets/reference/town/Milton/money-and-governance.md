# Milton: Money, Governance, the 2026 Deficit and the Feasibility Study

Part of the Milton research (town/Milton). Gathered November 22, 2026.

## Summary

**What's covered:** 13 topics, 83 facts and 4 number tables on how the Milton School District (SAU 64) builds, votes on and pays for its budget; why the 2026-27 default budget was higher than the budget voters approved; the split between the school board and the budget committee; the deficit of about $2.5 million found in summer 2026 and its causes; the FY2027 drop in state aid; what the Question 2 tax cap means for 2027-28; SAU 64's central office costs and its 2026 leadership change; the open enrollment article's costs and capacity; the feasibility study on Nute's future; staffing cuts, vacancies and what is known about turnover; the buildings, gym and fields; home sales; and the town's role in the school's cash shortfall.

- **Budget and default.** Voters approved the budget committee's $12,862,856 for 2026-27, $363,360 below the $13,226,216 default (**derived**). The default was higher because it was built from the 2025-26 budget plus $720,502 more for special education, and because it kept staff positions that the proposal moved to contracted services. The default has been above the proposal on each Milton warrant found, FY2024 to FY2027, by $15,713 to $363,360; the proposal passed each year a result was found.
- **The split.** The board's $13,274,964 was $48,747 above the default; the budget committee voted 5-2 to cut $412,108.03, spread evenly at about 3.1% per line (**derived**); the board voted 0-5 against recommending it. Voters rejected an amendment to cut to $11 million, 17 to 74, at the deliberative session.
- **Deficit.** New administrators found about $73,000 in the bank in July 2026. The district estimates the deficit at $2,547,444 (2025-26 overspending of $1,188,288, mostly special education; a $100,788 food service deficit; and $1,258,368 from tax rates set on revenue that never came). No audit has been finished since 2023. A deficit appropriation is planned for March 2027; raising it all in one year would add about $2.67 per $1,000 (**derived**). The town is borrowing in anticipation of taxes (Select Board vote 2-1).
- **State aid** fell $739,165 for FY2027 after rising in FY2026; most of the drop is in the extraordinary need grant, tied to fewer students eligible for free or reduced-price meals and higher property values per such student (**derived** from the site's data).
- **Tax cap.** The board recommended against Question 2 (3-1); Milton adopted it (site data), so from 2027-28 added spending above the cap needs 60% of voters, by the district's account including the deficit article.
- **Feasibility study.** Dr. Andrew Dolloff, hired in October 2026 for under $15,000, is studying options from improving Nute to tuitioning students out; report expected before the March 2027 vote.

## school-budget-overruns-and-reserves: Towns' Duty to Fund Schools and Tax Anticipation Notes

- description: What a New Hampshire school district can do when costs exceed its budget during the year: special meetings, emergency overexpenditures, retained fund balance, contingency funds and special education reserves

1. Under New Hampshire law, a town's selectmen must assess on the district's taxable property enough to meet the school district's voted obligations and pay it over to the district treasurer as the school board requires for the maintenance of schools.

   - as_of: 2026-11-22
   - review_by: 2027-08-15

2. Under New Hampshire law, a town may borrow in anticipation of the current year's taxes to pay current operating expenses, up to its total tax levy, through tax anticipation notes that must be repaid within one year.
   - citation: RSA 33:7, I and III
