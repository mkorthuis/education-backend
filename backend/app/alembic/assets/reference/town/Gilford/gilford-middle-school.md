# Gilford Middle School and Home Education: The Move From GES, Math, Phones, Buses, Absences, Bullying and Homeschool Access

Part of the Gilford research (town/Gilford). Gathered November 21, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers Gilford Middle School (GMS, grades 5–8): the move from Gilford Elementary (GES, K–4), the school day, grading and homework, advanced math (Algebra I in grades 7–8), gifted services, phones and Chromebooks, special education plans and moves between schools, the early middle school buses, absences (including for ski racing), the ski teams, homework help, bullying rules, class placement, and how home-educated and EFA students can join district classes and activities or come back to school.

**Sources:** the SAU 73 and GMS websites (sau73nh.gov and gms.sau73nh.gov, SchoolBlocks sites; the old gilfordschools.org domain no longer resolves), the GMS 2026-27 Student & Parent Handbook, Gilford School Board policies (Drive PDFs linked from the policy page), the 2026-27 bus route sheets, school board minutes and agendas (September 2025 to November 2026), the 2026 Special Education Procedures Plan (a scan, read by OCR), the 2023-24 technology plan, the 2026-27 calendar, GMS's 5th grade homework page, the GHS 2024-25 math course chart, ArbiterLive's GMS team list, and the federal Civil Rights Data Collection (CRDC) for 2023-24.

**Counts:** 12 topics, 76 facts, 1 table.

**Main findings:**
- **Algebra I in middle school is real but small.** In 2023-24 GMS taught one Algebra I class to 21 students in grades 7 and 8 (about one in seven of its 147 seventh and eighth graders, derived), and all 21 passed. GMS reported no gifted and talented program. Policy IMBD lets middle schoolers earn pass/fail high school credit for high school math and modern language. No posted rule says how students are chosen for Algebra I.
- **Early buses.** All seven 2026-27 high/middle school routes make their first stop between 6:37 and 6:49 a.m. and reach Gilford High between 7:12 and 7:20; GMS starts at 7:25 and ends at 2:14. The same buses' elementary runs start about an hour later (7:48–8:02).
- **Phones and Chromebooks.** Policy JICJ (September 2025) bans personal devices bell to bell; the GMS handbook requires phones to be off and in lockers, with graduated consequences. Every student has a district Chromebook that goes home nightly, with browsing filtered at home too; the 2023-24 plan gives students a new one in grade 5.
- **Home education.** Gilford's access policy JJJ was revised November 2, 2026 and covers home-educated, EFA, charter and private school students: written request to the principal, the same eligibility (except attendance), tryouts and fees as enrolled students. But Gilford's home education policy IHBG (last revised January 2023) still describes the annual evaluation, portfolio and notices that state law dropped on July 1, 2026.
- **Bullying.** Policy JICK was rewritten November 2, 2026: parents told within 48 hours, an investigation started within 5 school days and finished within 10 (extendable by 7). GMS's own bullying counts are in the site's data.
- **Absences.** Policy JH has no rule about sports travel; the principal decides whether a reason is "good cause", and family trips need a note two weeks ahead so a homework plan can be made.

## district-curriculum-and-assessment: Competency Grading, Homework Rules and Math Work at Gilford Middle

- kind: local
- question_type: local_practice
- description: The curriculum programs a district uses (math, reading, science), how reading is taught and supported, its local tests, and how teachers fit local content into lessons

1.
