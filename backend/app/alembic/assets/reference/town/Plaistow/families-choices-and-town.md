# Plaistow Families, Choices and Town Matters

Part of the Plaistow research (town/Plaistow). Gathered November 21, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers what Plaistow families ask about outside the classroom: who to call at the SAU office about an IEP; how Timberlane compares with Haverhill, Massachusetts; enrolling a child who moves in mid-year, and the papers that prove residency; free and reduced-price meals; Education Freedom Accounts (EFAs) for home-educated children, and special education evaluations for children not enrolled; a grandparent's papers for enrolling a grandchild and getting his records; PickUp Patrol and the PowerSchool parent portal; counselors and the social worker at Pollard; EFA money for Massachusetts private schools; busing for private school students; and home prices in Plaistow against Atkinson and Hampstead.

- **Main findings:**
  - Enrollment papers go to the **school's registrar** (Pollard's for a Pollard student), not the SAU office: an online PowerSchool Enrollment form, then an original birth certificate, **two** proofs of residency from an 11-item list, immunization records, a recent physical and any guardianship papers. Policy JG places a student moving in from another school after the principal reviews the old school's records with the parents. The policy doesn't mention repeating a grade.
  - At the SAU office (30 Greenough Road) the special education contacts are the **Director of Student Services**, a Special Education Coordinator for the elementary schools and an Out of District Coordinator. The middle school handbook says to start with the teacher, then the school counselor, then an administrator. Policy KE runs teacher, then principal, then superintendent, with the board hearing a written appeal only if a majority votes to.
  - Free and reduced-price meals take **one application per household**, online or on paper, at any time of year, sent to the Free & Reduced Coordinator at the SAU office. Children of SNAP or TANF households, foster children and homeless children qualify for free meals. Reduced-price breakfast is free and reduced-price lunch is $0.40. Policy EFAA lets students buy a meal even with too little money in their account.
  - Timberlane's Child Find covers children **who are home-schooled** or in private schools; their parents contact the Child Find Coordinator. Policy JJJ sends a non-enrolled child's requests for speech, counseling or special education to the Director of Student Services.
  - Pollard's staff directory lists **two school counselors, one social worker and a school psychologist**. Under policy JRA-R, "parent" includes a legal guardian or a person acting as a parent. Parents keep their rights to records unless a court order or legal document takes them away.
  - On November 21, 2026, **31 Plaistow students** were eligible for an EFA for 2026-27, with 5 on the waitlist. The EFA marketplace listed private schools in Haverhill, Lawrence, Methuen, North Andover, Andover, Amesbury, Newburyport and Byfield, Massachusetts.
  - Haverhill's 2026 MCAS results were below Massachusetts' averages: 29% of grades 3–8 students met or exceeded expectations in math, against 41% statewide. Its 2025 graduation rate was 86.7%, against 89.3%. 60.8% of its students are low-income, and in 2026 the state listed it as "requiring assistance or intervention." The two states' tests and proficiency bars differ, so the shares can't be compared across the state line.
  - From January to October 2026, Plaistow's median single-family sale price ($592,500) was about 11% below Atkinson's and 10% below Hampstead's (**derived**). Over ten years Plaistow's typical home value rose about as fast as both towns' values (Zillow). Plaistow and Atkinson share the same middle and high school.
