## Summary

## district-tax-and-state-aid: How the Seven Kearsarge Towns Split the School Tax, and New London's Tax Bill

- kind: local
- question_type: governance_money
- description: What drives a town's school taxes, beyond the site's tax rate and adequacy aid data: how a district's budget, its state aid and the tax rate fit together locally, its own projected rates, revaluations and their reasons, and village precinct rates

1. The Kearsarge Regional School District splits the amount it must raise by taxes among its seven towns by a formula, 60% by each town's resident students (average daily membership in residence, "ADM-R") and 40% by each town's equalized property value, then subtracts each town's own state education aid to get the town's local school tax.
   - citation: Kearsarge Regional School District, 59th Annual Report (March 2026), "Kearsarge Regional Estimated Tax Computation, School Year 2026-2027," pp. 47–48
   - source: https://www.kearsarge.org/sites/g/files/vyhlif731/f/pages/kearsarge_2026_full_report.pdf

   - review_by: 2027-11-22

   - note: Text: "Formula: 60% on ADM-R and 40% on EVAL"; Section III columns "Rate / Apportionment / Less Town Specific Revenues / Less Final State Aid / Estimated Local Tax Assessment"; "The 2024-2025 Average Daily Memberships were used to calculate the above tax assessment estimates along with 2024-2025 equalized valuations." The March 2024 report shows the same formula (raw: raw/money/district/2024_annual_report_-_done.txt, p. 38). A cooperative's articles of agreement set the split, and the formula may be reviewed by warrant article (library facts-terms-and-how-schools-work.md gov-cooperative-district 3, 5); Kearsarge's articles of agreement were not retrieved.
