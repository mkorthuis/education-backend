# Plymouth Regional High School and the Career Center, From Thornton

Part of the Thornton research (town/Thornton). Gathered November 22, 2026.

## Summary

Covers what Thornton families' questions about Plymouth Regional High School (PRHS, run by the Pemi-Baker Regional School District) need beyond Plymouth's and Campton's files: which hands-on and trades courses PRHS does and doesn't have (no welding, electrical, carpentry or construction course, and Automotive is typically a junior and senior course); a 2000 seat-sharing policy that still stands as PRHS's only posted career-center enrollment rule and that lists a Construction Technology program the school no longer shows; how the 40-minute bus ride works out stop by stop on the 2026-27 route sheet and handbook; the size of the jump from Thornton Central (172 students) to PRHS (651); the dances, league sports and late-work rules that bring Thornton's middle schoolers together with the other towns' students before 9th grade; the missing visit or shadow day; PRHS's math sequence and prerequisites; Pemi-Baker's acceleration policy (high school credit for Algebra I or a language taken in middle school, a PRHS course for a middle schooler with the home district paying the transportation, VLACS credit, and Plymouth State courses); its advanced course work and dual enrollment policy; study release for juniors and seniors; how a homeschooled or EFA student asks for a PRHS course (policy JJJ), what chemistry and welding look like for that student, and SAU 48's notification form; how a business can offer an internship; and what site data and Census figures show about Thornton families using other options. It cites Plymouth's file (`town/Plymouth/prhs-and-career-center.md`, `pes-support-and-choices.md`) and Campton's file (`town/Campton/plymouth-regional-from-campton.md`) for what they already hold and adds only Thornton's side.

**Counts:** 8 topics, 40 facts and 5 number tables.

**Main sources:** PRHS's 2026-27 Program of Studies, Student Handbook, bus route sheet and Parent Night slides (through Plymouth's and Campton's saved copies); six Pemi-Baker board policies (the Regional Center Enrollment Policy of 2000, the 1991 Acceleration Policy with its 1992 SAU 48 memo, policy LEB/IHCD, the Extended Learning Opportunities policy, JJJ and IHCA), read from the PDFs on PRHS's approved-policies page (two are scans, transcribed from images); Thornton Central's 2026-27 handbook, SAU middle school dance page and School Board minutes (January to November 2026); SAU 48's math curriculum page, homeschool notification form and the SAU 48 counseling site; VLACS's pages; NH DOE's 2025 CTE program grid and the center list in the reference library; the NH Education Facts database; CSF NH's EFA feed; and OpenStreetMap routing between school map points only.

**Main gaps:** No PRHS, SAU 48 or Thornton page describes an 8th grade visit or shadow day, an afternoon or late bus, how many students come from each town, CTE enrollment by town, or how students from different towns get along. Thornton Central posts no Algebra I course, gifted program or acceleration procedure, and NH DOE's gifted-and-talented reports are not public by school. Whether Pemi-Baker would send or pay for a Thornton student to attend another region's career center (for welding or construction) was not found. NH electrician apprentice rules were not retrieved (the OPLC page returned HTTP 403). Nothing says what PRHS graduates' work readiness looks like to employers.

**No new shared topic is proposed.** One topic here, `vlacs-online-courses`, reuses a library topic from Bethlehem's file with its kind, question type and description.
