# Berlin Elementary School and Young Families

Part of the Berlin research (town/Berlin). Gathered 2026-11-21.

## Summary

Covers Berlin Elementary School (BES, preschool to grade 5) in the Berlin School District (SAU 3) and the questions of families with young children: the Project Youth after-school program and care after it ends (5:40 p.m. for grades K–5), bus eligibility and paid busing, Title I and reading help, the public preschool and Head Start, meals, crossing guards and walking, family events, speech therapy and outside evaluations, a grandparent's standing with a caregiver letter, food help, counselors, advanced learners and child care before kindergarten.

15 topics, 49 facts and 2 number tables (`berlin_child_care_drive_from_bes`, `berlin_school_support_staff_crdc`). Two topics add Berlin to library topics that had no Berlin entry (`kindergarten-day-length`, `kindergarten-registration`).

**Sources.** On November 21, 2026 every sau3.org page tried answered automated requests with a JavaScript bot check ("Client Challenge"). The main session then read the after-school, transportation, bus schedule, food services and guidance pages in a browser (raw/elementary/browser-pass/sau3-pages-2026-11-21.txt); the Title I, newsletter and staff pages remain unread. Otherwise what the district says was taken from (a) the library's copies of the SAU 3 home page and Student Support page saved November 8, 2026; (b) board policies (EEA, EEAG, JFAA, JHC, JLD, JRA, KB) downloaded directly from the district's document storage; (c) the district's FY2027 budget book for the City Council (scanned; pages transcribed from images); and (d) the federal Civil Rights Data Collection (CRDC) 2023-24 file, which gives each school's preschool type, counselor and psychologist staffing and gifted programs. Also used: the City of Berlin's site, Tri-County CAP's Head Start pages, the NH Food Bank, the Family Resource Center, FRAC's community eligibility sheet, the federal NPI registry and the site's own child care data (drive times derived with OSRM).

**Main gaps:** how early storm closings and delays are announced (no closing policy or notice was found); whether any crossing guard works near State Street; Project Youth's fees and days; whether BES runs a schoolwide or targeted Title I program and whether reading help is pull-out; what a bus pass costs; whether BES's preschool is still limited to children with disabilities in 2026-27; any school food pantry or weekend food bags; outside evaluation wait times; and BES's gifted-and-talented report to NH DOE (NH DOE blocks automated requests).

Privacy note for reviewers: CRDC's district file names the civil rights coordinators; the saved extract keeps only the preschool and kindergarten columns. Staff are named in no fact; contacts are given as office phone numbers.
