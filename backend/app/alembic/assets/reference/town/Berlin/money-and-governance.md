# Berlin: Money, Taxes, Staffing and Governance

Part of the Berlin research (town/Berlin). Gathered November 21, 2026.

## Summary

**What's covered:** 13 topics, 86 facts and 10 number tables on the Berlin School District (SAU 3) budget and how the City Council sets it, the school share of the tax bill, state aid and EFAs, staffing and vacancies, buildings, regional sharing with Gorham, and the 2026 tax cap vote.
- **How the budget is approved.** Berlin's school district is a "dependent" city district. Under the city charter, school appropriations belong to the City Council, while the elected five-member Board of Education manages the schools. The Council sets the bottom line; the Board decides where the money goes. For 2026-27 the Board asked for $25,674,908. On May 4, 2026 the Council cut $550,000 and adopted $25,124,908 for school operations on June 15, 2026. The Council has appropriated less than the Board asked in 22 of the 23 years from 2003-04 to 2025-26 (**derived**).
- **What drives the budget.** Out-of-district special education placements are the largest driver of the 2026-27 request (+$546,028, about half the requested increase, **derived**). Health insurance fell 1.6% and energy fell slightly. A contracted speech-language pathologist and Chromebooks add the rest. Enrollment fell from 1,631 (2004-05) to 959 (2025-26). The school's non-tax revenue has stayed between about $11.3 million and $15.0 million while spending rose, so the part left to property taxes grew from $2.9 million (2011-12) to $10.1 million (2024-25).
- **Pay.** SAU 3's Mandatory Report to Voters (read in the browser pass) lists 2025-26 pay of $123,000 for the district's chief administrator and $96,135 to $98,751 for the two principals and an assistant principal. Its inflation-adjusted totals show administrator pay rising from $479,127 (2017) to $849,576 (2025), and the average teacher salary from $46,411 to $65,794 (2026). It counts three central office administrators.
- **Staffing.** The district reported 8 to 9 unfilled positions in March 2026. In April it described 3 classroom teacher vacancies (covered by combining grade 5 and 6 classes), 6 unfilled special education teaching positions and a chemistry vacancy. It added 4 classroom and 2.5 special education paraprofessionals to the 2026-27 budget. No source says why paraprofessionals leave.
- **Taxes and aid.** School taxes were 41.4% of Berlin's 2025 rate ($13.90 of $33.60). For 2026-27 the school budget, net of its own revenue, is about 37% of what the city's general fund leaves to property taxes (**derived**). A 2026 citywide revaluation was under way and the tax year 2026 rate was not yet set on November 16. Berlin's EFA phase-out grant fell to $493, and phase-out grants end for new EFA students from July 1, 2026. Berlin was not a ConVal plaintiff. On October 19, 2026 the Council voted to join an amicus brief in the Rand case.
- **Buildings and sharing.** Brown School closed after 2018-19. The city transferred it to a developer, which now owns it as a 20-unit apartment building. The Bartlett and Marston schools closed to students in 2009. A 2018 study the city commissioned recommended, in the long run, one SAU for the Androscoggin Valley. In March 2026 the administrator said studies had found no significant savings from regionalizing with SAU 20 but more opportunities.
- **Tax cap.** Berlin voted 63.9% yes on Question 2 (site data, unofficial). In September the superintendent said scenarios would come once legal guidance arrived.
