# Exeter: Money, Governance, Buildings and Community Support

Part of the Exeter research (town/Exeter). Gathered November 16, 2026.

## Summary

**What's covered:** 8 topics, 79 facts and 4 number tables on Exeter's 2026 tax rate and what drives the school part of it, how the Exeter Region Cooperative splits its costs among six towns, both districts' 2026-27 (FY2027) budgets and their drivers, paraprofessional staffing, the senior exemptions and deferral, how residents can follow and speak to the boards, the cooperative's open enrollment vote, school bonds and capital reserves, and the school and town help for families (weekend food, the Community Children's Fund, town-funded agencies) and for groups that want to help.
- **Taxes.** Exeter's 2026 rate is $19.54 per $1,000: school $12.79 (both districts), state education $1.09, municipal $4.98, county $0.68. The school part rose $0.33 from 2025 and the municipal part $0.50 (**derived**). School-related taxes are 71.0% of the rate, or $1,388 a year per $100,000 of assessed value (**derived**). The cooperative's FY2027 tax calculation, its split of the $2,778,598 increase by town (Exeter 44%, $0.35 per $1,000), its fund balance returned to taxpayers, and the town's 2024 revaluation and 2026 valuation.
- **Cost sharing.** In tax year 2025 Exeter paid 43.0% of the cooperative school taxes of the six towns, while 44.8% of their public school students in grades 6–12 lived in Exeter and Exeter held 41.3% of their property value (**derived** from the site's data; table `ercsd_town_shares_2025`). The cooperative's articles of agreement (its formula) could not be retrieved.
- **Budgets.** ERCSD $76,453,074 and ESD $28,628,762 for 2026-27, with health insurance (+15.5% and +14.8% medical rates; ESD +$568,920), salaries, special education tuition and the ESD's decision to cover school meal charges as the main drivers; the teachers' and paraprofessionals' contracts; the ERCSD's first FY2028 draft ($78,413,134).
- **Paraprofessionals and special education.** The ESD has 38 paraeducator employees and 8 contractors and aims to replace contractors with employees; aide budget lines fell 5.8% at the ESD and held flat at the ERCSD (**derived**); Exeter High's share of students on IEPs rose from 13.5% to 21%.
- **Seniors and voters.** Elderly exemptions of $198,000 to $307,000 off assessed value (198 granted in 2026), the disabled exemption, the tax deferral, policy BEDH's public comment rules, the elected Budget Advisory Committee, ParentSquare sign-up for community members, the 2026 results in Exeter, and the HB 1300 hearing.
- **Buildings.** The ESD's only bond (Main Street School, issued 2017) is paid off in FY2027; no bond article was on the 2026 ballot; Lincoln Street's LED lighting financed through Unitil; the ERCSD's middle school bond runs to 2040, and its capital reserve is saving toward Exeter High's roof.
- **Community help.** End 68 Hours of Hunger (weekend food), the Community Children's Fund ($13,000 in October 2026, used "privately"), the ESD's gift, solicitation, advertising and volunteer policies, and the agencies the town funds (St. Vincent de Paul food pantry, Rockingham Community Action, Seacoast Mental Health and others).

**Main sources:** the Town of Exeter's tax, assessing, exemption, election and human services pages and its 2026 MS-1; SAU 16's budget folders (the ERCSD FY2027 public hearing handout, the ESD FY2027 draft budget and warrant, deliberative session minutes, the ESD's 2025 annual financial report), ERCSD and ESD board and Budget Advisory Committee minutes and agendas (June–November 2026), ESD policies, SAU 16 news posts and the Community Children's Fund press release; the site's database for derived comparisons.
