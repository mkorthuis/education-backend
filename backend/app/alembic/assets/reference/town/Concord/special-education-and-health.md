# Special Education, 504 Plans and Student Health in Concord

Part of the Concord research (town/Concord). Gathered November 15, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers how the Concord School District (SAU 8) serves students with disabilities and students' mental and physical health: who runs special education and 504 plans, how 504 plans are coordinated at Concord High, school counselors and other mental health staff by school, Riverbend Community Mental Health's work in the schools, the district's tiered social-emotional supports and screening, its behavior intervention (CARE) team and crisis protocols, school nurses, Concord High's special education programs and transition programs (including Project SEARCH at Concord Hospital), case manager caseloads and the time IEP paperwork takes, related-service staffing and contractors, the use of AAC devices, and the reading and language training the district lists for staff.

- **9 topics, 79 facts and 4 number tables.** Five topics are shared district slugs with `place: district Concord` (`district-special-education-services`, `district-student-support`, `district-safety-and-security`, `district-classrooms-and-staff`, `district-curriculum-and-assessment`). Four add facts to existing topics: the library's `community-mental-health-centers` (Riverbend, `place: town Concord`), `school-staffing-and-class-size` and `special-ed-iep-basics` (state and federal rules, no place), and the shared `dyslexia-screening-and-intervention` (no place). No new topic is proposed.
- **New data concepts:** school counselors by school with derived students per counselor against New Hampshire's and the nation's 2024-25 ratios; special education case manager caseloads by school (March 2026); the district's own count of hours case managers spend on IEP paperwork and meetings at one school; students with disabilities by category (February 2026); 504 plan counts and how many are for mental health; district versus contractor cost by position; spending on contracted behavior staff, 2021-22 to 2025-26; AAC use at Mill Brook; Riverbend referral counts; universal social-emotional screening results.
- **Main sources:** the district's Student Services, Section 504, Child Find, School Counseling, Nurse/Student Health, Student Support Team, MTSS and SAEBRS pages; the Special Education Policies and Procedures Manual (revised February 25, 2025); the Student Services FY27 budget presentation and minutes (February 18, 2026); the case manager caseload list (March 4, 2026); board minutes of April 6, May 4, June 1 and October 5, 2026 and the November 2, 2026 packet; the June 1, 2026 packet's school counseling presentation; the Health Procedures Manual; Policies 201, 461 and 532; the Student Violence Prevention and Management Plan (August 2026); the Strategic Plan update (January 2026); the Concord High handbook (2025-26) and Program of Studies (2026-27); Riverbend's own pages; ASCA's 2024-25 ratios; RSA 200, Ed 306 and 34 CFR 300.
- **Main findings:**
  - Concord High lists 7 school counselors for 1,265 students (November 2026), about 181 students each (derived), plus a counselor therapist, a student assistance counselor, 2 social workers and a school psychologist. ASCA reports New Hampshire's 2024-25 ratio at 186 students per counselor for all grades, and the nation's at 372. Each elementary school has one counselor, for 256 to 345 students.
  - The district counted 295 students with 504 plans in February 2026, 263 of them for mental health. School counselors run 504 plans in each building; at Concord High, with the CHS Director of Special Education. No posted procedure says how 504 plans reach substitutes.
  - At Christa McAuliffe in 2025-26, the district counted 611.5 hours of case manager time on referrals, evaluations and IEPs. Case managers carried 1 to 22 students each in March 2026.
  - About 26% of Mill Brook's special education students use AAC devices; its Owl Program for students with autism enrolled 11. The district trained staff on AAC devices in 2024-25, and Concord High has a part-time teacher of the hearing impaired who is its AAC specialist.
  - Riverbend has a school mental health liaison in the district. The schools referred 85 students to it by February 2026, and a Project AWARE grant supports the tie with Riverbend and NAMI-NH. The district's CARE Team, a behavior intervention team that includes police and mental health partners, follows the NABITA model.
  - In October 2026 the district reported an occupational therapy vacancy and a need for 16 more educational assistants. In February 2026 it reported no increase in related-service staff for preschool since 2021-22, while preschool enrollment grew from 101 to 125.
- **Main gaps:** any count of overdue evaluations or therapy waitlists (NH DOE's special education pages, including the Indicator 11 timeliness data, refuse automated requests); full-time equivalents for counselors and nurses (pages list names, not hours); whether any Concord High staff hold Orton-Gillingham or similar certification; the district's written threat assessment procedure and what parents are told after a threat (the CARE Team page is the only public description); how 504 plans are shared with substitutes in practice.

## special-ed-iep-basics: Assistive Technology and AAC in an IEP

- kind: rule
- question_type: rules
- description: What an IEP and special education are, which children qualify in New Hampshire, and that services are free.

1. Federal special education rules require the IEP team to consider each child's communication needs and whether the child needs assistive technology devices and services, when writing and when reviewing the IEP.
   - citation: 34 CFR 300.324(a)(2)(iv)–(v) and (b)(2)
   - source: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-300.324
   - as_of: 2026-11-15
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text: "(2) Consideration of special factors. The IEP Team must— ... (iv) Consider the communication needs of the child ... Raw copy: raw/sped/ecfr-34cfr-300.324.txt
2. Under federal rules, an assistive technology device is any item, equipment or system used to increase, maintain or improve a child's functional abilities (not a surgically implanted device), and assistive technology services include evaluating the child's needs, buying or leasing devices, fitting and repairing them, and training the child, the family and staff.
   - citation: 34 CFR 300.5 and 300.6
   - source: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-300.6
   - as_of: 2026-11-15
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: 300.5: "any item, piece of equipment, or product system ... used to increase, maintain, or improve the functional capabilities of a child with a disability. The term does not include a medical device that is surgically implanted". 300.6 lists "(a) The evaluation of the needs of a child ... (b) Purchasing, leasing, or otherwise providing ... (c) Selecting, designing, fitting, customizing, adapting, applying, maintaining, repairing, or replacing ... (e) Training or technical assistance for a child ... or ... that child's family; and (f) Training or technical assistance for professionals". Raw copies: raw/sped/ecfr-34cfr-300.5.txt; raw/sped/ecfr-34cfr-300.6.txt
3. Federal rules require schools to make assistive technology devices and services available when a child's special education, related services or supplementary aids require them, and to let the child use a school-bought device at home or elsewhere when the IEP team decides the child needs it to get a free appropriate public education.
   - citation: 34 CFR 300.105
   - source: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-300.105
   - as_of: 2026-11-15
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text: "(a) Each public agency must ensure that assistive technology devices or assistive technology services, or both ... are made available to a child with a disability if required as a part of the child's— (1) Special education ... (2) Related services ... or (3) Supplementary aids and services ... (b) On a case-by-case basis, the use of school-purchased assistive technology devices in a child's home or in other settings is required if the child's IEP Team determines that the child needs access to those devices in order to receive FAPE."
