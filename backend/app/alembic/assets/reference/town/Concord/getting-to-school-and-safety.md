# Getting to School, Traffic and Safety in Concord

Part of the Concord research (town/Concord). Gathered November 15, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers how Concord students get to school and the safety questions around the trip: who gets a Concord School District bus (the walk zones), divided households, how storm delays are decided, the district's own bus fleet and staff, driver shortages and stop consolidations in 2026-27, bus monitors and bus conduct rules, Penacook's schools and buses in the Merrimack Valley School District, the traffic studies for the new middle school at both sites and the city's review of them, drop-off problems the city's traffic committees took up in 2025–2026, the city's winter sidewalk plowing priorities, the district's "See Something, Say Something" tip form, and Concord High's school resource officer.

- **5 fact topics, 56 facts and 4 number tables.** Places: `district Concord` for the school district's buses, policies, traffic studies and safety practices; `district Merrimack Valley` for Penacook's schools and buses; `town Concord` for the city's snow removal, traffic committees, City Council actions and police. Penacook is also its own row (id 429) in the site's `town` table; city facts about Penacook are placed `town Concord` as the brief directs, and the main session may want to tag them `town Penacook` too.
- **New data concepts:** walk-zone distances by grade and the exception and appeal path; bus-eligible student counts for each middle school site; the district's budgeted transportation staff and fleet; third-party transportation spending by year; scheduled bus ride times for Penacook's middle and high school routes (**derived**); the city's sidewalk plowing priorities; traffic study findings and the city's comments; City Council, TOC and TPAC actions on school traffic.
- **Main sources:** Concord School District Policies 711, 712 and 758; the transportation, cancellation and "See Something" pages; the 2026-27 Rundlett and 2025-26 Concord High handbooks; Board of Education minutes (February–October 2026) and the November 2, 2026 packet; the FY27 Facilities and Transportation budget presentation (March 2, 2026); the middle school project's traffic documents (VHB 2023 field notes and October 2024 draft study for Broken Ground; GPI study for the Rundlett site, December 2025, updated May 18, 2026); the City of Concord's January 21, 2026 Planning Board staff report and engineering memos, the City Manager's September 22, 2026 letter, Traffic Operations Committee (TOC) and Transportation Policy Advisory Committee (TPAC) minutes (September 2025–October 2026), the city's Winter Operations page and Winter and Inclement Weather Policy (scanned; read by OCR), and the Concord Police 2024 Annual Crime Report; the Merrimack Valley School District's profile, transportation and closing pages, Policy EEA (2023) and 2026-27 middle and high school bus route sheets.

- **Premises checked:**
  - "Moving the middle school to Broken Ground" (P2.1, P3.3, P18.1, P18.3): **out of date.** The new middle school is being built beside the current Rundlett building at 144 South Street. The board rescinded the Broken Ground choice on January 22, 2025 (6–3), approved the South Street project on October 23, 2025 (8–1) and broke ground on September 26, 2026; students are to move in during the 2028-29 school year (new-middle-school.md, district-facilities-and-capital 1, 2, 6, 7). The district's attorney told TPAC on October 22, 2026 the school "will not open until December 2028." Walk zones, bus eligibility and traffic studies below are answered for the South Street site.
  - "Bus ride times for kids living all the way up in Penacook" (P2.1): **Penacook's students don't attend Concord's middle school.** Penacook is in the Merrimack Valley School District; Merrimack Valley Middle School (14 Allen Street) and High School (106 Village Street) are in Penacook, and the 2026-27 Penacook route reaches them 17–22 minutes after its first pickup (**derived**). Whether any Penacook-addressed homes lie inside the Concord School District's line was not found in any source.
  - "The 1.5-mile walk zone" (P3.3, P15.2): **correct for grades 6–8.** Concord's Policy 711 buses grades 6–8 who live 1.5 miles or more from school; kindergarten to grade 5 at 1 mile; grades 9–12 at 2 miles with a fee ($25 in the 2025-26 Concord High handbook). Merrimack Valley's walk zone is 1.5 miles for all grades.
  - "Bus routes still getting consolidated or delayed during winter mornings" (P2.3): **partly supported.** In September and October 2026 the district reported a continuing driver shortage, bus stop consolidations, possible route consolidations when staffing is short, and winter safety concerns about stop locations. No count of late or combined runs is published.
  - "Middle schoolers now expected to walk" side streets (P18.3): the walk zone is unchanged and the school stays on its present site. The city plows designated school walking routes second, after the downtown metered area and after the streets.
  - "A traffic impact study for Loudon Road and Portsmouth Street if all middle school traffic shifts to Broken Ground" (P18.1): VHB's October 2024 draft study for the Broken Ground site covered Portsmouth Street, South Curtisville Road and East Side Drive (Route 132), **not Loudon Road**. The Rundlett site was then studied by GPI and reviewed by the city.
  - "Bus monitors" (P18.2): the district's posted monitor job is to help drivers carry special-needs students; 12 monitors are budgeted. No policy on monitors for regular routes was found.
  - "Chaotic morning drop-off corridors on Warren Street and Loudon Road" (P35.2): no coordinated plan for Warren Street (Concord High) was found, and no Concord school's drop-off is on Loudon Road. The city's 2025–2026 drop-off work concerned South Curtisville Road and Portsmouth Street (Mill Brook and Broken Ground), Noyes and Cypress streets (the new middle school) and Sewalls Falls Road (Beaver Meadow). Loudon Road is the city's highest-crash corridor.
  - "The state's 'See Something, Say Something' system" (P35.3): Concord's "See Something, Say Something" is the school district's own Quick Tip form, checked each school day. The state's school tip pages refused automated requests, so how they connect was not checked.

1. Concord School District Policy 711 entitles kindergarten to grade 5 students who live 1 mile or more from their school, grade 6–8 students who live 1.5 miles or more, and grade 9–12 students who live 2 miles or more (subject to a fee set by the school board) to district transportation; temporary permits may be issued to other students if bus space is available and the schedule allows, and entitled students may have to walk up to 1.5 miles to a bus stop.
   - citation: Concord School District Policy #711, Student Transportation Services (revised March 6, 2017)

Temporary permits may be issued to additional students if bus space is available and the bus schedule allows. Students entitled to transportation may be required to walk a distance not exceeding 1.5 miles to an established school bus stop." The policy doesn't say how the distance is measured. Adopted 1966.
