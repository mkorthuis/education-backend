# Student Life, Climate and Discipline at Rundlett and Concord High

Part of the Concord research (town/Concord). Gathered 2026-11-15.

## Summary

This file covers student life and school climate in the Concord School District (SAU 8), mostly at Rundlett Middle School (RMS) and Concord High School (CHS). It includes suspensions by race and ethnicity from the federal Civil Rights Data Collection (CRDC); the district's bullying, conduct, discipline and bus policies; behavior support and staff training; the move from grade 8 to high school; intramurals and no-cut teams; student representatives on the Board of Education; bathrooms; band and music; lunch periods; English learners and advanced courses; halal and vegetarian meals; and homeschooled and EFA students' use of district classes and activities.

**Counts:** 11 topics, 93 facts and 2 number tables (`concord_out_of_school_suspension_by_race`, `concord_chs_ap_enrollment_by_group`).

**Main findings**
- **Suspensions by race:** NH DOE's discipline counts, which the site already has, are not broken out by race. The federal CRDC data are, and the district reports them. For 2023-24 the CRDC shows 7.5% of Concord's Black students, 8.9% of Hispanic students and 6.0% of students of two or more races with at least one out-of-school suspension, against 3.3% of White and 3.5% of Asian students. The district-wide rate was 4.1%, below New Hampshire's 4.9% (all **derived**). The groups are small, and the gaps were smaller in 2021-22.
- **What the district has done:** In March 2025 its Equity Advisory Committee named "discipline data" as one of its focus areas. Restorative practices training has reached 90% of high school staff and 80% of middle school staff. A board-adopted equity audit (Great Schools Partnership, March 2026) looked at school buildings, programs, parent groups and club participation, not discipline. **No district report that analyzes suspensions by race was found.**
- **Behavior support:** Supports come from the MTSS-B framework, Check & Connect mentors at RMS and CHS, and a list of trainings paid for by the federal Project AWARE grant (Responsive Classroom, TBRI, PREPaRE and others).
- **Student voice:** State law (RSA 194:23-f) makes student board members **nonvoting**. It says the student body elects them for one-year terms. Concord's Policy 125 (revised 2016) instead calls for "whenever possible" a junior and a senior, serving one to two years.
- **Homeschool and EFA students:** Policy 518 and the Policy 620 appendix give access to classes and activities on a written request to the principal. Policy 620 still describes the home education rules repealed July 1, 2026, and Policy 518 doesn't yet name EFA students.

**Main gaps**
- Daily menus are JavaScript web menus that couldn't be read, so whether halal or vegetarian items appear daily is unconfirmed.
- No source on locked bathrooms was found.
- No CHS 2026-27 handbook is posted, so the 2025-26 one is cited.
- No district plan for band rooms in the new building was checked; that belongs to agent 1.
- The web search budget ran out midway, so news coverage wasn't searched.

## district-conduct-policies: Bullying and Conduct Policies

- kind: local
- question_type: local_practice
- description: A district's bullying policy and its rules for student conduct, including on school buses

1. Concord School District Policy 539 (revised January 2, 2023) defines bullying as a single significant incident or a pattern of incidents directed at another student. It covers bullying on district property, which includes public or private school buses, at school-sponsored activities, and off school property when it interferes with a student's education or disrupts school.
   - citation: Concord School District Policy #539, Student Safety and Violence Prevention – Bullying, sections I–II
   - source: https://files.smartsites.parentsquare.com/8931/policy_539_student_safety_and_violence_prevention-bullying.pdf
   - as_of: 2026-11-15
   - review_by: 2027-11-15
   - place: district Concord
   - note: Text: "District property means all real property and all physical plant and equipment used for school purposes, including public or private school buses or vans." Examples of school-sponsored events include "field trips ... athletic, academic, robotic ... marching band". Raw: raw/climate/policy_539_student_safety_and_violence_prevention-bullying.txt

2. Under Concord's Policy 539, any district employee or volunteer who sees or learns of possible bullying must tell the principal or an administrator by the end of that school day, or by the end of the next school day if it happened after hours. Principals may set up a way to report anonymously, but discipline can't rest on an anonymous report alone.
   - citation: Concord School District Policy #539, section VII
   - source: https://files.smartsites.parentsquare.com/8931/policy_539_student_safety_and_violence_prevention-bullying.pdf
   - as_of: 2026-11-15

   - note: Text: "no later than the end of that school day, or the end of the next school day if bullying occurs after hours"; "formal disciplinary action may not be based solely on an anonymous report." Students may report to "the Principal, any administrator, school counselor, advisor, athletic coach, club advisor, Title IX Coordinator, or any District employee they feel comfortable with." Report forms are in each principal's office and the High School Commons areas. Raw: raw/climate/policy_539_student_safety_and_violence_prevention-bullying.txt

3. Under Concord's Policy 539, the principal notifies the parents of both the reported victim and the alleged offender within 48 hours of a report. An investigation must start within 5 school days and finish within 10 school days, extendable by up to 7 school days in writing. Parents get the results by phone within 2 school days and a determination letter within 5 school days.
   - citation: Concord School District Policy #539, sections IX, XI and XIV

The state law minimums are in library bullying-school-duties. Raw: raw/climate/policy_539_student_safety_and_violence_prevention-bullying.txt

Consequences range from a written apology to expulsion, and remedies include peer mediation and restorative practices.

## school-meal-modifications: Meal Modifications for Disabilities and Dietary Preferences

- kind: rule
- question_type: rules
- description: What federal school meal rules require when a child's diet is restricted by a disability, and what schools may do for religious, cultural or other preferences

1. Under federal school lunch rules, schools must change meals at no extra cost for a child whose disability restricts their diet. A change that breaks the meal pattern needs a medical statement signed by a state-licensed healthcare professional or a registered dietitian. A changed meal that still fits the pattern needs no statement.
   - citation: 7 CFR 210.10(m)(1)
   - source: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-7/subtitle-B/chapter-II/subchapter-A/part-210/subpart-B/section-210.10

## gov-school-boards-and-superintendents: School Boards and Superintendents

- kind: term
- question_type: governance_money
- description: The division of duties between elected school boards and superintendents.

1. New Hampshire law (RSA 194:23-f) requires each public high school to select one or more students as nonvoting members of its district's school board. They have every right of a board member except the vote, are elected by a simple majority of the student body under procedures set by student government, serve one-year terms, and are excluded from confidential matters under RSA 91-A. A student who will graduate during the term may not run or vote in that election.
