# English Learners, Family Support, Food and Safety at Pick-Up in Concord

Part of the Concord research (town/Concord). Gathered November 15, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers how the Concord School District (SAU 8) serves multilingual learners, reaches families who speak other languages, supports students experiencing homelessness and children in foster care, and handles school meals, summer and vacation food, custody papers and pick-up, volunteers and community donations. Almost everything comes from the district's own documents: Board policies 326, 462, 514, 515, 529, 580, 650, 754, 831 and 890; the 2026-27 school handbooks; the Student Services, Multi Language Learners, Homeless Students, Student Support Team, Family Support Resources, Family Center, Volunteer and Rundlett Care Closet pages; the FY2027 Student Services and Grants presentations, budget book and 2026 Board minutes; and the strategic plan updated January 2026. Outside sources: USDA's 2026 summer meal site data, federal meal and McKinney-Vento law, New Hampshire's Address Confidentiality Program (RSA 7:41–7:48), the Friends Program and End 68 Hours of Hunger.

**Counts:** 11 topics (8 shared district and town topics, 2 existing rule topics and 1 proposed rule topic), 77 facts and 2 number tables (homeless students by school and year, and student support staff by school).

**New data concepts:**
- Students experiencing homelessness by school and year, from the district (the site has no homeless counts).
- Summer meal sites with dates, days and meal times.
- Interpreting contractors and one school's yearly interpreting use.
- Grant-funded family support positions.

**Main findings:**
- Concord counted 122 students experiencing homelessness in February 2026, up from 75 in 2024-25; the elementary count doubled (38 to 78). Each school has a homeless liaison, and Policy 529 sets the appeal steps and deadlines.
- Each school has a home-to-school liaison or social worker who visits homes. No role called "cultural liaison" or "bilingual family coordinator" was found. Interpreting comes from contractors (Language Line, Upriver Associates, Ascentria, Pairaphrase). The district translates documents when a family asks for it on the Home Language Survey.
- Elementary English learner services mix pull-out times with in-class support. Concord High has MLL content and support courses, and students exit at an ACCESS score of 4.5 with no part below 4.0.
- Rundlett's Blue Duke Care Closet sends weekly food backpacks (also to one elementary school) and runs a winter gear drive. Christa McAuliffe's PTO runs a confidential "CMS Cares" fund. Three open summer meal sites ran in 2026 (Mill Brook, the Heights community center and Keach Park).
- Policy 515 presumes both parents have equal rights and honors a restriction on one parent only with a court order. Elementary pick-up requires a PowerSchool family contact or caregiver approval, plus ID.

**Main gaps:**
- Whether pull-out English times avoid science and social studies, and how English learners are served at Rundlett.
- Any written guarantee that report cards are translated or that interpreters are provided at conferences.
- Front-office training on custody orders.
- How quickly counseling starts for a child who arrives mid-year.
- A written foster care transportation procedure (no Concord foster care policy is posted).
- Any vacation-week meal program.
- Guidance on family-tree or baby-photo assignments.
- How to set up a second household to receive notices.
- Web search was unavailable to this agent (the session's limit was used up), so community programs that only news or search would find (coat drives, other backpack programs) were not looked for.

## homeless-student-school-stability: Homeless Students: Privacy and Children in Foster Care

- kind: rule
- question_type: rules
- description: Federal and New Hampshire rules for homeless children and youth: who counts as homeless, staying in the school of origin, immediate enrollment, and transportation when a family moves between districts

1. Under the federal McKinney-Vento Act, information about a homeless child's living situation is treated as part of the student's education record, protected by FERPA, and may not be treated as directory information.
   - citation: 42 U.S.C. 11432(g)(3)(G)
   - source: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title42/html/USCODE-2024-title42-chap119-subchapVI-partB-sec11432.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-15
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text: "(G) Privacy: Information about a homeless child's or youth's living situation shall be treated as a student education record, and shall not be deemed to be directory information, under section 1232g of title 20." Directory information is what a school may release without consent unless a parent opts out (library: nh-student-records-and-privacy 3). Raw copy: raw/support/law/usc-42-11432.txt
2. The Every Student Succeeds Act of 2015 removed children "awaiting foster care placement" from the federal McKinney-Vento definition of homeless children, effective one year after December 10, 2015 (two years in states whose law defined that phrase), so children in foster care are covered by the separate foster care school stability rules unless they otherwise meet the homeless definition.
   - citation: 42 U.S.C. 11434a(2)(B)(i), as amended by Pub. L. 114-95, section 9105(a)(1) and (b)
   - source: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title42/html/USCODE-2024-title42-chap119-subchapVI-partB-sec11434a.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-15
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Amendment note: "2015—Par. (2)(B)(i). Pub. L. 114–95, §9105(a)(1), inserted 'or' before 'are abandoned' and struck out 'or are awaiting foster care placement;' after 'hospitals;'." Effective date note: "In the case of a State that is not a covered State, the amendment ... shall take effect on the date that is 1 year after the date of enactment ... In the case of a covered State ... 2 years". Concord's Policy 529 definition does not list children awaiting foster care placement. The foster care rules are in foster-care-school-stability (town/Conway/daily-life.md). Raw copy: raw/support/law/usc-42-11434a.txt

## nh-student-records-and-privacy: New Hampshire's Address Confidentiality Program

- kind: rule
- question_type: rules
- description: NH's own student privacy rules: records access, directory information, tracking and surveillance limits, online services and record retention.

1. Under New Hampshire's Address Confidentiality Program, a parent or guardian may apply to the attorney general on behalf of a child, swearing to a good reason to believe the child or applicant is a victim of domestic violence, stalking or sexual assault and fears for their safety; the attorney general certifies the applicant for 4 years and gives a substitute address, with mail forwarded.
   - citation: RSA 7:43 and 7:45, III
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/I/7/7-mrg.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-15
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text: "I. An adult person, a parent or guardian acting on behalf of a minor ... may apply to the attorney general to have an address designated by the attorney general serve as the person's address or the address of the minor ... (a) A sworn statement by the applicant that the applicant has good reason to believe that the applicant, or the minor ... is a victim of domestic violence, stalking, or sexual assault; and that the applicant fears for his or her safety, or his or her children's safety"; "Applicants shall be certified for 4 years". RSA 7:42, I defines "address" as "a residential street address, school address, or work address". RSA 7:48: the attorney general refers applicants to crisis centers for help applying. Raw copy: raw/support/law/rsa-7-41-to-48-address-confidentiality.txt
2. A participant in New Hampshire's Address Confidentiality Program may ask state and local agencies to use the attorney general's substitute address, and when creating a new public record an agency must accept it unless the attorney general has found the agency has a real legal or administrative need for the actual address and will use it only for that purpose.
   - citation: RSA 7:45, I
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/I/7/7-mrg.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-15
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text: "A program participant may request that state and local agencies use the address designated by the attorney general as his or her address. When creating a new public record, state and local agencies shall accept the address designated by the attorney general as a program participant's substitute address, unless the attorney general had determined that: (a) The agency has a bona fide statutory or administrative requirement for the use of the address which would otherwise be confidential under this subdivision; and (b) This address will be used only for those statutory and administrative purposes." The statute does not name school districts; whether a district counts as a "local agency" for this purpose is not stated in RSA 7:41–7:48. ## summer-and-vacation-meal-rules: Summer and Vacation Meals: Federal Rules

- kind: rule
- question_type: rules
- description: Federal rules for free meals when school is out: who may eat at open summer meal sites, when summer meal sites may operate, and meals at afterschool programs on weekends and school-year vacations

1. Under federal Summer Food Service Program rules, an "open site" serves meals to all children in the area and is located where at least half the children qualify for free or reduced-price school meals; "children" means people 18 and under (and older students with disabilities in school programs for them).
   - citation: 7 CFR 225.2, definitions of "Open site" and "Children"
   - source: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-7/subtitle-B/chapter-II/subchapter-A/part-225/subpart-A/section-225.2
   - as_of: 2026-11-15
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text: "Open site means a site at which meals are made available to all children in the area and which is located in an area in which at least 50 percent of the children are from households that would be eligible for free or reduced price school meals". "Children means: (1) Persons 18 years of age and under; and (2) Persons over 18 years of age who are determined by a State educational agency or a local public educational agency of a State to be mentally or physically disabled and who participate in a public or nonprofit private school program established for the mentally or physically disabled." Fetched through the eCFR API. Raw copy: raw/support/law/ecfr-7-cfr-225-2.txt
2. Federal rules let Summer Food Service Program sponsors serve meals from May through September for children on school vacation, at any time of year under a year-round school calendar, and from October through April only during an unplanned school closure such as a natural disaster, major building repairs or a labor dispute, so they do not cover planned February or April vacation weeks on a traditional calendar.
   - citation: 7 CFR 225.6(i)(1); 7 CFR 225.2, definitions of "Continuous school calendar" and "Unanticipated school closure"
   - source: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-7/subtitle-B/chapter-II/subchapter-A/part-225/subpart-B/section-225.6
   - as_of: 2026-11-15
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text: "(1) Operate a nonprofit food service during the period specified, as follows: (i) From May through September for children on school vacation; (ii) At any time of the year, in the case of sponsors administering the Program under a continuous school calendar system; or (iii) During the period from October through April, if it serves an area affected by an unanticipated school closure due to a natural disaster, major building repairs, court orders relating to school safety or other issues, labor-management disputes, or, when approved by the State agency, a similar cause." A "continuous school calendar" means students are on vacation 15 or more school days in a row between October and April and in classes most of May to September. The last clause of the statement is our reading of these rules. Raw copies: raw/support/law/ecfr-7-cfr-225-6.txt; raw/support/law/ecfr-7-cfr-225-2.txt
3. Under the federal Child and Adult Care Food Program, an approved "at-risk" afterschool program may, with state approval, serve one meal a day (breakfast, lunch or supper) and snacks on weekends and vacations during the regular school year to participating children 18 and under, but not during summer vacation unless the program is in the attendance area of a year-round school.
   - citation: 7 CFR 226.17a(b)(1)(i), (c) and (k)(1)–(2)
   - source: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-7/subtitle-B/chapter-II/subchapter-A/part-226/subpart-E/section-226.17a
   - as_of: 2026-11-15
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text: "Be organized primarily to provide care for children after school or on weekends, holidays, or school vacations during the regular school year"; "With State agency approval, any one meal may be served (breakfast, lunch, or supper) per day on weekends and vacations during the regular school year. Afterschool meals may not be claimed during summer vacation, unless an at-risk afterschool care center is located in the attendance area of a school operating on a year-round calendar." Whether any Concord program serves these meals during vacation weeks was not found. Raw copy: raw/support/law/ecfr-7-cfr-226-17a.txt
