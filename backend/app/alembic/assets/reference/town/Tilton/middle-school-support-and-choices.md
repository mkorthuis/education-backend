# Winnisquam Regional Middle School, Student Support and School Choices

Part of the Tilton research (town/Tilton). Gathered November 21, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers Winnisquam Regional Middle School (WRMS, grades 5–8, 76 Winter Street, Tilton) and student support and school choices for Tilton families in the Winnisquam Regional School District (SAU 59, shared with Northfield and Sanbornton): how WRMS handles bullying and its social-emotional program, math at WRMS, sports, band and clubs, after-school rides, special education staffing (paraprofessionals and aides), the move from Southwick to WRMS with an IEP, 504 plans and SAT accommodations, help in other languages, Compass Classical Academy (the charter school in Northfield) and busing to it, class sizes at Southwick, Tilton School's aid, sports and college counseling, and homeschooled and EFA students' access to district classes and sports.

- **13 topics, 80 facts, no tables.** Eleven are shared district slugs with `place: district Winnisquam Regional`; `town-school-options-nearby` holds Compass (`district Compass Classical Academy Charter School`), Tilton School (`district Tilton School`) and the district's own choices; one topic, `college-board-testing-accommodations`, is a **proposed new shared topic** (no place).
- **Main sources:** the WRMS 2026-27 Student and Parent Handbook (with the school's 2025-2026 Title I schoolwide plan inside it) and the principal's August 21, 2026 welcome letter; the WRHS 2026-27 Comprehensive Guide (which also holds the district athletic handbook); policy JICK as revised September 21, 2026; the board minutes of August 17, September 21, October 19 and November 16, 2026 (the last still a draft) and the September, October and November 2026 board packets (Student Services reports, enrollment by classroom, the approved policies JJJ and EEA and the 2026–2028 Anti-Discrimination Plan); district staff and wellness pages; Tilton School's merit scholarship, aid FAQ, day student, college counseling and academics pages and its 2025-26 School Profile; RSA 194-B:2 and 194-B:9; NHPR's September 2023 article on Compass's building; and College Board's accommodations pages.
- **Main findings:**
  - **Bullying:** policy JICK was rewritten after HB 131 and approved September 21, 2026: parents of both students are told within 48 hours, an investigation starts within 5 school days and ends within 10 (extendable by 7), and the alleged victim gets a written copy of rights and supports. WRMS treats bullying as a "Major" behavior to report to administrators immediately, and runs RULER, a social-emotional program, for the 7th year. The 2026-27 handbook still prints the March 2026 version of JICK. Only district-wide bullying counts are public (site data).
  - **Math:** the site's data show WRMS at 49% proficient in 2026, between Belmont Middle (44%) and Gilford Middle (51%), with the highest growth of the three. WRMS uses enVision, has a math specialist, adds half a year of extended math in grades 6–8, and offers honors math in grades 6–7 and Algebra 1 for high school credit in grade 8.
  - **Late bus:** none was found. The handbook says parents provide the ride home after activities unless the student "is receiving school transportation," and after-school teacher help requires students to provide their own transportation.
  - **Aides:** the 2025-2026 Title I plan counts 15 special needs paraprofessionals at WRMS; the 2026-27 staff list names six. The district's paraprofessional staff (site data) fell from 47.0 to 42.0 FTE in 2026. WRMS said in October 2026 it struggles to find enough substitutes. No vacancy or 1:1 coverage figures are published.
  - **Compass busing:** Compass is in Northfield, inside the Winnisquam district, in the former Union Sanborn School. State law and policy EEA (approved November 16, 2026) give Tilton charter students rides on the district's existing routes and stops; families cover the gap between the stop and the school.
  - **Homeschool and EFA access:** policy JJJ, re-approved November 16, 2026 after HB 1817, gives resident homeschooled, EFA, charter and private school students the same access to WRMS and WRHS classes and activities as enrolled students, by written request to the principal, with the same eligibility (except attendance), tryouts and fees.
  - **SAT accommodations:** a 504 plan alone does not carry over to the SAT; College Board must approve, which can take up to seven weeks, and a one-to-one setting is given only at the student's school.
- **Main gaps:** Compass's own site (lottery dates, waitlist, class sizes, homework) could not be reached; no WRMS-level bullying counts or climate survey; no late bus; no paraprofessional vacancy or 1:1 coverage data; no written IEP transition procedure beyond the Title I plan's steps; no posted interpreter or translation procedure; Tilton School's aid to local day students isn't broken out and its tax filing couldn't be downloaded; the district's board policy manual (IHBG, JJA-R, IHBA, IKB) wasn't found online.
