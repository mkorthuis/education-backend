# Clark-Wilkins School and Young Families in Amherst

Part of the Amherst research (town/Amherst). Gathered November 21, 2026.

## Summary

| P1.2, P2.2 | "The Wilkins project moving ahead" and "5th grade moves back to Wilkins in 2028" | Out of date. ## school-emergency-operations-plan-rules: School Emergency Operations Plans and Drills: New Hampshire Rules

- kind: rule
- question_type: rules
- description: New Hampshire's rules for school emergency operations plans and drills: what every school's plan must cover, yearly review and filing with the Department of Safety, and the all-hazard and armed-assailant drills required among fire drills.

1. Under RSA 189:64, every New Hampshire public, chartered public and nonpublic school must have a site-specific emergency operations plan covering hazards from acts of violence and threats to fires, storms and medical emergencies, coordinate it with local emergency authorities, review it at least yearly, and file it (or report it unchanged) with the Department of Safety's homeland security and emergency management division by October 15.
   - citation: RSA 189:64, I, II(a) and V
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XV/189/189-64.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-21
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Statute text: "Every public, chartered public, and nonpublic school shall develop a site-specific school emergency operations plan ... and submit such plan to the director of homeland security and emergency management, department of safety by October 15 of each year"; "The plan shall address hazards including but not limited to acts of violence, biological incidents, civil unrest, cyber incidents ... medical emergencies, structural fire, threats ..."; "Each school shall review its plan at least annually ... If after review, the plan is unchanged, the school shall notify the department of safety by October 15." Proposed new shared topic. Raw: raw/elementary/rsa-189-64.txt

2. Under RSA 189:64, III, at least 4 of a New Hampshire school's required fire evacuation drills must be all-hazard response drills, at least one testing the response to an armed assailant (which may be a discussion-based drill); each school chooses the types and timing with local public safety, emergency management and public health officials and may include students and first responders.
   - citation: RSA 189:64, III
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XV/189/189-64.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-21
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Statute text: "The plan shall provide that at least 4 of the currently required number of fire evacuation drills shall be emergency, all-hazard response drills of which at least one shall test emergency response to an armed assailant. The armed assailant drill may be discussion based. The types of all-hazard drills and exercises and the manner and time in which these activities take place shall be determined by the school in collaboration with local public safety, emergency management, and public health officials." The statute doesn't set the number of fire drills itself; earlier town files quote district policies requiring one a month. HB 1503 (2026) on emergency maps is in the library (students-hb-1503-school-emergency-maps). Raw: raw/elementary/rsa-189-64.txt
