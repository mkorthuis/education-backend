# Souhegan High School: Grading, College Courses, Career Centers, Sports and the Senior Project

Part of the Amherst research (town/Amherst). Gathered November 21, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers Souhegan High School (SHS, grades 9–12), run by the Souhegan Cooperative School District for Amherst and Mont Vernon: its competency grading, Work Study Practices (WSPs), GPA and reassessment rules; the block schedule, advisory, Humanities, diploma requirements and the Senior Project; Honors Challenge, AP, dual enrollment and VLACS; counseling and college planning; graduates' plans, AP and SAT results; Souhegan's side of career and technical education (CTE) at the Nashua Technology Center, Milford's Applied Technology Center and Hudson's Palmer Center; athletic user fees, the hockey co-ops, team levels and concussion care; what a default budget has put "at risk"; the science lab project; and internships through Extended Learning Opportunities (ELOs). It adds a few facts on the Academy for Science and Design (ASD) for the honors STEM comparison, and general facts on how colleges read competency-based transcripts.

## district-curriculum-and-assessment: Souhegan's Competency Grading, Work Study Practices and Reassessment

1. At Souhegan High School, teachers score work on a 4-point scale (4 Extending, 3 Meeting, 2 Approaching, 1 Beginning) against each course's academic competencies; a competency score is the average of the summative assignments tied to it, the course score is a weighted average of the competency scores with weights set the same for all sections of a course, and formative work, given for feedback, is not counted.

## competency-transcripts-and-college-admissions: How Colleges Read Competency-Based Grades and Transcripts

- kind: how_it_works
- question_type: after_high_school
- description: What college admissions offices say about competency- or proficiency-based grading and transcripts, the school profile that explains them, and habits-of-work scores (proposed from the Amherst research)

1.    - citation: New England Board of Higher Education, "How Selective Colleges and Universities Evaluate Proficiency-Based High School Transcripts: Insights for Students and Schools," Policy Spotlight, April 2016
   - source: https://www.greatschoolspartnership.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Policy_Spotlight_How_Colleges_Evaluate_PB_HS_Transcripts_April_2016.pdf

The paper notes "Some states use 'proficiency-based' while some states use 'competency-based' to describe similar learning systems." The colleges are not named in the paper. NEBHE is a compact of the six New England states; the paper is hosted by the Great Schools Partnership, which promotes proficiency-based learning.
