# Student Support in Portsmouth: Special Education, Counseling, English Learners, Meals and Belonging

Part of the Portsmouth research (town/Portsmouth). Gathered November 16, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers support for Portsmouth School Department (SAU 52) students: how special education works in the district, its programs from preschool to the high school's transition years, staffing, out-of-district placements and what special education costs; counselors, social workers and social-emotional learning at Portsmouth Middle School; English learner services; how the schools reach families in Spanish; the bullying and cyberbullying policy; school meal prices, applications and the proposed city-funded free meal pilot; nondiscrimination, belonging and the district's response to the state's 2025 anti-DEI law; and peer and inclusion clubs at the middle and high schools.

- **Main findings:**
  - School meals are not free for everyone in Portsmouth: in 2026-27 lunch costs $3.50 to $4.25 unless a family qualifies by application or through SNAP or TANF. On November 10, 2026 the school board voted 8-0 to ask the City Council to fund a pilot from January 1, 2027 giving free meals to students from households up to 260% of the federal poverty level (about $85,800 a year for a family of four, derived).
  - Portsmouth Middle School has three school counselors (one per grade), one licensed clinical social worker and one school psychologist; with 487 students in 2024-25, that is about 162 students per counselor (derived), under the state limit of 300. The district hired a K–5 social worker in November 2026 with opioid abatement grant money; the general fund budgets only the one middle school social worker.
  - The district served 429 students with IEPs in October 2026. It is building in-district programs (ALPS for intensive needs at Dondero, RISE for emotional and behavioral needs at the middle school) and projects out-of-district placements falling from 23 to 19 and tuition from $2,649,876 to $1,933,056 in 2026-27. Its high school program for students 14 to 21 covers life skills, vocational training and community-based instruction, and the FY2027 budget pays for five students in outside transition programs.
  - Policy JICK (revised August 11, 2026) covers off-campus and online bullying that disrupts school, lists doxxing, catfishing and deepfakes as cyberbullying, and sets timelines: parents told within 48 hours, an investigation started within 5 school days and finished within 10. Some district pages still link the 2020 version.
  - On September 2, 2025 the superintendent told the board he would sign the state's anti-DEI certification, if no court blocked it, with a statement that the district was not in violation; the middle school's 2026-27 club list still includes a GSA and a Unified Club, and the high school catalog still states a goal of widening access to honors courses for historically underrepresented students.
