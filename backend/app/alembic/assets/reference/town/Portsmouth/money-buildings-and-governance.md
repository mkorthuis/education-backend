# Portsmouth: Money, Buildings and Governance

Part of the Portsmouth research (town/Portsmouth). Gathered November 16, 2026.

## Summary

**What's covered:** 7 topics (one proposed), 81 facts and 8 number tables on how Portsmouth's school budget is set inside the city budget, what drove the fiscal 2027 (2026-27) increase and what the preliminary FY2028 estimate shows, health insurance and pay, staffing cuts in FY2026 and FY2027, the tax rate and what a school cost increase means for a home, Question 2 and the city charter, state aid and where the schools' money comes from, the AREA tuition that Greenland, New Castle, Newington and Rye pay, the school buildings, the draft FY2028–FY2033 capital plan and school debt, community use of school buildings and fields, how the School Board works, and what the district reports about its own performance besides test scores.
- **The budget's growth.** The city's adopted school appropriation rose every year from $43,236,310 (FY2016) to $71,637,655 (FY2027). FY2027 was up $4,380,351 (6.51%), of which $2,751,313 was health insurance (SchoolCare's rate rose 26.2%). The School Department cut a net 7.56 positions in FY2027 and 5.11 in FY2026. The preliminary FY2028 estimate is $75.45 million (+5.23%), before SchoolCare's 2027-28 rates, which averaged a 16.7% medical increase in October's preliminary indications.
- **Taxes.** The adopted budget projects a tax year 2026 rate of $12.05 (up 54 cents); the state hadn't set it on November 16, 2026. A city finance memo found the local school tax levy grew less than Question 2's cap would have allowed in 9 of the last 10 years. Question 2 failed in Portsmouth, and the posted city charter has no tax cap, so no override vote is needed for FY2028–FY2029; the City Council sets a yearly limit by guidance (FY2027: a tax rate increase under 5%).
- **Where the money comes from.** In FY2027, 83.0% of the School Department's funds are local property taxes (including $10,689,236 of statewide education property tax raised in the city), 10.3% tuition, 1.9% state aid and 2.4% federal aid.
- **AREA tuition.** Tuition is built from the prior year's high school spending per pupil plus a building rental charge, under an agreement dating from 1968. The 2026-27 SAU 50 high school rate is $25,529 (non-area $26,486). Tuition is the city's second-largest General Fund revenue: $7,278,373 budgeted for FY2027.
- **Buildings.** The draft FY2028–FY2033 capital plan has $52.5 million of school projects (derived), led by New Franklin's interior renovation ($6.0 million bond in FY2028), the CTE Center renovation ($20.7 million, $12 million expected from the state, FY2031–FY2032) and $6 million of high school HVAC, roof and systems work in FY2032–FY2033. School debt was 5% of its legal limit on June 30, 2025.

**Main sources:** the School Department's FY27 budget presentation and narrative (December 19, 2025), the FY26 Budget & Performance Measures book, the City Manager's FY27 and FY26 proposed budgets, the FY27 adopted budget and the adopted budget memos for FY2016–FY2026, City Council budget slides (January 14, May 11 and May 28, 2026), the draft FY2028–FY2033 Capital Improvement Plan (November 12, 2026), the city charter, the city finance department's HB 1300 memo and slides (October 2026), School Board minutes (October 6, October 27, November 10, 2026) and Finance and Facilities Committee minutes, the superintendent's updates (October 1 and 23, 2026), the 2026-27 tuition rate memo (October 30, 2026), the Strategic Plan 2024–2029 updates, policies DEAA and KF and the facilities fee schedule, Rye School Board minutes (October 21 and 26, 2026), NHMA's 2025 tax cap article, and the site's database (Question 2, cost per pupil, DOE-25, adequacy aid).

## district-budget-and-cost: School Budget, Costs and Pay

- kind: local
- question_type: governance_money
- description: A district's budget and its trend, what drives increases, what its cost per pupil covers, salary schedules and contracts, and where to find budget documents

1. Portsmouth's school budget is set inside the city budget: the nine-member elected School Board develops and approves a proposed School Department budget after at least one public hearing and sends it to the City Manager, who combines it with the other departments; the City Council adopts the city budget, usually in June, for the fiscal year starting July 1, and the School Board then has line-item control over the money appropriated for schools.
