# High School Choice for Nottingham Students

Part of the Nottingham research (town/Nottingham). Gathered November 22, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers how Nottingham's choice between Coe-Brown Northwood Academy and Dover High School works from Nottingham's side: what each arrangement says about who may enroll, who pays and switching; where Nottingham's high schoolers actually go (by school and grade, 2023 to October 2026); the 8th-grade steps and dates; the three high school bus runs (one to Dover, two to Coe-Brown), how early they start, how late they get home and the lack of a late bus; the Coe-Brown agreement voters approved in 2026 and its special education, fee, vocational and transportation terms; the three school calendars; how Nottingham students placed and scored at Coe-Brown (Coe-Brown's November 2024 report to Nottingham); arts at both schools; Oyster River; and what the district does and doesn't pay for private, charter or other schools. Coe-Brown's and Dover High's own programs are in `town/Northwood/coe-brown-from-northwood.md`, `town/Barrington/high-school-choice.md`, `town/Deerfield/high-school-choice.md` and `town/Dover/`; this file cites them and adds Nottingham's side.

Scanned pages were read by OCR and checked against page images.

| Question | Premise | What the sources show |

| P2.1 | The town covers the whole tuition if a student picks Dover High for its career center | **Mostly correct, with a gap.** Nottingham pays Dover a per-student tuition rate (Dover estimated $22,720.93 for 2026-27, for about 91 students), the career center is part of Dover High, and Nottingham's budget form shows no separate vocational payment ($0). Career programs start in grade 10 or 11 and admission is by application. The Dover–Nottingham contract itself (State Board materials) refused the request, so its CTC wording wasn't read. Families still pay some fees, such as Dover's $150 sports fee. |
| P2.2 | The Dover bus picks up earlier than the Coe-Brown bus | **Correct.** The Dover run's first stop is at 5:56 a.m. (the Pawtuckaway State Park lot); the two Coe-Brown runs start at 6:25 and 6:32. At the same stops in the town center the Dover bus comes about 35 minutes earlier (for example 6:15 against 6:50 at the Community Center); on the Mill Pond Road side the times are within a few minutes. |
| P5.1 | 8th graders "lock in" a choice, and may switch | **No single deadline is posted.** Nottingham records commitments by January (30 to Coe-Brown, 17 to Dover by January 21, 2026); the Coe-Brown agreement requires Nottingham to send names by January 15; Coe-Brown's own registration materials are due January 8 and its enrollment form February 11. Coe-Brown's "lock-in night" (March 27, 2026) is an evening event for 8th graders, not a deadline. Once enrolled, a Coe-Brown student stays through graduation unless a change of school is approved under RSA 193:3; tuition stops when Nottingham sends a withdrawal notice. No Nottingham rule on switching was found. |
| P5.3 | Twins could attend different high schools | **Nothing found prevents it.** Coe-Brown takes "up to all" Nottingham students and Nottingham counts each student's school separately; no source requires siblings to attend the same school. Twins at different schools would ride different buses and follow different calendars. |
| P8.3 | Oyster River could be an option | **Not under Nottingham's arrangements.** Nottingham has agreements only with Coe-Brown and Dover. A family could seek Oyster River only as any non-resident can: with Oyster River's approval and tuition paid by the family, or through a superintendent-approved change of school, with the family providing transportation. |
| P10.2 | Nottingham's special education coordinator stays involved at Coe-Brown, or Coe-Brown takes over | **Both.** Coe-Brown handles identification, screening and case management; Nottingham's student services coordinator is the district representative invited to every IEP meeting at Coe-Brown and Dover; Nottingham pays all special education costs. |
| P13.2 | A late bus after sports | **None found.** Each high school run has one morning and one afternoon trip; Coe-Brown gives Nottingham students the same game and field-trip buses as its own students but not daily rides. |
| P14.3 | Nottingham's calendar differs, and spring breaks may not line up | **Calendars differ; vacations line up.** In 2026-27 all three schools share December 24–January 1, February 22–26 and April 26–30. Coe-Brown opens August 20 and closes about June 8, Dover runs September 1 to June 16 and Nottingham School September 3 to June 21, and teacher workshop days differ. Nottingham's teachers' contract sets its start (the Monday before Labor Day), and election days at the school are used as workshop days. |
| P16.1 | The Coe-Brown bus gets a student home much later than the middle school bus | **Not by the posted times.** Coe-Brown's afternoon runs reach their stops from 2:35 to 3:19 p.m., and Nottingham School's from 2:40 to 3:32 p.m. The Dover bus is the late one: 2:55 to 4:07 p.m. A given stop's time depends on its place on the route. |
| P16.2 | Nottingham might pay AP exam, dual enrollment or sports fees | **No source shows it.** The Coe-Brown agreement limits fees to what all Coe-Brown families pay; Coe-Brown posts no sports fee but lists AP exams (about $90–$100, with aid), SNHU courses ($100) and Early College ($150); Dover charges a $150 sports fee per sport (reduced or waived with meal benefits). |
| P20.2 | Tuition help for a private school or an out-of-district charter school | **None found for a family's own choice.** Nottingham pays tuition to Coe-Brown, Dover and, for students placed by their IEP teams, special schools. Charter schools charge no tuition (the state pays them). Nottingham's 2011 transportation policy offers a flat-rate transportation reimbursement for other schools on written request before June 30; the policy was under revision in fall 2026. |
