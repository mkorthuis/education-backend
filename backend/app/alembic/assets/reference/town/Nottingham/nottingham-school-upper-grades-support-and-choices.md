# Nottingham School's Upper Grades, Support and Choices

Part of the Nottingham research (town/Nottingham). Gathered November 22, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers Nottingham School's middle grades (5–8) and the support and choices around them: algebra placement and enrichment, hands-on technology classes and the absence of shop, band, chorus and the after-school world language classes, clubs and trips; social studies, civics and patriotic exercises; middle school sports in the Southeast League, PE and outdoor programs; Chromebooks, screens and the bell-to-bell phone policy; the school nurse and counselors; special education staff and therapists; the bullying policy and bus conduct; homeschooled and EFA students' access to school programs and the district's homeschool count; preparing eighth graders for high school; and EFAs in Nottingham.

**Sources.** Nottingham School's website (nottingham.k12.nh.us) and its linked Google Sites and Drive files could all be read. Current facts come from the **2026–27 Student Handbook** (PDF titled "Student Handbook updated 8/2026"; the board reviewed and approved handbook updates on August 19, 2026), the school's policy manual (Drive folders for sections E, I, J and K), the grade, guidance, student support, technology, music, PE, nurse and sports pages, the staff directory and school calendar (November 2026), the November 2026 Knightly News, school board minutes (July 2025 to October 2026) and the November 18, 2026 board packet (which holds the November 4 draft minutes and the 2026–27 monthly enrollment report). Federal staffing figures come from the 2023–24 Civil Rights Data Collection; EFA figures from the library's November 8, 2026 pulls of the Children's Scholarship Fund feeds; Southeast League game dates from Epsom Central's schedules saved by the Epsom research.

**Counts:** 10 topics (all shared slugs), 75 facts and 1 number table (homeschooled Nottingham children by grade, September and October 2026).

**New data concepts:** a district's own monthly count of resident homeschooled children by grade; the content of a middle school technology class as the local answer to "shop"; an algebra prognosis test for eighth grade placement; a district policy revised after HB 1817 (2026) to give EFA students program access; the posted bullying policy compared with HB 131's August 1, 2026 change; federal CRDC nurse, counselor and psychologist FTEs for a single K–8 school.

**Main findings:**
- **No shop or woodworking class was found.** The Unified Arts are art, health and PE, library, music and technology. The technology class is the hands-on one: LEGO SPIKE robots in grade 5, stop-motion sets in grade 6, sensor-driven robots in grade 7, and 3D design and printing in grade 8.
- **Advanced math is handled through eighth grade algebra.** Seventh graders take an algebra prognosis test in the spring, and parents are told in writing whether their child may take algebra in grade 8. No separate accelerated group for younger grades is described; a math specialist and an after-school Math Club exist.
- **One nurse and two counselors.** The 2023–24 federal data report 1.0 FTE nurse, 2.0 FTE counselors and 0.5 FTE psychologist at the school; the 2026 directory lists one registered nurse, a K–3 and a 4–8 counselor, and a school psychologist.
- **Therapists are listed in the building:** two speech-language pathologists, an occupational therapist and an OT assistant, a physical therapist, a BCBA and an ABA instructor. The draft 2027–28 budget calls physical therapy and the school psychologist "contracted." No source gives their days per week.
- **Homeschooled and EFA students may join courses, sports and band.** Policy JJJ, revised September 16, 2026, gives them the same access and eligibility rules as enrolled students. The district counted 27 resident homeschooled children (K–12) in October 2026.
- **Phones are banned bell to bell,** including lunch and recess (policy JICJ, September 3, 2025), with a four-step discipline rubric in the handbook. Grades 6–8 take Chromebooks home; grade 5's stay at school.
- **The bullying policy posted (JICK, June 18, 2025) requires a call within 48 hours** to the parents of both students, a 5-day start and 10-day finish to the investigation, and a phone call plus a letter with the results; it covers buses. The board moved a revised JICK to a second reading on September 2, 2026; the posted version still has the definition HB 131 removed on August 1, 2026.
- **No world language is taught in the school day.** The November 2026 calendar shows two after-school "World Language Class" groups on Tuesdays, without a sponsor or language named; in June 2026 a board committee mentioned using surplus federal funds for "possibly world language programming."

**Main gaps:** the sponsor, language and cost of the after-school world language classes; which accelerated or enrichment options exist below grade 8; the therapists' schedules; the meaning of "SHARP" on the after-school calendar; the full Southeast League schedule against Northwood, Deerfield and Strafford (the league's linked website is gone); Nottingham's gifted-and-talented report and special education LEA determination (NH DOE pages returned 403); any outdoor program at Pawtuckaway State Park; and how local Nottingham history is taught.
