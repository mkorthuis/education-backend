# Loudon: Money, Taxes and Governance in the Merrimack Valley School District (SAU 46)

Part of the Loudon research (town/Loudon). Gathered November 21, 2026.

## Summary

**What's covered:** 13 topics, 100 facts and 6 number tables on how the Merrimack Valley School District (SAU 46) is paid for and governed, written so Boscawen's research can reuse the district-wide facts.
- **The five-town split:** the 1965 Articles of Agreement (operating costs split by each town's resident students, capital costs by property value, state aid credited town by town, a K–5 school kept in each town, two board seats per town), the 2026-27 shares and assessments for every town, the district's estimated 2026 tax rates and its per-pupil cost to each town.
- **Loudon's school tax:** why it rises (the district assessment grew $2.45 million for 2026-27 while state aid estimates fell), Loudon's falling student share, the 2026 revaluation, the district's 2026 estimate ($16.55 local plus state rate, up $0.93), the Oak Hill Road article, the fund balance kept in September 2026 and the board's vote against the Question 2 cap.
- **Budget and votes:** the 2026-27 budget ($51,893,330, up 3.38%) and how voters passed it, the 2025 floor cut to $49,747,050 and the 19 positions it removed (three LES teachers and two half-time aides), the 2023-24 overspending, the teachers' and support staff contracts, health insurance shares and cost-per-pupil comparisons.
- **Special education and buses:** out-of-district tuition and transport (about $3.08 million, 5.9% of the 2026-27 budget, **derived**), the district's aid-shortfall analysis, the in-house bus fleet, the April 2026 vote against contracting with First Student, new buses and the November 2026 driver vacancies.
- **ESSER:** the ARP ESSER plan ($3,383,306.80 awarded; first-year spending on middle school windows and tutoring); no source ties ESSER money to staff positions.
- **Buildings:** no bond payments in 2026-27, the $18.7 million 2025–2034 capital plan (LES $2.7 million), the trust balances, the high school roof, the Learning Center renovation, campus sidewalks and paving, and student parking.
- **Governance and input:** board meetings and public comment rules, the budget hearing, SB2 hearings in each town (Loudon's at LES), the annual meeting at MVHS, the 2026 SB2 vote (defeated 158–310), the Salisbury–Webster merger petition and the 2021 study.
- **Families and community:** curriculum, health and library material policies (IGE, IHAM, KEC, IJL, AB), ParentSquare, volunteer and partnership policies, Loudon groups that support LES, Loudon's elderly exemption and the 2026 warrant's proposed amounts, and Speedway race weekends.

**Main sources:** mvsdpride.org and its Google Drive folders (2025 and 2026 annual reports, warrant and minutes, Articles of Agreement, 2026-27 budget series: overview, line-by-line budget, revenues, tax rate slides, capital plan, special education slides, SAU efficiency summary, FAQ, teacher contract slides; board minutes January–October 2026 and fall 2025; policies; the 2021 Salisbury–Webster study; the ARP ESSER plan; the vacancy list), the Town of Loudon (FY2025 annual report with the 2026 warrant, assessing and tax pages, selectmen's minutes), New Hampshire Motor Speedway's pages, and the site's database. News (Concord Monitor, NHPR) is cited only where noted.
