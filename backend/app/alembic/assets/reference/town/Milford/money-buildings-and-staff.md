# Milford: Money, Governance, Buildings and Staff

Part of the Milford research (town/Milford). Gathered November 16, 2026.

## Summary

**What's covered:** 12 topics (11 local, 1 statewide rule), 80 facts and 7 number tables on how Milford's 2026-27 (FY2027) school budget was built and voted, the default budget, taxes and the coming revaluation, Question 2 in Milford, the SAU's central office costs, the union contracts and teacher pay, staffing changes, the school buildings, bonds and reserves, the failed CTE renovation bonds, the Applied Technology Center's sending districts and what they pay, enrollment history and projections, the School Board and Budget Advisory Committee, and where residents can find results.

- **The FY2027 budget:** the 5% target the School Board and Budget Advisory Committee set together, the 35% SchoolCare health insurance increase and $1 million special assessment, 9.85 full-time positions cut, the deliberative session's added $125,000 for a middle school social worker, and the March 10, 2026 vote ($52,722,151, passed 1,880 to 872). The FY2028 goal is at most a 3.4% increase.
- **Default budgets:** FY2024–FY2027 proposed and default budgets side by side. The default was higher in FY2026 and FY2027 and lower in FY2024 and FY2025; the FY2025 operating budget failed and the default took effect.
- **Taxes:** ballot tax impacts per $100,000, the $1,000,000 of FY2026 fund balance returned to lower the FY2027 rate, the town's 2026 revaluation, and a **derived** comparison showing that on full (equalized) value Milford's local school rate is close to Amherst's.
- **Buildings and bonds:** three bonds (debt service falls after FY2028), the 2025 MHS roof bond, five failed renovation bonds since 2023 (Bales twice, the CTE center three times; the 2026 CTE bond missed by 27 votes, confirmed by recount), reserve and trust balances, the energy audit, safety grants, and the plan to buy the Kenmore Stamp building and sell Bales.
- **ATC money:** 51 students from six sending schools in 2025-26, $76,224 of CTE tuition received in FY2025, and the state rules: sending districts pay 25% of the CTE share of cost per pupil, a receiving district may add a 3% capital fee, and the state funds 50–75% of CTE center construction.
- **Staff and pay:** the teachers' contract (2024–2028), the support staff and custodial contracts, the district's inflation-adjusted pay and cost trends, HR's report of nine departures from September to December 2025, and leadership changes in 2026.

**Main sources:** the FY27 Proposed Budget Book (December 15, 2025), the FY27 warrant, MS-26 and default budget (DRA forms, January 23, 2026), the FY25 warrant, the Mandatory Report to Voters, NESDEC's 2025-26 enrollment projections, School Board minutes (November 2025 to October 2026), the deliberative session minutes (February 5, 2026), the district's HB 1300 presentation, the teachers' contract, the district's contracts, budget process, Budget Advisory Committee, assessment and board meeting pages, the Town of Milford's assessing, voter and Budget Advisory Committee pages, RSA 188-E:6, :7 and :10, and the site's database (cost per pupil, scores, salaries, tax rates, school taxes, Question 2, DOE-25). The March 2026 ballot results come from a selectman's blog because the district's results page has been removed; the Union Leader and School Board minutes confirm the CTE outcome.

| P2.1 | The school budget is "over $52 million" | Correct. Voters approved a $52,722,151 operating budget for 2026-27 plus $105,833 for the custodial contract. The tax year 2026 rate isn't set yet; the ballot estimated $135.84 more per $100,000 of assessed value for the budget as posted (before the $125,000 amendment) and $5.07 for the contract, and the town is revaluing all property in 2026, which changes every assessment. |
| P2.2 | "That 6% tax cap question" in November 2026 | Partly right. Question 2 had two parts: a cap on growth of the school property tax levy (inflation plus new construction) and a 6% cap on SAU central office spending. Milford voted 58.9% yes, short of the 60% needed, so neither cap applies to the FY2028 or FY2029 budgets. The district's business administrator put Milford's central office at about 4–5% of the budget. |
| P2.3 | "Over $22,000 a kid" and "only about a third" proficient in math | Both correct. Cost per pupil was $22,084 in FY2025 (state $22,699). Math proficiency was 32% in 2025 and 35% in 2026 (site data; the district reported 32% for 2025). The data can't say why. |
| P4.2 | "Did that CTE renovation bond pass?" | No. The $14,850,000 project ($3,850,000 bond, $11,000,000 state) got 59.0% on March 10, 2026, 27 votes short of 60%; a recount confirmed it. Larger versions failed in 2024 (41.2%) and 2025 (57.9%). No new shop schedule exists (agent 2 covers timing). |
| P7.2 | Milford's school rate is "so much higher per $1,000" | Higher on assessed value ($16.98 local school in 2025) but Milford's assessments were only 62.75% of market value. On full value its local school rate was $10.56, close to Amherst's $10.67 and above Hollis ($9.77) and Bedford ($9.00) (**derived**, site data). |
| P8.1 | The default budget is "sometimes higher" than the proposed budget | Correct for FY2026 ($385,510 higher) and FY2027 ($399,780 higher than posted). In FY2024 and FY2025 the default was lower. By law the default carries last year's budget plus contract and other required increases (in FY2027, the 35% health insurance rise), while the proposed budget held to the 5% target with cuts. |
| P8.2 | Enrollment growing or shrinking | Shrinking. District enrollment fell from 2,339 (2017-18) to 1,904 (2025-26); NESDEC projects 1,680 by 2035-36. |
| P8.3 | Sending districts paying their "fair share for the building" | State law sets what sending districts pay: 25% of the career and technical portion of Milford's cost per pupil, plus an optional fee of up to 3% for a CTE capital reserve. The state pays 50–75% of CTE center construction; the 2026 plan had $11,000,000 from the state and $3,850,000 from Milford, with no sending-district share. Whether Milford charges the 3% fee wasn't found. |
| P12.2 | Losing teachers to Amherst and Bedford because of pay | Pay gap is real (2025-26 averages: Milford $73,578, Amherst $81,526, Bedford $78,194; site data), but no source tracks where departing teachers go. HR's September–December 2025 report counted nine departures (six support staff, two other staff, one retiree), some for "more money." The teachers' contract runs to June 30, 2028; support staff talks are next. |
| P15.1 | The School Board and Budget Advisory Committee "can't ever agree" | Not borne out for FY2027: they set the 5% target together and the committee recommended every article (6–1 on the budget). In FY2025 both recommended the budget on split votes (board 3–2, committee 4–2). The committee is advisory, appointed by the School District Moderator. |
| P15.2 | "Another huge roof or HVAC bond in two years" | No such bond is planned in the board's 2026-27 goals; the March 2027 items named are buying the Kenmore Stamp building, selling Bales and a second SRO. The MHS roof bond ($1.5 million, 2025) is paid through FY2031; MHS got ten new rooftop units. A 10-year capital plan and an energy audit's priority list are being worked on. |
| P20.2 | School board "turnover" | Two of five seats changed in 2026: the chair was not on the March ballot and a new member was elected, and a member resigned in July (replaced by appointment until March 2027). Two principals became interim in other buildings after a resignation. Morale isn't measured by any source found. |
