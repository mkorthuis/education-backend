# Newton: Money, Taxes and Governance

Part of the Newton research (town/Newton). Agent 4. Gathered 2026-11-21.

## Summary

**What's covered.** Newton's side of the Sanborn Regional School District's money and governance, building on the Kingston research (`town/Kingston/money-and-governance.md`), which already covers the district's budget, default budget, tax cap briefing, buildings, bond and board calendar. This file adds: why Newton's school taxes rose (its dollars, its share of the two towns' taxes and students, its falling adequacy grant, and the five-year revaluation cycle that pushed the assessed rate up and then down in 2025); the school and town parts of Newton's tax bill as the town presents them; where and when Newton votes on school articles; the articles of agreement (1965, amended 1972, text not posted); Newton's share of the district's October 2026 students; Memorial and Bakie enrollment and class sizes, and the 2020–2022 change from two K–5 schools to grade-span schools; the district's new bidding policy (DJE, October 2026), its local purchasing rule and where bids are posted; where agendas, minutes and meeting videos are; budget drivers raised in the Budget Committee's September 2026 questions; and the district's own comparison with Timberlane. 10 topics (9 shared and 1 proposed), 32 facts and 1 number table.

**Main sources.** The Town of Newton's website (voter page, 2024 and 2025 town reports, tax rate pages, 2026 Voter's Guide, 2025 and 2026 election results, public access schedule); the Sanborn district's Google Drive policy files (AA, AA-E, DJE, DJ, DJD, BEDJ, DBC), its Drive folders of agendas and minutes, its Budget Committee minutes of September 24, 2026, its October 1, 2026 enrollment sheet and its YouTube playlists; Sanborn documents saved by the Kingston research (FY2028 budget calendar and presentation, HB 1300 briefing, facilities study RFP); and the site's tax, aid and enrollment data for derived shares.

**Main gaps.** Sanborn's cost-sharing formula (the articles of agreement are posted only as a cover page; NH DRA's apportionment file refused automated requests); the Bids/RFP page itself (sau17.net shows a bot check); any district plowing or grounds contract; a building capacity figure for Memorial or Bakie (the 2027 facilities study will produce one); Newton's 2026 tax rate (not posted by November 21, 2026); the November 12, 2026 joint meeting minutes and the November 24 budget answers (not yet posted); Newton home sale prices from a primary source.

**Topic proposal:** `district-purchasing-and-bidding` (local, governance_money): "How a school district buys goods and services: bidding thresholds, local purchasing preferences, where invitations for bids and requests for proposals are posted, and how vendors can hear about them." No shared topic covers district purchasing; P14.2 asks how local businesses hear about district contracts.

**Questions answered wholly by earlier files:** 0 of 12. P11.3, P19.1 and P19.3 are mostly answered by the Kingston file and the library; this file adds Newton's own figures and the district's document and video locations.

| P11.1, P19.2 | Newton's share of the Sanborn school tax "keeps going up" while enrollment isn't booming | Mixed. Newton's school tax in dollars rose every year from 2021 to 2025 (31.7%), but its share of the two towns' local school tax has moved between 43.5% and 47.8% since 2011 and fell from 47.1% (2024) to 45.5% (2025). Newton's assessed local school rate rose from $14.82 (2021) to $19.18 (2024) largely because assessments fell behind market value between revaluations (the equalization ratio dropped from 97.3% to 62.2%); on full value it stayed between $11.13 and $12.03, and after the 2025 revaluation the assessed rate was $12.22. Newton's resident students fell only 5.0% (2021-22 to 2025-26) while Kingston's fell 11.7%, so Newton's share of the students rose to 47.9%, and Newton's adequacy grant fell by $491,054 from FY2023 to FY2027 (all derived from site data). |
| P11.3 | A "new state tax cap rule" | Correct that it is new, but it is a local-option cap that state law put on the ballot, not a cap the state imposed on every district. Kingston and Newton voters adopted it together on November 3, 2026 (Newton 1,510 to 565; 71.5% combined, site data). It limits growth in Sanborn's district-wide property tax levy, first for the 2027-28 budget voted March 9, 2027; a 60% vote can override it; bond payments are outside it. |

But the school budget is not taken up at Newton's Town Meeting: it has its own deliberative session in Kingston, and Newton's Voter's Guide covers only town articles. |
| P12.2 | The district is "paying for empty classrooms at Memorial or Bakie" | Not shown by any source. No capacity or empty-room count is published; the 2027 facilities study will calculate capacity. Memorial has 236 students in grades 3–5 with 13 classroom teachers (about 17 to 20 per class, derived), and enrollment there fell 14.5% since 2022-23. Part of the Newton campus (the former middle school addition) is already leased to two other schools. |
| P14.2 | The district puts out "big maintenance or plowing contracts" | No plowing or grounds contract was found. Under policy DJE (October 2026) purchases of $50,000 or more need a formal invitation for bid or request for proposals and School Board approval. Regular busing is contracted (Coach Co.). |
| P18.3 | Timberlane is "right next door in Plaistow" | Timberlane Regional High School is in Plaistow, but the district serves Atkinson, Danville, Plaistow and Sandown (town/timberlane/README.md). |
| P19.3 | "This year's default budget" has big increases over the proposed budget | Out of date for 2027-28. The November 2026 preliminary default ($41,994,754) is only $23,456 above the continuing-service proposal; both carry the 19% health insurance increase (about $1.3 million) and a 10.5% rise in the SAU portion. For 2026-27 the default was $1,464,541 higher than the budget voters passed, because the proposal cut about $1.7 million in positions. The Budget Committee votes final figures December 3–10, 2026. |
