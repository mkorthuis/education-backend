# Special Education, Foster Care, Counseling, Bullying and the School Calendar for Newton (Sanborn Regional)

Part of the Newton research (town/Newton). Gathered November 21, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers the Newton residents' questions on speech and occupational therapy at Memorial School, asking for a special education evaluation, an IEP moving to Sanborn Regional Middle School, home-educated and EFA students' access to Sanborn programs, returning to public school, enrolling mid-year after a move from Massachusetts, Sanborn's calendar against a Massachusetts district's, children in foster care, counseling and behavior support at Memorial, harassment on the bus or online, counselors and social workers at Memorial, and how families can tell a report is being followed up. Much of Sanborn's side was already gathered in the Kingston research (`town/Kingston/`), and this file cites it rather than repeating it.

- **7 topics, 41 facts, no number tables.** All topics use shared slugs (`town/shared-topics.md`) or an existing library topic (`community-mental-health-centers`). Raw copies are in `raw/support/` (about 6 MB): board policies in `raw/support/policies/`, calendars and the New Hampshire and Massachusetts school-year rules in `raw/support/calendar/`, special education documents in `raw/support/sped/`, foster care guidance in `raw/support/foster/`, Memorial School's handbook, staff list and one live-feed post in `raw/support/memorial/`, Budget Committee minutes in `raw/support/minutes/`, and the Center for Life Management's pages in `raw/support/clm/`.
- **Places used:** `district Sanborn Regional` for Memorial and district-wide facts, `town Newton` for the community mental health center, and no place for federal, New Hampshire and Massachusetts rules.
- **Main findings:**
  - **Foster care:** Sanborn's policy JFABE (adopted January 22, 2020) requires a Foster Care Point of Contact and an individual transportation plan for each child who stays in the school of origin. No Sanborn page, handbook or contact sheet names that person, and SAU 17 is missing from NH DOE's 2024-25 list of district foster care contacts. Memorial is the district's only school for grades 3–5, so a new foster home anywhere in Kingston or Newton doesn't change the school (derived). Under federal guidance the school-of-origin right lasts as long as the child is in foster care, not just to the end of the year.
  - **Counselors at Memorial:** Memorial lists one guidance counselor, who is listed only there. The district's one social worker and its school psychologist for preschool to grade 5 are listed at both Bakie and Memorial or on the district sheet, so they are shared. A Budget Committee handout put Memorial at 1 counselor for 233 students in 2025-26, under New Hampshire's limit of 1 per 500. Federal data for 2023-24 had shown 2.0 counselors at Memorial.
  - **Mental health care outside school:** Newton is in the Center for Life Management's region (Derry). Kingston, the other Sanborn town, is in Seacoast Mental Health Center's.
  - **Calendar:** Sanborn has both a February break (February 22–26, 2027) and an April break (April 26–30). Each comes one week after Haverhill, Massachusetts' breaks (February 15–19 and April 19–23), and Sanborn's weeks match those of the Exeter district, home of the Seacoast School of Technology. Sanborn's year runs August 31 to June 11, with 177 or 176 scheduled student days (derived). New Hampshire allows fewer than 180 days if the required hours are met, while Massachusetts requires 185 days scheduled and 180 held.
  - **Bus and online harassment:** Bus riders are under the school's authority from boarding to exiting, with a set ladder of consequences. Bus video and audio is kept no more than 10 school days unless needed for discipline. The bullying report form covers the bus, the trip to and from school and online conduct outside the school day. Reports involving students from another district, such as the charter school students who ride Memorial's buses, go to the principal of the victim's school.
  - **Follow-up on a report:** a bullying notice to parents must include the steps taken to keep the child safe. A spoken notice of remedies is confirmed by letter within 2 school days, and the investigation form logs each parent contact. The district won't share another student's discipline. JICK sends appeals to policy ACA, which isn't in the district's posted policy listing.
  - **Evaluations:** the district plan gives 15 business days to respond to a referral, 30 calendar days from eligibility to the first IEP meeting, and 10 days' notice of IEP meetings. Policy IHBC limits outside evaluators to New Hampshire and its neighboring states, including Massachusetts.
- **Main gaps:** how often Memorial students are pulled out for speech or OT (it is set in each IEP, and no source gives typical schedules); any public measure of how quickly Sanborn completes evaluations (NH DOE's Indicator 11 and monitoring pages refused automated requests, HTTP 403); whether Memorial's counselor is full-time (no source gives the FTE); the name of the Foster Care Point of Contact and the homeless liaison; policy ACA's text.

## foster-care-school-stability: School Stability for Children in Foster Care

- kind: rule
- question_type: rules
- description: Federal and New Hampshire rules for keeping children in foster care in their school of origin, best-interest decisions, immediate enrollment, records and transportation

1. Federal guidance defines a foster child's "school of origin" as the public school the child is enrolled in when entering foster care or when the child's foster placement changes, and says a child who has never been enrolled in a public school has no school of origin and is enrolled at once in a new school.
   - citation: US Department of Education and HHS, "Non-Regulatory Guidance: Ensuring Educational Stability and Success for Students in Foster Care" (November 2024), questions D-1 and D-2
   - source: https://www.education.nh.gov/sites/g/files/ehbemt326/files/inline-documents/sonh/ed-hhs-foster-care-non-regulatory-guidance_2024-11-15.pdf
   - as_of: 2026-11-21
   - review_by: 2027-11-21
   - note: Text: "The school of origin is the public school a student is enrolled in when they enter foster care or when they change foster care placements. (See ESEA section 1111(g)(1)(E))"; "A child in foster care who has never been enrolled in a public school does not have a school of origin and should be immediately enrolled in a new school." NH DOE's September 2024 guidance defines it as "the school that a child or youth attended when they were placed in foster care" (raw/support/foster/nhdoe-foster-care-guidance.txt). Non-regulatory guidance, not law. Both copies were saved by the Conway research from the Wayback Machine, since NH DOE refuses automated requests (PDFs in town/Conway/raw/daily/foster/). Raw: raw/support/foster/ed-hhs-foster-care-guidance-2024-11-15.txt
2. The same federal guidance says a student in foster care must stay in the school of origin for as long as the student is in foster care, not only to the end of a school year, unless a best-interest decision finds that staying is not in the student's interest.
   - citation: ED–HHS Non-Regulatory Guidance (November 2024), question D-3; ESEA sections 1111(g)(1)(E)(i) and 1112(c)(5)
   - source: https://www.education.nh.gov/sites/g/files/ehbemt326/files/inline-documents/sonh/ed-hhs-foster-care-non-regulatory-guidance_2024-11-15.pdf
   - as_of: 2026-11-21
   - review_by: 2027-11-21
   - note: Text: "D-3. For what length of time does the school of origin provision apply? A student in foster care must remain in their school of origin for the duration of their time in foster care unless a determination is made that it is not in their best interest to remain in the school of origin." The law's transportation duty also runs "for the duration of the time in foster care" (Conway's daily-life.md, foster-care-school-stability 3). Raw: raw/support/foster/ed-hhs-foster-care-guidance-2024-11-15.txt
3. Under the federal guidance, the school-stability rules stop applying when a student leaves foster care, but states and districts are encouraged to let a student who leaves foster care during the year stay at the school of origin through at least the end of that school year, and to keep providing transportation there through the end of that year and longer where feasible.
   - citation: ED–HHS Non-Regulatory Guidance (November 2024), question D-4 and the transportation question under ESEA section 1112(c)(5)
   - source: https://www.education.nh.gov/sites/g/files/ehbemt326/files/inline-documents/sonh/ed-hhs-foster-care-non-regulatory-guidance_2024-11-15.pdf
   - as_of: 2026-11-21
   - review_by: 2027-11-21
   - note: Text: "While the Title I educational stability provisions do not apply when a student exits foster care, SEAs and LEAs are encouraged to prioritize educational stability for these students ... consider adopting policies that allow a student exiting foster care during the school year to remain enrolled at their school of origin through at least the end of the school year"; "the LEA is encouraged to provide transportation to the school of origin through the end of the school year in which the student exits foster care, and the LEA is encouraged to provide transportation to the school of origin past the end of the school year ... to the extent that it is feasible to do so." Raw: raw/support/foster/ed-hhs-foster-care-guidance-2024-11-15.txt
4. Under the federal guidance, when a student in foster care finishes the last grade of the school of origin, the next school in the district's feeder pattern becomes the school of origin, so a child who completes elementary school moves to the middle school the district assigns.
   - citation: ED–HHS Non-Regulatory Guidance (November 2024), question D-5
   - source: https://www.education.nh.gov/sites/g/files/ehbemt326/files/inline-documents/sonh/ed-hhs-foster-care-non-regulatory-guidance_2024-11-15.pdf
   - as_of: 2026-11-21
   - review_by: 2027-11-21
   - note: Text: "When a student completes the final grade level serviced by the school of origin, the next school in the feeder school pattern becomes the school of origin. For example, when a student in foster care completes the final grade at an elementary school, their school of origin becomes the middle school the student is assigned to in the LEA's feeder school pattern." In Sanborn, that is Memorial (grade 5) to Sanborn Regional Middle School (grade 6). Raw: raw/support/foster/ed-hhs-foster-care-guidance-2024-11-15.txt

## school-calendar-rules: New Hampshire's School-Year Length, and Massachusetts' for Comparison

- kind: rule
- question_type: rules
- description: New Hampshire rules that shape school calendars, such as aligning sending districts' calendars with regional career and technical education centers

1. New Hampshire law requires every school board to provide school for at least 180 days a year, or the equivalent number of hours set in the Department of Education's rules.
   - citation: RSA 189:1
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XV/189/189-1.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-21
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text: "The school board of every district shall provide standard schools for at least 180 days in each year, or the equivalent number of hours as required in the rules of the department of education." Raw: raw/support/calendar/rsa-189-1.html and .txt
2. New Hampshire's school approval rules let a school's calendar provide either 180 days of instruction or the required hours, which "may result in fewer than 180 days": at least 450 hours in kindergarten, 945 in grades 1–6 and 990 in grades 7–12, with a student's instructional day capped at 5.75 hours in elementary school and 6 hours in middle and high school, and with remote learning days and elementary recess allowed to count.
   - citation: N.H. Code Admin. Rules Ed 306.15(a)–(c), (f), (h) (eff. 12-13-24)
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rules/state_agencies/ed300.html
   - as_of: 2026-11-21
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text: "(a) Each school shall maintain a school calendar which provides for 180 days of instruction or the required number of instructional hours, which may result in fewer than 180 days"; "(1) At least 450 hours of instructional time in kindergarten; (2) At least 945 hours of instructional time in elementary school grades 1-6; and (3) At least 990 hours of instructional time in each year grades 7-12"; "(c) The instructional school day of an individual student shall not exceed 5.75 hours of instructional time in elementary schools and 6 hours of instructional time in middle and high schools"; "(f) Remote learning conducted in accordance with Ed 306.18(c) shall count"; "(h) ... Elementary schools may count recess as instructional time for pupils in kindergarten through grade 6." Lost time is rescheduled before a waiver is sought (d). Remote learning day rules: Rochester's getting-to-school-and-choices.md, remote-learning-and-snow-day-rules. Raw: raw/support/calendar/ed-306-15-excerpt.txt; raw/support/calendar/ed300.txt
3. For comparison, Massachusetts' student learning time rules require each school committee to schedule at least 185 school days and to hold at least 180, with at least 900 hours of structured learning time a year for elementary students and 990 for secondary students, and neither state's school-year rule sets vacation weeks, which each district's school board or school committee chooses.
   - citation: 603 CMR 27.03(1)–(3) and 27.04(1)–(2) (Massachusetts Board of Elementary and Secondary Education; most recently amended May 25, 2021)
   - source: https://www.doe.mass.edu/lawsregs/603cmr27.html?section=all
   - as_of: 2026-11-21
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text: "(2) Every school committee shall schedule a school year which includes at least 185 school days"; "(3) Every school committee shall operate the schools within its district at least 180 school days in a school year"; "every elementary school student is scheduled to receive a minimum of 900 hours per school year of structured learning time"; "every secondary school student is scheduled to receive a minimum of 990 hours"; "(1) Prior to the beginning of each school year, every school committee shall establish school year schedules for each of the public schools under its supervision." Recess, lunch and homeroom do not count in Massachusetts (27.04). **Derived:** neither 603 CMR 27.00 nor Ed 306.15 mentions vacations; Sanborn's board sets its calendar under policy IC (district-school-day-and-calendar 3).
