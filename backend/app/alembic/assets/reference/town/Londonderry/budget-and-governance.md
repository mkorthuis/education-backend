# Londonderry: Budget, Taxes and Governance

Part of the Londonderry research (town/Londonderry). Gathered November 15, 2026.

## Summary

**What's covered:** 7 topics (one proposed), 75 facts and 6 number tables on how the Londonderry School District's budget is built, voted and paid for, and what the November 2026 tax cap vote changed.
- **The 2026-27 (FY2027) budget.** The vote (the School Board's $94,960,409 over the $95,234,013 default, 3,628 to 804), the $208,000 cut when full-day kindergarten passed, the $95,885,819 total with special articles, what drove the increase (health insurance +$2.4 million, contracts), a plain-English breakdown by function, the 17.9 staff positions cut over three budgets, and what the superintendent said would shrink as enrollment falls.
- **Taxes.** The district's own estimated rates for the proposed and default budgets ($9.73 against $9.76 per $1,000), the adopted levy ($73,811,898, an estimated $9.81 local school rate against $9.28 in 2025), why less surplus is going to lower the 2026 rate, the school share of the bill (71.6% in 2025, from the town's own table), comparisons with Derry and Hudson, the 2025 revaluation, the elderly exemption and when the December bill comes.
- **The tax cap.** Question 2's 51.9% in Londonderry (short of 60%, so no state cap for 2027-28 or 2028-29), the district's estimate that a cap would have meant about $2 million in cuts and its list of what it would have cut, the 2025 local tax cap petition that the deliberative session turned into a study, and the superintendent's November 10, 2026 memo on the vote's "long shadow."
- **The default budget.** Londonderry is a charter district, not an SB2 district: voters pick option A (the board's budget) or B (the default) on one ballot question. The default won once in eight years (March 2020).
- **Governance.** The charter, the Friday-evening deliberative session (115 voters in 2025) and the Saturday town session, turnout (25.5% in March 2026), the 2027-28 budget calendar (presentation November 24, workshops December 3 to January 7), the Budget Committee, and a January 2026 hearing where public comment restored a high school library assistant.
- **Hooksett.** The 2023–2028 memorandum (up to 40 students a grade at Pinkerton's rate), what Londonderry charges and collects, and how the tuition compares with the high school's cost per pupil.
- **State aid, bonds and escrow.** Adequacy aid since FY2013, Londonderry's 2006 Supreme Court case, the district's small remaining debt, how a past bond proposal would have reached the tax rate, and the federal rules on how mortgage escrow payments adjust.

**Main sources:** the district's business office pages and Google Drive budget folders (FY2027 Superintendent's Budget Book, January 15, 2026 budget hearing, warrant and deliberative session minutes for 2025 and 2026, election results 2019–2026, the 2025 tax cap study, the Mandatory Report to Voters), School Board minutes and packets (November 2025 to November 10, 2026), the district charter and budget FAQ, the Hooksett tuition page and the signed Hooksett–Londonderry memorandum; the Town of Londonderry's assessing, budget, Budget Committee and FAQ pages and its November 7, 2026 budget presentation; the NH Supreme Court's 2006 *Londonderry* opinion (NCES copy); 12 CFR 1024.17 (eCFR); and the site's database.

## property-tax-bills-and-escrow: Property Tax Bills, Rate Setting and Mortgage Escrow

- kind: how_it_works
- question_type: governance_money
- description: How and when property tax rates are set and bills issued, and how mortgage escrow payments adjust when taxes change

1. Under federal mortgage rules (Regulation X), a loan servicer that keeps an escrow account for property taxes must analyze the account at the end of each yearly escrow period to set the next year's monthly payment, which may be one-twelfth of the taxes and other bills it expects to pay plus a cushion of no more than one-sixth of the year's total.
   - citation: 12 CFR 1024.17(c)(1) and (c)(3)
   - source: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-X/part-1024/subpart-B/section-1024.17
   - as_of: 2026-11-15
   - review_by: 2027-11-15
   - note: Text: "(3) Subsequent escrow account analyses. For each escrow account, the servicer must conduct an escrow account analysis at the completion of the escrow account computation year to determine the borrower's monthly escrow account payments for the next computation year"; "one-twelfth ( 1/12 ) of the total annual escrow payments which the servicer reasonably anticipates paying from the account. In addition, the servicer may add an amount to maintain a cushion no greater than one-sixth ( 1/6 ) of the estimated total annual payments". A tax increase therefore usually reaches a monthly payment at the servicer's next yearly analysis; each servicer's computation year differs. Raw: raw/money/state/ecfr-12cfr1024-17.txt
2. Under federal mortgage rules, if a servicer's yearly escrow analysis finds a shortage of at least one month's escrow payment (for example after a tax increase), it may leave it or have the borrower repay it in equal monthly amounts over at least 12 months; a smaller shortage may also be billed within 30 days.
   - citation: 12 CFR 1024.17(f)(3)
   - source: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-X/part-1024/subpart-B/section-1024.17
   - as_of: 2026-11-15
   - review_by: 2027-11-15
   - note: Text: "(i) If an escrow account analysis discloses a shortage of less than one month's escrow account payment, then the servicer has three possible courses of action: (A) ... allow a shortage to exist ...; (B) ... repay the shortage amount within 30 days; or (C) ... in equal monthly payments over at least a 12-month period. (ii) If ... a shortage that is greater than or equal to one month's escrow account payment, then ... (A) ... allow a shortage to exist ...; or (B) ... repay the shortage in equal monthly payments over at least a 12-month period." Raw: raw/money/state/ecfr-12cfr1024-17.txt
3. Under federal mortgage rules, a servicer must send an annual escrow account statement within 30 days of the end of each escrow year and must tell the borrower at least once a year if there is a shortage or deficiency.
