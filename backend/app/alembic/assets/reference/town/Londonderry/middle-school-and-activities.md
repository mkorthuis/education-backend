# Londonderry Middle School, Activities and the Arts

Part of the Londonderry research (town/Londonderry). Gathered November 15, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers Londonderry Middle School (LMS, grades 6–8) and activities across LMS and Londonderry High School (LHS): the move from grade 5 to grade 6 and the workload that comes with it, how bullying is handled (including on buses) and what the district's behavior reports show, the phone rules at LMS and LHS and how they are enforced, middle school teams and tryouts, varsity levels and Unified Sports at LHS, what families pay for activities and the help available, music and theater funding, how LHS welcomes freshmen, and what the schools say about cursive, math facts and civics.

- **9 topics, 92 facts and 4 tables.** Raw copies are in `raw/middle/` (school pages, handbooks, policies and programs of studies), `raw/middle/board/` (School Board minutes, agenda packets and the FY2027 budget book) and `raw/middle/parent-updates/` (the LMS principal's newsletters), about 46 MB.
- **Main findings:**
  - **Phones are bell-to-bell at LHS, and enforcement shows in the data.** LHS requires phones and other devices to be off and out of sight from 7:05 a.m. to 2:14 p.m., including lunch and hallways; LMS requires them off and in a backpack or locker. The superintendent's July 2026 report says 497 LHS students got a detention at some point in 2025-26 because a phone was out (about 41% of the 1,219 students, **derived**), and the share of LHS students with no office referral fell from 79% to 54%.
  - **Bullying reports follow policy JIC's timeline, and the district publishes its own counts.** Parents of both students are told within 48 hours, an investigation starts within 5 school days and is to finish within 21. District-wide, 21 bullying or harassment investigations were done in 2025-26 and 12 were substantiated (23 and 16 in 2024-25). At LMS, the share of students with no office referral rose from 66% to 77%.
  - **The arts were on the district's list of possible cuts, alongside much else.** For the November 2026 tax cap vote, the district listed "Trim Band, Choral, & Orchestra ensembles," "Reductions in drama productions & crew" and "Cut freshmen & M teams" among discretionary areas for a possible $2.0 million cut, next to busing, AP sections, electives and administration. The cap got 51.9% of the vote, short of the 60% it needed (unofficial). Separately, the FY2027 budget book predicts "a few less options in the related arts for the middle school" because of enrollment-driven staff cuts.
  - **Help paying is district policy.** Policy JNA (1981) says family hardship should not keep a student out of any school-sponsored activity, with aid given by need and privately. The FY2027 LMS budget lists $15,000 for 8th grade Washington, D.C. trip scholarships and $3,000 for field trip scholarships. No athletic participation fee was found.
  - **Unified Sports exist, but adaptive PE isn't named.** LHS fields co-ed Unified soccer, basketball and track and field, and offers a Unified PE course (2 sections, 30 requests) among nine Unified courses. No LHS page, handbook or program of studies mentions "adaptive" or "adapted" PE.
- **Main gaps:** no data on cuts, roster sizes or playing time at LMS or LHS, or what the LMS "M" and "L" teams mean; no homework-time guideline for LMS (the elementary schools' guide is 10 minutes per grade); no Londonderry source on cursive instruction; no climate or belonging survey for LHS freshmen; no participation counts for Unified teams; the superintendent's budget and tax cap videos were not transcribed.

Source line: "2015, 41:1, eff. July 8, 2015. 2023, 17:1, eff. July 3, 2023."
