# Special Education, Student Support and Health in Londonderry

Part of the Londonderry research (town/Londonderry). Gathered November 15, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers how the Londonderry School District (SAU 12) serves students with disabilities, students' health and family support: special education programs and staff (speech-language pathologists, paraprofessionals), co-teaching and support classes at the middle and high schools, the study skills and executive-functioning courses at Londonderry High School, 504 plans, counselors and the new social worker, calm and quiet spaces, English learner (ESOL) teachers, translation, school nurses, medications and epinephrine, food allergies and nut rules, menus and vegetarian choices, free and reduced-price meals and how the district keeps them private, and the paperwork a guardian needs.

- **Main sources:** the district's Student Services, Dining, Lunch Menus and Employment pages; its Special Education Policies and Procedures Manual (revised January 2025, posted as "Local Plan 2025"); board policies IHBA, IHBA-R, IGBAA, KED, JHCD, JLCK, JLDA, EFAA, JHC, JRA-R, JFAA and EEA-R; the FY2027 Superintendent's Budget Book (staffing charts and the Pupil Services summary); school board minutes from January 2025 to October 2026; school special education, counseling and ESOL pages; the nurse site; the Matthew Thornton, South and North handbooks; the Matthew Thornton 2026-27 and North 2025-26 staff directories; Londonderry High School's 2026-27 Program of Studies; the district's LINQ Connect menus; 7 CFR 245.8 and Saf-C 1305.02.
- **Main findings:**
  - Londonderry High School has scheduled support courses for executive functioning: "Resource" (special education, 0.25 credit a semester) and "Lancer Lab" (for 504 students), plus grade-level Study Labs and a double-period freshman World Studies course with study skills. The high school pages describe in-class support and pull-out core classes but don't use the word "co-taught"; the middle school offers "team taught" classes at every grade.
  - The FY2026 budget funds 13.6 FTE of speech-language staff (0.2 unfilled) and 1,010.2 daily hours of special education assistant time district-wide, and says some positions stay vacant while the district recruits. In September 2026, 11 paraprofessionals were moved from Moose Hill to Matthew Thornton because of higher-than-expected needs in kindergarten and grade 1; Matthew Thornton's 2026-27 directory lists 27 special education assistants.
  - Nurses are budgeted in every building: 2 FTE at the high school, middle school, Matthew Thornton and South, 1.7 at North and 1 at Moose Hill (FY2026). The board doubled the substitute nurse rate to $25 an hour in October 2025 after trouble finding substitutes.
  - The elementary kitchens use only peanut- and tree-nut-free products, and Matthew Thornton's handbook says all elementary schools follow a non-food birthday rule; no district policy makes classrooms nut-free, and no allergy management plan was found posted. Bus policies say nothing about allergic reactions.
  - Free and reduced-price applications and eligibility are confidential, every student uses the same meal account system, and meals are served even when an account is negative; but Matthew Thornton's handbook bars students in debt with dining services from field trips.
  - Elementary schools have 2 to 2.6 counselor FTE each (Matthew Thornton lists 3 counselors in 2026-27); the district's only school social worker is shared by the middle and high schools.
- **Main gaps:** whether report cards or IEP documents are translated (no district statement either way); how the 8th-to-9th grade IEP handoff works step by step; waitlists or disputes over speech or aide hours; current job openings (the district's openings page returned an error); the high school and middle school staff directories (JavaScript embeds); any allergy management plan or bus procedure for allergic reactions; English learners' results.
