# Rye: Money, Governance, Portsmouth Tuition and the Facilities Study

Part of the Rye research (town/Rye). Gathered November 22, 2026.

## Summary

**What's covered:** 12 topics, 83 facts and 7 number tables on Rye's school budget and what drives it, the money left over each year and how it lowers the next year's school taxes, how much goes to Portsmouth High School tuition and how that tuition is budgeted, Rye's share of SAU 50 and of the SAU budget, the school tax and state aid (Rye gets no adequacy grant and keeps its statewide education tax), Question 2, the $100,000 facilities study and its four scenarios (including consolidating into one school), the 10-year capital plan, enrollment against building capacity and the enrollment projections, the school board and budget calendar, how the town and school ballots fit together, non-resident students who move away, community use of school buildings and fields, who pays for field trips, and sidewalks and the Safe Routes to School plan near Rye Junior High.

- **The budget.** The 2026-27 operating budget is $19,206,618, up $298,401 (1.58%); voters approved it 989–373. Total appropriations rose 40.4% from 2015-16 to 2025-26 (**derived**). The drafted 2027-28 increase was 4.8% in late October; the board approved its 2027-28 budget on November 4, 2026, but the amount isn't posted.
- **Where the money goes.** In the 2026-27 general fund, Rye Elementary is 32.3%, Rye Junior High 30.6%, the high school (mostly Portsmouth tuition) 24.6%, district-wide costs (including the SAU) 11.6% and preschool 0.9%. Portsmouth tuition paid in 2025-26 ($3,496,078.73) equals about $1.06 per $1,000 of Rye's assessed value, or 12.1% of all 2025 property taxes in Rye (**derived**).
- **Money left over.** 2025-26 ended $490,085.85 under budget, 60.7% of it from the Portsmouth tuition line; special education ended $5,815.67 under. Rye used $479,725 of the prior year's surplus to lower 2025-26 school taxes and projects $473,322.17 at June 30, 2026. SAU 50 policy DIA says unspent tuition from enrollment shifts is returned to offset taxes.
- **SAU 50.** Rye pays $1,033,994 (47.97%) of SAU 50's $2,155,675 budget for 2026-27, about 5.4% of Rye's own budget (**derived**). The SAU share and Portsmouth tuition, both set by formulas, are 26.0% of Rye's operating budget (**derived**).
- **The building study.** The March 2026 $100,000 study passed 738–613. The Turner Group's first drafts (October 2026) cover both schools; the report is expected by January 2027. No bond is proposed, and the district has no debt.

**Main sources:** the Rye School District annual report printed in 2026 (warrant, MS-27, default budget); Rye School Board draft minutes of October 21 and 26, 2026 and agendas for November 4 and 18; SAU 50 business administrator's memos and budget book sections (November 2025–January 2026), the June 30, 2026 and October 15, 2026 financial reports, the fund balance and trust fund statements (Rye Civic League copies of board documents, several of which are view-only on the board's Drive); the Town of Rye Budget Committee's December 2025 questions with district answers and its January 14, 2026 school hearing minutes; the March 10, 2026 official tally; NH DRA's 2025 tax rate report; SAU 50 policies DIA, JFAB, KF, KF-R and IICA; the facilities master plan RFP and its September 2025 facilities and enrollment appendices; the Town's 2025 Existing Conditions Analysis, the Rye 2035 Master Plan action worksheet (April 2026) and TAP Grant Committee minutes; the Coalition Communities 2.0 MOU and a 2019 Select Board letter on SWEPT; the Rye Education Foundation and PTA grant pages; and the site's database for derived comparisons.
