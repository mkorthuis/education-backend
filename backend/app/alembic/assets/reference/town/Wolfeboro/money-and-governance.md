# Money and Governance: Governor Wentworth Regional School District and Wolfeboro

Part of the Wolfeboro research (town/Wolfeboro). Gathered November 21, 2026.

## Summary

**What's covered:** 7 topics, 63 facts and 5 number tables on the Governor Wentworth Regional School District's (GWRSD, SAU 49) budget, default budget and ballot history; how its costs are split among its six towns and what Wolfeboro pays; projected 2026-27 school tax rates and Wolfeboro's 2025 revaluation; revenue by source; staff contracts and the votes on them, support staff and bus driver pay; tuition students (Middleton, Freedom, Madison, Tamworth) and the policy on staff members' children; buildings, bonds and reserves; and how and when residents can weigh in on the budget. District-wide facts are placed with the district so the Ossipee research can use them.

**Main sources:** GWRSD's Annual Meeting page (warrants 2023–2026, ballot results 2023–2026 and the 2025 recount, the 2026-27 default budget form, the "Financial Reports" handouts with each year's Distribution of School Costs sheet, the January 31, 2026 deliberative presentation, the 2026 annual report); board, Finance, Buildings & Maintenance and Human Resources committee minutes (April 2025–November 2026); policies JFAB and BEDH; the Town of Wolfeboro's 2025 Town Report and tax FAQ; Middleton's 2025 warrant; BLS wage data; read-only queries of the site's tax data.

**Main findings:**
- **Default budget:** in 2026 the default ($70,792,454) was $236,502 *higher* than the proposed budget, so a "no" vote would not have cut spending. In 2025 the budget failed by 9 votes after a recount; the $852,889-lower default froze new positions and some maintenance.
- **Wolfeboro's share:** costs are split 25% by property value and 75% by students. Wolfeboro holds 47.7% of the district's property value and about 30% of its students, for a combined 34.7%. It paid 37.0% to 40.1% of the six towns' school taxes in every year from 2011 to 2025 (**derived**). It gets no state adequacy grant.
- **Why bills climb with fewer students:** Wolfeboro's school taxes rose 48% from tax year 2014 to 2025 while its resident students fell 22.5% (**derived**). The district's drivers are health insurance (+21.3% in 2026-27, plus a $1.64 million SchoolCare assessment), special education, contracts and falling state building aid. Wolfeboro's 2025 revaluation roughly doubled assessed values and halved the rate.
- **Contract votes:** voters approved every staff contract on the 2023, 2025 and 2026 ballots, with 53% to 66% yes; Wolfeboro voted yes each time.
- **SAU 13:** no SAU 13–wide agreement was posted as of November 21, 2026. A five-year Freedom agreement was approved in August 2025, two Madison students were accepted in August 2026, and an "SAU13 Tuition Agreement" was on the November 2, 2026 agenda.

**Main gaps:** the support staff (GWSSA) contract and paraprofessional wage scale are not posted, and the state labor board's library refused automated requests in earlier research. The outcome of the November 2, 2026 SAU 13 agenda item is not posted. Middleton's tuition rate is not posted. Wolfeboro's tax year 2026 rate had not been posted (NH DRA refused automated requests). No source says whether tuition covers building wear. No source documents New Hampshire's lack of a broad income or sales tax (the Census API needs a key).
