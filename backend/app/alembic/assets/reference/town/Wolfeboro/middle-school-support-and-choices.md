# Kingswood Regional Middle School, Student Support and Choices

Part of the Wolfeboro research (town/Wolfeboro). Gathered November 21, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers Kingswood Regional Middle School (KRMS, grades 7–8, 404 South Main Street, Wolfeboro) and the supports and choices around it for Governor Wentworth Regional (SAU 49) families: the morning routine and before- and after-school options, the district's own bus department and fleet, late buses, ride lengths, how storm closings are decided across six towns, phones and bullying rules, fee help and the district's expanded free-meal program, special education funding, homeschooled and EFA students' access to district classes and sports, the move from KRMS to Kingswood Regional High School (KRHS), Chromebooks and the district network, world languages, counselors and mental health staff, and community use of school gyms.

- **16 topics, 102 facts, 3 tables.** All topics use shared slugs. Most facts are placed at `district Governor Wentworth Regional` (district-wide, so Ossipee's research can reuse them); the Kingswood Youth Center, the town recreation department and the EFA marketplace counts are placed at `town Wolfeboro`. No new state-rule facts: state rules are cited from the library and earlier towns.
- **Main sources:** the KRMS 2026-27 Student Handbook; Governor Wentworth policies (EBCD and EBCD-R on closings, EEA transportation, JICJ phones, JICK bullying, JJJ and JJJ-R access for non-enrolled students, IHBG home education, IMBA remote learning, IMBD high school credit in grades 7–8, JJIC athletic eligibility, JLD counseling, ECB and ECB-R facility use); school board and committee minutes (September 2025 to November 2026); the FY2027 warrant, the 2025 and 2026 voting results, the January 2026 deliberative presentation and the 2026 annual report; the district's food service pages and expanded-meal letter; the Chromebook agreement; the KRHS 2026-27 handbook, Program of Studies, school profile and counselor assignments; the KRMS staff directory; the Kingswood Youth Center's site; federal Civil Rights Data Collection (CRDC) staffing counts; the library's EFA marketplace list; and derived drive times (OpenStreetMap).
- **Main findings:**
  - **Morning and after school:** KRMS students who arrive before 7:00 AM must wait outside; breakfast runs 7:00–7:24 and advisory starts at 7:24. No before-school program at KRMS was found. After school, Homework Club runs about 2:30–4:30, the free Kingswood Youth Center runs 2:30–4:45 Monday to Thursday (GWRSD buses take students there), and late buses leave at 5 PM Monday to Thursday only, for "central locations" in each town that are not published.
  - **Buses:** the district runs its own transportation department with 38 vehicles (30 77-passenger buses, 4 wheelchair buses plus one, 6 vans). It leases 3, recently 4, buses a year and buys them at the end of each lease. Route sheets and pickup times are not posted online (families use the Stopfinder app pilot or call the department).
  - **Storm days:** the district's bad-weather guidelines (revised November 2, 2026) have the transportation supervisor call the road agents in all six towns from 3:50 AM and recommend by 5:30 AM. "All district schools will not be closed because of unacceptable road conditions in a single community"; instead bus service in that town is delayed. Drivers may refuse a hazardous route, and parents may keep children home with an excused absence.
  - **Fee help:** voters approved $250,000 for free meals up to 300% of the federal poverty level in March 2025 (2,177–1,105) and March 2026 (3,409–1,765). For 2026-27 a family of four earning up to $99,000 may qualify, well above the federal reduced-price limit. Athletic policy lets parents request a participation-fee waiver.
  - **Phones and bullying:** phones are banned bell to bell (policy JICJ, August 2025), with escalating consequences in the handbook. The board revised the bullying policy JICK on October 5, 2026 for the 2026 state law, but the 2026-27 KRMS handbook still prints the older definition with the "imbalance of power" clause the law repealed.
  - **Counselors:** KRMS lists 2 school counselors for about 345 students; KRHS lists 4 counselors and 1 social worker for about 700–730. In March 2026 voters rejected a district Student Wellness Coordinator (2,244–2,759; Wolfeboro 1,060–1,075).
  - **World languages:** KRMS offers French and Spanish as exploratory classes; KRHS offers French I–V and Spanish I through AP.
- **Main gaps:** bus route sheets and pickup times (no public copy; Stopfinder and Transfinder need sign-in); actual ride lengths; the late-bus stop locations; KRMS's dismissal time; the athletic participation fee amount; KRMS phone-violation or bullying figures beyond the site's state counts; Chromebook age and replacement cycle; whether KRMS French or Spanish can count for high school credit; any written agreement on town recreation use of school gyms; the KRHS Class of 2029 registration site (Google sign-in).
