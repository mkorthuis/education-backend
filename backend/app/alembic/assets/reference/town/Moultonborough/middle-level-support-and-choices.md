# The Middle Level, Student Support and Choices

Part of the Moultonborough research (town/Moultonborough). Gathered November 22, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers the Moultonborough Middle Level (MML, grades 6–8, which holds all its classes in the Moultonborough Academy building at 25 Blake Road) and the support and choices around it for Moultonborough School District (SAU 45) families: how the middle grades are kept apart from (and mixed with) the high school, early algebra and computer science, special education and 504 plans when a child moves from Moultonborough Central School (MCS), the shared school psychologist and social worker and the rest of the counseling team, academic help and the after-school program, homeschooled and EFA students' access to classes, sports and band, homeschool credit on return for grade 9, middle school basketball, how a family might attend a school outside the district, and the district's student mentoring program.

- **Main findings:**
  - **Separation in a shared building:** MML has its own principal, its own door and morning drop-off, its own Basecamp advisory and SEL program, and a bell schedule whose breaks and lunch fall at different times from the high school's; art, technology education, band, PE, family and consumer science, world languages, health and the library are taught by shared grade 6–12 staff. Seventh and eighth graders may play on high school teams with an NHIAA waiver, and MML students may not attend high school dances.
  - **Early algebra:** Algebra I Honors (a high school credit course) is open from grade 8 on the math department's recommendation, and policy IMBD lets 7th and 8th graders earn high school credit with the high school principal's prior approval. Federal data show 9 eighth graders (of 26, about 35%, derived) took Algebra I at MML in 2023-24, and all 9 passed. High school programming courses start in grade 9; MML has computer science and STEM as unified arts and robotics clubs.
  - **Counseling staff:** the premise is right. One school psychologist (who is also Director of Counseling Services) and one social worker serve all three schools; federal data for 2021-22 split each as a third of a position per school. The team also has a counselor in each school, a K–12 mental health clinician, a licensed drug and alcohol counselor, and a Northern Human Services counselor on site twice a week.
  - **Homeschool and EFA access:** the board's proposed revision of policy JJJ (first read November 10, 2026) gives home-educated, EFA, charter and private school residents the same access as enrolled students to courses, sports and activities, with no added tuition or fee for EFA students. Policy IK (March 2026) lets credit be earned through home education portfolios or assessments regardless of age; the older policy JF (2001) says placement of home-educated transfers in grades 9–12 is not for credit, and the manual says the most recent policy controls.
  - **Basketball:** MML fields boys' and girls' basketball teams; 2026-27 preseason practice began November 16, 2026.
  - **Choice:** where a family lives decides its district. Moultonborough's March 2026 warrant asked voters to adopt open enrollment with no residents allowed out and up to 1% non-residents in; the result was not found. Policy JCA (May 2026) allows best-interest reassignment by the superintendent and manifest-hardship reassignment by the board, with parents providing transportation.
  - **Mentoring:** the district-wide Mentor Program pairs trained adult volunteers and grade 9–12 students with K–12 mentees for about 40 minutes a week during the school day; it is listed among MA's 2026-27 clubs, and the April 2026 NEASC report says it targets needs in the elementary school.
