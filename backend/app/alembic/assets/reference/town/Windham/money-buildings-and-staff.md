# Windham: Money, Buildings, Governance and Staff

Part of the Windham research (town/Windham). Gathered November 20, 2026.

## Summary

Covers the Windham School District's (SAU 95) budget and what drives it, against its own enrollment counts; what the 2026 school tax cap (Question 2, HB 1300) would have meant and how Windham voted, including the November 14, 2026 recount; the 2026 tax rate and the school and town shares; the 2025 revaluation and how school taxes have changed from year to year; the 2017 Golden Brook and middle school bond, the failed 2026 track bond and the Windham Center School repair estimates; special education spending, aid and out-of-district tuition; the cost per pupil comparison the district publishes; the Parental Bill of Rights policy and objection and survey procedures; the teachers' and paraprofessionals' contracts and how the deliberative session and ballot handle them; paraprofessional, counselor and mental health staffing by school, resignations and substitutes; the school resource officer and school marshals; volunteer and substitute background checks; school assignment by address; and home values.

**13 topics, 84 facts and 8 tables.** All topics are shared slugs from `town/shared-topics.md`. Raw copies are in `raw/money/` (about 40 MB): the district's HB 1300 flyer and hearing slides, the Question 2 recount tally and minutes, the March 2026 official tally, the 2024 election and recount minutes, 9 school board minutes (of 37 public minutes searched, July 2025 to November 2026), the resignation lists from 34 board packets (July 2025 to November 2026, saved as a names-removed count with the packet links), the October 20 (excerpt), October 21, November 2 and November 17, 2026 board packets (with the FY2028 proposed budget, the SAU budget and the Windham Center School estimates), the WEA 2024–2027 and AFSCME 2026–2029 contracts, the FY2025 and FY2026 budgets and 2024–2025 warrants, 9 policies, the volunteer pages, the town's 2026 tax rate press release and assessing pages, excerpts of the 2017, 2018 and 2025 town reports and the 2017 and 2018 school reports, a names-removed copy of the town's 2025–26 sales report, and Zillow's ZIP values. The FY2027 budget, hearing slides and Voters' Guide cited are the copies already in `additional-data/raw/districts/batch-6/`.

**Main findings:**
- Windham's Question 2 (the school tax cap and the SAU cap) failed: 4,460 yes to 3,143 no after the November 14, 2026 recount (58.66%), short of the 4,561 yes votes needed. No cap applies for FY2028–FY2029. The district's own table shows its levy (without debt) would have exceeded a cap in each of FY2023–FY2027, and puts its central office at 4.29% of the FY2028 budget, under the 6% SAU limit.
- The FY2027 operating budget is $72,503,095 (passed 1,914 to 1,078); the FY2028 proposal stood at $75,089,149 (+3.57%) on November 13, 2026, driven by transportation (regular busing $1,883,952 to $2,592,000) and special education (+$1,176,054). The district's own enrollment counts fell from 3,049 (2021–22) to 2,786 (October 1, 2026).
- The 2026 tax rate is $14.57 (local school $10.46, state education $1.03, town $2.46, county $0.62); the school parts are about 79% (derived). The local school levy grew about 4.7% a year from tax year 2015 to 2025 (derived from the site's data).
- The 2017 bond ($38,907,102 for Golden Brook, the middle school and land) runs to 2038; the high school bonds are paid off, so debt service fell 39% in FY2027. Voters rejected a $4,992,908 track bond in March 2026 (57.8% yes). On November 17, 2026 the facilities director put Windham Center School repairs at $5,996,550 plus $2,270,000 of optional items; no bond article had been proposed.
- Special education cost $16,651,644 in FY2025, with $1,323,025 of state, federal and Medicaid revenue; the district paid $15,328,620 (about 92%, derived). Special day school tuition rates are rising 20% a year.
- One Windham police school resource officer covers all four schools; the district lists two full-time school marshals district-wide (Golden Brook lists one) and proposes funding for a third.

**Main gaps:** no published teacher turnover rate (a count of resignations in board packets is given instead, with caveats); no data on whether paraprofessional hours or IEP services were missed; the FY2028 default budget, the November 17 SAU budget hearing minutes and the 2027 warrant were not yet posted; whether a Windham Center School bond will be proposed for March 2027 is not settled; the district publishes no test-score or ranking comparison with Londonderry or Salem beyond state proficiency.
