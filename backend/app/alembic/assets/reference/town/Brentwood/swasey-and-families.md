# Swasey Central School and Young Families in Brentwood

Part of the Brentwood research (town/Brentwood). Gathered November 21, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers Swasey Central School (SCS), the Brentwood School District's preschool-to-grade-5 school at 355 Middle Road, for young families: the part-day preschool, the school day, drop-off and the new pickup routine, the YMCA's before- and after-school care in the building, snow days and the 2026-27 calendar, buses, kindergarten (full day, the cutoff, class sizes), classes and staff, scores against Stratham Memorial, Exeter's Lincoln Street and the other cooperative towns, advanced learners, special education, reading help and Title I, counseling, meals and their prices, how the school reaches families, a youth center in town, and what research says about starting kindergarten a year later.

- **15 topics, 71 facts, 2 number tables.** Twelve topics are shared slugs from `town/shared-topics.md`; `kindergarten-day-length` and `kindergarten-entry-cutoff` are the library's own topics (same kind, question type and description). One new shared topic is proposed: `research-kindergarten-entry-age` (research, research): "What national data and research show about children who start kindergarten a year later than their birthday allows (delayed entry), how common it is and what studies find about its effects." Raw copies are in `raw/elementary/` (about 17 MB; board minutes in `raw/elementary/board/`, policies in `raw/elementary/policies/`, federal civil rights rows in `raw/elementary/crdc/`, research in `raw/elementary/research/`).
- **Sources:** the SCS 2026-27 Student and Family Handbook; SCS web pages (home, preschool, Title I, student support, staff listing, food services, PFG, registration); SAU 16's SCS meal page and the 2026-27 SCS meal letter, application packet, charging policy (EFAA) and menus; SCS's August 14, 2026 newsletter; the 2026-27 SCS calendar; the SCS counseling site; Brentwood School Board minutes from September 2025 to November 2, 2026 and the FY27 budget options presentation; Brentwood policies EEA, EEAE, IFA, IHBA, IKE, IMBA, JCA, JEB and JLD; SAU 16's Child Find notice and transportation page; the Southern District YMCA's school-age care pages; Austin17House's program page; the EDies 2023 award list; federal Civil Rights Data Collection rows; NCES and NBER research; and the site's own database (test scores, class sizes, child care).
- **Main gaps:** bus route times (emailed to families, not posted); whether every kindergarten room has an aide; any measure of how quickly the special education team responds; the YMCA's Brentwood price and waitlist; Brentwood Recreation's programs (the town and recreation sites refused or dropped the connection); how Swasey takes parent input on class placement and twins; and whether the preschool's sessions are mornings only.

## research-kindergarten-entry-age: What Research Says About Starting Kindergarten a Year Later

- kind: research
- question_type: research
- description: What national data and research show about children who start kindergarten a year later than their birthday allows (delayed entry), how common it is and what studies find about its effects

1. In the federal Early Childhood Longitudinal Study's national sample of 2010-11 kindergartners, 5.9% were "delayed entrants" whose parents waited until they were older than school rules allowed, 86.6% started on time, 1.5% started early and 6.0% were repeating kindergarten; boys were more often delayed than girls (6.8% against 4.9%).
   - citation: U.S. Department of Education, National Center for Education Statistics, Digest of Education Statistics 2012, Table 136 (ECLS-K:2011)
   - source: https://nces.ed.gov/programs/digest/d12/tables/dt12_136.asp
   - as_of: 2026-11-21
   - review_by: 2027-11-21
   - note: Table: "Total 4,054 ... 1.5 (0.17) 86.6 (0.91) 5.9 (0.41) 6.0 (0.87)"; "Male ... 1.1 ... 85.0 ... 6.8 ... 7.1"; "Female ... 1.9 ... 88.3 ... 4.9 ... 4.9". Footnote 2: "classified as an early, on-time, or delayed kindergarten entrant depending on whether the parent reported enrolling the child early, enrolling the child when he or she was old enough, or waiting until the child was older relative to school guidelines". Parent-reported, national, standard errors in parentheses; no New Hampshire figure. Raw: raw/elementary/research/nces-digest-2012-table-136.flat.txt (.html)
2. A study of Danish children (Dee and Sievertsen, 2018) found that starting school a year later sharply reduced inattention and hyperactivity at age 7 (effect size −0.7), an effect that lasted to age 11, while effects on other mental health measures were smaller and faded; the authors note that earlier studies found "little evidence" that delaying school improves educational and economic outcomes.
   - citation: Thomas S. Dee and Hans Henrik Sievertsen, "The Gift of Time? School Starting Age and Mental Health," NBER Working Paper 21610 (2015); published in Health Economics 27(5), 781–802 (2018)
   - source: https://www.nber.org/papers/w21610
   - as_of: 2026-11-21
   - review_by: 2027-11-21
   - note: Abstract: "a growing body of empirical studies provides little evidence that such schooling delays improve educational and economic outcomes ... We find that a one-year delay in the start of school dramatically reduces inattention/hyperactivity at age 7 (effect size = -0.7) ... this large and targeted effect persists at age 11. However, the estimated effects of school starting age on other mental-health constructs ... are smaller and less persistent." Uses Denmark's birth-date rule ("children typically enroll in school during the calendar year in which they turn six"), so it compares children near a cutoff, not a family's choice for one child. Only the abstract was read. Raw: raw/elementary/research/nber-w21610-dee-sievertsen.abstract.txt (.html)
