# Pittsfield High and Career Programs

Part of the Pittsfield research (town/Pittsfield). Gathered November 22, 2026.

## Summary

Covers the high school grades at Pittsfield Middle High School (PMHS, 23 Oneida Street; grades 6–12) and the career and college programs its students use, for the questions assigned to agent 3:
- Pittsfield students at the Concord Regional Technical Center (CRTC): who may apply, how many applied and were accepted for 2025-26, the bus from PMHS and its daily contract rate, the drive to Concord, the CRTC's construction (electrical and plumbing) and health science programs as PMHS's catalog prints them, and what the district budgets for vocational programs;
- what Pittsfield's High School Options Study Committee asked potential receiving schools about career-center seats, sports, AP and late buses, and what the schools had answered by October 2026;
- PMHS's college-credit courses (five Early College courses with NHTI, no AP courses taught at PMHS), the September 2026 agreement letting Pittsfield students take advanced courses in Concord, NH Scholars, the new Honors and High Honors diplomas, ELOs and the IMPACCT program;
- science courses and labs, including Anatomy and Physiology; the absence of shop courses at PMHS; Chromebooks and devices;
- PMHS sports in the NHIAA's 2026-27 alignments (no co-op teams; Pittsfield absent from baseball), the sportsmanship awards, the pay-to-participate fee and help with it, the Drama Club, and the NHIAA's transfer rule (new statewide fact);
- the late bus and the bus contract; open enrollment (the March 2026 vote, policy JFABA and the 2026-27 seat counts);
- guidance staffing and college and financial aid help; local scholarships and the Foss Family Scholarship Trust; graduation rates and the class of 2026's plans.

**Counts:** 13 topics, 52 facts and 4 number tables.

**Main sources:** PMHS's 2025-26 Program of Studies and 2026-27 Student and Parent/Guardian Handbook; the PMHS guidance, athletics and college-planning pages; Pittsfield School Board minutes (December 2024 to November 5, 2026) and the High School Options Study Committee's minutes (June 9 to November 3, 2026); the district's 2024-25 and 2025-26 annual reports, 2026 warrant, FY2027 MS-27 and policies (JFABA, IHCD, IKF, IKFG, IKG, JLD); the Town of Pittsfield's 2026 election results and 2025 trust fund report; the NHIAA 2026-27 handbook by-laws (copied from the Northfield and Epping research); OpenStreetMap and OSRM for one drive time.

**Main gaps:** the PMHS bell schedule and the CRTC bus times (the handbook says they are on the school website; they weren't found); late-bus days and times; how many Pittsfield students attend the CRTC in 2026-27 and how many seats any receiving school would give them; the receiving schools' written answers to the committee and the community survey results (not posted); the text of the 2026 Concord advanced-coursework agreement and the revised policy IHCD; the Foss trust's governing terms; the 2026-27 Program of Studies (only 2025-26 is linked); the NH Supreme Court's 2025 Pittsfield opinion (HTTP 403).

## school-sports-rules: The NHIAA Transfer Rule

- kind: rule
- question_type: rules
- description: New Hampshire law and NHIAA rules for school sports: concussions, medical coverage at practices and games, physicals, cooperative teams and their no-cut rule (proposed from the Epping research)

1. Under the NHIAA's 2026-27 by-laws, a student who changes high schools without the family moving into the new school district must attend the new school for one year before becoming eligible for sports, unless the NHIAA's executive director grants a waiver on a principal's affidavit, and may not be added to any team's roster after that sport's season has opened; a student whose family moves into the new district is eligible at once.
   - citation: NHIAA Handbook 2026-27, By-Law Article II (Eligibility), Sect. 4, Transfer Rule, A, C and D, and waiver conditions
   - source: https://www.nhiaa.org/about-nhiaa/handbook/
   - as_of: 2026-11-22
   - review_by: 2027-11-22
   - note: Text: "A. A student who transfers enrollment with a corresponding move into a new school district by his/her parents or guardians shall be declared eligible immediately"; "C. A student who transfers enrollment without a corresponding move into a new school district ... shall be required to be in attendance in the new school for one (1) year from the date of enrollment in order to establish athletic eligibility"; "D. ... may not be added to the roster of a sports team (at any level) after the opening of a sports season"; "These requirements may be waived, if ... The student has not transferred for the purpose of participating in interscholastic athletics ... The Transfer Rule Affidavit is completed and submitted to the NHIAA and approved in writing by the Executive Director." The by-laws don't mention open enrollment by name. Copied from the Epping research. Raw: raw/high/nhiaa/4HB-26-27-II-Eligibility.txt (and .pdf)
2. The NHIAA's 2026-27 transfer rule makes students placed in a school by outside agencies, including "other school districts," eligible on enrolling in the receiving school if they meet the other requirements, and allows hardship eligibility for transfers caused by unforeseen, unavoidable circumstances, naming "loss of school accreditation" among the examples.
   - citation: NHIAA Handbook 2026-27, By-Law Article II, Sect. 4, F.2 and Hardship Eligibility
   - source: https://www.nhiaa.org/about-nhiaa/handbook/
   - as_of: 2026-11-22
   - review_by: 2027-11-22
   - note: Text: "Students placed in a school by outside agencies (i.e. courts, State Department of Welfare, other school districts, medical referrals) become eligible upon registration and enrollment in the receiving school provided all other eligibility requirements are met. The principal is to verify eligibility to the Executive Director"; "Hardship Eligibility may be granted to a transfer student who does not meet the NHIAA Transfer Rule when sufficient evidence ... is provided to show that it was necessary for the student to transfer because of unforeseen, unavoidable, or unusual circumstances including, but not limited to, broken home conditions ... loss of school accreditation." The by-laws don't say how they apply when a district closes its high school and tuitions students elsewhere; that would be the NHIAA's decision. Raw: raw/high/nhiaa/4HB-26-27-II-Eligibility.txt
