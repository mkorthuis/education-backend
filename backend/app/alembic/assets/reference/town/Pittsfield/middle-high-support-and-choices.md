# Pittsfield Middle High School: Support and Choices

Part of the Pittsfield research (town/Pittsfield). Gathered November 22, 2026.

## Summary

Covers Pittsfield Middle High School (PMHS) for agent 2's questions: middle school test results and the help offered, advanced math and science, counseling, the attendance and truancy rules and the police agreement, homeschooled students' access and returning to school, comparisons with neighboring schools, the 2026 Safe Grant security work, where 6th grade is taught, bullying and behavior, teacher turnover, chronic absence, phones and Chromebooks.

**Counts:** 12 topics (11 local topics with `place: district Pittsfield` and 1 research topic with no place), 69 facts, 2 number tables.

**Main sources:** the PMHS 2026-27 Student and Parent/Guardian Handbook (posted November 13, 2026), the 2025-26 Program of Studies, Pittsfield School Board policies (attendance policy JH and its procedures, adopted May 7, 2026; the police attendance agreement; phone policy JICJ; access policy JJJ), and school board, policy committee and facilities committee minutes from May 2025 to November 12, 2026. Many 2026 minutes are drafts. Test results and comparisons come from the site's own database.

## research-chronic-absence: What Happens When Students Are Chronically Absent, and What Helps?

- kind: research
- question_type: research
- description: What careful studies find about how missed school days affect learning and graduation, and which efforts to raise attendance have been tested.

1. A study of 23,386 third and fourth graders in 175 Philadelphia public elementary schools found that students with more chronically absent classmates scored lower in reading and math, even after accounting for their own attendance, by about 0.04 and 0.05 standard deviations, roughly the size of the test gap between students who did and didn't get free lunch in the same model.
   - citation: Michael A. Gottfried, "Chronic Absenteeism in the Classroom Context: Effects on Achievement," Urban Education (2019), author's accepted manuscript posted by Attendance Works
   - source: https://www.attendanceworks.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/01/AW-gottfried_chronic_peers-2.pdf
   - as_of: 2026-11-22
   - review_by: 2027-11-22
   - note: Text: "Overall, the analytic sample N = 23,386 consists of third and fourth grade students within 175 public, neighborhood schools"; "The effect sizes here are -0.04σ in reading and -0.05σ in math. In context, these are approximately the same size as the free lunch recipient/nonrecipient test gap"; "students suffer academically from having chronically absent classmates". The study is non-experimental, from one large urban district's elementary grades; middle and high school classes, where students change rooms, were not studied. It answers P18.1's question about students who do attend. The library's research-chronic-absence 1–8 covers the absent students themselves and tested attendance programs.
