# Albany School District: Money, Tuition Contracts and Governance

Part of the Albany research (town/Albany). Gathered November 13, 2026.

## Summary

**What's covered:** 14 topics, 107 facts and 2 number tables on the Albany School District's money, its three tuition contracts with the Conway School District, its taxes and state aid, special education costs, high school choice, SAU 9 and its school board.
- The budget from 2017-18 to 2026-27: what it buys, why it fell to 2024-25 and rose in 2025-26 and 2026-27, and the 2025-26 actual spending split (**derived** shares).
- All five Albany–Conway contracts, read page by page from scanned PDFs: K–6 (2026–2034, signed 2025), the 2003 grade 7–8 and 9–12 contracts (ending June 2027), and the new 7–8 and 9–12 contracts (2027–2032). Formulas, true-ups, bonds and capital plans, special education, capacity, reports, joint meetings, termination and disputes.
- Taxes and aid: the 2025 local school tax nearly doubling (with the fall in the adequacy grant), the hold harmless phase-out, why Albany gets no extraordinary need grant (equalized value per free or reduced-price meal pupil) or fiscal capacity disparity aid, and how EFA students drop out of the aid count. NH DOE's adequacy aid calculation for Albany, FY2020–FY2027, is in the site's data (adequacy_aid), and so are its DRA tax rates (2014–2025) and NH DOE school taxes (2011–2025) (property_tax), so this file does not repeat them.
- The March 2026 open enrollment vote (RSA 194-D, 0% outside Conway), the manifest hardship policies, and the absence of any Fryeburg Academy agreement.
- The school board: seats, terms, vacancies, write-in elections, stipends and appointments, 2019–2026; public input at Conway's board; Albany's place on SAU 9.
- The school options committee and termination notices of 2019–2024 that led to the new contracts.

**Main sources:** Albany School District warrants (2019–2026), annual reports (2018–2026, scanned; budget, enrollment and apportionment pages rendered and read), school board minutes (2019–2026) and annual meeting minutes, the September and November 2026 board packets, Albany policies JECA and JECAA, the five contract PDFs on SAU 9's tuition page, the Town of Albany 2025 annual report, Conway policy BEDH, RSA 186-C:13, 186-C:18, 194-D, 194:3-f, 671:3, 671:33, 72:35, 72:36-a, 72:28-b and chapter 77, the library's NH DOE adequacy and DRA tax files, and read-only queries of the site database.

**Premises checked:**
- "5- and 8-year agreements" and "8-year K–6 contract": correct. The K–6 contract runs a minimum of eight school years from July 1, 2026; the new 7–8 and 9–12 contracts run five school years from about September 1, 2027.
- "K–6" contract while Conway's grade 6 is at Kennett Middle: correct. The contract covers K–6 wherever Conway teaches grade 6.
- "Annual tuition true-up bills and credits": the K–6 contract and the 2003 contracts adjust each year for actual costs, folding a credit or added payment into the next bill. The 2027 grade 7–8 and 9–12 contracts have no such clause. No separate true-up bill or credit was found in Albany's budgets or minutes.
- "RSA 194-D open-enrollment policy capping town-funded transfers": partly correct. It is not a board policy but a warrant article voters passed on March 10, 2026. It adopts RSA 194-D and sets a 0% limit on Albany students attending open enrollment schools outside the Conway School District. The reason given was protection while the law was changing.
- "Phase-out of Hold Harmless grants and shift toward Equalized Valuation per Free & Reduced Meal student": the phase-out is current law (80% of the FY2025 grant in FY2026–27, falling to zero in FY2034). The grant based on value per free or reduced-price meal pupil (the extraordinary need grant) exists, but Albany has received none since FY2026.
- "No Fryeburg Academy contract": correct. The "Albany" on Fryeburg Academy's day-student page is Albany, Maine.
- "Three-person board": correct. Recent seats were filled by write-ins with one or two votes, or by appointment.
- "Massive bond" (P2.2): no Conway bond proposal was found. The 2027 contracts charge for a five-year capital plan rather than sharing bonds.

**Main gaps:**
- No record of the dollar amount of past tuition adjustments (credits or added charges), and Conway's yearly per-pupil sheets for sending towns are not posted.
- The amounts of Albany's adopted veterans' credits (DRA's site refused automated requests; the town report gives only the total).
- The 2027-28 draft budget ("backup to follow" in the November 10, 2026 packet).
- A primary source on New Hampshire's tax structure beyond the repeal of RSA chapter 77.

**Proposed shared topics:**
- `property-tax-relief` (rule, rules): veterans' and other property tax credits and exemptions, and how they apply to the school parts of a tax bill.
- `special-education-advocacy-help` (programs, help): the Disability Rights Center – NH's education help and contacts.

## gov-school-boards-and-superintendents: School Boards and Superintendents

- kind: term
- question_type: governance_money
- description: The division of duties between elected school boards and superintendents.

1. In a New Hampshire school district, the school board fills vacancies on the board, and its appointees serve only until the next district election, when voters elect someone for the rest of the term; if the whole board is vacant, or the remaining members cannot agree by majority vote, the town's selectmen appoint.
   - citation: RSA 671:33, II(a)

In the case of a vacancy of the entire membership of the school board, or if the remaining members are unable, by majority vote, to agree upon an appointment, the selectmen of the town or towns involved shall appoint members by majority vote in convention." ## open-enrollment-status: Open Enrollment Between Districts: Current Law and the 2026 Bills

- kind: rule
- question_type: rules

1. A New Hampshire district adopting open enrollment must set limits, as any percentage from zero to 100% of its enrollment, on how many of its students may attend open enrollment schools inside and outside the district, and in a district with an annual meeting, if no limitation question gets a majority, the limit is zero.
