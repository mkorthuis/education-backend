# Albany Community and Families: Homeschooling, Child Care, Summer, Help With Costs, Population, Housing and Emergency Services

Part of the Albany research (town/Albany). Gathered November 13, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers the community and family side of the Albany residents' questions: whether Albany homeschoolers can join Kennett Middle School activities and how home education notices work after the July 2026 law change, private child care costs near Albany, summer programs, volunteering at Conway's schools, scholarships reserved for Albany residents, early childhood therapy and preschool special education, food pantries, and Albany's population, income, poverty, children, births, housing, town government, emergency services and winter roads.

- **10 topics, 79 facts and 11 number tables.** On November 14, 2026, ACS figures that the site's Census data now holds (Albany's, Conway's and the state's income, poverty, SNAP, median age, housing units, seasonal and owner shares, home values and rents) were removed; Carroll County figures and ACS age groups and housing types stay. Raw copies are in `raw/community/` (census, childcare, early, emergency, food, homeschool, housing, recreation, roads, scholarships, town, volunteer), about 70 MB.
- **Format check:** the file passes the backend parser (`parse_file`) except for the five facts placed `sau 9` (district-special-education-services facts 1–2; district-classrooms-and-staff facts 1, 2 and 4), which the brief asks for but the current parser rejects, as in the Bartlett research. Spot-checked against raw copies: the SAIPE Albany rows, the Conway Rec camp eligibility sentence, the Lora Johnson Pierce entry, the 176 Albany fire calls and the teen center eligibility sentence.
- **It builds on** the Conway file `community-and-choice.md` (home education in SAU 9, early supports and therapy providers, Kennett local scholarships, EFA marketplace), the Conway files `schools-k-8.md` (Conway policy JJJ for non-enrolled Conway residents) and `daily-life.md` (End 68 Hours of Hunger weekend food), the Jackson file `community-and-families.md` (licensed child care near Conway, Head Start, Tin Mountain camp scholarships) and the library topics `home-education-rules`, `child-care-scholarship-eligibility`, `fcess-early-supports` and `early-supports-to-preschool-special-ed`. Those facts are named in the question table, not repeated. No assigned question was answered wholly by earlier files; P7.3 was mostly answered by the library.
- **Main findings:**
  - **Homeschool access is unsettled in writing for Albany.** RSA 193:1-c ties access to the programs "offered by the school district in which the pupil resides," and the Albany School District runs no schools. Albany's posted policies (11) include no home education or program-access policy, Conway's JJJ is written for pupils residing in Conway, and Conway's policy committee sought legal advice in June 2026 on out-of-district homeschoolers taking Conway courses or co-curriculars. No document found answers the question for an Albany family.
  - **No annual homeschool notice exists any more.** Since July 1, 2026, RSA 193-A requires a declaration only to use public school programs or on withdrawal from a public school, and the "participating agency" (which included a nonpublic school head) is no longer a defined term.
  - **Albany pays into Conway Recreation.** Albany owed $37,077 for 2025 (a 5.94% share), and Conway's 2026 summer day camp is open to children living in Conway and Albany, ages 6–13, 8:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m. with the paid early and late option. The Chip Kennett Teen Center (grades 6–8) is open to Conway, Albany and Eaton children for $35 a session.
  - **Median age rose; school counts fell.** Albany's median age went from 45.6 (2010 Census) to 50.7 (2020 Census). The Albany School District's own counts of its students fell from 96 (December 2019) to 58 (February 2026), and the town reports list 1 to 5 resident births a year.
  - **School-age poverty estimates move a lot.** The Census Bureau's school district model counted 13 of 94 school-age children in poverty for 2024 (13.8%, derived), down from 25 of 95 for 2023. Albany's ACS poverty estimates, with their margins, are in the site's Census data.
  - **Housing:** no sale-price report exists for Albany; Conway's single-family median was $535,000 for January to October 2026. Seasonal homes fell from 223 (2010) to 155 (2020).
  - **Emergency services are contracted.** Albany has no police, fire or ambulance department. The Carroll County Sheriff patrols under contract, and Conway Fire answered 176 Albany calls in 2025. The fire contract is in its final year, and new Fire/EMS and Recreation agreements with Conway were being drafted in October 2026.
  - **Scholarship:** one Kennett local scholarship, the Lora Johnson Pierce Scholarship, is limited to Albany residents. The town's trust funds include no scholarship fund.
- **Main gaps:** Conway Recreation's summer camp fees (not posted in November); how long provider waitlists are; cell coverage along the Kancamagus (FCC map not retrieved); NHDOT's plowing standards for NH 112; 2026 town meeting results for the petitioned nonprofit articles; any SAU 9 statement on Albany homeschoolers at Kennett Middle.
- **Proposed shared topics:** `town-government-and-services` (local, local_practice): "A town's select board, town offices and hours, town meeting, and the services the town contracts for" (town government facts sit under `town-community-profile` here); `school-volunteering` (local, help): "How residents can volunteer or mentor in a district's schools: forms, background checks and roles" (under `district-classrooms-and-staff` here). This file also supports the Conway file's proposals `local-scholarships` and `town-health-and-therapy-providers`, and suggests widening the description of `town-streets-and-safe-routes` to cover winter road maintenance generally.

New Hampshire's RSA 193:1-c gives home-educated, nonpublic, charter and EFA pupils access to the courses and co-curricular programs "offered by the school district in which the pupil resides" and tells the local school board to adopt a policy on it; for an Albany child that district is the Albany School District, which runs no schools of its own and tuitions its students to Conway.
