# Money and Governance: New Durham's Side of the Governor Wentworth Regional School District

Part of the New Durham research (town/New Durham). Gathered November 22, 2026.

## Summary

**What's covered:** 9 topics, 49 facts and 6 number tables on what New Durham pays the Governor Wentworth Regional School District (GWRSD, SAU 49) and why: its share of the district's costs, the 2026-27 projection, the "$8.8 million" figure on the state's tax rate sheet, school taxes against resident students, the 2025 revaluation, the school share of the tax bill and of the town's property base, exemptions, and the November 2026 school tax cap vote; how the district's money is divided by function and where the nurse spending stands; New Durham Elementary's staffing and the support-staff and paraprofessional job postings; the new vehicle policy that names "designated volunteers"; New Durham's one board seat and how its voters have voted since 2023; the district's 1963 origin, New Durham's share of the Kingswood debt, and what leaving would involve; New Durham School's building issues and the Kingswood turf reserve; and the town's own road and capital reserve plans for comparison. The Wolfeboro and Ossipee money files (town/Wolfeboro/money-and-governance.md, town/Ossipee/money-and-governance.md) already hold the district's budget, drivers, contracts, formula and buildings; this file cites them and adds New Durham's side.

**Main sources:** GWRSD's Annual Meeting page (FY2027 "Financial Reports" handout read from page images, the January 31, 2026 deliberative slides, warrants and final ballot results for 2023–2026, the Mandatory Report to Voters), the district's Financials & Audits monthly reports (June, September and October 2026), its policy manual (EEBA, EEAG, GBCD, IJOC), board and committee papers (October 2025 to November 2026), the job board, the 2025 Report Card; the Town of New Durham's 2025 Annual Report (state tax rate sheet, MS-1, 2026 warrant, budget committee report), its 2026–2035 Capital Improvement Plan, its 2026 elderly exemption form and its November 3, 2026 election results; the federal Civil Rights Data Collection (2023-24); and read-only queries of the site's tax, enrollment, cost and adequacy aid data.

**Main findings:**
- **The "$8.8 million":** the state's 2025 tax rate sheet for New Durham lists "Net Cooperative School Appropriations $8,881,609". That is New Durham's share of GWRSD's net budget before the state grant ($671,420) and the state education tax ($896,537). It is not a separate New Durham school budget, and no source splits it by classroom or central office (district-wide split in table gwrsd_budget_by_function).
- **Why the bill climbs with a small school:** New Durham pays for all its students, not one building. In 2025-26, 136 of its 288 resident students were in grades 7–12 at Kingswood. Its school taxes rose 46.3% from 2014 to 2025 (**derived**) while resident students fell 27.1%. GWRSD projects another 12.5% for 2026-27, with a combined school rate of $9.36 per $1,000, up $0.96.
- **School tax cap:** it was on the November 3, 2026 ballot as Question 2, not a future vote. New Durham voted 1,080 to 414 (72.3%) for it and the six towns together 65.2% (unofficial), above the 60% needed. It applies from the 2027-28 budget now being drafted.
- **Designated volunteers:** policy EEBA (Vehicle Operation Safety) covers employees and "designated volunteers" who drive on school business. The policy's own adoption line reads November 2, 2026. Separate policy EEAG already requires prior written authorization and $500,000 of liability coverage naming the district for private vehicles carrying students.
- **Nurses:** no "$600,000 salary overage" appears in the posted papers. The October 2026 report shows the Health Services function $465,240.84 over its budget once committed money is counted ($451,061.25 on September 28); the district's slides called the in-house nurse cost an "increased cost", and the savings estimate in the slides was for Lakeside Academy.
- **Votes:** New Durham holds 1 of 9 board seats (11.1%) against 16.5% of the students. It cast 9.8% to 14.0% of the ballots on the budget from 2023 to 2026. Its voters were on the winning side on 21 of 28 articles; only the 2025 budget result would have changed without them (**derived**).

**Main gaps:** no source says why the Health Services function is over budget, and the November 2, 2026 board minutes are not posted. The paraprofessional wage scale is not posted (the support staff contract is not online and the state labor board refused automated requests in earlier research). No teacher turnover rate for New Durham Elementary is published; only a one-year federal count. The district's articles of agreement and formation papers were not found, and no record of a New Durham withdrawal study or vote was found. The town's March 10, 2026 ballot results (roads, exemption) and New Durham's tax year 2026 rate were not found. No school-by-school budget is published.
