# Hampton: Money, Taxes, Governance and Buildings

Part of the Hampton research (town/Hampton). Gathered November 16, 2026.

## Summary

**What's covered:** 11 topics (one proposed), 66 facts and 6 number tables on Hampton's two school budgets and how they reach the tax bill, for both the Hampton School District (SAU 90, preschool to grade 8) and the Winnacunnet Cooperative School District (SAU 21, grades 9–12).
- **Two ballots, two budgets.** Each district has its own SB2 ballot. In March 2026 voters passed SAU 90's $32,220,010 budget (1,515–1,295) and Winnacunnet's $32,803,073 budget (2,965–2,058 across four towns; Hampton 1,684–1,125). Winnacunnet results are the four-town total. The tables give every article's result in Hampton.
- **What drives the increases.** SAU 90's increase over the default budget ($1,203,566) comes mostly from union contracts, health and other benefits, and transportation. Its requested $379,544 on top paid for 3 ABA technicians and raises for 59 non-union staff. Winnacunnet cut 3 teaching positions through retirements, grew 1.76% and gives non-union staff a 3.2% raise. The facts include contract terms, budget histories (FY2021–FY2027 and FY2023–FY2027), debt and reserves.
- **The tax split.** Winnacunnet divides its costs half by property value and half by resident enrollment. For 2026-27 Hampton's share is 45.73% (44.37% of value, 47.10% of students). Its estimated share of the locally raised taxes is 47.3% for 2025-26 and 46.4% for 2026-27 (**derived**). In tax year 2025 about 34% of Hampton's local school tax went to Winnacunnet (**derived**, site data). The town has posted its 2026 rates: $12.78 in total, of which the local school rate is $6.24.
- **Buildings.** SAU 90 has a yearly maintenance article: $300,000 a year, and $350,000 in 2026 for the Centre School boiler and pond clean-up. Voters approved $2,000,000 for Centre School air quality in 2024. The Hampton Academy bond ($19,405,000 outstanding) runs to 2043. Winnacunnet has no bonds and pays for its five-year plan through yearly articles (roof, HVAC, lighting, library, seats).
- **Revaluation and relief.** The 2024 revaluation covered all property in town, and the facts give the assessors' and DRA's explanations. Hampton's elderly exemption is $175,000 to $300,000; its income limits were raised in March 2026, and 143 owners receive it. The facts also cover other exemptions, tax deferral, the tax year and billing.
- **Voters and voting.** The fiscal impact note petition (passed 1,826–850) and the failed Winnacunnet amendments. Absentee voting from away, the domicile rule, where the documents are posted, the 2026 calendar and turnout: about 2,990 ballots in March against 9,012 in November. Census turnout by age for New Hampshire.

**Main sources:** SAU 90's 2026-27 warrant, sample ballot, budget presentation (December 11, 2025), budget summary, executive summaries (2021-22 to 2026-27), default budget form (MS-DSB), MS-27, Mandatory Report to Voters, 2025 and 2024 budget flyers, earlier warrants and the FY2025 audit. Winnacunnet's 2026 warrant, ballot, budget presentation, budget summary, revenue estimate, default budget, deliberative session minutes, and its 2025-26 and 2024-25 annual reports. SAU 21's 2026 election results and member districts' ballots. The Town of Hampton's assessing, exemption, revaluation, tax, election and absentee pages, the 2026 town warrant, sample ballots, the March and November 2026 tally reports, the 2026 MS-1, the MRI revaluation memo and the DRA letter. RSA 32:5, 72:6, 72:38-a and 654:1. New Hampshire Housing's 2024 rental survey and the Census Bureau's voting supplement. The site's database: school taxes with the cooperative share, tax rates, enrollment and Question 2.

## property-tax-bills-and-escrow: How Property Value Becomes a Tax Bill

- kind: how_it_works
- question_type: governance_money
- description: How and when property tax rates are set and bills issued, and how mortgage escrow payments adjust when taxes change

1. New Hampshire law says "All real estate, whether improved or unimproved, shall be taxed except as otherwise provided," so school and other property taxes fall on a property by its value whether or not the owner lives there year-round or has children in school.
   - citation: RSA 72:6
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/V/72/72-6.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-16
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: The second clause of the statement is the plain reading of the rule; the statute doesn't mention residence or children. Exemptions and credits are "otherwise provided" (for example RSA 72:39-a for elderly owners, which requires New Hampshire residence and that the home be the owner's residence). Raw: raw/money/rsa-72-6.txt
2. A guide by the New Hampshire Municipal Association, posted on Hampton's revaluation page, explains that the money to be raised by taxes, appropriations minus all other revenue, "is the major factor which drives the property tax rate," and that value only divides that amount among owners: a revaluation keeps values proportional, so if every property's value rises by the same share, each owner's tax stays the same.
   - citation: Katherine Heck and Barbara Reid, New Hampshire Municipal Association, "Understanding the Math, Dispelling the Myths (Tax Rate)" (linked from the Town of Hampton's 2024 Revaluation page)
   - source: https://www.hamptonnh.gov/DocumentCenter/View/8635/Understanding-the-Math-Dispelling-the-Myths-Tax-Rate
   - as_of: 2026-11-16
   - review_by: 2027-11-16
   - note: Text: "The amount of money which must be raised through taxes—appropriations minus all other revenue expected to be received—is the major factor which drives the property tax rate. The value of property is the basis on which the tax money to be raised is apportioned to each property owner"; its two-property examples show "no tax impact, because the proportionality between the properties did not change." When some properties rise in value more than others, their owners pay a larger share. Raw: raw/money/town-reval-understanding-the-math.txt
3. The same New Hampshire Municipal Association guide says exemptions and credits don't change the amount of tax to be raised but shift it, raising the rate for owners who don't qualify, and that equalization, the state's yearly adjustment of each town's values to market value, is used to share county, state education and cooperative school district taxes fairly among towns.
   - citation: New Hampshire Municipal Association, "Understanding the Math, Dispelling the Myths (Tax Rate)"
   - source: https://www.hamptonnh.gov/DocumentCenter/View/8635/Understanding-the-Math-Dispelling-the-Myths-Tax-Rate
   - as_of: 2026-11-16
   - review_by: 2027-11-16
   - note: Text: "Granting exemptions and credits for any purpose does not change the amount of property taxes that need to be raised—it merely shifts the responsibility for payment ... to ensure that public taxes shared by municipalities, such as the state education tax, cooperative school district taxes and county taxes, are reasonably apportioned among municipalities, the playing field must be leveled.
