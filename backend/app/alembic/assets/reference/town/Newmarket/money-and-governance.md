# Newmarket: Money, Taxes, Buildings and Governance

Part of the Newmarket research (town/Newmarket). Gathered November 21, 2026.

## Summary

**What's covered:** 11 topics, 66 facts and 6 number tables on the Newmarket School District's (SAU 31's) budget, the 2017 renovation bond, its buildings and reserves, school taxes and relief, voting and public input, community use of school buildings, rents and family costs.
- **The bond and the tax bill.** Voters approved a $39 million renovation bond in 2017 ($38,943,083 in bonds; the elementary and junior-senior high projects finished in 2021). The payments are level, about $2.1 million a year (about 7% of the budget), and end August 15, 2046, with no state building aid. Because the payment stays flat, the increases come from the operating budget: union contracts, health insurance (up 20% planned for 2026-27, after a $417,961 mid-year assessment) and special education. The 2026 tax rate is $20.68, of which $13.70 is local school and $1.08 state education.
- **Buildings.** A 2023 facilities study led to a 20-year plan (2025–2043, $4.9 million of needs, mostly roofs). Roof sections at the junior-senior high were replaced in 2025 and 2026, and the elementary school's old rear windows in 2024–25. The October 2026 budget workshop says HVAC in several elementary rooms "were not commissioned properly" and had to be repaired. The facts also cover reserve balances and why the 2026 reserve article was $100,000 instead of $200,000.
- **Where the money goes.** SAU central office costs, as HB 1300 defines them, were $1,321,959 in 2026-27, 4.6% of the budget less the bond. The facts also cover top administrator salaries, instruction's share (56.2% of FY2024 expenses) and staffing, cut from 213.86 to 194.58 FTE since 2021-22.
- **Unfilled positions and leftover money.** The FY27 budget lists 8.5 unfilled budgeted FTE. 2.4 of them became an MTSS-B coach and an elementary counselor. Vacancy savings partly pay for contracted services. Unspent money lapses into fund balance. The district keeps part of it (RSA 198:4-b), voters move some into trust funds, and the rest lowers the next tax rate ($757,249 forecast for 2026-27).
- **Voting and input.** The facts give turnout and results for 2024–2026. Only 21 residents came to the 2025 deliberative session. They also cover board meeting times, public comment and the FY28 calendar. The FY28 budget stood at $32,431,021 (+4.85%) on November 19, 2026, with cut options listed.
- **Relief, rents, fees, facility use.** Newmarket's exemptions are for elderly, disabled, blind and veteran owners, with none based on income alone. The state's low and moderate income relief tops out near $208 here (**derived**). Rents in the area, the facility use fee schedule and the Rec Department's use of school gyms are covered, along with Chromebook and parking costs.

**Main sources:** the FY27 approved budget report, FY28 budget workshop packet (October 17, 2026), HB 1300 hearing packet (October 27, 2026) and November 19, 2026 board packet (with November 5 minutes), 2023–2026 warrants, 2026 deliberative session slides, 2026 and 2025 election results, the 2025 school annual report (deliberative minutes, election minutes, debt schedule, special education costs), the FY2024 audit, the FY26 Q2 financial report, the 2025–2043 capital improvement plan, the RSA 189:76 report to voters, the facility use form, and the 2026-27 handbooks. From the Town of Newmarket: the 2026 tax rate notice, the FY2027 Taxpayer Guide, assessing and exemption pages, the 2024 annual report and the FY28–32 forecast. Also RSA 32:7 and 32:10, NHFPI's 2026 property tax report, New Hampshire Housing's 2024 rental survey and the site's database (tax rates, DOE-25, Question 2).

## school-budget-overruns-and-reserves: Unspent School Money at Year's End

- kind: rule
- question_type: rules
- description: What a New Hampshire school district can do when costs exceed its budget during the year: special meetings, emergency overexpenditures, retained fund balance, contingency funds and special education reserves (added from the November 13, 2026 towns)

1. Under New Hampshire's municipal budget law, a district's appropriations cover one fiscal year and lapse at its end; money not spent cannot be spent without a new appropriation unless it is under a binding contract by year's end, placed in a capital reserve or trust fund, borrowed for a bonded project, grant money, or a special warrant article voted as nonlapsing (for up to 5 years).
   - citation: RSA 32:7
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/III/32/32-7.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-21
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text: "All appropriations shall lapse at the end of the fiscal year and any unexpended portion thereof shall not be expended without further appropriation, unless: I. The amount has, prior to the end of that fiscal year, become encumbered by a legally-enforceable obligation ... II. The amount is legally placed in any nonlapsing fund ... VI. The amount is appropriated under a special warrant article and is explicitly designated ... as nonlapsing ... no case shall be later than 5 years". Lapsed money becomes fund balance; voters may let a district keep part of it (RSA 198:4-b, II; Chatham's school-budget-overruns-and-reserves fact 4 and library `budget-hb-1610-unassigned-funds-veto`), and the rest is used to lower the next tax rate. Raw: raw/money/rsa-32-7.html (.txt)
2. During the year a school board may move unspent money from one budget line to another when needs change, as long as total spending stays within the total voters approved; money voted in a special warrant article or for a capital reserve can be used only for that purpose, and citizens may see the records of transfers but cannot overrule them.
   - citation: RSA 32:10, I(a)–(d)
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/III/32/32-10.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-21
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text: "the governing body may transfer to that appropriation an unexpended balance remaining in some other appropriation, provided ... (a) The total amount spent shall not exceed the total amount appropriated at the town or district meeting. (b) Records shall be kept ... neither the budget committee nor other citizens shall have any authority to dispute or challenge the discretion of the governing body in making such transfers ... (d) Any amount appropriated at the meeting under a special warrant article, or to a capital reserve fund pursuant to RSA 35:5, may be used only for the purpose specified". This is why money budgeted for an unfilled position may pay for a contractor instead (district-budget-and-cost fact 15). Raw: raw/money/rsa-32-10.html (.txt)

## For another file

- **Elementary (agent 1):** The FY27 budget (raw/money/nsd-fy27-approved-budget.txt, p. 7) adds a 1.0 literacy specialist and a second school counselor at NES. It also cuts 1.6 tutor FTE, and NES grade sections are 5 in grade 1 and 4 in K and grades 2–5. The October 2026 workshop shows NES iReady and class size guidelines (18 in K–2). The NES and NJSHS 2026-27 handbooks are linked from https://www.newmarket.k12.nh.us/families/student-handbooks; copies are in raw/money/. The NJSHS handbook gives meal prices: breakfast $2.00, lunch $3.50, milk $0.50, a $3.25 online payment fee and a $25 returned check fee.
- **Junior-Senior High (agent 2):** For 2026-27 the computer education position was absorbed into STEM, and 0.5 FTE music and 0.5 FTE social studies were cut (FY27 budget p. 7, relevant to P19.1). The board approved the NH Seal of Biliteracy and Seal of Civic Excellence on November 5, 2026 (raw/money/nsd-board-2026-11-19-packet.txt). Mass General Brigham will end its athletic trainer program after 2026-27, and the district plans a direct hire (November 19 memo). SST enrollment from Newmarket fell, saving about $90,000 in 2025-26 (Q2 report).
- **Support (agent 3):** The Q2 FY26 report has 4 vacant paraeducator and 1 vacant tutor positions, filled in part by contracted services, which bears on P6.1. The NSSA contract has 4% raises (district-budget-and-cost fact 21). The board's Policy Committee was reviewing student transportation policies in fall 2026.
- **Earlier towns:** the elementary windows and roof replacements here could be compared with other towns' capital plans. No contradictions with earlier files were found. The library's `budget-form-and-vote-dates` fact 99 (Newmarket 2026 dates) matches the 2026 warrant.
