# Whitefield: Money, Governance, Taxes, Schools' Costs and the Grade 7-8 Move in the White Mountains Regional School District (SAU 36)

Part of the Whitefield research (town/Whitefield). Gathered November 22, 2026. It adds Whitefield's side to the district-wide money and governance facts in `town/Lancaster/money-and-governance.md` (cited here as "Lancaster money file" with its topic and fact numbers) and to Lancaster's student life, elementary and high school files.

## Summary

**What's covered:** 16 topics, 48 facts and 7 number tables on Whitefield's school taxes and how the five towns share the district's cost, the grade 7-8 move to the middle high school (what the board said it would save, what the FY27 budget shows, the vote and the first months), the school board and how parents can be heard, the budget vote, EFAs, homeschool access and paperwork, school choice for the arts, teacher and paraprofessional departures, the rooms Whitefield School no longer needs for grades 7 and 8, supplies and DonorsChoose, sports teams and co-ops, bus lines and start times, and graduation and test results against Littleton.
- **Why the Whitefield school tax keeps rising.** Whitefield's school taxes (local plus state education) rose 45.3% from tax year 2018 to 2025 ($2.55 million to $3.71 million) while its resident students fell 16.4% (293 to 245), so school tax per student went from $8,710 to $15,138 (**derived**, site data; the 2019 start year of 258 students gives +42.1% against -5.0%). The tax rate itself fell after the town's 2024 revaluation ($25.89 in 2023 to $16.40 in 2024 per $1,000), but the town's total tax commitment still rose 13.2% in 2024 and 2.5% in 2025. For 2026-27 the district estimates Whitefield's school tax at $4,124,570, up 11.2%, because its state aid falls 14.9% ($298,607) and its share of the cost split rises 2.1% while its students fall 5.4% (**derived**). Bills are not issued in March: March is when the budgets are voted; the town says bills come twice a year and the first is half of the previous year's total.
- **Whitefield's share.** Whitefield carries 24.7% of the district's 2026-27 cost split from 28.3% of its students and 19.4% of its equalized value. After state aid its taxpayers owe about $15,957 per student, against $12,074 in Lancaster, $15,157 in Dalton, $24,784 in Jefferson and $76,500 in Carroll, and about $9.25 per $1,000 of equalized value in both Whitefield and Lancaster against $5.13 in Carroll (**derived**). The formula (60% students, 40% value) is the Lancaster money file's (district-tax-and-state-aid 9); nothing here says what is "fair."
- **Did the 7th and 8th grade move save money?** Not shown either way. The district's own framing (April 24, 2025) was that staying put would mean cuts worth "just south of 1.7 million dollars" and moving would save "just north of 1.7 million dollars," so the move was presented as avoiding cuts, not lowering the current budget. In the FY27 budget lines Whitefield School's lines fall 10.6% ($597,490), the new middle school lines are $2,290,148, and the four school buildings together rise 3.8% against 4.1% for all lines (**derived**). The board voted unanimously on June 18, 2025, saying the first year would be "front loaded with some costs." No before-and-after cost comparison has been published.
- **Parents and the board.** The board is the White Mountains Regional School Board; SAU 36 is its one-district office. It met away from the high school at 5 of 21 meetings in 2024-25 (Lancaster, Whitefield, Carroll, Jefferson, Dalton) but every meeting since July 2025 has been in Whitefield. Policy KE sends an individual parent's complaint through teacher, principal and superintendent first; the Parental Bill of Rights (Policy AB, September 18, 2025) lists parents' rights to inspect materials and opt out; the Ed Programming committee meets monthly and a board member said public attendance at committees is poor.
- **Teacher turnover (new counts).** The board accepted 9 teacher departures at its 2024-25 meetings, 18 in 2025-26 (16 resignations) and 8 in 2026-27 through November 19; at Whitefield Elementary 3, 8 and 3, and 7 paraprofessional resignations in 2025-26 (**derived**, board minutes). It also approved one-year certificates of eligibility for 3, 4 and 5 teachers or long-term substitutes in those years. The district has published no turnover rate.
- **Graduation and tests against Littleton.** Littleton's four-year graduation rate was higher than White Mountains Regional's in 8 of the 10 years 2016-2025 (averages 90.4% and 86.2%), but White Mountains Regional's was higher in 2017 and 2025 (80.95% against 75.56%); both classes are small (**derived** cohorts of about 63 and 45 in 2025). On the grade 11 state tests White Mountains Regional's reading proficiency was above Littleton's in 2022-2025; Littleton's spring 2026 results are higher, and White Mountains Regional's 2026 high school results aren't in the site's data yet.
- **Bus lines and start times.** The district's 2026-27 route sheet is one 8-page PDF with 7 lines, all of which carry grade 7 to 12 students to the middle high school and four of which (Whitefield Pink, Whitefield Blue, Twin Mt Yellow and Dalton Brown) also serve Whitefield Elementary; all 17 links on its "School Year Information" page opened on November 22, 2026. Times: Whitefield Elementary 7:45 to 2:25, the middle high school classes 7:50 to 2:25. The district's In House Transportation page still sends other questions to the previous bus company.
- **Main gaps:** the district's own savings calculation behind the April 2025 figures; any posted plan for the rooms freed at Whitefield School; turnover rates; Whitefield's tax year 2026 rate (not yet loaded) and the town's 2026 budget documents; NHIAA division and co-op records (pages blocked); the DonorsChoose project lists; whether the athletic participation fee in Policy JJIC is charged.

**Main sources:** the site's database (tax rates, school taxes, resident students, EFA counts, graduation, state tests, staff); the district's annual report, FY27 budget detail (read by building) and budget presentation (through the Lancaster files); 54 sets of White Mountains Regional School Board minutes (July 2024 to November 2026) and the 7/8 Transition Advisory Committee minutes (2022-2023); board policies AB, KE, KEC, KCB and IHBG; the district's 2026-27 route sheet, start and end time sheet and handbooks; the town of Whitefield's tax collector page and 2023 annual report; the library's EFA, home education, charter school and open enrollment topics; the Children's Scholarship Fund EFA statistics and marketplace feeds (as saved by the library); DonorsChoose's school pages; and a 2023 local news report.
