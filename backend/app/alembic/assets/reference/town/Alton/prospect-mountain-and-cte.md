# Prospect Mountain High School and Career and Technical Education

Part of the Alton research (town/Alton). Gathered November 21, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers Prospect Mountain High School (PMHS, grades 9–12, site school id 28215), run by the Prospect Mountain JMA (joint maintenance agreement) of the Alton and Barnstead school districts (site district id 970, SAU 301), and the career and technical education its students take at the Lakes Region Technology Center (LRTC) in Wolfeboro. It covers honors, AP and college-credit courses, hands-on courses, counselors, school resource officers and building security, phones, clubs, retakes and late work, the daily schedule, buses (including the LRTC bus), and graduates' plans. Barnstead's research follows and can cite every fact placed with `district Prospect Mountain JMA`.

- **Main findings:**
  - **Career center:** PMHS's career center is the **Lakes Region Technology Center in Wolfeboro**, not the Huot Center in Laconia. LRTC counted 38 PMHS students among its 244 in 2025-26. PMHS lists 17 LRTC programs, open by application from grade 10, and its handbook says students are bused there (cte-center-programs 1–2, 10–11).
  - **Honors, AP and college credit:**
    - Honors work is built into grade 9 and 10 classes ("Embedded Honors"). Grades 11–12 have General, College Prep and Honors levels.
    - The 2026-27 catalog lists **two AP courses**: AP Art, and LRTC's AP Business/Personal Finance.
    - It lists **15 Early College courses taught at PMHS**, including Calculus and Statistics, and an Associates Degree Pathway with LRCC worth 60 college credits.
    - In 2023-24 PMHS had about 28 dual enrollment students per 100, against a median of about 10 among New Hampshire high schools (school-programs-after-grade-6 1–8, 15–20; table).
  - **Rules on retakes and late work:**
    - Summatives can be retaken within 7 days, if the first try was a good faith effort and the practice work is turned in.
    - Late work is accepted up to 10 school days, with 10% off per day, up to 30%.
    - Phones may not be used during school hours, under the state law (district-curriculum-and-assessment 4–5; district-technology-and-phones 3).
  - **SROs:** Alton's police department provides the SRO. The handbook says PMHS has "the equivalent of a full-time SRO". The town's second full-time SRO position went unfunded in 2026 (district-safety-and-security 1–6, 11).
  - **Buses:** First Student's Alton buses carry PMHS and Alton Central students together and reach PMHS by 7:45. On Wednesdays and Thursdays, when PMHS starts at 8:51, there are later high school runs. On the Route 11 side, Alton 4's first morning stop is at 6:52 (district-transportation 2–4; district-school-day-and-calendar 1).

| P6.1 | Community college classes for dual credit, and whether the school pays | Yes, PMHS offers them. The catalog says two CCSNH courses a year are free and later ones cost about $100–$150; SNHU courses cost $100 for 3 credits. Students pay books, lab fees and campus transportation. No source says PMHS pays, and its policy says it pays no tuition or fees for advanced courses taken elsewhere. |
| P6.2 | Counselors' one-on-one time for juniors and seniors | Time per student isn't published. PMHS assigns students to a counseling director and two counselors by class and last name; the handbook says four. That is about 98 students per counselor in 2023-24 federal data, among the 10 lowest caseloads of 81 New Hampshire high schools reporting. |
| P11.1 | "No one taught taxes, budgeting and loans" | PMHS now requires half a credit of financial literacy (classes of 2027–2030). It can be met by Living on Your Own (budgeting, loans, credit cards, insurance) or SNHU's Financial Literacy (including income tax planning), and Consumer Math covers calculating taxes and interest. For students who entered before 2026-27, the state required half a credit of economics including personal finance. |
| P11.2 | The school "only celebrates college acceptances" | Can't be measured. Most PMHS graduates don't go to four-year colleges (32 of 89 in 2025 by the site's data). The handbook recognizes students at monthly assemblies and a June awards day. The catalog lists apprenticeships, trade schools and the military as options, and FBLA and robotics earn ELO credit. No apprenticeship or military signing events are described. |
| P11.3 | Unlimited retakes and passing without turning work in | Partly. A summative may be retaken within 7 days, only if the first try was a good faith effort, a plan is made with the teacher, and the practice work is turned in; a first score of 90 or more allows one retake, and teachers may allow more. Late work is accepted up to 10 school days, at 10% off per day, capped at 30%, and is flagged "Missing" to parents. |
| P14.1 | The high school bus pickup in Alton Bay and the ride "up Route 28" | Alton 4's first morning stop is Route 11 at Heron Point Road at 6:52, reaching PMHS at 7:45 (about 53 minutes). Alton 6's Route 11 stops run 6:57–7:27, with students moving to bus 4 or 5 at Alton Central. On Wednesdays and Thursdays the pickups are about an hour later. By car, Alton Bay is about 3.3 miles and 9 minutes from PMHS. |
| P14.2 | "Lock them up," or still a distraction | Phones (and connected watches and earbuds) may not be used during school hours. Phones in use are taken, and repeat offenders may have to leave theirs in the Main Office each day. There is no rule that all phones be locked up. |
| P14.3 | Robotics, theater or art clubs | Yes. There is a FIRST robotics team (Team 319, meeting Tuesdays and Thursdays and worth ELO credit), a drama club that is restarting (no shows listed since 2022), an acting club, the National Art Honor Society, Tri-M, and bands among about 20 groups. |
| P17.1 | "A full-time SRO at both Alton Central and Prospect Mountain" | Partly wrong. The PMHS handbook says PMHS has "the equivalent of a full-time SRO" from Alton police. Alton's police describe one SRO covering both schools; the second full-time position was vacant in early 2026, covered by rotating patrol officers, and its funding was dropped from the revised 2026 town budget. PMHS visitors must use the main entrance, check in and wear a badge. |
| P19.3 | Scores "without digging through a giant PDF" | The site's school pages show PMHS's and Alton Central's state test results by year. |
