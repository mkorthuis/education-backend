# Alton Central's Upper Grades, Student Support and Choices: Algebra, Counseling, Bullying and Phones, Sports and Clubs, the Move to Prospect Mountain, Special Education, Teachers Staying, Home Education and EFAs

Part of the Alton research (town/Alton). Gathered November 21, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers Alton Central School's (ACS, preschool to grade 8) middle grades and the help around them: Algebra I in grade 8, enrichment, electives and world language; counselors, social workers and mental health help at ACS and at Prospect Mountain High School (PMHS); bullying, behavior supports and the phone rule; middle school sports and clubs; the move to PMHS with Barnstead's students; special education staffing, IEP and 504 counts and where to take concerns; how many teachers stay; and how home-educated and EFA students can use ACS and PMHS programs.

**Sources:** the ACS Student and Parent Handbook 2026–2027 (a Google Drive PDF linked from the ACS site); the Prospect Mountain High School JMA ("SAU 301") policy book, a 668-page PDF linked from the SAU's policy page; the Prospect Mountain Athletics Handbook for Athletes and Parents 2025–2026 (a Google Doc); ArbiterLive's ACS team list and two 2026-27 team schedules; the ACS athletics site; ACS's online bullying report form; federal Civil Rights Data Collection (CRDC) rows for 2017-18, 2020-21, 2021-22 and 2023-24; and the site's database for derived comparisons. The SAU website itself (pmsau.nh.gov) answers scripts with a JavaScript bot check, and WebFetch sees only page titles, so its program of studies, counseling, special education, homeschool, athletics, Alton School Board policy and minutes pages were not read (see Not retrieved).

**Counts:** 9 topics, 59 facts, 2 tables.

**Main findings:**
- **Algebra I in grade 8, not honors tracks.** ACS places eligible 8th graders in Algebra I using state and STAR test results, the grade 7 average and the teacher's recommendation. In 2023-24 one class of 11 students took it, about a third of the 34 eighth graders (derived), and all passed. Grade 7 had no Algebra I, and no honors-labeled middle school classes are described. PMHS policy IMBD can give high school credit for the same course taken in grade 8, with the PMHS principal's prior approval.
- **Sports and clubs are local, with games around the Lakes Region.** ArbiterLive lists 13 ACS teams for 2026-27 (soccer, cross country, volleyball, basketball, baseball, softball). About half the games are at home, and the school buses teams to away games; families pick athletes up at school. Clubs include band, chorus, drama, NJHS, student council and after-school enrichment.
- **Counseling staff.** ACS's 2026-27 handbook lists two guidance counselors (preschool–3 and 4–8) and a social worker. In 2023-24, PMHS reported 4 counselors (about 98 students each, derived) and a social worker, but no school psychologist.
- **Bullying rules lag the 2026 law.** The ACS handbook's bullying section still includes the "imbalance of power" wording that HB 131 removed from state law on August 1, 2026. Its timelines (48-hour parent notice, a 5-day start and 10-day finish) match the law. An online form lets anyone report, and administration says it will act within 24 hours.
- **Home education policy lags too.** PMHS policies JJJ and IHBG (2022) give home-educated residents equal access to courses and activities, but IHBG still describes the annual evaluations that state law repealed July 1, 2026, and JJJ doesn't mention EFA students, whom HB 1817 added in August 2026.
- **Teachers staying.** In 2023-24, 82.1% of PMHS teachers had also taught there the year before, close to the 83.8% statewide (derived). ACS's own retention figure is a reporting error. Neither school reported a first- or second-year teacher.
