# Seabrook: Money, Taxes, Governance and Buildings

Part of the Seabrook research (town/Seabrook). Gathered November 21, 2026.

## Summary

**What's covered:** 8 topics, 54 facts and 5 number tables on the Seabrook School District's budget and its failed budget votes, how Seabrook's school taxes are split between its own schools and Winnacunnet, the nuclear plant's share of the tax base, the elderly exemption, Seabrook's votes in SAU 21 and at Winnacunnet, the 2024–2025 study of leaving SAU 21, the buildings, fees and staff changes. Winnacunnet's own budget, warrant and each town's cost share are in `town/Hampton/money-and-governance.md` and are cited, not repeated.
- **The budget keeps failing.** Seabrook voters rejected the operating budget in March 2024 (671–743), 2025 (584–701) and 2026 (507–614), so 2026-27 runs on the $19,040,759 default budget; the district counts default budgets in FY20, FY23, FY25 and FY26 as well. The proposal was $166,400 above the default; the default itself rose $704,041 for contracts and special education (IEP) obligations, mostly out-of-district special education transportation.
- **Where the school tax goes.** In tax year 2025, 37.7% of Seabrook's local school tax ($8,673,861 of $22,999,129) went to Winnacunnet and 62.3% to the Seabrook School District (**derived**, site data). For a homeowner, about 16 cents of each tax dollar went to Winnacunnet, 26 cents to Seabrook's own schools and 9 cents to the state education tax.
- **The nuclear plant.** The town and the plant's owners agreed on $45 million of taxes for 2024–2026 ($40 million for 2021–2023). Utility property was 35.3% of Seabrook's taxable value in 2025, down from 54.5% in 2011 (**derived**, site data), and its value fell about 10% from 2024 to 2025, which the assessor named as the main reason the rate rose. Winnacunnet's cost formula counts the plant's value, so Seabrook pays 32.59% of Winnacunnet's costs while having 30.68% of its resident students.
- **Governance.** Seabrook has one of five Winnacunnet board seats, elected by all four towns; in March 2026 its voters cast 21.6% of the Winnacunnet budget votes and voted no on all six articles, five of which passed. On the SAU 21 board Seabrook has 3 of 15 regular votes and pays 29.24% of the SAU budget. A 2024 study committee voted 4–3 against leaving SAU 21 (a stand-alone SAU was estimated at $750,000 a year against $658,000 paid), and voters agreed 722–579 in March 2025. No study of leaving Winnacunnet was found.
- **Buildings.** No bonds since the last payment in 2024-25. Repairs are voted yearly: a $379,995 middle school roof (2024), $192,100 for two middle school boilers and $54,000 to remove two oil tanks (both passed 2026). Two other boilers failed in 2024-25, nearly emptying the building trust; a deposit to rebuild it failed in 2026.
- **Exemptions and fees.** Seabrook's elderly exemption starts at 65, not 70: $230,000 to $310,000 off assessed value, worth $2,852 to $3,844 a year at the 2025 rate. The middle school handbook lists no sports fee; band instruments are rented from music stores.

**Main sources:** the Seabrook School District's 2026 warrant, ballot, budget summary, revenue estimate, FY27 budget presentation, default budget form (MS-DSB), deliberative session minutes, and its 2023-24, 2024-25 and 2025-26 annual reports (results, staff lists, SAU 21 budget, special education spending); SAU 21's 2026 election results, voting, audit, Joint Board and Seabrook board pages and the November 2026 vacancy notice; the Seabrook Middle School handbook, sports form, supply list and music department page; the Seabrook Elementary handbook (library copy); the Town of Seabrook's 2025 tax detail, assessing page, exemption sheets and 2024 and 2025 town reports; RSA 194-C:7 and 194-C:8; and the site's database (school taxes with the cooperative share, valuations with and without utilities, test results, free and reduced lunch, average teacher salary, Question 2).
