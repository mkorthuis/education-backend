# Hart's Location Community and Families: People, Land, Housing, Child Care, Early Supports, Home Education, EFAs, Emergency Services, Roads and Connectivity

Part of the Hart's Location research (town/Hart's Location). Gathered November 13, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers the community side of the Hart's Location residents' questions: who lives in the town (Census counts of people, children, ages and households), how much of its land is national forest and state park, its housing and zoning, town government and the midnight-voting tradition, child care and preschool within reach of Route 302, early intervention and speech services before age five, home education and Education Freedom Accounts (EFAs) after the 2026 law changes, and the town's police, fire, ambulance, warnings, cell and internet service, and Route 302 closures.

It builds on the Bartlett file `daily-life-and-community.md` (home education rules, EFA tutors, infant care distances from JBES, Bartlett Recreation, Hart's Location storm days), the Jackson file `community-and-families.md` (SAU 9 home education, licensed child care near Conway, Head Start, home sale prices in Bartlett and Conway) and the Conway file `community-and-choice.md` (early supports and therapy providers). Those facts are named in the question table, not repeated.

- **8 topics, 76 facts and 7 number tables.** Raw copies are in `raw/community/` (about 33 MB): census, land, voting, town, housing, childcare (with routing), homeschool, efa, emergency, broadband and roads.
- **New data concepts:** Hart's Location's own 2010 and 2020 Census counts (missing from the library's Census file); a **derived** share of the town's area in the national forest and state park from NH GRANIT map data; ballots cast in recent elections; derived road distances from the Town Hall to child care north and south; Children Unlimited's posted child care and preschool rates; the town's zoning limits, accessory dwelling unit rules and master plan survey; the town's emergency setup from the February 2026 draft hazard mitigation plan; the scheduled fiber build.
- **Main sources:** the Town of Hart's Location website and its 2025 Annual Report (town and school, March 2026), including the draft 2026 Master Plan; the Bartlett & Hart's Location Hazard Mitigation Plan Update 2026 (draft, February 19, 2026) and Emergency Operations Plan (2018); the 2026 Zoning Ordinance and 2018 wireless facilities ordinance; Census 2010 and 2020 tables and the ACS 2020-2024 age table; NH OPD population estimates and projections; NH GRANIT conservation lands and the Census TIGERweb town boundary; RSA 659, 193-A and 193:1-c; the Hart's Location School District's board page, policy folder and minutes; the CSF EFA statistics feed; NH DHHS child care data; Children Unlimited's website.
- **Main findings:**
  - **Population:** the 2020 Census counted 68 residents (41 in 2010), including 7 under 18 and 1 under age 5. The 2020 age counts carry privacy noise. The Census Bureau's survey estimates (ACS) for the town are in the site's Census data.
  - **Land:** **derived** from GRANIT maps, about 90% (89.9%) of the area inside the town line is national forest (45.7%) or state park (44.0%); a 2018 GIS analysis in the town's emergency plan gave 87.8%.
  - **Housing:** 59 housing units in 2020, 21 of them seasonal (35.6%, derived); 2.5-acre minimum lots; accessory dwelling units allowed but not as short-term rentals; no other short-term rental rule; 63% of master plan survey respondents said the town should not encourage more housing.
  - **Midnight voting is suspended.** Midnight polls were last held for the February 2020 primary. The 2020 general election, both 2024 elections and the November 2026 general election used 11 a.m. to 7 p.m. polls.
  - **No licensed child care in the town** or in Bartlett or the town of Carroll (Bretton Woods, Twin Mountain). Programs in North Conway are 18 to 20 road miles from the Town Hall and in Conway village about 24 miles (derived).
  - **Emergency services are contracted:** Bartlett Fire, Bartlett-Jackson Ambulance and the Carroll County Sheriff, with the Notchland Inn as emergency center and shelter. There is no cell tower in town and no service on a 10-mile stretch of Route 302. Fiber internet was scheduled to go live in July 2026; whether it did was not confirmed.
  - **Home education:** since July 1, 2026 no SAU 9 administrator acts as a "participating agency" because the law no longer has one; the Hart's Location School District posts no home education or program-access policy.
  - **EFAs:** site data show 0 Hart's Location EFA students in every year from 2021-22 through 2026-27, and the CSF feed lists no Hart's Location entry.
- **Main gaps:** the number of registered voters (the Secretary of State's site refused access); the FCC broadband and mobile maps (refused access); whether the fiber build went live; NHDOT winter maintenance pages (refused access); prices posted by valley child care programs other than Children Unlimited (the weekday hours and rates they list with NH DHHS are in the site's data); home sale prices for Hart's Location (no public report).
- **Proposed shared topics:** town government and land facts have no shared topic, so they sit under `town-community-profile` here, and internet and cell service sit under `town-emergency-services`. I propose:
  - `town-government-and-elections` (local, governance_money): "A town's government, meetings, officers, town meeting and election arrangements";
  - `town-land-and-conservation` (local, local_practice): "How much of a town's land is public or conserved, its zoning and limits on building";
  - `town-internet-and-cell-service` (local, local_practice): "Broadband and cell coverage in a town, and build projects".

**Premises checked:**
- "Rare young family" (P1): **consistent.** The 2020 Census counted 7 residents under 18 of 68, and 1 under age 5; the 2010 Census counted 6 under 18 and none under 5. Site data show one public school student living in town in 2025-26.
- "Under 50 voters" (P9.3): **consistent with ballots cast, not verified against the checklist.** The most ballots found in one election were 43 presidential votes in November 2020 (derived); 34 voted in 2018, 30 in the 2024 primary and 16 at the March 2025 town election. The Secretary of State's registration counts could not be reached.
- "Roughly 90%+" federal and state land (P19.1): **about right but slightly high.** Derived from GRANIT, 89.9% of the town's area is national forest or state park; the 2018 emergency plan's GIS analysis gave 87.8%. The two are split almost evenly between federal and state.
- Midnight voting tradition (P10): **out of date.** The town last opened polls at midnight on February 11, 2020; it used regular hours in November 2020, January and November 2024 and November 2026, and in March 2025 residents discussed but did not vote on resuming it in 2028.
- The town website's "population of 43": **out of date or unsourced**; the 2020 Census count and OPD's 2025 estimate are both 68.
- The town website's statement that NH law allows midnight voting in "towns with fewer than 100 residents": **not found in the statutes read.** RSA 659:4 sets only a latest opening time (11 a.m.), and RSA 659:3 and 659:3-a allow early closing once all on the checklist have voted; none sets a population limit. RSA 659:3-a names Hart's Location.
- "Cellular dead zones inside Crawford Notch" (P8.3): **correct.** The 2026 draft hazard plan says there is no cell tower in town and no service for a 10-mile stretch of Route 302.
- "Participating agency" for home education (P6.1): **out of date.** The July 2026 law removed participating agencies, notices and annual evaluations for home-educated children.
- "One of the few buildable lots" (P20): **consistent.** About 10% to 12% of the town's area is outside conservation land, and lots must be at least 2.5 acres with 150 feet of frontage.

**Answered wholly by earlier files:** none of the seven assigned questions. P6.3 (family side) is answered mostly by the site's own EFA data, which this file confirms; P6.1 and P11.3 rest on library rules and the Conway and Jackson files, with Hart's Location specifics added here.
