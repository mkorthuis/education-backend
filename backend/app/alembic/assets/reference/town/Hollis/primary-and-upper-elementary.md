- **Premises checked:**
  - "HPS starting at 8:23 and HUES at 8:30" (P1.3): **correct.** SAU 41's FAQ gives 8:23 a.m.–3:05 p.m. at HPS and 8:30 a.m.–2:55 p.m. at HUES (and 7:35 a.m.–2:20 p.m. at the middle school). The handbook's instructional day is 8:23–2:57 and 8:30–2:50. The same buses serve both schools, arriving at HPS at 8:08–8:10 and HUES at 8:12–8:15.
  - "Guaranteed spots in the HPS after-school program" (P1.2): **no guarantee is offered.** The after-school program at both schools is run by the Nashua Adult Learning Center, which takes applications first come, first served. Families must reapply every year, prior attendance doesn't guarantee a spot, and part-time spots are limited.
  - "A lottery" for preschool (P9.1): **not a lottery.** Registration opens in January, first come, first served. The program was at capacity, with an ongoing waitlist worked in timestamp order.
  - Preschool tuition (P9.1): $270 a month for 10 months ($2,700 a year), for typically developing children only.
  - "Only two and a half hours" (P9.2): **correct.** Integrated sessions run 8:45–11:15 a.m. or 12:00–2:30 p.m., Monday to Thursday, and the district provides no transportation. The intensive-needs class runs 6 hours a day.
  - "Full-day public kindergarten" (P20.2): **correct.** HPS calls its kindergarten full day. No kindergarten tuition or afternoon fee is posted. Paid after-school care is separate.
  - "The 3:05 pickup window" (P6.1): **partly.** The FAQ lists HPS as ending at 3:05, but the handbook ends the instructional day at 2:57 with bus dismissal about 3:00. The after-school program takes attendance from 3:05. The district's 2026-27 plan moved pickups after 3:15 p.m. to the office.
  - For agent 4 (P20.1): HPS had 375 students, preschool included, on October 25, 2026. Its September starting enrollment was 370 in 2026 and peaked at 394 in 2023, so the "400 students" premise is above the reported counts.

## research-prekindergarten-effects: What Research Says About Preschool and Readiness for School

- kind: research
- question_type: research
- description: What research finds about whether public preschool (pre-k) makes children more ready for kindergarten, in academic and social-emotional skills, and why programs differ

1. A 2017 consensus statement by researchers who reviewed evaluations of state and school district pre-k programs found "convincing evidence" that children who attend are more ready for school at the end of the pre-k year than children who don't, with the most common gains in literacy and numeracy; the fewer studies of social-emotional and self-regulation skills "generally show more modest improvements," and that evidence "is not as robust or convincing."
   - citation: Phillips, Lipsey, Dodge, Haskins, Bassok and others, "Puzzling It Out: The Current State of Scientific Knowledge on Pre-Kindergarten Effects — A Consensus Statement" (2017), "Evidence for Impacts Shortly After Pre-K Participation"
   - source: https://www.brookings.edu/wp-content/uploads/2017/04/consensus-statement_final.pdf
   - as_of: 2026-11-21
   - review_by: 2027-11-21
   - note: Text: "Convincing evidence shows that children attending a diverse array of state and school district pre-k programs are more ready for school at the end of their pre-k year than children who do not attend pre-k. Improvements in academic areas such as literacy and numeracy are most common; the smaller number of studies of social-emotional and self-regulatory development generally show more modest improvements in those areas."; "The effects on social-emotional skills reported in the few studies that addressed them were generally positive, but this evidence is not as robust or convincing as that for academic outcomes." The statement compares pre-k with no pre-k. It does not compare public with private preschools. Raw: raw/elementary/prek-consensus-statement-2017.txt
2. The same 2017 consensus statement says "pre-k programs are not all equally effective," naming a well-implemented, evidence-based curriculum, coaching for teachers and orderly but active classrooms as factors that may help, and notes that children arrive with different earlier care and home experiences, which shapes how much any program adds.
   - citation: "Puzzling It Out: The Current State of Scientific Knowledge on Pre-Kindergarten Effects — A Consensus Statement" (2017), "Impacts of Experiences During Pre-Kindergarten" and introduction
   - source: https://www.brookings.edu/wp-content/uploads/2017/04/consensus-statement_final.pdf
   - as_of: 2026-11-21
   - review_by: 2027-11-21
   - note: Text: "Pre-k programs are not all equally effective. Several effectiveness factors may be at work in the most successful programs. One such factor supporting early learning is a well implemented, evidence-based curriculum. Coaching for teachers, as well as efforts to promote orderly but active classrooms, may also be helpful."; "Children enter pre-k with divergent prior early care and education—and home—experiences".
