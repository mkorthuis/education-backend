# Money and Governance in Hollis

Part of the Hollis research (town/Hollis). Gathered November 21, 2026.

## Summary

**What's covered.** The Hollis Primary School (HPS) bond that failed on March 17, 2026, why voters said no, and what the Hollis Renovation and Expansion Committee (HREC) has done since: its survey, its options (the old pharmacy, the Farley Building, three Banwell designs at about $8.95 million, $6.7 million and $3.99 million, or waiting), its November 16 votes, the debt that rolls off the tax bill, and the town's placeholder in the capital plan; HPS's enrollment since 2011, what "340" and "400" refer to, the district's estimate of lost capacity, and the 2026 enrollment projections; the Hollis-Brookline Cooperative's budget and enrollment since FY2010, what its spending per student does and doesn't include, its FY2027 cost drivers, special education and out-of-district tuition, and its FY2028 drafts under the new tax cap (including a draft that cuts athletics and performing arts and lists 18 job reductions); the Hollis School District's FY2027 and FY2028 budgets and where the money goes; both support staff contracts (2025–2028) and paraeducator pay against Nashua; SAU 41's budget, how it is split, and the failed 2026 SAU budget vote; how the cooperative splits costs between Hollis and Brookline, and why Hollis pays a larger share of the cooperative's taxes but a lower rate; state aid for Hollis against Brookline; fund balances returned to taxpayers; the 2026 Question 2 vote in Hollis (59.3%) and the pending recount; current use, the land use change tax and the elderly exemption; the 2016 cooperative dissolution report and Brookline's 2025–26 expansion study; how to follow the boards, HREC and the budget committees; how warrant articles are written and the 2026 petition articles; and the status of the AI and sexual harassment policies.

**Counts.** 8 topics (7 shared, 1 proposed: `current-use-taxation`), 103 facts, 5 number tables.

**Main sources.** SAU 41's committee pages and HREC agendas and minutes (June–November 2026, the November 16 minutes still a draft); the Hollis and COOP 2026 annual meeting minutes, hearing presentations, budgets (FY2027 final rounds and FY2028 drafts), warrants and June 2026 special meeting minutes; the SAU 41 FY2027 budget presentation; both HESSA contracts (salary tables read from page images); the COOP Articles of Agreement (2021); the 2016 dissolution report and Brookline's study reports; Diligent Community agendas, policy memos and November 2026 board attachments; NESDEC's October 15, 2026 SAU 41 report (read from page images); the Town of Hollis 2025 annual report, assessing pages, Budget Committee minutes, election results and recount notice; the 2012 HPS facility audit; and RSA 79-A.

**Main gaps.** The 2026 Hollis tax rate (bills went out November 13, 2026, but no rate was posted; the state's PDF is on a site that blocks requests). The November 4 and November 18, 2026 board minutes and the policy committees' minutes, so no stated reason for holding the AI and sexual harassment policies. Paraeducator turnover and Massachusetts pay. Hollis's current use acreage and any data on farmland sales. Hollis-only NESDEC 2026 tables (only the SAU-wide report was posted).

| P19.3 | The Policy Committee tabled the sexual harassment and AI policies this month | Partly shown. The Hollis Policy Committee listed both policies "to review" on October 26; it sent the AI policy (EHAG) to the November 4 board for a first reading and listed the sexual harassment policy (ACA) "to review" again on November 16. ## current-use-taxation: Current Use: Farm, Forest and Open Land Taxes

- kind: rule
- question_type: rules
- description: How New Hampshire's current use program assesses farm, forest and other open land at its use value, and the land use change tax owed when enrolled land is developed (proposed from the Hollis research)

1. Under New Hampshire's current use law, local assessors value land classified as open space (farm, forest and similar land) at the "current use values" set by the state's Current Use Board rather than its market value, excluding any buildings; owners must apply by April 15 of the tax year.
   - citation: RSA 79-A:5, I–II
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/V/79-A/79-A-5.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-21
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text: "The selectmen or assessing officials shall appraise open space land, as classified under the provisions of this chapter, excluding any building, appurtenance or other improvement on the land, at valuations based upon the current use values established by the board"; "unless he shall have applied to the assessing officials on or before April 15 of said year". Raw: raw/money/rsa/rsa-79-A-5.txt
2. When land in current use changes to a use that doesn't qualify, its owner owes a land use change tax of 10% of the land's full market value, in addition to the regular property tax; the tax is on the change of use, not the land, and no one is exempt from it.
   - citation: RSA 79-A:7, I
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/V/79-A/79-A-7.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-21
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text: "the tax shall be at the rate of 10 percent of the full and true value determined without regard to the current use value of the land ... This tax shall be in addition to the annual real estate tax imposed upon the property ... The tax imposed by this section is a tax on the change of use of the land and not a tax on the land itself ... no person or entity shall be exempt from payment of the land use change tax." Raw: raw/money/rsa/rsa-79-A-7.txt
3. Land counts as changed in use when construction begins (such as building a road, installing utilities or grading for buildings), when topsoil, gravel or minerals are dug for sale, or when the parcel becomes too small to qualify; roads for farming, forestry or recreation don't count, and usually only the acres actually changed are taxed.
   - citation: RSA 79-A:7, IV–V
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/V/79-A/79-A-7.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-21
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text: "(a) Actual construction begins on the site causing physical changes in the earth, such as building a road ... except that roads for agricultural, recreational, watershed or forestry purposes are exempt. (b) Topsoil, gravel or minerals are excavated or dug from the site ... (c) By reason of size, the site no longer conforms"; "only the number of acres on which an actual physical change has taken place shall become subject to the land use change tax". Raw: raw/money/rsa/rsa-79-A-7.txt
