# Farmington: Money, Taxes, Buildings and Governance

Part of the Farmington research (town/Farmington). Gathered November 21, 2026.

## Summary

**What's covered:** 10 topics, 83 facts and 5 number tables on how the Farmington School District (SAU 61) builds, votes on and pays for its budget; the 2026 ballot (the failed operating budget, the $18.1 million default, the failed teachers' contract and the failed $160,000 bleacher article); the district's 2025-26 shortfall, layoffs and unfinished audits; how the school tax rate is set and the 2024 revaluation; state aid; Question 2; the school capital reserve funds and what happened to the $700,000 voters approved in 2024; the buildings' known needs; community use of school buildings and its fee tiers; gifts, donations and scholarships; staffing and turnover; special education placements; outside ratings against Strafford and Alton; and the board's talks with Milton and Alton.

- **Budget.** Voters rejected the 2026-27 operating budget on March 10, 2026, 239 to 337, so the default budget of $18,076,646.01 took effect: $735,900 (4.2%, **derived**) above 2025-26's $17,340,746. The board had proposed $21,078,972.42 (posted tax impact +$4.88 per $1,000); the deliberative session raised the default by $780,000 (health insurance and a lighting lease) and cut the proposal to $19,376,646.01 (about +$3.48 per $1,000, as stated there).
- **Why costs jumped while enrollment fell:** the 2025-26 budget had been set below the prior year's spending; a mid-year shortfall ($405,000 in November 2025), an unbudgeted SchoolCare health insurance bill ($321,519.32), higher insurance, transportation (+$548,431 proposed) and special education (+$760,103 proposed) costs. The board laid off staff mid-year (the district's "first Reduction in Force (RIF) in years"). The 2023-24 and 2024-25 audits were still unfinished on November 2, 2026.
- **Reserves.** The school's trust and capital reserve funds held $874,432.59 on December 31, 2025, with no new money added in 2025. Voters had approved moving $700,000 of 2024 fund balance into nine school funds in March 2024; the funds' balances don't show those deposits (**derived**), and district officials said in November 2025 that the $700,000 "keeps rolling over ... even though there is no $700,000."
- **Question 2.** Farmington voted 1,722 to 745 (69.8%) for the school tax cap; it limits growth in the school tax levy starting with the 2027-28 budget. It does not lower taxes.
- **Ratings.** On GreatSchools, Henry Wilson (7 of 10) rates above Strafford School (6) and Alton Central (5); Valley View (3) and Farmington High (1) rate below them. Farmington has a much larger share of students from low-income families (38.6% eligible for free or reduced-price meals against 19.2% in Alton and 11.5% in Strafford, **derived** from the site's data).
