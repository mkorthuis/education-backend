# High School From Wakefield: Spaulding, Kingswood Seats, Career Programs, Buses, Scholarships and Choices

Part of the Wakefield research (town/Wakefield). Gathered November 22, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers how Wakefield's high school students are placed and served: the Rochester–Wakefield AREA plan's terms on choice, the 10% allowance for other schools, joint board meetings and amendments; how many Wakefield students are at Spaulding, Kingswood and Bud Carlson and how Governor Wentworth admitted them; Wakefield's own policy for best-interest and hardship transfers; who transports students and how far the schools are; the career programs open to Wakefield students for electrical work and nursing assistant (LNA) training; Wakefield's high school special education counts; the local scholarships open to Wakefield graduates and which ones cover trade or two-year schools; private high schools near Wakefield; and what is known about Wakefield students leaving high school early.

- **10 fact topics, 52 facts and 4 tables.** Raw copies are in `raw/high/` (about 33 MB, most of it the 21 MB district annual report and the 7 MB budget book): the State Board's 2018 certificate and the amended Rochester–Wakefield AREA plan (scanned; OCR), the Wakefield School District 2025-2026 annual report (with OCR of its scanned deliberative-session pages), the FY2027 budget book (scanned; OCR and a page image), policy JCA, Wakefield school board minutes of October 1, 2024 and April 7, 2026, Governor Wentworth's minutes (August 2025 to October 2026) and October 1, 2025 enrollment sheet, Spaulding's 2026-27 Program of Studies (Google Doc export), the SHS Counseling scholarship page and eight scholarship applications, the Lions' application form, the Lovell Lake Association's page and application, RSA 195-A:3, 4 and 5, the NCES private school search for ZIP 03872, and OSRM drive routes.
- **Places:** 29 facts use `district Wakefield`, 5 `district Rochester`, 2 `district Governor Wentworth Regional`, 12 `town Wakefield`, and 4 have no place (RSA 195-A).
- **Main findings:**
  - **No lottery was found.** The AREA plan lets Wakefield's board approve and pay tuition elsewhere for up to 10% of grades 9–12 each year, telling Rochester by June 1; approvals can last to graduation. Governor Wentworth's board then votes on each admission. On October 1, 2025, 22 Wakefield students were at Kingswood (11 in grade 9, 8 in grade 10, 2 in grade 11, 1 in grade 12), about 12% of Wakefield's 182 high schoolers (derived). Wakefield's FY2027 budget book counts only 1 or 2 Kingswood students in its tuition line; the sources don't explain the difference.
  - **Mid-high school moves happen.** Governor Wentworth approved a Wakefield "transfer request" in November 2025 and best-interest admissions in March and April 2026, the April one only while the parent works for Governor Wentworth. Wakefield's policy JCA sets the request, meeting and hearing steps; families transport.
  - **Buses:** state law makes the sending district (Wakefield) responsible for transport to Spaulding; Wakefield contracts First Student. No late bus from Spaulding to Wakefield was found. By car, Spaulding is about 22 minutes from Union village and 28 from Sanbornville (derived).
  - **Electrical:** Creteau has no electrical program; Spaulding students can apply to Dover's Electrical Technology. LRTC serves Kingswood and four other high schools, not Spaulding.
  - **LNA:** Spaulding's Health Science Technology II includes an LNA option with 70+ clinical hours at a local nursing home and 5 Lakes Region Community College credits, with limited seats.
  - **Scholarships:** the Greater Wakefield Lions award names "college, apprenticeship or a technical school"; the Greater Wakefield Chamber's three $1,500 awards include two-year, technical and vocational schools and a trade scholarship; the Flora Belle Weber trust is for four-year colleges only.
- **New data concepts:** the AREA plan's 10% allowance, notice date and amendment process; Kingswood enrollment of Wakefield students by grade; Governor Wentworth's admission votes for Wakefield students; Wakefield's budgeted high school counts; drive distances from Wakefield villages to Spaulding, Kingswood and Dover; Spaulding's LNA option; a local scholarship table with the kinds of schools each covers; private high schools near Wakefield with drive distances.
- **Proposed new shared topic:** none. Local scholarships are under `town-family-help`, as in the Wolfeboro file. RSA 195-A rules are added to the library's `gov-area-agreements` topic, using its own kind and question type.

## high-school-tuition-options: Wakefield's High School Arrangement: The Rochester AREA Plan, Kingswood Seats and Transfers

1. Wakefield School District students in grades 9–12 attend the "Rochester Area School" in Rochester under an Authorized Regional Enrollment Area (AREA) plan in which Wakefield is the sending district and the Rochester School District is the receiving district responsible for grades 9–12; Rochester's operating responsibility began July 1, 1970, and an amended plan took effect July 1, 2018 after Wakefield's district meeting (March 14, 2018), Rochester's City Council (April 3, 2018) and the State Board of Education (May 10, 2018) approved it.

## gov-area-agreements: AREA Schools: Who Runs Them, Who Transports and the Sending Towns' Voice

- kind: term
- question_type: governance_money
- description: What an AREA agreement is and how it differs from a cooperative.

1. Under New Hampshire law, an AREA school is run by the receiving district's school board under the general school laws, the receiving district must educate all resident pupils of the sending districts along with its own, and each sending district must assign and send its resident pupils to the area school and raise their tuition each year.
   - citation: RSA 195-A:4
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XV/195-A/195-A-4.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-22
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text: "An area school shall be maintained and operated by the receiving district and its school board in accordance with all the general school laws ... The receiving district shall be obligated to provide for the elementary or secondary school education, or both, of all the resident pupils of the sending districts as well as its own ... The sending districts shall be obligated to assign and send their resident pupils to the area school ... and to raise and appropriate annually the tuition of each such pupil". Raw: raw/high/law/rsa-195-A-4.txt
2. Under New Hampshire law, transporting a sending district's students to the AREA school is the responsibility of that sending district, under the general school transportation laws, and the area school is every resident pupil's assigned school for the attendance laws except as RSA 193:3 (change of school assignment) allows.
   - citation: RSA 195-A:4
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XV/195-A/195-A-4.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-22
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text: "Transportation of resident pupils of the sending districts to the area school shall be governed by the general school laws applicable thereto and shall be the responsibility of each sending district. An area school shall be deemed the assigned school for all resident pupils in the region which it is established to serve, for purposes of the school attendance laws, except as provided in RSA 193:3." Raw: raw/high/law/rsa-195-A-4.txt
3. Under New Hampshire law, the State Board of Education holds joint meetings of all the school boards in an AREA, at reasonable intervals or when a board asks, with up to 3 representatives from each board and a state agent presiding; the meetings are "advisory, consultative, and informational" and may not infringe on the receiving district board's authority over its schools.
   - citation: RSA 195-A:5
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XV/195-A/195-A-5.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-22
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text: "Each school board shall be entitled to 3 representatives at such meetings, which shall be presided over by an agent of the state board designated by the commissioner of education. Such meetings shall be advisory, consultative, and informational in nature and shall not infringe upon the legal authority and responsibility of the school board of the receiving district over the schools within such district." Raw: raw/high/law/rsa-195-A-5.txt
4. Under New Hampshire law, an AREA plan's written terms must include, among other things, a procedure for improving or changing curriculum and other school programs and services, how the plan may be amended, and "the manner in which the interests of the school boards of the sending districts will be addressed."
   - citation: RSA 195-A:3, V(l), (m) and (o)
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XV/195-A/195-A-3.htm

   - note: Text: "(l) Procedure for improvement or changes in curriculum and other school programs and services; (m) The method, time, and manner in which the plan may be amended, subject to state board approval ...; (o) The manner in which the interests of the school boards of the sending districts will be addressed".
