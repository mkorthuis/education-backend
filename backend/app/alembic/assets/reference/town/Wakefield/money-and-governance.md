# Money and Governance: Wakefield's School Budget, Tax Cap, Rochester Tuition and School Board

Part of the Wakefield research (town/Wakefield). Gathered November 22, 2026.

## Summary

**What's covered:** 9 topics, 90 facts and 5 number tables on the Wakefield School District (SAU 101): the November 2026 school tax cap and what the board has said and done since; the 2026-27 budget, its drivers (health insurance, Spaulding tuition, special education and buses), 2025-26 actual spending and the superintendent's November 2026 draft 2027-28 budget under the cap; the AREA agreement's tuition terms, the rate Wakefield paid in 2025-26 and will pay in 2026-27, and the withdrawal study committee voters were asked to form in March 2026; how the town hands over school tax money; SAU 101's one-district central office and its cost; trust fund deposits, balances and withdrawals; the teacher and paraprofessional contracts and the votes on them in 2022 and 2025, turnover and hiring; and how school board meetings run and can be watched.

**Main sources:** the district's 2026 warrant, sample ballot and deliberative session minutes; its FY2027 proposed budget (line items with FY2025 actuals) and January 2026 budget book (budget summary, enrollment and tuition sheet, staff rosters, the teacher and paraprofessional contracts); the DRA MS-27 form and revenue sheet; the Mandatory Report to Voters; the district's HB 1300 slides; the 2025-26 annual report; 35 sets of school board minutes (July 2025 to November 5, 2026) and, from the district's document library indexed on November 22, 2026, the 2022 and 2025 election results, January 2025 minutes, the October 2023 joint AREA meeting minutes, the FY2028 budget drafts and central office budgets, the 2026-27 board calendar and recent packets, all downloaded from the district's file host; the 2018 Rochester-Wakefield AREA agreement; RSA 195-A:14; and read-only queries of the site's database. Most of the district's PDFs are scans; they were read with OCR and by eye (transcriptions in raw/money/sau101/transcribed-pages.txt).

**Main findings:**
- **Tax cap and the 2027-28 draft:** Wakefield voted 70.07% for Question 2, and the superintendent advises no override. The district's November 18, 2026 sheets cap the 2027-28 levy at $11,550,178 ($11,095,272 × 1.041). The draft budget rises 1.54% with no trust deposits yet; it lowers elementary teacher salaries by $288,092 and special education busing, and raises special education by 23.85%, mainly for contracted services and private placements.
- **Spaulding tuition:** Wakefield paid $15,508.91 per student in 2025-26 because Rochester's notice came late under the AREA agreement, leaving about $712,000 unspent in the tuition line by May 2026. The 2026-27 rate is $20,043, about 29% more (**derived**). Regular high school tuition is about 26% of the 2026-27 general fund (**derived**).
- **The "Area Agreement Committee"** is a withdrawal study committee under RSA 195-A:14, put to voters in March 2026. The district says it aims to "open up options" with Spaulding kept as an "anchor school"; its first meeting was set for mid-November 2026, and state law gives it 180 days to report.
- **Trust funds:** deposits were $297,000 (FY2023), $325,000 (FY2025), $215,000 (FY2026) and $225,000 adopted for FY2027 of $430,000 proposed; the draft has none for FY2028. About $105,000 was withdrawn in FY2026 and $211,000 in July–August 2026 for repairs (**derived** sums).
- **Health insurance** rose 28.5% for 2026-27, plus a one-time SchoolCare deficit assessment of $147,787.19 taken from the 2025-26 budget. Special education busing actually cost $687,693 in 2025-26 against $125,000 budgeted.
- **Contracts:** one board member voted no (4-1) on the 2025–2028 teachers' contract; voters approved it 543-247 in March 2025. The paraprofessionals' contract ends June 2027 and was on the November 17, 2026 agenda.

**Main gaps:** the official March 10, 2026 vote counts are not in the district's library (the FY28 draft shows which money articles were adopted); wakefieldnh.gov (HTTP 403) refused requests, so the town budget and the trustees' trust fund reports were not read; no audits since FY2022 were posted; the 2023–2024 election results and the January 2024 AREA committee's minutes weren't in the library.
