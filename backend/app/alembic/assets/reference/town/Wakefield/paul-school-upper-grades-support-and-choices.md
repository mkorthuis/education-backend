# Paul School's Upper Grades, Support and Choices: Home Education, Special Education, Sports and Clubs, the State's "Good Standing" Label, Technology, Snow Days, Transferring In, the Principal, Behavior and Phones, English Learners, World Languages and Bullying

Part of the Wakefield research (town/Wakefield). Gathered November 22, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers Paul School's grades 6–8 and the help and choices around them: how home-educated Wakefield children can use Paul School, and how they come back; special education staffing and services; school sports, clubs and what families pay; what NH DOE's "Good Standing" status means and where Paul School stands; devices, technology staff and the phone rule; storm closings and the 2026-27 calendar; what a student transferring in for grades 7–8 finds; the principal; behavior supports; English learner (ESOL) services; world languages; and the bullying procedure.

**Sources:** Paul School's Student and Family Handbook 2026-2027 and the district's Employee Handbook 2026-2027 (both approved by the school board August 4, 2026); the 2026-27 calendar (approved January 20, 2026; a scanned image, transcribed); the older Paul School Acceptable Use Policy; the FY2027 Proposed Budget and the FY2027 Budget Book dated January 9, 2026 (both scanned, read by local OCR and checked against page images); SAU 101's Special Education and 504 page and home page (November 22, 2026); NH DOE's "School Designations for Fall 2025" (library copy); the federal Civil Rights Data Collection (CRDC) 2023-24; and Wakefield Parks and Recreation's basketball page. **Second pass (November 22, 2026):** the main session listed SAU 101's document library (`raw/browser/sau101-documents-index.tsv`), and the district's board policies (JJJ, IHBG, IHBBA, JICK, JICJ, JJIB, JJIC, JJA, JQ, JICEA, IC, JG, JFAA, EBCD), school board minutes from January to October 2026, and board-packet reports (principal, superintendent, student services, class enrollment, the technology plan) were downloaded from the district's file host and read. **Main findings:**
- **"Good Standing" is real NH DOE wording, but it isn't Paul School's status on the latest list found.** On NH DOE's fall 2025 list, Paul Elementary School is a continuing Additional Targeted Support and Improvement (ATSI) school for students with disabilities, identified in 2023; "Good Standing" is the status of schools that have exited. In January 2026 the superintendent told the board the school could exit in fall 2026 or stay on the list under new 2026 standards. The fall 2026 list wasn't reachable (NH DOE returns 403), and no later minutes say how it came out.
- **The principal is interim, and a search was promised.** On May 5, 2026 the board accepted Principal Melissa Dorsey's resignation and named Assistant Principal Aaron Nason interim principal (3-2), saying a search committee of teachers, parents and community members would be formed. Since July the minutes list him as interim principal, with an interim assistant principal hired July 7 (3-1). No search committee meeting or permanent appointment appears in the minutes through November 5 or the November 17 agenda.
- **Homeschoolers have equal access by policy.** Policy JJJ (October 6, 2026) gives home-educated, EFA, charter and private school residents the same access as enrolled students to courses, activities and the school library, under the same rules and fees; in October five or six homeschoolers were taking some classes. An older policy, IHBG (2013), with different steps, is still posted.
- **Special education relies on contracted specialists.** Speech-language, therapy, psychology, ESOL, a behavior analyst (BCBA) and a transition consultant are contracted ($522,000 proposed for FY2027), and one case manager post was open. The district reports 100% compliance with state special education timelines. In 2024-25, 13.7% of its students with IEPs had a speech or language impairment as their main disability, against 10.9% statewide. The Paws classroom for high-needs students was being rebuilt with a $15,000 foundation gift.
- **ESOL is a contracted service, and the coordinator is leaving.** The FY2027 budget lists ESOL among contracted services. The November 17, 2026 agenda lists the resignation of the Director of Student Services, who is also the 504 and ESOL coordinator.
- **A world language teacher was added for 2026-27.** The board created the position in April and filled it in August 2026; world language is now part of the middle school's integrated arts rotation. The language isn't named.
- **The middle school was reshaped for 2026-27:** grade 6 joined the middle school, with leveled classes, enrichment blocks, shorter periods, a full year of science and new math, ELA and science programs. The school reported 90 incident reports by September 30, 2026, against 204 a year earlier, after a February report that incidents had doubled and were concentrated in grades 7–8.
- **Fees.** Policy JJIC requires a sports participation fee with a waiver on request; no amount was found. The 2026 Washington, D.C. trip cost about $1,700 per student, largely covered by fundraising and $10,000 from the budget. Chromebook screens broken by negligence cost families $45.
- **Snow days.** Policy IC says closed days are made up at the end of the year or during vacations; no remote learning days are described. The 2026-27 calendar was built on hours, not days: 176 student days by count (**derived**), against policy IC's 180-day minimum.
