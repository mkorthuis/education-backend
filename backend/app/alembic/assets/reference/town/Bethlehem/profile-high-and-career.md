# Profile High School and Career Programs

Part of the Bethlehem research (town/Bethlehem). Gathered November 22, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers Profile School (grades 7–12, a single-school district in SAU 35 serving Bethlehem, Easton, Franconia and Sugar Hill) as it bears on 12 resident questions: how big the school is and what advanced classes it offers (AP, math, science, technology), VLACS and other outside credit, college credit (Early College, eStart, dual enrollment), the career centers in Littleton and Whitefield and the bus to them, credit for internships, work study and apprenticeships, outdoor and environmental programs, sports co-ops, what graduates do, college and career advising, science and technology equipment, and local scholarships.

- **11 fact topics (one of them a proposed new shared topic), 75 facts and 3 number tables.** Raw copies are in `raw/high/` (about 5 MB). Scripts are in the session scratchpad folder `bethlehem-high/`.
- **Places:** `district Profile` for the school's courses, advising, buses, outcomes, sports and capital work; `district Littleton` for one Gallen CTC course description that fills a gap in the Littleton research; `town Bethlehem` for the town's scholarship trust and the Bethlehem fund at the NH Charitable Foundation; no place for VLACS (statewide).
- **Sources:** Profile's 2026-27 Program of Studies and Student Handbook (Google Docs), the 2025-26 school profile it sends colleges, the school's web pages, the counseling Google Sites and the Class of 2027 letters, the February 2026 "Passports to Possibilities" slide deck, the principal's monthly board reports (September 2025 to September 2026), Profile School Board minutes (July 2025 to June 2026), SAU 35 board policies (IHBH, IHCD, IKFG), NHIAA's 2026-27 handbook files, the SAU 35 capital project page, the school's scholarship spreadsheet, and NH Education Facts data. Two requests were refused (see Not retrieved); every other page opened without a bot check.
- **State rules and the Gallen CTC are cited, not rewritten:** dual and concurrent enrollment, AP exam fees, CTE access and cost rules, youth employment and apprenticeship rules, NHIAA cooperative-team rules, and FAFSA information come from the library and earlier towns' files, named by file, topic and fact number.
- **Source conflicts:** (1) Profile's school profile says ELOs "do not count towards a student's Profile GPA or class rank", while SAU 35 policy IHBH says credits earned "may be calculated towards the overall Grade Point Average" (school-programs-after-grade-6 19). (2) The clubs page says only middle schoolers "can compete at meets" in Math Team, while the April 2026 principal's report says a high school team placed 4th of 41 at the state competition (district-enrichment-and-advanced-learners 2). (3) The school profile lists no softball in NHIAA's 2026-27 list while Profile's athletics page lists a girls' varsity softball team (district-pe-outdoor-and-athletics 8). (4) Enrollment counts differ by source (table profile_enrollment_grades_7_12_by_source).
- **Proposed new shared topic:** `vlacs-online-courses` (kind programs, question type help): "VLACS, New Hampshire's online public charter school: who may enroll, what it costs, what it offers, and how it counts for a student's high school credit and AP courses." One fact is filed under it; other towns' files mention VLACS only inside school-programs-after-grade-6.
- **Main gaps:** no science lab or classroom technology renovation was found (only heating and ventilation work); no count of Profile students at the Gallen CTC or at White Mountains Regional, or of AP exam scores; the 2026-27 bus times for the CTE run; how Profile's "work study" earns credit and whether paid work counts; the Internship Program Manual (404); NHIAA's list of approved cooperative teams (members-only, as the Littleton research also found); and the Town of Bethlehem's own page on the Jason C. | P2.1 | Profile has "only around 200 kids in grades 7 through 12" | Roughly right, but it moves. Profile's home page says "approximately 200"; the principal reported 193 in September 2026; the budget hearing gave 211 for 2024-25 and 199 for 2025-26; the site's NH DOE data show 216 in 2024-25 (83 in grades 7–8, 133 in grades 9–12). | P2.2 | Profile lets students take a class through VLACS when it isn't offered in person | Correct, with conditions. Profile prioritizes in-person classes; outside credit is approved only for courses it doesn't offer or that conflict with the schedule, after an ELO form and counselor review, and the student reports progress every 4 weeks. The board notes of May 2026 say VLACS courses had been "pushed not being given as credit" and "now they are being offered for credit". Profile pays the AP exam fee for an AP course taken on VLACS when Profile doesn't offer it. |
| P5.1 | Profile students can reach "Littleton's tech center" | Correct. Profile sends eligible grades 10–12 students to the Gallen CTC in Littleton and the Arthur T. Profile's school profile lists 74 colleges and no employers or trades programs. |
| P12.2 | Dual enrollment with WMCC or Plymouth State | WMCC: yes (Early College courses at Profile, eStart, and on campus or online). Plymouth State: none of Profile's documents mentions it; PSU runs its own Accelerated High School Program that any eligible junior or senior may apply to (Plymouth file). |
| P12.3 | Students can co-op with Littleton | Not found. Profile's only co-op discussion in the notes is a possible golf co-op with Lisbon for fall 2026. In NHIAA's 2026-27 lists no team combines Profile with Littleton, and Littleton's own co-op talks were with White Mountains Regional (field hockey). |
| P18.2 | Science labs and technology equipment may have been updated | No lab update was found. Profile's 2025–2026 capital work is heating, ventilation and controls (an energy project with a $2,670,253 lease-purchase). Equipment items found are school-issued Chromebooks for all students, a CAD course with 3D printers and CNC machines, a grant-bought telescope, a donated woodshop lathe set-up and grant-funded coaching for engineering and robotics. |
