# Profile School: Student Life and Support

Part of the Bethlehem research (town/Bethlehem). Gathered November 22, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers student life and support at Profile School (grades 7–12, Bethlehem, SAU 35): what a family coming in at 7th grade meets, part-time access for home-educated students to soccer and band, special education (the move from Bethlehem Elementary, who provides services, how to disagree), counseling and mental health support, the phone rule, 504 plans and quiet spaces, getting home after activities, and how students from four towns share one school.

## district-homeschool-access: Homeschooled and EFA Students at Profile School

- kind: local
- question_type: local_practice
- description: How homeschooled and EFA students can take part in a district's classes, activities and sports, and the district's homeschool notice and evaluation process

1. In the Profile School District, SAU 35 board policy JJJ gives home-educated, nonpublic and charter school students and Education Freedom Account (EFA) students who live in the district the same access as enrolled students to the district's courses and its co- and extra-curricular programs (which RSA 193:1-c says include clubs, athletics, performing groups and service to school and community), and says they may not face policies, procedures, fees or standards more restrictive than those for enrolled students.

Non-enrolled district students shall not be subject to any policies, procedures, fees, or standards with respect to participation in the District's courses or programs that are more restrictive than those governing the District's enrolled students." ## school-sports-rules: School Sports Rules: Concussions, Medical Coverage and Cooperative Teams

- kind: rule

1. Under the NHIAA's 2026-27 by-laws (Article II, Section 13A), a home-educated student is immediately eligible to try out for a member school's team if the parents send the principal a written request, the student meets the school board's policies and the NHIAA's age, scholastic standing and semester rules, the principal is satisfied there was no recruiting and certifies eligibility, and the student lives with the parents or guardians; the student may represent a school in the district where they live, only one school in a year, and the principal must review scholastic standing each marking period and keep the student's transcript.
