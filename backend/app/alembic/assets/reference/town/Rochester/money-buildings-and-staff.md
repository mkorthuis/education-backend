# Rochester: Money, Buildings, Staff and Governance

Part of the Rochester research (town/Rochester). Gathered November 15, 2026.

## Summary

**What's covered:** 9 topics, 82 facts and 6 number tables on how Rochester's school budget is set and paid for, its buildings, its staff and its school board.
- **The 2026-27 (FY2027) budget from start to finish.** The $4.0–4.5 million gap the superintendent described in January 2026; the 39 positions and the closing of Maple Street Magnet School proposed in February; the three options of March 3 (one of them cutting up to four classroom teachers per school); the City Council's use of the charter's debt service exception on June 2 "to restore the loss of 15 teaching positions"; and the final $83,812,156 budget ($78,016,601 general fund). What drove it: state adequacy aid budgeted $2.44 million lower, special education tuition, and a local property tax levy up 8.2% while the general fund stayed flat.
- **The city charter tax cap** (Section 43-a): its CPI-plus-new-construction formula as the city explains it, the FY2026 and FY2027 cap figures, the use of unassigned fund balance, and the state law that lets a charter cap exclude bonded debt by a supermajority (RSA 49-C:33).
- **The tax bill:** the 2025 rate ($15.30) and why it rose, the city's estimate of $0.63 more for 2026 and what that means for a median home, the 2024 revaluation, and Rochester's elderly, disabled and veterans' exemptions.
- **Buildings:** Salmon Falls School's plan, funding and final cost (about $32.49 million spent of $34 million appropriated; $1,350,655.24 returned), the closings of Nancy Loud (2023), School Street and William Allen (2025) and Maple Street Magnet (2026), the FY2027 capital plan, the building assessments under way (Rochester Middle School's is scheduled), the Spaulding fields paid for with JUUL settlement money, and the Gonic entry project.
- **Wakefield's tuition:** the 2026-27 rate formula ($20,042.93 for high school), what it leaves out, payments in 2025-26 and the Area Agreement Committee.
- **Staff:** vacancy counts reported to the board, the 22 paraeducator postings open on November 15, 2026, the new Intensive Special Education Paraeducator role, the 2023 paraprofessional contract, substitute pay, and administrator versus teacher salary lines.
- **Public input:** when and where the board meets, its public comment rules (policy BEDJ), and how meetings are broadcast.
- **Rules for families:** the federal asbestos (AHERA) rules on management plans and yearly notices, and New Hampshire's law on lead testing of school drinking water.

**Main sources:** the district's FY2027 approved budget; Rochester School Board packets and minutes (December 2025 – November 2026) posted as PDFs linked from the city's news site; the district's 2026-27 Wakefield tuition worksheet; the district's AppliTrack job postings; The Rochester Post (the city's own news site); NH statutes (RSA 49-C, 485:17-a); 40 CFR 763; NHMA; and the site's database.

**Premises checked:**

| Question | Premise | What the sources show |
|---|---|---|
| P3.1 | "The City Council used a special exception to restore 15 teaching jobs in the FY27 budget" | Correct. On June 2, 2026 the Council used the debt service exception clause in City Charter Section 43a, Paragraph B, which lets capital spending and bond payments be left out of the CPI-based budget limit by a two-thirds vote of all councilors, for one budget year at a time (district-tax-and-state-aid 14), "to allow the School Department to restore the loss of 15 teaching positions." The city's proposed budgets had been $175,168 under the cap. State law lets charter caps exclude bond principal and interest by a supermajority vote. Whether that means the cap "doesn't hold" is a judgment the sources don't make. |
| P3.3 | "The $83 million we're spending on schools" | Correct for the all-funds total: FY2027's grand total is $83,812,156. That includes $3.8 million in grants and $2.0 million for food service. The general fund is $78,016,601, of which $44,632,703 comes from the local property tax. |
| P8.1 | Whether Wakefield's tuition covers the real cost | The 2026-27 high school rate ($20,042.93) is Rochester's budgeted cost per high school pupil, leaving out debt service, transportation, food service, grants and special education tuition, plus a building rent charge ($657.01). Special education is billed separately. It is $1,878.93 above NH DOE's FY2025 cost per pupil for Rochester's high schools ($18,164; **derived**, different years and definitions). No source measures the added cost of Wakefield's students. |
| P8.3 | "Cutting classroom teachers first" | Not what the documents show as the district's plan. The superintendent said cuts would come through attrition, retirements and vacancies first. Option A (March 3) "avoid[ed] cutting classroom teachers"; only Option B, keeping Maple Street open, would have cut up to four classroom teachers per school. Still, the FY2027 budget cut regular-instruction teacher salaries by 15.1%, and the Council's exception was used to restore 15 teaching positions. |
| P14.3 | "Spending $36 million on Salmon Falls" after closing older schools "years ago" | Not supported. The project was estimated at $33.6 million, the Council approved up to $34 million (October 2023), and the city's ledger shows $32,271,244.76 spent plus $218,100 committed; $1,350,655.24 was returned in 2026. The state paid $15.77 million. The closings were part of the same plan: Nancy Loud in 2023 (unsafe foundation), School Street and William Allen in 2025, as Salmon Falls opened. |

## budget-city-school-districts: School Budgets in Cities

- kind: how_it_works
- question_type: governance_money
- description: Why some city school budgets are set by the city government rather than at a district meeting.

1. New Hampshire law lets a city charter include a limit on yearly spending increases that raise the amount collected in taxes (a tax cap), and requires such a cap to set a supermajority vote, fixed in the charter, for exceeding it.
   - citation: RSA 49-C:33, I(d)
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/III/49-C/49-C-33.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-15
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Statute text: "A limit on the annual spending increases that increase the amount raised by taxes under the city budget adopted pursuant to RSA 49-C:23. Such a tax cap shall provide for an override threshold on a vote to exceed the limit on annual increases which shall be by a supermajority as determined in the charter." Raw: raw/money/rsa-49-c-33.txt
2. A New Hampshire city's charter tax cap may exclude dedicated, enterprise or self-supporting funds, capital reserve funds, grants, non-tax revenue, interest and principal on municipal bonded debt, or capital spending, but each such exclusion must be made by a supermajority vote set in the charter.
   - citation: RSA 49-C:33, I(d)
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/III/49-C/49-C-33.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-15
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Statute text: "A tax cap provision in the city charter may provide for specific exclusions for dedicated, enterprise, or self-supporting funds or accounts, capital reserve funds, grants, or revenue from sources other than local taxes, or interest and principal payments on municipal bonded debt, or capital expenditures which shall be by a supermajority vote as determined in the charter." Raw: raw/money/rsa-49-c-33.txt
3. Since August 20, 2021, New Hampshire law also requires a city's supermajority vote for any ordinance or accounting practice that moves excludable budget items from inside a capped budget to outside it.
   - citation: RSA 49-C:33, I(d) (2021, 88:2)
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/III/49-C/49-C-33.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-15
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Statute text: "An ordinance or accounting practice that redistributes excludable budget items from within the limits of the capped budget to outside the limits of the capped budget shall be by a supermajority vote as determined in the charter." Source note: "2021, 88:2, eff. Aug. 20, 2021." NHMA's legal counsel summarizes: "city and town charter exclusions, ordinances and accounting practices that have the effect of an override of a tax cap require a supermajority vote of the legislative body." Raw: raw/money/rsa-49-c-33.txt; raw/money/nhma-taxcaps.txt
4. When a New Hampshire city charter includes a tax cap override threshold, the city's elected body must adopt its annual budget by a supermajority vote.
   - citation: RSA 49-C:12, III
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/III/49-C/49-C-12.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-15
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Statute text: "Notwithstanding any contrary provision in paragraph I, the adoption of an override threshold provision to a tax cap included in a charter pursuant to RSA 49-C:33, I(d) shall provide for a supermajority vote of the elected body to adopt the annual budget." Added 2011. Raw: raw/money/rsa-49-c-12.txt

   - citation: NH Municipal Association, Stephen C. Buckley, "Municipal Tax Caps – An Overview and Update in Light of Recent Legislation," Town & City

## school-environmental-health-rules: Asbestos and Lead in School Buildings: What the Rules Require

- kind: rule
- question_type: rules
- description: Federal and New Hampshire rules on asbestos in school buildings and lead in school drinking water: what schools must do and what records families can see

1. Under the federal asbestos rules for schools (AHERA), every school must keep a complete, updated asbestos management plan in its office and let parents and the public inspect it within 5 working days of a request, and the district office must keep plans for all its schools open to inspection during business hours, without cost; copies may cost a reasonable fee.
   - citation: 40 CFR 763.93(g)(1)–(3)
   - source: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-40/chapter-I/subchapter-R/part-763/subpart-E/section-763.93
   - as_of: 2026-11-15
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Regulation text: "Each school shall maintain in its administrative office a complete, updated copy of the management plan for that school ... The school shall make management plans available for inspection to representatives of EPA and the State, the public, including parents, teachers, and other school personnel and their representatives within 5 working days after receiving a request for inspection. The school may charge a reasonable cost to make copies". Retrieved from the eCFR API (version 2026-11-01). Raw: raw/money/ecfr-40-763-subpartE.xml (.txt)
2. AHERA requires each school district, at least once each school year, to notify parent, teacher and employee organizations in writing that the management plans are available, and to keep workers and building occupants or their guardians informed each year about inspections, asbestos work and surveillance.
   - citation: 40 CFR 763.93(g)(4); 40 CFR 763.84(c)
   - source: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-40/chapter-I/subchapter-R/part-763/subpart-E/section-763.93
   - as_of: 2026-11-15
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text: "at least once each school year, the local education agency shall notify in writing parent, teacher, and employee organizations of the availability of management plans"; §763.84(c): "Ensure that workers and building occupants, or their legal guardians, are informed at least once each school year about inspections, response actions, and post-response action activities, including periodic reinspection and surveillance activities that are planned or in progress." Raw: raw/money/ecfr-40-763-subpartE.txt
3. AHERA requires a school district to reinspect all known or assumed asbestos-containing material in its buildings at least once every three years and to check it at least once every six months.
   - citation: 40 CFR 763.85(b)(1); 40 CFR 763.92(b)(1)
   - source: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-40/chapter-I/subchapter-R/part-763/subpart-E/section-763.85
   - as_of: 2026-11-15
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text: "At least once every 3 years after a management plan is in effect, each local education agency shall conduct a reinspection of all friable and nonfriable known or assumed ACBM in each school building"; "At least once every 6 months after a management plan is in effect, each local education agency shall conduct periodic surveillance in each building ... that contains ACBM or is assumed to contain ACBM." ACBM means asbestos-containing building material. Raw: raw/money/ecfr-40-763-subpartE.txt
4. New Hampshire law requires public and private schools to test every drinking water outlet for lead (at least three rounds) or install filtered bottle-filling stations, and when a result is at or above 5 parts per billion, to tell parents within 5 business days in at least two ways (the school or SAU website and an email or flyer), give children only safe drinking water, and carry out a state-approved fix within 180 days.
   - citation: RSA 485:17-a, I–III
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/L/485/485-17-a.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-15
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Statute text: "If test results ... demonstrate the presence of lead at a concentration at or exceeding 5 parts per billion, the school or licensed child care facility shall, within 5 business days, notify parents and guardians and shall, as an interim measure, ensure that the children are provided only drinking water that meets the standard. Notification shall be made in at least 2 places including ... the school or school administrative unit/child care facility website and at least one method of direct communication via email or printed flyer ... implement a remediation plan, as approved by the department, within 180 days". Existing schools were to finish sampling between January 1, 2016 and June 30, 2024. Raw: raw/money/rsa-485-17-a.txt
