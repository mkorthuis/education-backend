# Special Education, Student Support and Families in Rochester

Part of the Rochester research (town/Rochester). Gathered November 15, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers special education in the Rochester School District (SAU 54) and the Monarch School of New England, school nurses, counselors and social workers, English learner staff, foster care and homeless student contacts, school meals and how to apply, how the schools reach families in other languages, parent groups and family engagement, suspensions (with federal civil rights data by race and disability), and community mental health help for Rochester families.

**Sources.** The district website (rochesterschools.com) served a "Client Challenge" bot check to every automated request on November 15, 2026, so its department pages, policies and handbooks could not be read (see Not retrieved). The district's own documents were reached instead through the links the City of Rochester's news site (The Rochester Post) posts with each school board meeting: the board packets of May 14, June 11, July 9, September 10, October 8 and November 12, 2026, which hold the minutes of the April–October 2026 board and committee meetings, the April 9, 2026 staff nomination lists for 2026-27, job descriptions and new policies (AB, KB, JFABD, JFARD-A, ACE). Also used: the 2026-27 meal application letter, policy IHBAA, the FY2027 approved budget (saved November 8), the district's RSD Wellness site, the federal Civil Rights Data Collection (CRDC) for 2023-24, NH DOE's 2024-25 foster care contact list, the Monarch School of New England's site, Community Partners' site, and 7 CFR 245.8.

**Counts:** 9 topics (6 shared district topics, 1 shared town topic and 2 existing library topics), 74 facts and 4 number tables (support staff by school in the 2023-24 civil rights data, support staff nominated for 2026-27, out-of-school suspensions by group in 2023-24, and special education budget lines FY2023–FY2027).

**New data concepts:** the state's special education program review (PAGS) and Rochester's federal special education rating; counts of related service providers, nurses, counselors, social workers, interventionists and ESOL teachers by school; federal civil rights data on suspensions by race and disability and on race-based harassment allegations; the district's universal meals study; a private special education day school's admission and funding terms.

**Main findings:**
- NH DOE's six-year special education review (reported April and May 2026) found Rochester compliant with certified staff, but named special education students' graduation, dropout, proficiency and transition planning as areas to improve, plus IEP paperwork findings. Rochester's federal rating rose from "Needs Intervention" to "Needs Assistance." A follow-up review was expected in November 2026.
- Rochester has its own speech, occupational and physical therapists (7, 9 and 3 nominated for 2026-27, one of each at McClelland), added three itinerant speech pathologists, a behavior analyst (BCBA) and four behavior technicians in summer 2026, and covers some special education vacancies with contracted providers.
- The FY2027 budget nearly doubles "tuition to private & other" special education placements ($2.74 million to $5.68 million) while cutting "purchased educational services" ($7.15 million to $3.76 million). Monarch School of New England, in Rochester, is a state-approved private day school for students 5–22 whose tuition is paid by sending districts.
- In 2023-24, 12.6% of Rochester students had at least one out-of-school suspension, against 4.9% across New Hampshire (derived from federal civil rights data); rates were higher for students with IEPs (23.2%) and for Black, Hispanic and multiracial students, though some groups are small.
- Each elementary school had a full-time-equivalent nurse in 2023-24, and the 2026-27 nominations list a nurse for every elementary school plus a float, with several nurse resignations and itinerant hires since.
- Students who can't pay still get a full meal; elementary breakfast is free; the board is studying no-cost meals for all, with a recommendation due in February 2027.

**Main gaps:** wait times for therapy, counseling or evaluations (no source); how ESL services are delivered (pull-out or in class); Rochester's 504 procedures (IHBA, IHBA-R) and its foster care policy; a military family liaison; which schools have active PTOs; how many Rochester students attend Monarch or other out-of-district programs; the district's own special education, food service, health and military family pages (all behind the bot check).

## school-meals-eligibility: Free and Reduced-Price School Meals: Who Qualifies

- kind: rule
- question_type: rules
- description: NH's school meal requirement and the federal income limits and categorical eligibility for free or reduced-price meals for July 2026–June 2027.

1. Federal school meal rules forbid publishing, posting or announcing the names of children who get free or reduced-price meals, using special tokens, tickets or any other means that openly identifies them, making them work for meals, or making them use a separate line, dining area or entrance or eat at a different time, and they must get the same meal choices as paying students.
