# Rochester's Elementary Schools and the Early Years

Part of the Rochester research (town/Rochester). Gathered November 15, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers the Rochester School District's elementary schools (Chamberlain Street, East Rochester, Gonic, McClelland and Salmon Falls, kindergarten to grade 5) and the years before them: whether Salmon Falls has a pre-K, REACH Preschool and its waiting list, Head Start and child care near downtown, before- and after-school care at the schools, the kindergarten day, what kindergarten entry requires, Title I at East Rochester and Gonic, summer learning, whether results are improving, class sizes next to Manchester's, school librarians, placing twins in classrooms, screen time, volunteering, and how the schools and the Rochester Public Library run summer reading together.

- **13 topics, 60 facts and 2 number tables.** Nine topics use shared slugs from `town/shared-topics.md`; three add Rochester facts to library topics (`kindergarten-day-length`, `kindergarten-registration`, `school-staffing-and-class-size`); one is a proposed new general topic (below). The state twins law is not repeated: it is already written as `twins-classroom-placement` in the Salem research. Raw copies are in `raw/elementary/` (about 15 MB): `news/` (Rochester Post articles), `pages/` (Rochester Child Care Center, Community Action Partnership of Strafford County, Rochester Public Library, NHPR), `docs/` (the Head Start family handbook, the library's child care directory), `law/` (RSA 193:3-a, the Ed 300 rules, 20 U.S.C. 6314–6315), `nhdoe/` (the 2024-25 Title I school list and the fall 2025 school designations, with page images), `district/` (the library's November 8 copies of district pages and the FY2027 budget) and `data/` (extracts of the site's database used for derived figures).
- **The district's website refused automated requests.** Every rochesterschools.com page returned a "Client Challenge" bot check on November 15, 2026 (WebFetch got only the page title), and rochesternh.gov returned HTTP 403. District facts therefore come from the library's November 8 copies of district pages, the FY2027 budget, the city's news site (The Rochester Post), NH DOE files already saved in the library and other towns' folders, and the site's own data. School pages, staff lists, handbooks, policies and board minutes are listed under Not retrieved for the main session to open in a browser.
- **Main gaps:** what reading intervention Gonic School runs and who staffs it; East Rochester's Title I summer or tutoring offers beyond the district-wide ones; bus pick-up times; the district's volunteer rules and background-check steps; how much class time third graders spend on Chromebooks; whether Rochester's library staff are certified; and NH DOE's 2025-26 and 2026-27 Title I lists and fall 2026 school designations.
- **Proposed new shared topic:** `title-i-school-programs` (rule, rules): "Title I Schoolwide and Targeted Assistance Programs: how federal Title I, Part A money is used in a school, which children it serves, and what schoolwide and targeted assistance programs mean". No library topic or shared slug covers it.
- **Spot-checked** against raw copies: the UP Program's weekly rates "AM Only: $85.00 PM Only: $105.00 AM & PM: $180.00" (`pages/rccc-enrollment.txt`); Salmon Falls opening "officially opened to students on September 2" and "up to 420 students in kindergarten through fifth grade" (`news/salmon-falls-school-to-host-citywide-open-house-on-september-20.txt`); and East Rochester School's row in NH DOE's exited ATSI list, "East Rochester School Rochester Students with Disabilities Good Standing" (`nhdoe/school-designations-fall-2025.txt`, page image `nhdoe/designations-p7.png`). All match.

## title-i-school-programs: Title I Schoolwide and Targeted Assistance Programs

- kind: rule
- question_type: rules
- description: How federal Title I, Part A money is used in a school, which children it serves, and what schoolwide and targeted assistance programs mean

1. Under federal law, a school where at least 40 percent of the children are from low-income families may run a Title I "schoolwide program," combining Title I money with other federal, state and local funds "to upgrade the entire educational program" of the school; a school below 40 percent needs a state waiver to do so.
   - citation: 20 U.S.C. 6314(a)(1)(A)-(B) (ESEA section 1114)
   - source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/20/6314
   - as_of: 2026-11-15
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text: "may consolidate and use funds under this part, together with other Federal, State, and local funds, in order to upgrade the entire educational program of a school that serves an eligible school attendance area in which not less than 40 percent of the children are from low-income families, or not less than 40 percent of the children enrolled in the school are from such families." Raw: raw/elementary/law/usc-20-6314.txt

2. A school running a Title I schoolwide program does not have to pick out particular children as eligible for Title I, so its extra staff and programs can serve any student in the school; the money must add to, not replace, what state and local funds would provide.
   - citation: 20 U.S.C. 6314(a)(2)(A)-(B)
   - source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/20/6314
   - as_of: 2026-11-15
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text: "No school participating in a schoolwide program shall be required to identify— (i) particular children under this part as eligible to participate in a schoolwide program; or (ii) individual services as supplementary." and "shall use funds available to carry out this section only to supplement the amount of funds that would, in the absence of funds under this part, be made available from non-Federal sources". The reading of (A) as letting services reach any student is a plain paraphrase. Raw: raw/elementary/law/usc-20-6314.txt

3. In a Title I targeted assistance school, Title I serves the children the school identifies as failing, or most at risk of failing, to meet state standards, using multiple objective criteria set by the district and the school; children from preschool through grade 2 are chosen solely on criteria set by the district and supplemented by the school.
   - citation: 20 U.S.C. 6315(c)(1)(A)-(B) (ESEA section 1115)
   - source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/20/6315
   - as_of: 2026-11-15
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text: "eligible children are children identified by the school as failing, or most at risk of failing, to meet the challenging State academic standards on the basis of multiple, educationally related, objective criteria established by the local educational agency and supplemented by the school, except that children from preschool through grade 2 shall be selected solely on the basis of criteria, including objective criteria, established by the local educational agency and supplemented by the school." Raw: raw/elementary/law/usc-20-6315.txt

4. In a targeted assistance school, children do not have to be from low-income families to receive Title I help; children with disabilities, English learners and economically disadvantaged children are eligible on the same basis as others, as are children who attended Head Start or Title I preschool in the past two years, and the school must review eligible children's progress and change their help when needed.
   - citation: 20 U.S.C. 6315(b)(2), (c)(2)(A)-(B)
   - source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/20/6315
   - as_of: 2026-11-15
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text: "Children who are economically disadvantaged, children with disabilities, migrant children or English learners, are eligible for services under this part on the same basis as other children selected to receive services under this part."; "A child who, at any time in the 2 years preceding the year for which the determination is made, participated in a Head Start program … is eligible"; "on an ongoing basis, review the progress of eligible children and revise the targeted assistance program under this section, if necessary". Eligibility is by academic need (fact 3), not family income. Raw: raw/elementary/law/usc-20-6315.txt
