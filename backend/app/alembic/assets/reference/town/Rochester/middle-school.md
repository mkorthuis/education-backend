# Rochester Middle School: The Move From Elementary School, Programs, Activities and Support

Part of the Rochester research (town/Rochester). Gathered November 15, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers Rochester Middle School (RMS, grades 6–8): the move from elementary school, bullying and conduct rules, advanced math, sports tryouts, the parent portal, after-school options, Chromebooks, the school day and physical activity, band, chorus and theater, instrument costs and loans, and counselors and social workers. It also covers city and nonprofit options for middle schoolers after school (the Rochester Opera House's arts center, Rochester Recreation and the public library).

**The district's website (www.rochesterschools.com) served a bot check to every automated request**, so RMS's own pages (staff directory, athletics, clubs, handbook, bell schedule, live feed) weren't read. The main sources are instead: Rochester School Board policies (PDFs on the district's file host), the federal Civil Rights Data Collection (CRDC) for 2021-22 and 2023-24, the FY2027 approved budget, the RMS band director's 2025 and 2026 letters and band website, the Spaulding Music Department's site, the NCES school directory, the Infinite Campus portal, the city's news site (The Rochester Post), the Rochester Opera House's 2026-27 class page and one Rochester Voice news report.

**Counts:** 13 topics, 60 facts, 1 table.

**Main findings:**
- **Sports tryouts.** RMS's athletic rules (policy IGDJA-RMS, 2016) say all students may try out and are "chosen based upon their ability, skill level and the quality of their tryout performance," so teams can cut. The 2026 eligibility policy JJIC allows tryouts and fitness tests, requires a participation fee (with a waiver on request) and a physical, and designates every team in grades 5–12 as boys, girls or coed.
- **Advanced math.** In 2023-24 RMS taught Algebra I to 51 eighth graders in three classes (about one in five, derived); no seventh graders took it and no Rochester school reported a gifted program. A new policy (IMBD, July 9, 2026) lets grade 7 and 8 students earn high school credit for advanced courses.
- **Counselors.** RMS reported 6 full-time counselors for 776 students in 2023-24 (about 129 students each, derived), well under the state limit of 300 and below the median of 160 for New Hampshire's 43 grades 6–8 schools (derived). It reported no social worker or psychologist that year (one social worker and a 0.2 psychologist in 2021-22).
- **Music.** Band is a daily class (6th grade Concert Band, 7th–8th grade Symphonic Band, plus a jazz band). The RMS band letters ask students to bring their own instruments and charge a $10 refundable lock deposit and a $7–$10 method book, with help offered for "a financial problem." No RMS loaner program was found; Spaulding's music department lends school-owned instruments to "many" ensemble members.
- **After school.** RMS lets out at 2:15 p.m. Rochester has no federally funded 21st Century after-school program, and the district budgets nothing for one. RMS has a video club and a robotics (CoderZ) team started with a $7,500 grant; club schedules weren't reached. The public library's Teen Room has drop-in programs Wednesdays after school and is open until 8 p.m. Monday to Wednesday; the Opera House's arts center runs paid after-school music and theater classes, with scholarships.
- **Budget.** The FY2027 approved budget raises co-curricular activities 84.9% (to $172,929) and athletics 29.8% (to $659,086); music, art and theater staffing aren't shown separately.
