# Lebanon Middle School, Student Support and School Choices

Part of the Lebanon research (town/Lebanon). Gathered November 21, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers Lebanon Middle School (LMS, grades 5–8): how its grade teams keep 5th graders apart from 8th graders, its counselors and mental health support, its phone rule, its quiet-break and reset spaces, bullying and how parents hear about it, and how new students settle in. It also covers special education across the Lebanon School District (SAU 88): evaluations, paraeducator openings and contracted or out-of-district services. On school choice it covers home education and access to district programs, Ledyard Charter School, charter students in Lebanon High School (LHS) sports and prom, Chromebook and Alma (parent portal) help, Wi-Fi hotspots and the late bus, and the makeup of students and teachers.

- **Places used:** `district Lebanon` (SAU 88, its schools, board and staff), `district Ledyard Charter School` (the charter school's own facts), `town Lebanon` (the Lebanon Public Libraries and Lebanon Recreation). No general rules were written; state rules are cited from the library and earlier towns by file, topic and fact number.
- **Main findings:**
  - **Grade teams:** LMS teaches each grade as its own team. Pine is grade 5, with a pair of teachers for humanities and STEM. Since 2024-25 grades 7 and 8 have had separate teams (Willow and Elm) instead of mixed 7–8 teams, and each team has its own area of the building. Dances are for 7th and 8th graders only. School sports start in grade 7; 5th and 6th graders play through Lebanon Recreation.
  - **Counselors:** LMS lists a school psychologist, two school counselors (one for grades 5–6) and a Student Assistance Provider. The 2023-24 federal count was 3.0 counselors, 1.0 psychologist, 0.25 social worker and 1.0 nurse for 459 students, about 153 students per counselor (**derived**). Since 2025-26 the district has also offered Cartwheel, a grant-funded online therapy program with licensed clinicians.
  - **Phones:** LMS's rule is "off and away" from arrival to dismissal, smartwatches and earbuds included. The board re-adopted policy JICJ and a new checklist (JICJ-R) in August and September 2026.
  - **Breaks:** grades 6–8 use the STAR room, which has a "Zen Den" for students who need to calm down. 5th graders use "Take a Break" inside their classroom or a buddy classroom.
  - **Paraeducators:** in November 2026 the district's openings folder still listed a May 2026 notice for two full-time special education paraeducators at Hanover Street, plus para openings at LMS and Mount Lebanon. Whether students have lost service hours isn't published.
  - **Outside services:** the district contracts for some speech-language services. Out-of-district day placements cost $90,000–$150,000 before transportation, and in April 2026 the board withdrew $900,000 from its special education trust fund.
  - **Ledyard Charter School:** a tuition-free charter high school at 39 Hanover Street, with three teachers on its staff page. Its board was told it would start 2026-27 with 35 students, down from 50 a year earlier. Lebanon has supplemented its funding for about a decade, a sum the district planned to cut by $30,000. State law and NHIAA rules let a Lebanon resident at Ledyard play LHS sports. LHS's handbook admits non-LHS students to prom only as an LHS student's registered guest.
  - **Hotspots:** no SAU 88 hotspot program was found. The Lebanon Public Libraries lend Wi-Fi hotspots, and their Wi-Fi reaches the parking lots around the clock. LMS runs a 4:00 p.m. late bus on most days for students who stay for help or activities.
  - **Makeup of students and teachers:** in 2023-24, 19.4% of Lebanon's students and 5.0% of its teachers were not White, against 17.7% and 2.3% statewide (**derived**, federal data). The district's English learners speak 44 listed languages.
- **Main gaps:**
  - The texts of Lebanon's policies (JICJ, JICK bullying, JJJ access for home-educated, charter and private school students, IHBG home education) weren't read: the board's policy site loads only by script.
  - No Lebanon figure was found for how long evaluations take (NH DOE's Indicator 11 data was not requested; other towns found it blocked).
  - Nothing published shows whether IEP service hours go undelivered when paras are out.
  - No student survey on inclusion was found.
  - The LMS bell schedule (Google Doc, HTTP 401) wasn't readable, so lunch periods by grade are unknown.
- **Proposed shared topic:** none.
