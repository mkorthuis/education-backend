# Charter, Private and Home Education for Nashua Families

Part of the Nashua research (town/Nashua). Gathered November 15, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers the choice side of the Nashua residents' questions: getting into the Academy for Science and Design (ASD) and what its program, workload, sports and social life look like; Riverwood Academy (the former MicroSociety Academy) and the move from it to Nashua High School South; Gate City Charter School for the Arts' scores, sports access for charter students and what happens if a charter school closes; Bishop Guertin's tuition and Education Freedom Accounts (EFAs); World Academy's grades and where its graduates go; and home education in Nashua after the July 1, 2026 law change, part-time access to Nashua classes and activities, and EFA reporting.

- **8 topics, 72 facts, no tables.** Five topics are shared town topics (`town-school-options-nearby`, `district-homeschool-access`, `district-pe-outdoor-and-athletics`, `school-programs-after-grade-6`, `town-recreation-and-summer`); three add facts to existing library topics (`charter-schools`, `efa-how-to-apply`, `efa-uses-and-requirements`). Raw copies are in `raw/choices/` (about 12 MB), with six Policy Committee minutes in `raw/choices/minutes/`.
- **Places used:** `district Academy for Science and Design Charter School`, `district MicroSociety Academy Charter School Foundation` (Riverwood), `district Nashua`, `town Nashua` (Bishop Guertin, World Academy, city recreation and EFA counts), and no place for state law, NHIAA rules and the EFA program's own rules. World Academy's facts use `town Nashua` because the site's `district` table spells its name "World Academy " with a trailing space (see "For another file").
- **Main findings:**
  - **ASD's 2027-28 lottery deadline is Wednesday, January 6, 2027 at 1:00 p.m.,** with the lottery on January 20. Applications open each fall (about mid-October). There is no entrance exam. Fifth grade is ASD's "primary entry point" but is the smaller class (48 fifth graders against 120 in each of grades 6–8 in 2024-25, site data). ASD reported a waitlist of 176 for 2025-26.
  - **ASD's posted rules give no preference to Nashua residents,** while RSA 194-B:2, IV gives residents of a charter school's district "absolute admission preference". Both are stated neutrally; the sources don't say how the two fit together.
  - **MicroSociety Academy Charter School became Riverwood Academy Chartered Public School on August 31, 2026.** It serves preschool (4-year-olds) to grade 8 on West Hollis Street. Its eighth-grade math units are all labeled pre-algebra; Nashua South's Program of Studies offers Pre-Algebra, a two-part Algebra 1 and Honors Algebra 1 to freshmen.
  - **Charter, private and home-educated Nashua residents can join Nashua's sports, band and classes** under policy IHBG-R and RSA 193:1-c, by written request to the building principal. NHIAA lets an ASD or private school student play for the Nashua high school of the district where the student lives. ASD fields only a middle school cross-country team.
  - **Nashua's home education policy and FAQ still describe the pre-July 2026 rules.** Policy IHBG (December 21, 2020) describes notices, a portfolio and an annual evaluation that state law no longer requires; the FAQ still asks families to file an intent form. The Policy Committee's 2026 minutes (May–November) don't mention it. IHBG-R doesn't yet name EFA students.
  - **Bishop Guertin's pages say EFA funds may be applied to its tuition** ($17,900 for 2026-27, plus fees). BG and World Academy are both on the EFA marketplace. The 2026-27 EFA cap was reached; Nashua had 780 eligible students and 23 on the waitlist on November 15, 2026.
  - **World Academy runs from infants through grade 8,** with no high school. It publishes the private high schools its graduates have entered, but no counts by school and no public high schools.
- **Main gaps:** Gate City's own website (its domain now shows unrelated content), so nothing from the school itself on its scores, curriculum or plans; NH DOE's charter pages and reports (the site blocks automated requests); any data on how charter or private K–8 students do after moving to Nashua South; any measure of homework time or stress at ASD or Nashua's high schools.
- **Proposed shared topic:** none. Sports access for non-enrolled students sits under `district-pe-outdoor-and-athletics`, and charter and private school programs under `school-programs-after-grade-6`.

## charter-schools: Chartered Public Schools in New Hampshire

- kind: rule
- question_type: rules
- description: What a NH chartered public school is, cost, admission and lotteries, transportation, and special education.

1. Under New Hampshire law, a resident pupil enrolled in a chartered public school is considered reassigned to that school for school attendance purposes.
   - citation: RSA 194-B:9, IV
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XV/194-B/194-B-9.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-15
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Statute text: "For the purpose of this chapter, any resident pupil enrolled in a chartered public school is to be considered reassigned to the chartered public school for purposes of school attendance." The library's charter-schools fact 8 covers withdrawing and enrolling in the local public school (RSA 194-B:9, II). Raw: raw/choices/rsa-194-B-9.txt
2. If a New Hampshire charter school's charter expires or is revoked, the school is dissolved under its charter, a parent may apply to another eligible charter school, and the pupil's home district is not relieved of its duty to educate the pupil; the school district where the charter school is located is not liable for the dissolved school's obligations.
   - citation: RSA 194-B:16, VII–VIII
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XV/194-B/194-B-16.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-15
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Statute text: "If a school's charter expires or is revoked, the parent of a pupil attending that school may apply to any other chartered public school eligible to receive tuition under the provisions of this chapter adopted by the school district. The pupil's sending district shall not be relieved of its obligation to educate that pupil in accordance with the district's policies." For a Nashua resident at a charter school in Merrimack, the sending district is Nashua. Raw: raw/choices/rsa-194-B-16.txt
3. New Hampshire's State Board of Education may revoke a charter for reasons including material violations of the charter, poor fiscal management, significant violations of law, insolvency or failure to report, may first place the school on probation for up to a year under a remedial plan, may revoke immediately when pupils face an extraordinary risk of harm, and may not renew a charter whose school has not met its academic goals by the end of its final contract year.
   - citation: RSA 194-B:16, I, IV–VI
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XV/194-B/194-B-16.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-15
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Statute text: "The state board may place a chartered public school on probationary status for up to one year to allow the implementation of a remedial plan"; "Nothing contained in this section shall prevent the state board from immediately revoking a school's charter in circumstances posing extraordinary risk of harm to pupils"; "If the school does not meet these results or standards and goals, it shall not be eligible for renewal of its charter." A parent of a pupil at the school may petition the board to revoke (RSA 194-B:16, II). The board's rule Ed 318.15 requires 90 days' notice and a hearing before revocation (checked at https://gc.nh.gov/rules/state_agencies/ed300.html; not saved). Raw: raw/choices/rsa-194-B-16.txt
