# English Learners, Counseling and Family Support in Nashua

Part of the Nashua research (town/Nashua). Gathered November 15, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers how the Nashua School District (SAU 42) identifies and serves English learners, how it reaches families who speak other languages, school meals and fee waivers, school counselors and mental health help in the middle and high schools, attendance and chronic absence rules, military families, and children in foster care. Almost everything comes from the district's own pages and documents: the 2023 Lau Compliance Plan (the district's English learner plan), the 2021 U.S. Department of Justice settlement on English learner services and the May 2025 letter closing it, the ELL Department's February 2026 budget presentation, the FY2027 budget and personnel files, Board of Education policies (IHBBA, JH, JHA, JFABE, JLDBB, JJJC, EFAA, EFC, JRA, JFAA, KA), the school counseling pages, two school handbooks and the food service pages. State rules added here: the military children's compact provisions on deployment absences, powers of attorney and activities (RSA 110-D:6, V and 110-D:7).

**Counts:** 7 topics (5 shared district topics and 2 existing library topics), 108 facts and 3 number tables (school counselors and caseloads at the middle and high schools, English learner service groups and ELL teachers by level, and 2026-27 meal prices).

**New data concepts:**
- English learner service groups by proficiency level, ELL teacher counts by level and the share of English learners reaching proficiency each year.
- Counselors per school with their caseloads, and derived students per counselor.
- The district's written translation rules (the "essential information" list and "major languages").
- Meal charge rules and school food pantries.

**Main findings:**
- Nashua screens students whose Home Language Survey lists another language with the WIDA Screener; a composite below 4.5 qualifies. Students exit at 4.5 on the yearly ACCESS test, are then monitored for four years, and the district's goal is proficiency within five years. Between 8.5% and 9.5% of English learners reached proficiency each year from 2022-23 to 2024-25.
- In February 2026 the ELL Department reported 1,663 students needing services, 56.8 ELL teachers, and service minutes not being met in half of the elementary schools. The FY2027 budget adds 4 ELL teachers.
- The Department of Justice found the district's English learner program out of compliance in 2021. In May 2025 it found the district had met the settlement and closed monitoring.
- Translated "essential information" goes to Spanish and Portuguese speakers (and any language spoken by 50 or more parents). Other languages, such as Arabic, get translation or an interpreter on request.
- In the middle and high schools, each grade-level or alphabet-caseload counselor has about 188 to 252 students (derived), below the state limit of 300.
- Nashua's foster care point of contact is the Director of ELL. Children in foster care get free meals without an application, and that free status also waives activity fees and the high school bus fee.

**Main gaps:**
- Which languages staff speak at Dr. Crisp or any single school.
- Whether notices can be had in Arabic in practice, and what interpretation vendor the district uses now.
- Chronic absence rates by school, and any program aimed at anxiety-related school avoidance.
- A Nashua military family liaison (none found).
- Field trip fee waivers.
- How long students actually stay in the program on average (the district gives a goal, not a measured average).

## district-student-support: Nashua's English Learner Program, Counselors, Foster Care and Student Support

1. In the Nashua School District, families registering a student complete the state-approved Home Language Survey; an English learner (EL) teacher reviews each incoming student's survey and decides whether the student needs an English screening, which must be done within 30 days of the start of the school year, or within 14 days of registration at any other time.
   - citation: Nashua School District, LAU Compliance Plan (revised Summer 2023), Section 2
   - source: https://nashuaedu.finalsite.com/fs/resource-manager/view/dee9a832-3b1d-456d-af96-46379bd1402e
   - as_of: 2026-11-15
   - review_by: 2027-11-15
   - place: district Nashua
   - note: Text: "Administrative assistants provide EL teachers a copy of each incoming student's completed HLS. The EL teacher determines if the student requires screening based on the HLS. This screening must be completed within 30 days of the beginning of the school year and within 14 days of registration at all other points in the school year." The 2021 DOJ settlement (para. 4) had set 20 days from the start of the year and 10 days from a later enrollment for assessment and placement. Raw copy: raw/support/nsd-2023-lau-plan.txt (pp. 8–9)
