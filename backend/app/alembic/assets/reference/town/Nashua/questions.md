# Nashua Residents' Questions

The questions the Nashua research is meant to answer. Forty resident profiles, three questions each (120 in all, twice the usual twenty profiles), numbered P<profile>.<question> so the research files can say which facts answer which question. The profiles came grouped by kind of family; the group headings are kept. Received November 15, 2026. The people are invented profiles, not real residents.

Nashua is its own SAU (SAU 42) and the state's second-largest district: the Nashua School District enrolled 9,699 students in 2024-25 in 12 elementary schools (kindergarten through grade 5), Franklin Street School (preschool), three middle schools (grades 6–8: Brian S. McCarthy, Fairgrounds and Pennichuck), Nashua High School North and Nashua High School South, and Brentwood Academy. Elm Street Middle School is no longer on NH DOE's school list. The city has public charter schools (the Academy for Science and Design, grades 5–12, and Riverwood Academy Chartered Public School, run by the MicroSociety Academy Charter School Foundation) and private schools including Bishop Guertin High School, World Academy and St. Christopher Academy; Gate City Charter School for the Arts is in Merrimack. The Board of Education prepares the school budget and the Board of Aldermen adopts the city's under a charter spending cap. Several questions contain premises (school names, a budget total and its growth, an enrollment figure, programs, a charter school's name and grades) that the research should check rather than assume.
