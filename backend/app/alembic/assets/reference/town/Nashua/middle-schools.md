# Nashua's Middle Schools: McCarthy, Fairgrounds and Pennichuck

Part of the Nashua research (town/Nashua). Gathered November 15, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers Nashua's three middle schools (grades 6–8): Brian S. McCarthy (opened August 2024, replacing Elm Street Middle School), Fairgrounds Middle and Pennichuck. It answers the residents' questions on the Elm Street move and crowding, sixth-grade teams, sports and clubs, bullying and hallway rules, phones, Fairgrounds math, grade 8 Algebra 1 and world languages, honors placement for ninth grade, Chromebooks, whom to contact, middle school zones, grading, shop classes and the Aspen X2 parent portal.

- **11 topics, 90 facts and 2 tables.** Raw copies are in `raw/middle/` (schools, policies, district, boe, data, maps), about 17 MB.
- **Main findings:**
  - **Elm Street was the crowded school; McCarthy is not.** A 2019 building committee summary said Elm Street had "more than 1,200 students, which is too large," and set a goal of about 800 per middle school. NH DOE counted 895 at Elm Street in October 2023. In October 2024, after McCarthy opened and the lines were redrawn, the largest middle school was Pennichuck (768); McCarthy had 649. In October 2026 the master-plan architect put McCarthy at 653 students against a capacity of 917, and said the middle schools run at about 80% of capacity. Early-fall attendance rose from 83% at Elm Street (2022, 2023) to 92% at McCarthy (2024), in a district chart. Behavior can't be compared cleanly: the student groups changed with the new lines.
  - **Teams.** Since 2024-25 the district uses four-teacher teams (English, math, science, social studies) and a six-period day. McCarthy's building puts each team's classrooms in an academic "village" in one of three wings and lets the school group grades by floor or wing. Its grades eat lunch at separate times.
  - **Phones are off and stored, not locked up.** Policy JICJ (July 2025) bans personal devices from the first bell to dismissal, including lunch and passing time. Phones must be off in a locker, backpack or handbag. There's no locked pouch. Repeated use can lead to confiscation until a parent picks the phone up.
  - **Grade 8 high school courses.** McCarthy offers full-year high school–level French or Spanish in grade 8. A C-range grade earns high school credit. Pennichuck and Fairgrounds list grade 8 French and Spanish too. The high schools' Program of Studies refers to students who complete "Algebra 1 in 8th grade with B or better." Policies IMBD and IMBC-R let 8th graders earn high school credit if the high school principal approves first. Fairgrounds' handbook describes math acceleration through VLACS.
  - **Ninth-grade levels follow teacher recommendations.** The Program of Studies says "The recommendations of teachers should be followed when selecting courses and levels." For math the general guideline is a B or better plus the teacher's recommendation.
  - **Chromebooks.** Every student in grades 6–12 gets one to take home, keeps it through grade 8, and may be charged for damage or loss. The fee is weighed against documented financial need. Loaners are given during repairs.
  - **Grading.** The middle schools use letter grades (A+ 97–100 to F 0–59) on trimester report cards in Aspen X2. Teachers must update grades at least every two weeks. The high schools grade by competencies.
- **Main gaps:** which sports cut players and the middle school fee amounts; which middle schools offer Algebra 1 in grade 8 (no middle school program of studies is posted); whether McCarthy now keeps each grade in one wing; any published school climate survey; whether Aspen shows attendance to parents; elementary attendance-zone maps.
