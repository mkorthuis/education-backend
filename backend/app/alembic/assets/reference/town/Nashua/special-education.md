# Special Education, 504 Plans, Evaluations and Student Support in Nashua

Part of the Nashua research (town/Nashua). Gathered November 15, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers the Nashua residents' special education questions: how speech services and aides are decided, who to call when a plan isn't followed, 504 plans in middle school, paraprofessional and substitute staffing, EFAs and special education rights, out-of-district placements, independent educational evaluations (IEEs), guardianship papers, grief and social-emotional support, and evaluations for a foster child who has moved between districts.

- **9 topics, 85 facts and 7 tables.** Raw copies are in `raw/sped/` (about 29 MB): the district's special education, student services and enrollment pages, 13 Board policies and the policy list, the 2014 Section 504 handbook, the 2026-27 elementary handbook, the April 2025 special education presentation, the FY2027 budget presentation to the aldermen (May 5, 2026) and personnel detail, the teacher and paraeducator contracts, Board of Education, HR and Policy Committee minutes and agendas (May–November 2026), three middle school counseling pages, Home Health & Hospice Care's bereavement pages, and the statutes and rules cited (RSA 186-C, 194-F, 463; Ed 1100; 34 CFR 300 sections in `raw/sped/ecfr/`).
- **Main findings:**
  - **Who to call.** The district's special education office lists a director, an assistant director, a middle school coordinator, an out-of-district coordinator and an office manager with phone numbers; both elementary coordinator posts were open. The director is also the district's Section 504 coordinator (policy AC-E, 2024) and reports straight to the superintendent. The case manager is named as the family's main contact. Three special education advisory meetings are set for December 2, 9 and 16, 2026.
  - **Staffing.** The HR committee's November 16, 2026 list shows 92 openings, including 39 paraprofessionals ("most being covered by contractors"), 13 behavior technicians, 3 school psychologists and 2 speech-language pathologists. Special education contract services rose from $33,305 in FY2022 to an estimated $7.94 million in FY2026, which the district puts down to hiring difficulty and a growing share of special education students (2,235 of 9,501 students in 2025-26, 23.5%, **derived**). Seven contracts approved August–October 2026 add 5.2 FTE speech-language pathologists and 2.8 FTE assistants (**derived**).
  - **Out-of-district placements.** Nashua had 111 students in out-of-district special education placements in 2025-26 (129 in 2024-25). Out-of-district tuition was $7.24 million in FY2025 and is budgeted at $7.2 million plus $1 million in special revenue for FY2027.
  - **IEEs.** Policy IHBAA says the district "is unable to impose timelines" on obtaining an IEE at public expense, and limits evaluators to New Hampshire, within 75 miles. A new policy, IHBAB, on second reading November 16, 2026, would allow evaluators in contiguous states. In October 2026 the district said it had not turned away an IEE request under the current director, with no data at hand.
  - **EFAs and private placement.** Nashua's regulation IHBG-R (2020) says requests from home-educated, charter and nonpublic school pupils for related services such as speech or occupational therapy "shall generally be denied", and doesn't mention EFA students. Federal rules give children placed in private schools by their parents no individual right to the services they would get in public school.
  - **Grief support.** No Nashua page describes a grief program. Each elementary school has a guidance counselor post, the elementary handbook says counselors ask parents' permission before seeing a child individually or in a group, and a 2026-27 agreement with Harbor Care brings school-based behavioral health services, on referral by school staff. Home Health & Hospice Care in Merrimack offers community bereavement help and lists children's grief groups.
- **Premises checked:**
  - P12.1 "six different teachers": Nashua's FY2027 projections put middle school students on teams of **4 teachers** per team. The number of other teachers a student sees (arts, PE, electives) isn't published.
  - P6.2 "get an aide assigned": aides are part of the IEP, decided by the IEP team as supplementary aids and services. Nashua publishes no separate request process. 39 paraprofessional posts were open in November 2026.
  - P13.2 "teachers constantly covering other classes": no Nashua source counts class coverages or substitute fill rates. The HR committee reported specific cases (high school math teachers covering a math vacancy; special education staff covering vacancies at Amherst Street and Main Dunstable).
  - P25.1 "an EFA for Orton-Gillingham tutoring or a private school": EFA money may pay for tutoring and therapies (library efa-uses-and-requirements fact 2). The EFA agreement bars full-time enrollment in the district (fact 5).
  - P25.3 "the timeline for requesting an IEE": no rule sets a deadline for the parent's request. Federal rules bar districts from imposing timelines on obtaining one, and Nashua's policy says the same. The district must answer "without unnecessary delay".
- **Main gaps:**

## special-ed-iep-basics: Special Education and IEPs: What They Are and Who Qualifies

- kind: rule
- question_type: rules
- description: What an IEP and special education are, which children qualify in New Hampshire, and that services are free.

1. Under federal special education rules, every IEP must state the special education, related services and supplementary aids and services to be provided to the child, where supplementary aids and services means the aids, services and other supports given in regular classes and other settings so a child with a disability can be educated with children without disabilities as much as appropriate.
   - citation: 34 CFR 300.320(a)(4); 34 CFR 300.42
   - source: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-300.320
   - as_of: 2026-11-15
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Rule text (300.320(a)(4)): "A statement of the special education and related services and supplementary aids and services ... to be provided to the child, or on behalf of the child, and a statement of the program modifications or supports for school personnel". 300.42: "aids, services, and other supports that are provided in regular education classes, other education-related settings, and in extracurricular and nonacademic settings". Neither names aides; a paraprofessional's support is written into the IEP this way when the team decides it is needed. Raw: raw/sped/ecfr/34-CFR-300.320.txt; raw/sped/ecfr/34-CFR-300.42.txt
2. Under federal special education rules, each IEP must give the date services begin and the anticipated frequency, location and duration of the services, so whether speech therapy happens in the child's classroom or elsewhere, and how often, is set in the IEP.
   - citation: 34 CFR 300.320(a)(7)
   - source: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-300.320
   - as_of: 2026-11-15
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Rule text: "The projected date for the beginning of the services and modifications described in paragraph (a)(4) of this section, and the anticipated frequency, location, and duration of those services and modifications." 300.320(a)(5) also requires "An explanation of the extent, if any, to which the child will not participate with nondisabled children in the regular class". The second half of the statement explains the rule's effect in plain words. Raw: raw/sped/ecfr/34-CFR-300.320.txt
3. Under federal rules, a child's placement is decided by a group that includes the parents, is set at least once a year, is based on the IEP and is as close as possible to home, and unless the IEP requires otherwise the child attends the school he or she would attend without a disability.
   - citation: 34 CFR 300.116; N.H. Code Admin. Rules Ed 1111.03(a)
   - source: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-300.116
   - as_of: 2026-11-15
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Rule text: "The child's placement— (1) Is determined at least annually; (2) Is based on the child's IEP; and (3) Is as close as possible to the child's home"; "(c) Unless the IEP of a child with a disability requires some other arrangement, the child is educated in the school that he or she would attend if nondisabled". Ed 1111.03(a): "The IEP team shall make placement decisions in accordance with 34 CFR 300.116." Raw: raw/sped/ecfr/34-CFR-300.116.txt; raw/sped/nh-rules-ed1100.txt
4. New Hampshire's rules list the settings, from least to most restrictive, in which a student aged 6 to 22 may receive special education: a regular class with supports, a resource room (no more than 60% of the day), a self-contained special class (more than 60% of the day), a separate approved special education program or school, a residential placement, home instruction, and a hospital or institution.
   - citation: N.H. Code Admin. Rules Ed 1111.02 and Ed 1111.03(d), Table 1100.3
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rules/state_agencies/ed1100.html
   - as_of: 2026-11-15
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Rule text: "Resource room | A child with a disability attends a regular class and receives assistance at or through the special education resource room for no more than 60% of the child's school day."; "Separate Approved Special Education Program or School | A child with a disability attends a publicly or privately operated special education program or school." Ed 1111.01 allows removal from regular classes only when supplementary aids and services cannot make regular class work satisfactorily. Raw: raw/sped/nh-rules-ed1100.txt
5. Under federal rules, a child's IEP must be accessible to each regular education teacher, special education teacher, related service provider and other provider responsible for carrying it out, and each must be told their specific responsibilities and the accommodations, modifications and supports the IEP requires.
   - citation: 34 CFR 300.323(d)

## special-education-placements-across-states: Private School Placements and Parentally Placed Students
