# High Schools: Nashua North and South, Courses, Credit, Support and Outcomes

Part of the Nashua research (town/Nashua). Gathered November 15, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers Nashua's two comprehensive high schools, Nashua High School North and Nashua High School South, and the district's other high school options (Brentwood Academy, summer school, competency recovery and the district's adult diploma program for high school students). It answers the high school questions in profiles P16–P20, P24, P29 and P40: AP and dual enrollment, North versus South, counselors, scholarships, FAFSA help, shuttle buses between the schools, VLACS, graduation requirements, credit transfer, credit recovery, truancy and how the schools' results compare.

- **7 fact topics, 83 facts and 3 number tables.** Raw copies are in `raw/high/` (about 11 MB): the 2026-27 Program of Studies ("Footsteps to the Future," shared by both schools), both 2026-27 student handbooks, both schools' current college profiles, South's 2026-27 organization chart and freshman guide, both schools' counseling pages, 12 Board of Education policies (IK, IK-R, IKF, IKF-R, IKFB, IHBI, IHCA, IHCD, IMBA, IMBC-R, JH and JHA) and the VLACS form, the May 11, 2026 Board of Education agenda (with the AP presentation, read from slide images) and minutes, the district's adult education page and its 2026 summer school, competency recovery and fall 2026 diploma documents, Brentwood Academy's pages, VLACS's high school page, Granite Edvance's help page, three NH Charitable Foundation pages, the Nashua Historical Society scholarship page and one Nashua Telegraph article.
- **Places:** 78 facts use `district Nashua`, including the scholarships tied to the two schools and the derived comparisons with Merrimack and Bedford; 1 uses `town Nashua` (the Nashua Historical Society scholarship); 4 have no place (Granite Edvance, VLACS and the NH Charitable Foundation's statewide four-year scholarships).
- **New data concepts:** the number of AP students, exams and students scoring 3 or higher at each school, 2021–2025 (table nashua_ap_students_and_scores_2021_2025); which courses run at only one school and the shuttle buses between them; the district's competency grading and "post-course recovery"; summer school and competency recovery fees; the adult diploma program's afternoon classes for high school students; the attendance letters and referral steps in policy JHA; and derived comparisons with Merrimack and Bedford, including how many grade 11 test-takers are from low-income families (tables nashua_hs_outcomes_compared_2025 and nashua_grade11_sat_by_income_2026).
- **Premises checked:**
  - P16.3 "1,500-plus kids at South": holds. The site's data have 1,663 students at South on October 1, 2024; South's profile gives 1,627 for 2024-25 and its home page says "1,700+".
  - P16.1 "how many AP ... classes South offers": the sources disagree. South's home page says 17, both profiles say 22, the district's May 2026 presentation listed 19 AP courses taught across both schools, and the 2026-27 Program of Studies describes 25, running only those with enough students (school-programs-after-grade-6 1).
  - P18.1 "do they still bus kids back and forth": yes. The 2026-27 Program of Studies and both handbooks say shuttle buses run between North and South every day. Most theater and digital media courses are at South itself (district-transportation 1–3, school-programs-after-grade-6 14).
  - P17.2 "scholarships specifically for Nashua High graduates": there are two Nashua high schools (the site's school list gives North an opening date of July 1, 2002); the Class of 1963 scholarship is for South ("formerly Nashua High School") seniors, and its vocational and STEM funds are open to both schools. No scholarship limited to Nashua graduates in nursing or business was found.
  - P20.2 Brentwood Academy as an alternative program: Brentwood is a therapeutic special education high school program entered by referral from a district, usually with an IEP, not a general alternative school. The smaller, after-school option for any Nashua student is the district's adult diploma program, and the Phoenix Program is not on NH DOE's current school list.
  - P40.1 "test scores look mediocre even though lots of graduates go to great colleges": partly supported. In spring 2026, grade 11 math and reading proficiency at both schools was at or below the state's, and 40–44% of 2025 graduates planned four-year college, against 49.9% statewide; North's profile lists a sample of four-year colleges its graduates attend, but no source counts graduates by college.
