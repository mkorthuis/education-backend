# Pelham Memorial School and Pelham High School

Part of the Pelham research (town/Pelham). Gathered November 21, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers Pelham Memorial School (grades 6–8) and Pelham High School (grades 9–12): the Memorial renovation and what happened to class sizes, middle school math results and what the district is doing about them, Algebra I in grade 8, Pelham High's graduation requirements (including math every year and 40 hours of community service), AP, honors and college credit courses, the GPA rules for summer and online courses, senior early release and internships, sports tryouts, eligibility and buses at both schools, tutoring and extra help, college counseling, graduates' plans and SAT results, Pelham High's building and technology, and placing twins in classes.

- **11 topics, 86 facts and 2 number tables.** All facts use `district Pelham` except the two general state-rule facts (no place): one added to the library topic `graduation-requirements` (Ed 306.22(o), engaging with math every year) and one added to the shared topic `twins-classroom-placement` (how the state's rules describe an elementary and a middle school). Tables: `pelham_math_goal_tracking_2018_2026` and `phs_dual_enrollment_registrations_2023_2026` (both `district Pelham`).
- **New data concepts:** the district's own math goal and its rank among 12 peer districts (grades 3–8, and Pelham Memorial alone through 2024); math teacher turnover at Memorial; course replacements for students behind in math (a grade 8 math class in place of Spanish, grade 6 placement instead of a Unified Arts class, an intensive two-semester Algebra course in grade 9); the Memorial bond vote, budget and stated reasons; Memorial's teacher teams by grade; Pelham High's GPA weights by course level and which outside courses count in GPA; dual enrollment registrations by year; the community service rules and the parking-pass thresholds; senior early release and late arrival rules; academic and conditional eligibility for sports; the district's technology replacement schedule, including Pelham High's specialty labs.
- **Main sources:** the Pelham High 2026-27 Program of Studies, student handbook, athletic handbook (board-approved May 6, 2026) and scanned 2026-27 school profile; Pelham Memorial's 2026-27 handbook, athletics handbook page, clubs page and profile page; the counseling, college credit and community service pages and booklet; board policies IKF, IHCD/LEB, IHBH, IMBD and JG; the board's goals for 2025-26 and 2026-27 and its progress reports (November 2024, May 2025, June 2026); the 2022 Math Task Force report; the Memorial project page (bond resolution, May 2024 budget update, building committee minutes), the January 2021 bond hearing slides and the 2018 facilities assessment; the 2021 and 2014 official ballots (scanned, transcribed); the 2026 facility maintenance plan; the October 1, 2026 enrollment report; the 2025-2028 technology plan and its replacement schedule; RSA 193:3-a and the Ed 300 rules; the site's database for derived comparisons.
- **Premises checked:**
  - **Renovated Pelham Memorial, and class sizes (P2.3): right about the renovation.** Voters approved $31,980,000 for "a major renovation and additions" in March 2021 (1,960 to 1,220; 1,908 needed), after a 2020 vote fell 31 votes short. Construction ran from August 2021 to spring 2024. Average class size fell from 21.7 (2020-21) to 17.8 (2023-24), while the school was still under construction and as enrollment fell about 22%. It was 18.4 and 17.8 in the two years since. The district's stated reasons for the project didn't include class size.
  - **Middle school math "around 30%" (P4.3): right for 2022–2025, out of date for 2026.** Memorial's share proficient was 24%, 29%, 29% and 30% in 2022–2025 and 45% in 2026, against 40–44% statewide in grades 6–8 in 2026. In 2024 Memorial ranked 12th of the district's 12 peer districts.
  - **40 hours of community service (P7.1): right.** It has been required since 1992, and the hours are due by March 15 of senior year.
  - **Four years of math (P12.1): right.** Pelham requires 3.5 math credits and "a math or math intensive course" every year. A state rule (Ed 306.22(o)) requires students to use math every year, but it lets that happen inside other subjects.
  - **Summer online courses left out of the GPA (P12.3): mostly right.** Summer, night, online and adult education courses can earn credit but don't count in the GPA. Courses from VLACS, the state's free online school, do count.
  - **"Free" dual enrollment (P4.2): partly.** Community college (CCSNH) courses cost $150, and state scholarships cover two a year for grades 10–12 (library). SNHU courses cost $100 plus $25 for a lab, paid by the family. Board policy IHCD/LEB says there is no cost for up to 4 credits a year.
  - **"Most kids get cut" (P9.1): not measured.** Pelham High says some sports cut and guarantees three days of tryouts, but it publishes no counts. Memorial says interest "far exceeds" team sizes.
  - **Twins (P18.2): no Pelham twins policy.** The principal decides class placement (policy JG). The state law gives parents a right to ask, but only for twins "in elementary school". No source says whether that covers grades 6–8.
  - **Lab equipment "from 15 years ago" (P19.3): not measured.** Pelham High was built in 1972, and voters approved a $22,654,000 addition and remodel in 2014 (a classroom addition in 2016). No inventory or age of lab equipment is published. Technology is on 4- to 6-year replacement cycles.

## district-classrooms-and-staff: Pelham Memorial's Class Sizes, Teams and Class Placement

- kind: local
- question_type: local_practice
- description: How a district's schools group classes and grades, their staff and roles, staffing changes, substitutes and aides, and how the schools describe themselves

## twins-classroom-placement: Classroom Placement of Twins and Other Multiples

- kind: rule
- question_type: rules
- description: New Hampshire's law on placing twins and other multiples in the same or separate classrooms: parents' requests, deadlines and the principal's decision

1. New Hampshire's law giving parents of twins or other multiples the right to request the same or separate classrooms (RSA 193:3-a, II) applies to twins "in elementary school", and the state's school approval rules describe an elementary school as "groupings of grades beginning with kindergarten and no grade higher than 8th" and a middle school as "any grouping of grades beginning with 4th grade and no grade higher than 8th", as organized by the local school board; the ban on school board policies that automatically separate or group twins (RSA 193:3-a, I) has no grade limit.
   - citation: RSA 193:3-a, I–II; N.H. Code Admin. Rules Ed 306.01(b)
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XV/193/193-3-a.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-21
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: RSA text: "I. No school board shall adopt a policy of automatically separating or placing together twins or other multiples ... II. A parent or guardian of twins or other multiples in elementary school may, no later than 60 days before the first day of each school year ... request". Ed 306.01(b) (https://gc.nh.gov/rules/state_agencies/ed300.html): "public schools composed of grades K through 9 may be organized consistent with RSA 189:25 as follows: (1) Elementary school, which offers groupings of grades beginning with kindergarten and no grade higher than 8th; (2) Middle school, which offers any grouping of grades beginning with 4th grade and no grade higher than 8th". The law doesn't define "elementary school" itself, and no source found says whether it covers a grades 6–8 school.
