# Indian River School, Student Support and Choices

Part of the Enfield research (town/Enfield). Gathered November 22, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers Indian River School (IRS, grades 5–8, in Canaan), which Enfield's children attend after Enfield Village School (EVS), and the support and choices around it in the Mascoma Valley Regional School District (SAU 62, a single-district SAU): the move from EVS in grade 5, math results and what the schools are doing about them, challenge for advanced students, special education staffing and who to talk to in a disagreement, the move to Mascoma Valley Regional High School (MVRHS) for students with IEPs, home-educated and EFA students' access to classes, sports and band, the high school's "MAPs" pathways, attendance and truancy rules, the lack of a late bus, help for grandparents with Alma and Chromebooks, counselors and the district social worker, help with coats, sneakers and fees, 504 plans, the phone rule and bullying on buses.

- **Main findings:**
  - **The move to IRS:** IRS combines the 4th graders of EVS and Canaan Elementary. On the first day of 2026-27 it had 280 students in four classrooms per grade. New 5th graders were "clapped in" by the rest of the school, and for the first week they left 10 minutes early while staff held up route signs at the buses. Every grade has a one-hour WIN (intervention) block each morning, letter grades by quarter, lockers and 30–60 minutes of homework 3–5 times a week.
  - **Math:** the site's data confirm about 40% of Mascoma students were proficient in math in 2023–2025 (44% in 2026, the same as the state). The lower results are in grades 5–8 and 11. On NWEA tests in 2025-26, IRS's grade 5 and 6 math medians fell from fall to spring (64th to 50th, 52nd to 41st percentile), with low growth medians (32nd and 34th). District documents describe responses (daily Reflex math-fact practice, Title I math help, new 7th and 8th grade math teachers, a five-year curriculum cycle) but don't attribute results to curriculum or staffing.
  - **Advanced students:** all four Mascoma schools told the federal civil rights survey they had no gifted and talented program (2021-22 and 2023-24). Policy IHBB defines gifted students and requires a yearly report to the state. In 2023-24 IRS ran one Algebra I class for 8th graders (18 enrolled, 16 passed), and 7th and 8th graders can earn high school credit (policy IMBD).
  - **Special education:** IRS has one special education case manager per grade, one more for higher-need students, a behavior specialist and ten paraprofessional slots (one "TBA" in the 2026-27 handbook). No IRS openings were posted on November 22, 2026. The district's 2026 state monitoring found no noncompliance in 13 IEPs reviewed. Nothing published shows whether IEP services are delivered in larger groups.
  - **Home education and EFAs:** the board re-adopted policy JJJ on October 27, 2026 to cover EFA students. It gives home-educated, EFA, charter and private school residents the same access to classes, sports and band as enrolled students, at the same fees, on a written request to the principal. The district's home education policy (2023) and webpage still describe the pre-July 2026 notice and evaluation rules.
  - **Bus bullying:** since August 2026 all grades ride the same buses because the district is three drivers short. On September 8, 2026 a board member reported a driver's account of middle schoolers picking on younger students; the driver now has an aide. Bullying policy JICK (re-adopted August 11, 2026) counts buses as school property. Most buses have cameras; audio is kept no more than 10 school days unless needed.
  - **No late bus:** no late or activity bus from Canaan to Enfield was found. The 2023–2028 strategic plan set a March 2025 target to estimate the cost of a late bus for middle and high schoolers.
  - **Phones:** under policy JICJ, phones and smartwatches are off and stored away from the first bell to dismissal, lunch and recess included. They aren't locked in pouches. A phone that's out is held in the office; the second time a parent must pick it up.
- **Main gaps:** the IRS sports participation fee amount (policy JJIC mentions a fee and a waiver, but no amount is posted); Mascoma's yearly gifted-and-talented report to NH DOE (NH DOE refuses automated requests); any written procedure for moving an IEP from IRS to MVRHS; whether paraprofessional slots are filled day to day; several board packet files Google Drive wouldn't release.
