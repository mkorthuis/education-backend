# Derry: Money, Taxes and Governance

Part of the Derry research (town/Derry). Gathered November 15, 2026.

## Summary

**What's covered:** 9 topics, 78 facts and 9 number tables on how Derry's school budget is set, paid for and voted on.
- **The budget and its growth.** The 2026-27 operating budget ($113,427,524, approved 1,187–575), what drove the $9.16 million (8.79%) increase, where the money goes (salaries and benefits 51%, Pinkerton 28.7%), and the district's enrollment since 2015-16 (5,335 to 4,153 projected). The draft 2027-28 budget of November 9, 2026 ($118.5 million).
- **Taxes.** The district's revenue and net assessment, its own estimated tax rates ($13.83 in February, $12.24 in November for 2026-27), adequacy aid falling by $429,167 in 2026-27, Derry's local school tax against inflation since 2015 (**derived**), Question 2 (65.0% yes in Derry) and the district's estimate that it would have to cut $2,465,395 from the 2027-28 draft to meet the new cap. Derry's elderly exemption, deferral and veterans' credits.
- **Pinkerton.** How Pinkerton is governed (a private nonprofit with trustees elected for life; each sending town represented by contract; 4 of 14 current trustees from Derry), how its tuition is set and trued up (Derry's $149,746.10 refund for 2024-25), Derry's 2026-27 Pinkerton costs by program, and the Fiscal Advisory Committee's report that Pinkerton's trustees declined contract changes giving sending towns more say.
- **Next Charter School:** Derry pays tuition for its residents under a memorandum of understanding ($11,568.61 per student in 2026-27); the state pays for students from other towns.
- **Buildings and bonds:** the failed 2024 bond and new-school votes, the $24.6 million bond passed in 2025, its 15-year payment schedule, what has been fixed and what hasn't started, the maintenance trust and the capital plan through 2033.
- **Governance and voting:** the $110 million amendment that failed at the February 7, 2026 deliberative session, turnout (8.8% in March 2026; 12,677 ballots in November), voting at Pinkerton's gym, absentee voting, the 2024 Derry Village School closing and the 2026 law letting the town council make the school district a town department.
- **Home prices:** median single-family sale prices in Derry, Hampstead, Windham and Londonderry for January–October 2026.

**Main sources:** SAU 10's election page, warrant, budget presentation, deliberative session FAQ, annual reports (2023–2025), Fiscal Advisory Committee and school board minutes (March–October 2026), the "School Board at a Glance" page, the HB 1300 estimate and November 9, 2026 draft budget; the Town of Derry's election returns and assessing pages; Pinkerton Academy's trustees page, budget narrative and finance narrative; Next Charter School's pages; RSAs and HB 1331's bill record; NH REALTORS® market updates; BLS CPI; the site's database; one Derry News article.

## charter-schools: Chartered Public Schools in New Hampshire

- kind: rule
- question_type: rules
- description: What a NH chartered public school is, cost, admission and lotteries, transportation, and special education.

1. For a New Hampshire charter school authorized by a school district, the pupil's resident district pays the school at least 80 percent of the district's average cost per pupil, while for New Hampshire pupils who attend full time from other districts the state pays adequacy tuition directly to the school; a charter conversion school charges no tuition for pupils from its own district.
   - citation: RSA 194-B:11, I(a)
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XV/194-B/194-B-11.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-15
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text: "For a chartered public school authorized by the school district, the pupil's resident district shall pay to such school an amount equal to not less than 80 percent of that district's average cost per pupil as determined by the department of education ... For pupils resident in this state who attend full-time a chartered public school authorized by a school district other than the pupil's resident school district, the state shall pay tuition amounts pursuant to RSA 198:40-a directly to the chartered public school". State-authorized charters are funded by the state (library charter-schools fact 4; choice-charter-district-special-education fact 7). Raw: raw/money/rsa/rsa-194-B-11.txt
2. New Hampshire law lets a charter school accept pupils at tuition rates below the amounts the charter law sets, lets a sending district give a charter school extra money, services, equipment or staff under its own policies, and keeps the resident district responsible, including financially, for the special education in a charter student's IEP.
   - citation: RSA 194-B:11, V(a)–(b) and III(c)
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XV/194-B/194-B-11.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-15
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text: "(a) A sending district may provide funds, services, equipment, materials or personnel to a chartered public school, in addition to the amounts specified in this section in accordance with the policies of the sending school district. (b) A chartered public school may accept pupils at tuition rates at less than the amounts established by this chapter." III(c): "The child's resident district shall have the responsibility, including financial responsibility, to ensure the provision of the special education and related services in the child's IEP". The special education duty is also library charter-schools fact 6. Raw: raw/money/rsa/rsa-194-B-11.txt
