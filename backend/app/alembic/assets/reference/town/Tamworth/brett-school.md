# The Kenneth A. Brett School (K–8)

Part of the Tamworth research (town/Tamworth). Gathered November 13, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers the Kenneth A. Brett School, the Tamworth School District's only school (kindergarten to grade 8): how classes are grouped and how many students it has now, paraprofessional and specialist staffing, reading and math support, grade 8 math, music and the arts, outdoor learning and the winter ski program, counseling, behavior training, bullying, phones, volunteers and donations, and how the school talks with families.

## dyslexia-screening-and-intervention: New Hampshire's Dyslexia Screening and Intervention Law

- kind: rule
- question_type: rules
- description: New Hampshire's law on screening students in kindergarten through grade 3 for dyslexia and related reading difficulties, its timelines, follow-up assessment, interventions and what parents are told (RSA 200:58–62)

1. New Hampshire law defines dyslexia as a specific learning disability that is neurobiological in origin and marked by trouble with accurate or fluent word recognition and by poor spelling and decoding, usually from a weakness in the sound (phonological) part of language.
   - citation: RSA 200:58, I
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XV/200/200-58.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-13
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Statute text: "(a) Neurobiological in origin; (b) Characterized by difficulties with accurate or fluent word recognition and by poor spelling and decoding abilities that typically result from a deficit in the phonological component of language". "Related disorders" include phonological processing disorder, reading fluency disorder and dysphasia (200:58, III). Raw copy: raw/school/state/rsa-200-58.txt
2. New Hampshire school districts must screen every student entering public school in kindergarten through grade 3, including English learners, with an evidence-based screener for signs of dyslexia within 60 school days, repeat it once more that year, and screen at least twice a year through grade 3.
   - citation: RSA 200:59, I
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XV/200/200-59.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-13
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Statute text: "The initial screening shall be completed no later than 60 school days of a student entering public school in kindergarten through third grade. The screening shall be repeated one additional time during the current school year, and repeated at a minimum of twice yearly through third grade to monitor progress." Raw copy: raw/school/state/rsa-200-59.txt
3. Under New Hampshire law, if a screening shows a student below benchmarks in foundational reading skills, the school must give a second assessment within 30 days to decide on extra evidence-based reading instruction or a special education referral, must tell the parents the results, and must start general education accommodations within 21 days of the first screening while any referral is pending.
   - citation: RSA 200:59, I-a (added 2023)
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XV/200/200-59.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-13
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Statute text: "the school must complete a secondary assessment within 30 days ... The school shall inform the student's parent or legal guardian of the screening results. If a special education referral is made ... general education accommodations shall be initiated within 21 days of the initial screening, and continue until a special education determination is made." Raw copy: raw/school/state/rsa-200-59.txt
4. Under New Hampshire law, schools must begin age-appropriate, evidence-based interventions by January 1 of the school year for students identified with signs of dyslexia, must give parents all screening information, and must consider an independent evaluation by a licensed reading or intervention specialist that parents submit at their own cost.
   - citation: RSA 200:59, II–IV
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XV/200/200-59.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-13
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Statute text: "shall provide age-appropriate, evidence-based, intervention strategies to begin by the January 1 of the school year"; "has the right to submit the results of an independent evaluation from a licensed reading or intervention specialist trained in dyslexia and related disorders ... A parent or legal guardian who submits an independent evaluation shall assume all fiscal responsibility". Raw copy: raw/school/state/rsa-200-59.txt
5. New Hampshire's dyslexia law requires "evidence-based" screening and intervention but does not name a method such as Orton-Gillingham or require schools to employ a certified dyslexia specialist; it requires NH DOE itself to have a reading specialist who gives districts technical help and keeps a reading support resource guide.
   - citation: RSA 200:58 to 200:62
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XV/200/200-60.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-13
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Read in full: 200:58 (definitions), 200:59 (screening and intervention), 200:60 (department reading specialist: "shall provide technical assistance for dyslexia and related disorders to school districts"), 200:61 (teacher awareness materials), 200:62 (resource guide). None names a specific program. 200:63 concerns head injuries, not dyslexia. School approval rules separately require each elementary school to have a licensed reading specialist (school-staffing-and-class-size). Raw copies: raw/school/state/rsa-200-58.txt to rsa-200-62.txt
