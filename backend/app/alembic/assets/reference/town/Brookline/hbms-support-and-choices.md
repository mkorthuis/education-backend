# Hollis-Brookline Middle School, Student Support and School Choices for Brookline Families

Part of the Brookline research (town/Brookline). Gathered November 21, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers what a Brookline family meets when a child leaves Captain Samuel Douglass Academy (CSDA, grades 4–6, Brookline School District) for Hollis-Brookline Middle School (HBMS, grades 7–8, Hollis-Brookline Cooperative): how many Brookline and Hollis students share each grade, HBMS's teams, orientation and advisory, homework and lockers, special education staff and small support classes at HBMS, bullying investigation timelines, making a team, math acceleration and math competitions, and what the cooperative spends on athletics against academic activities. It also covers Brookline's new access policy for homeschooled, private, charter and EFA students (policy JJJ, adopted October 28, 2026) beside its 2014 home education policies, homeschoolers at the Nashua Technology Center, busing to charter and private schools, and, for people looking to work in the schools, substitute and paraeducator requirements, pay and health benefits under Brookline's new 2026–2028 support staff contract, and current staffing gaps.

- **Counts:** 12 topic sections, 57 facts and 3 number tables. Places: `district Hollis-Brookline Cooperative` 27, `district Brookline` 21, `sau 41` 6, no place 3 (state rules).

### Premises checked

| Question | Premise | What the sources show |
|---|---|---|
| P5.3, P11.1 | At HBMS a Brookline child "merges with all the Hollis kids" in a bigger building | **Correct.** HBMS has 353 students in grades 7–8 in 2026-27, against 243 at CSDA (grades 4–6). Brookline residents are 81 of 179 seventh graders (about 45%) and 82 of 174 eighth graders (derived from NESDEC). Seventh graders are split into two teams with their own core teachers (school-programs-after-grade-6 1–3). |
| P6.1 | Homeschool paperwork is filed "with SAU #41 directly" | **Out of date.** Since July 1, 2026 no declaration or yearly evaluation is required unless a family wants to use school classes or activities or is withdrawing from a public school, and the law doesn't say where a declaration goes. SAU 41's page sends questions to the Assistant Superintendent; Brookline's 2014 policy sends requests to the Superintendent and its new JJJ policy to the building principal (district-homeschool-access 1–6; home-education-rules 1). |
| P6.3 | Homeschoolers might be able to apply to the Nashua Technology Center | **Correct.** The NTC takes homeschooled students by application, reviewed case by case, with no tuition; the Region 16 agreement makes the student's home district the sending district (district-homeschool-access 8; Nashua `pathways-and-adult-learning.md` cte-center-programs 5–6). |
| P13.1 | Brookline might bus students to ASD in Nashua or a private school | **No district busing to ASD.** State law requires a district to bus to a charter school only inside the district; ASD says Nashua residents may get Nashua's buses. Private school pupils get the same busing as public school pupils only within the district (district-transportation 1; library school-transportation-rules 2, 4). |
| P13.2 | New Hampshire has a "school voucher program" | **Partly.** The state's program is the Education Freedom Account (EFA), money in an account a parent spends on approved costs, not a voucher paid to a school. Any resident student has been eligible since June 2025, but the 2026-27 cap of 12,500 was reached in August 2026 (priority groups can still enroll). The site's data counts 55 Brookline EFA students for FY2027. |
| P15.1 | HBMS or HBHS "cut a lot of kids" | **Partly checkable.** Not all teams hold tryouts, and some do make cuts; no count of students cut is published. HBMS basketball rosters were 13 each in 2025-26 after tryouts, while outdoor track had 86 and cross country 39 (district-pe-outdoor-and-athletics 1–3; table hbms_sports_rosters_by_team). |
| P16.1 | "SAU #41" sets paraprofessional pay | **Partly.** Each district has its own support staff contract: Brookline's BESSA contract (2026–2028) pays paraeducators $17.69 to $26.19 an hour in 2026-27; the cooperative's and Hollis's HESSA contracts differ. Substitute day rates ($100 or $125) are set SAU-wide (district-budget-and-cost 1–7). |
| P16.2 | Subs and paras at RMMS or CSDA may need only "a couple years of college" | **Roughly correct for substitutes.** SAU 41 asks elementary substitutes for two years of college or work experience (three for grades 7–12); state rules let an unlicensed substitute work up to 20 days in a row in one position. Brookline's para posting asks only for a background check, with a degree and certification "preferred, but not required" (district-classrooms-and-staff 2, 6–7; educator-licensing-pathways 1–2). |
| P17.2 | HBMS has "honors or accelerated" math tracks leading to Algebra I in grade 8 | **Correct in substance.** HBMS's courses are leveled rather than labeled honors: in 2026-27, 64 eighth graders take Algebra and 23 take Geometry; 20 seventh graders take Algebra (Hollis `middle-and-high-school.md` district-enrichment-and-advanced-learners 1–4). |
| P17.3 | "Most of the extra funding" goes to sports | **Larger for sports, by the budget lines.** The cooperative's FY2027 budget puts about $886,651 in athletics (plus $103,018 for athletic buses) against $118,377 for extracurricular activities, including $4,100 for academic competitions, and $48,419 for performing arts (derived; district-budget-and-cost 9). |

## educator-licensing-pathways: Substitutes and Paraeducators: What New Hampshire's Credential Rules Require

- kind: rule
- question_type: rules
- description: New Hampshire paraeducator certification and the routes from paraeducator to a teaching license

1. Under New Hampshire's educator credential rules, a substitute teacher or school nurse without a state credential may be hired for up to 20 consecutive days in the same position, after which the person must hold a credential or a one-year certificate of eligibility.
   - citation: N.H. Admin. Rules Ed 503.01(b) (ss by #14575, eff. 5-13-26)
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rules/state_agencies/ed500.html
   - as_of: 2026-11-21
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text: "A non-credentialed substitute teacher or school nurse may be hired for up to 20 consecutive days in the same position without holding a New Hampshire educator credential issued by the state board, after which time the individual shall be required to hold a credential or a one-year certificate of eligibility pursuant to RSA 189:39-b." Districts set other requirements (SAU 41's are district-classrooms-and-staff 6–7). Raw: raw/middle/nh-ed500-rules.html (.txt)
2. New Hampshire's rules define a paraeducator as a person who works under the supervision of a teacher or other licensed professional to give instructional support to students and who "may be licensed" by the state board, so a state paraeducator certificate is optional under the rules; the Paraeducator I certificate requires only a high school diploma or HiSET.
   - citation: N.H. Admin. Rules Ed 501.03(h) and Ed 504.07
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rules/state_agencies/ed500.html
   - as_of: 2026-11-21
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text: "'Paraeducator' means a person who works under the supervision of a teacher or other appropriate licensed education professional to provide specialized or concentrated non-initial instructional support to students at the pre-, elementary, or secondary level and may be licensed by the board"; "To be certified as a Paraeducator I, an individual shall possess a high school degree or HiSET equivalent." **Derived:** "optional" follows from "may be licensed." Paraeducator II's requirements (an associate degree or 48 credits, the ParaPro test or a portfolio) are Madison `money-and-governance.md` educator-licensing-pathways 1. Brookline's contract pays $1.15 or $1.25 an hour more for the certificates (district-budget-and-cost 2). Raw: raw/middle/nh-ed500-rules.txt
