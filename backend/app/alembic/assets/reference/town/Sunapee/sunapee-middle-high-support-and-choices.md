# Sunapee Middle High School, Support and Choices: Special Education Staffing, Counselors, Substitutes, Phones, Bullying, Teams and Robotics, Sherburne Gym and the Fields, Mount Royal and EFA Students, and Homeschooling

Part of the Sunapee research (town/Sunapee). Gathered 2026-11-22.

## Summary

Covers Sunapee Middle High School (SMHS, grades 6–12, 10 North Road) and the district policies around it for families in Sunapee (SAU 85, one district): who staffs special education and therapy and what the public record says about open positions; the counselors, adjustment counselors and the social worker question; substitute teachers; the phone rule (policy JICJ and the 2026-27 handbook); bullying (policy JICK adopted August 27, 2026) and how a family can ask for a change of school; sports, co-op teams, the eighth-grade rule and robotics for a student who wants competitive teams; Sherburne Gym and the fields (use rules, fees, reserve funds, planned work); how Mount Royal Academy students and Education Freedom Account (EFA) students ride the district's buses, join teams and band, and transfer in (credits, math placement); and homeschool notice, class access and testing.

15 topics, 102 facts, no number tables. Main sources: the district's board policies (JJJ, JJJ-R, IHBG, JICJ, JICK, JCA, EEA, KG, KG-R, IK, JG, JLD, JJA, IMBD, IHBA); the SMHS 2026-27 student handbook and Program of Studies; the 2023-24 SMHS athletic handbook; the district's 2025 annual report (year ending June 30, 2025); School Board minutes from January 2025 to November 2026 (cleaned copies); the district's capital improvement pages (December 2025 fund balances); the SMHS and SAU 85 web pages; the federal Civil Rights Data Collection 2023-24 (Sunapee rows); Mount Royal Academy's website and 2026-27 family handbook; and the site's own enrollment data.

Main gaps: the Town of Sunapee's site (sunapeenh.gov, where Sunapee Recreation lists youth leagues and open gym) and sunapeeathletics.org refused automated requests, so youth basketball, who maintains Sherburne Gym day to day, and team schedules weren't read; the district's bus route spreadsheet needs a Google sign-in, so whether a stop serves Mount Royal wasn't read; no public count of open special education or paraeducator positions, therapy minutes delivered or missed, or substitute fill rates was found; the 2026-27 athletic handbook (approved April 1, 2026) wasn't posted, only the 2023-24 edition; whether the Robotics Club competes isn't stated; the district's board policies still describe the pre-July 2026 home education rules; NHIAA's rules for a Mount Royal student (a member school's student) trying out for a sport Mount Royal doesn't offer weren't read.

## district-attendance-zones-and-transfers: Asking for a Different School When a Student Is Struggling

- kind: local
- question_type: local_practice
- description: How a district assigns students to schools by home address, its feeder pattern from elementary to middle to high school, redistricting history and plans, proof of residency, and requests to attend a different school

1. Under Sunapee's policy JCA (second reading and approval February 4, 2026), a parent can ask the Superintendent in writing to reassign a student to another public school, public academy or approved private school, in or outside the district; outside the district the Superintendent meets the parent within 10 days and decides in writing within 5 school days, weighing the student's "academic, physical, personal, or social needs."
   - citation: Sunapee School District policy JCA, Change of School Assignment – Best Interests & Manifest Hardship, section A
   - source: https://www.sau85.org/common/pages/GetFile.ashx?key=JH7PBRSE

   - note: Text: "the Superintendent will schedule a meeting with the parent/guardian to be held within 10 days of receiving the written request"; "Within five school days of the reassignment meeting, the Superintendent shall deliver to the parent/guardian a written determination." Within the district the Superintendent considers only "existing school capacity." The state rule is RSA 193:3; the policy also says an open enrollment school doesn't need this process. Raw: raw/middle/sunapee-policy-JCA.txt
2. If the Superintendent declines, JCA lets the parent ask the School Board for a hearing on "manifest educational hardship," defined as a documented hardship in the current placement that harms the student's "academic achievement or growth, physical safety, or social and emotional well-being" and is "so severe, pervasive, or persistent that it interferes with or limits the ability of the student to receive an education."
   - citation: Sunapee School District policy JCA, section B.1
   - source: https://www.sau85.org/common/pages/GetFile.ashx?key=JH7PBRSE

   - note: Text: "'manifest educational hardship' means that a student has a documented hardship in his or her current educational placement; and that such hardship has a detrimental or negative impact on the student's academic achievement or growth, physical safety, or social and emotional well-being." The application is due within 30 days of the Superintendent's denial. Raw: raw/middle/sunapee-policy-JCA.txt
3. At a manifest hardship hearing JCA puts the burden on the parent "by clear and convincing evidence," has the Board hold the hearing within 15 days of the request and decide in writing within 7 days, lets the parent appeal to the State Board of Education within 30 days, and has the resident district pay tuition and provide transportation if hardship is found.
   - citation: Sunapee School District policy JCA, sections B.2–B.6
