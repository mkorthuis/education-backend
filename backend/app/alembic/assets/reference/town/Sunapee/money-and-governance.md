# Money and Governance: Budget, School Taxes, Reserves, the SAU, Bonds and the Future of Sunapee Middle High School

Part of the Sunapee research (town/Sunapee). Gathered 2026-11-22.

## Summary

This file covers the money and governance of the Sunapee School District (SAU 85, a single district): the 2026-27 budget ($15,715,564, approved 573 to 367) and what drives it; the district's own enrollment and tuition students (480 students on October 1, 2025, including 90 who pay tuition); how the school tax, the statewide education property tax and state aid fit together; the roof and other capital reserve funds; special education reserves and the contingency fund; the SAU office; the school bond votes of 2019 to 2022; consolidation and closing rules; where residents can find budget documents; and the town's own votes and capital items. Main sources: the district's 2026-27 budget hearing slides, warrant, function and object budget and deliberative session slides; its 2025 annual report (budget lines, revenue, default budget, contingency fund, the February 2025 deliberative session minutes and the auditor's report); its capital improvement fund balances page (December 2025), tuition page and Mandatory Report to Voters; Valley News Town Meeting reports (2019 to 2026); the NH Supreme Court's Rand and ConVal opinions (read from the library's saved copies); and the site's database for cost per pupil, salaries, taxes, adequacy aid and special education aid.

Counts: 10 topics, 76 facts, 9 number tables. One proposed new shared topic: `money-nh-school-funding-history` (how the Claremont cases and the statewide property tax led to today's mix of state, local and tuition money; four facts here).

Main gaps: the Town of Sunapee's website refused automated requests, so Sunapee's own elderly exemption amounts (in PDFs), the town's budget book and its capital plan were not read (P6.1, P20.3); the elderly exemption criteria were read later in a browser (property-tax-relief 1); Kearsarge Regional's tuition rate for other districts' students (its site also refused); how the 2022-23 elementary school renovation was financed and how the 2024 biomass boiler article is paid; paraeducator pay; the district's school board minutes and document libraries (loaded by script, no file list); the 2027 warrant and the tax year 2026 rates (not yet published); the whole-school-year special education expenditures page of the annual report (an image). No search block; six of eight web searches were used.

## money-nh-school-funding-history: How New Hampshire Came to Fund Schools With a Statewide Property Tax and Local Taxes

- kind: how_it_works
- question_type: governance_money
- description: How New Hampshire's school funding system took shape: the State Constitution's duty to fund an adequate education, the Claremont cases of 1993 to 1999 that struck down unequal local education tax rates, the uniform statewide education property tax that followed, and what pays for the rest

1. In its 1993 Claremont I decision the New Hampshire Supreme Court held that Part II, Article 83 of the State Constitution "imposes a duty on the State to provide a constitutionally adequate education to every educable child in the public schools in New Hampshire and to guarantee adequate funding," and the court has reaffirmed that holding repeatedly since.
   - citation: Contoocook Valley School District v. State, 2025 N.H. 29, ¶ 3 (quoting Claremont School Dist. v. Governor, 138 N.H. 183, 184 (1993))
   - source: https://www.courts.nh.gov/sites/g/files/ehbemt471/files/documents/2025-07/2025029conval.pdf
   - as_of: 2026-11-22
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Opinion ¶3: "Over thirty years ago in Claremont I, 138 N.H. at 184, we held that Part II, Article 83 of the State Constitution 'imposes a duty on the State to provide a constitutionally adequate education to every educable child in the public schools in New Hampshire and to guarantee adequate funding.' We have, time and again, reaffirmed our holding in Claremont I." To comply, "the State must 'define an adequate education, determine the cost, fund it with constitutional taxes, and ensure its delivery through accountability.'" The court's page refused this agent's request; the opinion text was read from the library's saved copy (additional-data/raw/terms/courts/2025-NH-29-ConVal-v-State.pdf), copied here. Raw: raw/money/law/2025-NH-29-ConVal-v-State.pdf; raw/money/law/2025-NH-29-ConVal-v-State.txt
2. The Supreme Court struck down education property tax schemes that taxed towns at different rates, in Claremont II (1997), in a 1998 advisory opinion on a plan that gave "excess" towns a special abatement and in a 1999 case about phasing in the full uniform rate for some towns, which left a uniform statewide rate, set by the Department of Revenue Administration to raise $363,000,000, as the way the state taxes property for education.
   - citation: Rand v. State, 2025 N.H. 27, ¶¶ 11 and 14 (citing Claremont II, 142 N.H. 462 (1997); Opinion of the Justices (School Financing), 142 N.H. 892 (1998); Claremont (Statewide Property Tax Phase-In), 144 N.H. 210 (1999)); RSA 76:3
   - source: https://www.courts.nh.gov/sites/g/files/ehbemt471/files/documents/2025-06/2025027rand.pdf
   - as_of: 2026-11-22
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Opinion ¶14 lists the holdings: "an education property tax involving disproportionate tax rates between towns violated Part II, Article 5"; a scheme that "subtracted, from the tax bills of taxpayers in 'excess' communities, a special abatement ... thereby reducing the effective rate of the state tax for those taxpayers" was unconstitutional; and a scheme that "phased in the full uniform tax rate over five years for certain towns, while imposing the full rate immediately on the remaining towns" violated Part II, Article 5. ¶11: "the DRA sets the SWEPT tax rate 'at a level sufficient to generate revenue of $363,000,000 when imposed on all persons and property taxable pursuant to RSA 76:8.'" The opinion doesn't tell why the state relies on property taxes more than on other taxes; it describes what the court allowed and struck down. Read from the library's saved copy. Raw: raw/money/law/2025-NH-27-Rand-v-State.pdf; raw/money/law/2025-NH-27-Rand-v-State.txt

   - citation: Rand v. State, 2025 N.H. 27, ¶ 13; library money-education-trust-fund 1-2; library money-school-revenue-by-source-trend 1 and 6
   - source: https://www.education.nh.gov/sites/g/files/ehbemt326/files/inline-documents/sonh/summary-of-rev-exp-fy2025.pdf
   - as_of: 2026-11-22
