# Exeter High School and the Seacoast School of Technology for Stratham Students

Part of the Stratham research (town/Stratham). Gathered November 21, 2026.

## Summary

Stratham's students in grades 9–12 attend Exeter High School (EHS), and those who choose a career program attend the Seacoast School of Technology (SST), both run by the Exeter Region Cooperative School District. Exeter's research already covers both schools in depth (`town/Exeter/exeter-high-school.md` and `town/Exeter/cms-sst-and-getting-to-school.md`); this file cites those facts and adds only what they lack: how to call a student out and the 2026-27 calendar, snow-day make-up and closing rules, Canvas pairing codes, sports tryouts, team levels, eligibility and costs, the cooperative's athletics budget lines, booster clubs and gifts, how businesses can take interns or post jobs, work-based learning in SST programs, the high school dual-enrollment course list, the Class of 2030's financial literacy requirement, work-study practices, New Hampshire's electrician licensing rules, Stratham's share of the cooperative's high school students, and a federal comparison of AP and dual-enrollment participation at Exeter, Portsmouth and Winnacunnet. No source treats Stratham students differently from students of the other five towns.

## occupational-licensing-rules: Electrician Licenses and Apprentices in New Hampshire

- kind: rule
- question_type: rules
- description: New Hampshire licensing rules for trades and health careers that career programs prepare students for: plumbing and fuel gas licenses, apprentice and trainee licenses, required training and work hours, exams and minimum ages, and nursing assistant (LNA) licensing

1. Under New Hampshire law, a journeyman or master electrician license requires 8,000 hours of service as an apprentice electrician (the Electricians' Board may give credit for a course at a school it recognizes), at least 600 hours of education meeting the board's criteria and a passing exam, and apprentice electricians must register with the state's Office of Professional Licensure and Certification.
   - citation: RSA 319-C:7, II and IV
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XXX/319-C/319-C-7.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-21
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text: "(a) Completion of 8,000 hours of service as an apprentice electrician. The board may give credit toward such service for the satisfactory completion of a course of instruction in the field at a school recognized by the board or experience in the field received in military service"; "(b) Complete not less than 600 hours of education that meet criteria established by the board"; "(c) Satisfactory passing of an examination"; "IV. Apprentice electricians shall register with the office." RSA 319-C:2, I defines an apprentice electrician as one "learning and assisting in making electrical installations under the direct supervision of a master or journeyman electrician." Whether the board recognizes SST's Electrical Technologies program for credit was not found. Raw: raw/high/rsa-319-C-7.txt and .html; the whole chapter in raw/high/rsa-319-c.txt
2. New Hampshire's electrician licensing law does not cover electrical installations in homes or businesses done by students in a high school vocational electrical program approved by the Department of Education, as long as the work is supervised by a teacher holding an electrician's license or by a licensed electrician supervising students in cooperative education placements.
   - citation: RSA 319-C:3, IX-a
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XXX/319-C/319-C-3.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-21
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text: "Any electrical installations in residential or commercial buildings performed by students enrolled in a high school vocational electrical program, college vocational electrical program, and apprenticeship training program, approved by the department of education, provided such work is performed under the supervision of either a teacher holding an electrician's license or by a licensed electrician who is a supervisor of students in cooperative education placements from such programs". Installations in a new building built as part of the program must be inspected. Raw: raw/high/rsa-319-C-3.txt and .html
3. Under New Hampshire law, a journeyman electrician may directly supervise one apprentice electrician in the first year after being licensed and two after that, and a master electrician may supervise two apprentices, with limits on how many electricians at one work site may supervise two.
   - citation: RSA 319-C:2-a, I–IV
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XXX/319-C/319-C-2-a.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-21
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text: "I. A journeyman electrician shall directly supervise no more than one apprentice in the first year following his or her initial date of licensure. A journeyman electrician may have 2 apprentice electricians under his or her direct supervision beginning on the first anniversary"; "III. Each master electrician on a work site may have 2 apprentice electricians working under his or her direct supervision, to a maximum of 6 master electricians per work site."; "IV. The maximum number of licensed electricians with 2 apprentice electricians working under their direct supervision on a work site shall not exceed 6." Raw: raw/high/rsa-319-C-2-a.txt and .html
