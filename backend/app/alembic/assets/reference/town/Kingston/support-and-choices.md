# Special Education, Student Support, Safety and School Choices in Kingston (Sanborn Regional)

Part of the Kingston research (town/Kingston). Gathered November 21, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers the Kingston residents' questions on IEPs and paraprofessional support when a child moves up to Sanborn Regional Middle School, what happens to aides and reading help when special education costs rise, home-educated students' access to Sanborn sports and classes and the home education paperwork, building security and school resource officers, how many families leave for private or charter schools, the bullying and phone policies, private and charter costs and buses, moving back to Sanborn from a charter school, counselors and social workers at Sanborn Regional High School, 504 plans, a quiet place during the day, and the attendance and truancy policy.

- **10 topics, 66 facts and 2 number tables.** All topics are shared slugs from `town/shared-topics.md`. Raw copies are in `raw/support/` (about 8 MB): board policies in `raw/support/policies/`, school board minutes in `raw/support/minutes/`, special education documents in `raw/support/sped/`, Seacoast Classical Academy pages in `raw/support/sca/`.
- **Places used:** `district Sanborn Regional` for the district's schools, policies, staff, budget and board (district-wide facts Newton's research can cite); `district Seacoast Classical Academy Chartered Public School` for the charter school in Newton; `town Kingston` for drive distances from Kingston and the derived count of Kingston resident students by grade.
- **Main findings:**
  - **Sanborn adopted policy JJJ on September 16, 2026.** Home-educated, EFA, charter and private school students who live in Kingston or Newton may take Sanborn courses (band, art, a lab science) and join activities and teams, by a parent's written request to the principal, on the same eligibility, tryout and fee terms as enrolled students (attendance excepted). Middle school teams play in the Tri-County League.
  - **No yearly home education paperwork is due to SAU 17.** New Hampshire dropped the yearly evaluation and most notices on July 1, 2026. Sanborn's posted policy IHBG (last revised January 4, 2023) still describes the old evaluation rules (a district test passed at the 40th percentile, or a portfolio review).
  - **Moving up with an IEP:** the IEP carries over and must be in place when the school year starts. Sanborn's plan says transition supports from school to school are written into the IEP. Grades 6–12 have their own assistant director of special education. The middle school runs the Zones, Connections and Connections Resource Room programs and lists 11 paraprofessionals, 6 of them contracted (August 2026, with 1 opening).
  - **Aides have been cut more than special education teachers:** from 2019-20 to 2026-27, paraprofessionals fell from 64.80 to 53.00 full-time equivalents and special education teachers from 27.00 to 26.80, while "teacher specialists" rose from 22.50 to 28.00. The FY28 proposal sets $5,089,713 for special education outside personnel. The 2026 warrant asked voters to add up to $108,712 from year-end funds to a Special Education Expendable Trust Fund.
  - **Security:** the first set of outside doors at every Sanborn school is unlocked during the day. The facilities director said the schools fail a state security survey every year because of this; locking them would cost about $3,500 per school ($8,000 at Bakie). A $54,650 state SAFE grant is paying for card readers on the middle school's classroom doors. One school resource officer is listed for the middle and high school, and a Newton officer is assigned to the Memorial campus.
  - **Phones are banned bell to bell** under policy JICJ (revised November 19, 2025): phones must be off and in a locker or backpack all day, lunch included. The bullying policy JICK was rewritten on August 19, 2026, with 48-hour parent notice and a 10-school-day investigation.
  - **Seacoast Classical Academy**, a tuition-free charter school on Sanborn's Newton campus, now runs kindergarten to grade 10. Sanborn buses Kingston and Newton children in kindergarten to grade 5 there on Memorial's routes. Hampstead Academy is about 6.7 miles from the Sanborn middle and high school campus (derived).
  - **Kingston's grade counts barely changed from one year to the next in the latest data:** Kingston's resident public school count was 41 in grade 5 (2024-25) and 41 in grade 6 (2025-26), and 53 in grade 8 and 52 in grade 9 (derived from the site's data).
  - **Counselors:** in 2023-24 Sanborn Regional High School reported 3.5 counselor positions for 526 students, about 150 each (derived), and a quarter-time social worker. Its November 2026 directory lists four counseling staff, a social worker and two school psychologists. 99 high school students (18.8%, derived) had 504 plans.
- **Main gaps:** the 2026-27 middle and high school handbook (phone confiscation steps, passes and any calming room) was not found online; which Sanborn middle school teams exist and the Tri-County League's own rules; whether a student keeps the same paraprofessional (no source addresses named staff); official 2026 vote results and the balance of the special education trust; the Kingston Police Department's school resource officer arrangement (the town site refused automated requests); a 504 timeline in days (the handbook says only "a reasonable time").

The library's rules (IEPs, 504 plans, home education, charter schools, truancy, transportation, phones and bullying law) and earlier towns' facts (Hampstead Academy's tuition, the NHIAA home-education by-law, overspending rules, ASCA's state counselor ratio) are cited in the table.

| P12.3 | "A lot of families switch to private or charter schools by middle school" | **Not shown in the latest year.** Kingston's resident public school count held at 41 from grade 5 to grade 6 and went from 53 to 52 from grade 8 to grade 9 (2024-25 to 2025-26, derived). | P14.1 | Teachers "take phones away during class" | **The policy goes further.** JICJ bans personal devices from the first bell to dismissal, lunch included; they must be off and stored in a locker or bag. Consequences follow the code of conduct, and administrators may hold a device during an investigation. The handbook's step-by-step consequences weren't found online. |
| P14.2 | Hampstead Academy and a "regional charter" are options, and Kingston might cover transportation | **Partly right.** Hampstead Academy (grades preschool–8) charges $15,650 for kindergarten to grade 5 and $17,750 for grades 6–8 (Hampstead's file). Seacoast Classical Academy in Newton is tuition-free. The school district, not the town, provides buses: Sanborn buses Kingston children in kindergarten to grade 5 to Seacoast Classical on Memorial's routes, and it has no duty to bus children to a private school outside the district. |
| P18.2 | The 504 process has a usual length | **No set length.** Sanborn's Section 504 Handbook says the team meets, evaluates and decides "within a reasonable time," and federal 504 rules set no number of days (library section-504-plans 5). |
| P19.3 | A truancy letter comes after "a few days" of absence | **Only for unexcused absences.** Under JH, illness, appointments and principal-approved trips are excused. Habitual truancy is 10 unexcused half-days in a year, and the principal sends a letter when a student reaches it or is in danger of reaching it. |
