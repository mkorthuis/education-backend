# Kingston: Money, Taxes and Governance

Part of the Kingston research (town/Kingston). Agent 4. Gathered 2026-11-21.

## Summary

**What's covered.** The Sanborn Regional School District's money and governance, written for both member towns so the Newton research can cite it: which school each grade attends; how the district is governed (board and Budget Committee seats, the SAU office, the new separate SAU budget); the 2026-27 budget, its default budget and the March 2026 results; the 2027-28 (FY2028) budget now being built (continuing-service proposal, default, spending by category, health insurance, the teachers' contract); why school taxes rose faster than the budget (the loss of Fremont tuition); the Question 2 tax cap as Sanborn's own briefing explains it; staffing and retention; the $12.9 million building repair list, a possible bond and the facilities study; the community budget survey; meeting dates, the 2027 calendar and filing period; and shared services with the towns. 9 topics (8 shared and 1 proposed), 62 facts and 3 number tables.

**Main sources.** The district's own Google Drive files, linked from sau17.net's menus (minutes and agendas of the School Board and Budget Committee, the FY2028 budget calendar, the 2026-27 board calendar, the November 4, 2026 FY2028 budget presentation, the October 21, 2026 HB 1300 briefing and the facilities study RFP); the Town of Newton's posted 2026 school ballot with tallies and Siemens slides; the January 15, 2026 Budget Committee hearing minutes; the site's tax, enrollment and Question 2 data for derived comparisons. sau17.net itself now shows a bot check, so two of its pages are cited from the copies the library saved on November 8, 2026.

### Premises checked

| Question | Premise | What the sources show |
|---|---|---|
| P2.1 | Kingston and Newton children are split between Bakie and Memorial | Not by town. The district groups schools by grade: every child from both towns attends Bakie (in Kingston) for preschool to grade 2, Memorial (in Newton) for grades 3–5, and the middle and high school campus (in Kingston) for grades 6–12. |
| P3.1 | Sanborn is "down to around 1,200 kids" | Out of date. The district counts 1,100 students in 2026-27 (1,217 in 2024-25, 1,148 in 2025-26), down 478 (30.3%) since 2019-20. The site's latest school enrollment total is 1,207. |
| P3.1 | The school tax bill "goes up every single year" | Mostly, recently. The district's property tax revenue fell $632,686 for 2024-25, then rose 8.62% and 6.21% for 2025-26 and 2026-27 while the operating budget rose 2.23% and 1.75%; the Budget Committee chair tied part of the jump to Fremont tuition falling from $3.8 million to $2.88 million. Kingston's local school rate per $1,000 (site data) was $10.34 (2023), $10.26 (2024) and $10.79 (2025). A bill also depends on each home's assessment. |
| P3.2 | Passing the default budget would cut spending | Not in the last two cycles. For 2026-27 the default ($42,493,451) was $1,464,541 more than the proposed budget voters passed ($41,028,910). For 2027-28 the November 2026 preliminary default ($41,994,754) is $23,456 more than the continuing-service proposal. A default removes only eliminated positions and one-time costs from last year's budget. |
| P11.2 | Kingston may pay "more than our fair share" | The formula was not found. Derived from site data: in tax year 2025 Kingston paid 54.5% of the two towns' local school tax while holding 58.8% of their equalized property value and about 52% of their resident students; Kingston's equalized local school rate ($9.35) was lower than Newton's ($11.59). "Fair" is a judgment the data can't make. |
| P11.3 | A budget survey asked residents "what to cut" | Partly. The district's FY2028 Community Budget Survey (September 25–October 27, 2026, 143 submissions) asked each respondent to pick three priorities and weigh factors; the published summary shows no "what to cut" question, though written comments "call for cost restraint and for protecting staff and opportunities." The survey is the school district's; the master plan is the Town of Kingston's, and its Planning Board was updating it in September 2026. |
| P15.1 | A teacher turnover problem around 2018–2022 | No turnover rate is published. Indicators: 47.9% of staff have under 5 years at Sanborn; classroom teacher FTEs fell from 125.31 to 88.50 (2019-20 to 2026-27) as enrollment fell; teacher pay averaged 8.6% below the state in 2024-25; staff retention became a board goal in December 2025, with quarterly retention data and exit interviews. Whether it is "fixed" can't be measured from these sources. |
| P17.1 | "New state school budget cap rules" | It is a cap on the local property tax levy plus a 6% cap on SAU central-office spending (RSA 32:5-i), which Kingston and Newton voters adopted together on November 3, 2026 (site data). It limits the levy, not the budget; it first applies to the FY2028 budget voted March 9, 2027; a 60% vote can override it; bond payments are outside it. |
| P17.2 | "A huge bond coming down the pike" | Correct as of November 2026: the district is preparing a building repair bond. Siemens' October 2026 list totals $12,885,000 (roof, rooftop units, siding, Kalwall, masonry, two boilers); the September range was $15–20 million. No amount has been set. The buses are contracted (Coach Co.), not district-owned. |

## intermunicipal-agreements-rules: Sharing Services Between Towns and School Districts

- kind: rule
- question_type: rules
- description: How New Hampshire towns, school districts and SAUs may share services and facilities by agreement under RSA 53-A: who may join, what an agreement must say, and the Attorney General's review.

1. New Hampshire law lets any two or more public agencies, including towns, school districts and SAUs, exercise their powers jointly by agreement, including shared financial, human resource, information technology and other administrative functions, once each governing body approves it.
   - citation: RSA 53-A:2; RSA 53-A:3, intro and I
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/III/53-A/53-A-3.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-21
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: RSA 53-A:2: "the term "public agency" shall mean any political subdivision of this state ... including but not limited to school districts, school administrative units, village districts". RSA 53-A:3: "Any power or powers, privileges or authority exercised or capable of exercise by a public agency of this state may be exercised jointly with any other public agency of this state ... I. Any 2 or more public agencies may enter into agreements with one another for joint or cooperative action ... Appropriate action by ordinance, resolution or other action pursuant to law of the governing bodies of the participating public agencies shall be necessary". Raw: raw/money/rsa/rsa-53-A-2.txt; raw/money/rsa/rsa-53-A-3.txt
2. A New Hampshire joint agreement between public agencies must state its duration, purpose, financing and budget and how it ends, must name an administrator or joint board if it creates no separate entity, and takes effect only after the Attorney General reviews it, with approval automatic if not disapproved within 30 days.
   - citation: RSA 53-A:3, II, III and V
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/III/53-A/53-A-3.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-21
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text: "II. Any such agreement shall specify the following: (a) Its duration; ... (c) Its purpose; (d) The manner of financing the joint or cooperative undertaking and of establishing and maintaining a budget therefor; (e) The method to be employed in accomplishing the partial or complete termination"; "V. Every agreement made hereunder shall, prior to and as a condition precedent to its entry into force, be submitted to the attorney general ... Failure to disapprove an agreement submitted hereunder within 30 days of its submission shall constitute approval thereof." Purpose: RSA 53-A:1 (raw/money/rsa/rsa-53-A-1.txt).
