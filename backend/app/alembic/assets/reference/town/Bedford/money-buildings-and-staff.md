# Bedford: Money, Buildings, Staff and Special Education Services

Part of the Bedford research (town/Bedford). Gathered November 16, 2026.

## Summary

Covers the Bedford School District's (SAU 25) budget trend and what drives it, the failed FY2027 operating budget and the default budget that replaced it, what the 2025 local tax-cap petition and the 2026 state tax cap (Question 2) would have meant, the 2026 tax rate, the teachers' and support staff contracts, administrator pay, technology spending and IT staff, textbook leases and curriculum review, the capital plan, bonds and debt, building repairs and elementary space, lead testing of school drinking water, special education staffing and who helps in a disagreement, and community use of school buildings, fields and adult classes.

**10 topics, 92 facts and 6 tables.** All topics are shared slugs from `town/shared-topics.md`. Raw copies are in `raw/money/` (about 16 MB): the FY2027 and FY2028 budget documents from the district's Google Drive (superintendent's summaries, default-budget reductions, technology, maintenance and capital plans, special education staffing, textbook leases), 15 of the 40 school board minutes read (June 2025 to November 2026; the cited ones), special education parent council minutes, the March 10, 2026 election results, the 2025 annual report (OCR of the scanned PDF already in `additional-data/raw/districts/batch-3/`), the BEA and BEPA contracts, the Mandatory Report to Voters, Round 3 lead test reports for all six schools, Policy KF-R, the town's assessing page and November 3 tally.

**Main findings:**
- Voters rejected the FY2027 operating budget on March 10, 2026 (2,032 yes, 2,158 no), so the default budget of $97,136,083 applies; on April 13, 2026 the board approved $673,649 in cuts, including deferred building projects, software and cameras.
- The district's own figures put the FY2027 local school levy at $67,285,323 and show contracts and health insurance growing faster than the 3.6% inflation figure in the state tax cap; Bedford's Question 2 vote (59.2% yes) fell short of 60%, so no cap applies for FY2028–FY2029.
- The middle/high school construction bond's last payment is in FY2030, when yearly debt service falls from about $3.3 million to about $0.42 million; the long-term capital plan lists about $26.5 million of projects through 2037–38, including a $7.08 million BHS/Lurgio HVAC estimate. No new bond has been proposed as of this date.
- Special education: 560 students with IEPs and 149 support staff (paraprofessionals, ABA technicians and others) across the schools on October 1, 2026; paraprofessional pay runs $17.65 to $32.65 an hour.
- Round 3 lead testing (spring 2024) found 15 of 290 outlets at or above 5 parts per billion, 7 of them at Memorial; results are posted.

**Main gaps:** no public data on whether IEP service minutes are delivered in full; the school share of the $14.73 2026 tax rate (in the town's interactive report and on NH DRA's blocked site); home sale prices by elementary attendance zone; any lead testing after 2024; whether BHS has a pool (no district document lists one).
