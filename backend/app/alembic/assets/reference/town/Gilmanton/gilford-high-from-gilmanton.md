# Gilford High and the Huot Center From Gilmanton

Part of the Gilmanton research (town/Gilmanton). Gathered November 21, 2026.

## Summary

Covers Gilford High School (GHS) and the career centers from Gilmanton's side, for the questions assigned to agent 3: how Gilmanton students reach the Huot Technical Center and the Winnisquam Career Center (WiCC); the move from a K–8 school of about 300 to GHS; how IEPs and 504 plans move under the AREA plan; hands-on, shop and agriculture courses; the late bus; how Gilmanton students fit in; driver education; Gilmanton's 2026-27 high school bus runs; and whether a Gilmanton student can go to another public or private high school, including open enrollment at Prospect Mountain High School and Gilmanton's change-of-assignment policy (JCA).

**Counts:** 10 topics, 31 facts and 2 number tables.

**Main sources:** the SAU 79 and Gilmanton School websites, which no longer require a Google sign-in (they did on November 8, 2026, per `additional-data/district-practices.md`): Gilmanton School District policies JCA, EEA, EEA-R, IKFG and IHBA; the Gilmanton Special Education Handbook; the Student Services and Bus Routes pages; Gilmanton's 2026-27 route document; and 14 sets of Gilmanton School Board, joint Gilford–Gilmanton board and AREA subcommittee minutes from October 2025 to November 2026. Also used: the Gilford–Gilmanton AREA plan and GHS documents already saved by the Gilford research (`town/Gilford/raw/high/`), RSA 193:3, 194-D, 195-A:4-a and 263:19, and road distances from OpenStreetMap and OSRM (school addresses only).

**Main gaps:** No count of Gilmanton students at Huot or WiCC. No posted late bus schedule, and the record conflicts on whether one runs in 2026-27. No GHS driver education course or price was found, and the NH DMV's list of driving schools refused automated requests. No graduates' plans for Gilmanton residents alone, and nothing on how many stay in the Lakes Region. No survey of how Gilmanton students feel at GHS. Gilmanton's 2025 town report (UNH Scholars) refused automated requests.

**Proposed new shared topic:** `driver-education-rules` (kind rule, question type rules): "New Hampshire's driver education requirement for license applicants under 18: who may teach it, the classroom, driving and observation hours, and the supervised driving log." No existing topic covers it; earlier towns put local driver education fees under `district-fees`, as this file does for GHS.

**Earlier files:** 3 of the 14 questions (P10.3, P12.2, P15.3) are answered wholly by earlier files and the site's data; the Gilford file's facts are cited, not rewritten.

In June 2026 the joint boards were told students "have access" to Huot and WiCC (cte-center-programs 1–2). No count of Gilmanton students at Huot was found. |

| P16.3 | Gilmanton students are "locked into Gilford" | **Not entirely.** Policy JCA allows best-interest and manifest-hardship reassignments, and the AREA law allows hardship exemptions. | P17.2 | Open enrollment would need New Hampshire to pass it | **Partly.** The 2026 statewide bills died (library `open-enrollment-status` 6–7). A voluntary law already exists, though: Prospect Mountain High School is an open enrollment school, and Gilmanton has paid its tuition bills. No open enrollment option was found at Gilford, whose board chose in January 2026 to wait, or for Gilmanton's elementary grades (high-school-tuition-options 3; district-attendance-zones-and-transfers 5). |
| P19.1 | The town pays nothing toward a private school such as Tilton or Bishop Brady | **Mostly, with an exception.** Under policy JCA the board may agree to pay an "approved private school" in a best-interest or hardship placement. This is at its "sole discretion" and not when a viable public option exists. Two students attended Bishop Brady under best-interest placements in September 2026, and the minutes don't say who pays (district-attendance-zones-and-transfers 2, 4). Otherwise families pay, or use a state Education Freedom Account. |

## district-attendance-zones-and-transfers: Asking for a Different High School From Gilmanton

- kind: local
- question_type: local_practice
- description: How a district assigns students to schools by home address, its feeder pattern from elementary to middle to high school, redistricting history and plans, proof of residency, and requests to attend a different school (added from the Manchester research)

1. Gilmanton School District policy JCA (revised June 9, 2026) lets a parent ask the superintendent in writing to reassign a student to a public school, public academy or "approved private school" outside the district because it is in the student's best interest; the superintendent meets the parent within 10 days, weighs the student's academic, physical, personal or social needs, answers in writing within 5 school days, and any reassignment lasts no longer than the end of the next school year, subject to review.
   - citation: Gilmanton School District Policy JCA, Change of School Assignment – Best Interests and Manifest Hardship (adopted November 14, 2023; revised June 9, 2026), Section A
   - source: https://drive.google.com/file/d/19oANkU1hihxpmMnPRmlxLbtlhBV5lIYq/view
   - as_of: 2026-11-21
   - review_by: 2027-11-21
   - place: district Gilmanton
   - note: Text: "the parent/guardian shall submit to the Superintendent a written request stating why and/or how the child's best interests warrant the change"; "the Superintendent will schedule a meeting ... to be held within ten (10) days of receiving the written request"; "the Superintendent shall consider the student's academic, physical, personal, or social needs"; "Within five (5) school days of the reassignment meeting, the Superintendent shall deliver to the parent/guardian a written determination"; "A reassignment on the basis of best interest of the student shall be limited to no longer than the end of the ensuing school year." A transfer outside the district "requires agreement of the other school/district." The June 2026 revision followed 2025's SB 97 (policy committee minutes, May 12, 2026). Linked under Section J on https://www.sau79.org/policies. State rules: library `change-of-school-assignment` 1–8. Raw: raw/high/sau79/JCA.pdf (.txt)
2. Under policy JCA, for a best-interest reassignment to a public school or academy in another district the superintendents set a tuition rate that the board approves, while an "approved private school" (one the board has approved and contracted with) may charge the parent or reach a payment agreement with the district, which is at the board's "sole discretion," weighs the fiscal impact and "shall not be granted" if there are other viable public school options; parents provide transportation for best-interest reassignments.
   - citation: Gilmanton School District Policy JCA, Section A.2 (Tuition Determination) and A.3 (Transportation), and definition of "approved private school"
   - source: https://drive.google.com/file/d/19oANkU1hihxpmMnPRmlxLbtlhBV5lIYq/view
   - as_of: 2026-11-21
   - review_by: 2027-11-21
   - place: district Gilmanton
   - note: Text: "'approved private school' means a school that has been approved and contracted by the School Board to provide a student or students with the opportunity to acquire an adequate education"; "If the student is reassigned to an approved private school under this policy, that school may charge tuition to the parent/guardian or may enter into an agreement for payment of tuition [with the] district in which the student resides ... Any such Agreement shall be subject to approval by the Board on behalf of the District and shall be at the sole discretion of the Board with due consideration given to the fiscal impact of such approval of the District, and shall not be granted if, in the opinion of the Board, there are other viable public school options for reassignment"; "Transportation for a student reassigned to a school in another district under this Section A (best interest) shall be the responsibility of the parent/guardian." The state's constitution bars tax money for schools of a religious denomination (`public-funds-and-religious-schools` 1, Hudson file); this policy doesn't address that. Raw: raw/high/sau79/JCA.txt
3. ## change-of-school-assignment: Asking to Change Schools Within or Outside the District

- kind: rule

- description: NH's process for requesting a different school (RSA 193:3, as amended by SB 97 in 2025), manifest educational hardship, and tuition-based enrollment in another district.

## gov-area-agreements: AREA Schools (Authorized Regional Enrollment Areas)

- kind: term
- question_type: governance_money
- description: What an AREA agreement is and how it differs from a cooperative.

1. New Hampshire's AREA law lets the school boards of the sending and receiving districts agree, on a showing of hardship, to exempt a sending district's pupils from having to attend the area school; the sending district then pays tuition to the school the pupil actually attends, and each exemption lasts one school year and is renewed only if both boards agree.
   - citation: RSA 195-A:4-a
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XV/195-A/195-A-4-a.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-21
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text: "the school board of a receiving district and the school board of a sending district may mutually agree upon a showing of hardship by pupils from a sending district to exempt such pupils from any agreement requiring them to attend the receiving district's schools. A pupil exempted from such agreement would make suitable arrangements to attend school outside the receiving district. The sending district shall be liable for tuition payments to the district of actual attendance. Any exception so granted shall be for the period of one school year and shall be renewed only upon mutual agreement." The Gilford–Gilmanton plan's tuition section refers to it: Gilmanton pays for each resident at GHS "except as provided under RSA 195-A:4-a or through mutual agreement" (town/Gilford/raw/high/district/area-agreement-2023-2035.txt, p. 2). ## driver-education-rules: Driver Education for New Hampshire Teens

- kind: rule
- question_type: rules
- description: New Hampshire's driver education requirement for license applicants under 18: who may teach it, the classroom, driving and observation hours, and the supervised driving log.

1. In New Hampshire a person under 18 can get a driver's license at 16 only with a certificate of completing a driver education course, given by a public or nonpublic high school with an approved course or by a licensed driving school, of at least 30 hours of classroom instruction (no more than 15 of them online), 10 hours of behind-the-wheel training and 6 hours of observation.
   - citation: RSA 263:19, I
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XXI/263/263-19.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-21
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text: "A driver's license may be issued ... to a person under the age of 18 years who has attained his or her sixteenth birthday, if such person shall present a certificate of successful completion of a driver education course given by a public or nonpublic secondary school and approved by the department of education in cooperation with the department of safety or given by a motor vehicle drivers' school licensed under the provisions of this chapter. An approved driver education course ... shall consist of not less than 30 hours of classroom instruction, not less than 10 hours of behind the wheel driver training and not less than 6 hours of observation"; "No more than 15 hours of the classroom instruction may be satisfied through the completion of an online driver education course." Proposed new shared topic (see Summary). Raw: raw/high/law/rsa263-19.html (.txt)
2. A New Hampshire license applicant under 18 must also certify 40 hours of supervised driving with a licensed parent or guardian (or, if there is none, a licensed adult over 25), at least 10 of them at night, and a person with a disability covered by the ADA may ask the commissioner of safety to waive a requirement.
   - citation: RSA 263:19, II and IV
