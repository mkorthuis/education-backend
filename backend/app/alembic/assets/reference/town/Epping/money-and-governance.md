# Epping: Money, Governance, Buildings and the Years Ahead

Part of the Epping research (town/Epping). Gathered November 21, 2026.

## Summary

**What's covered:** 13 topics (11 local, 2 statewide library topics), 88 facts and 5 number tables on Epping's 2026 tax rate and what drives the school part of it, the school tax cap Epping adopted by about 5 votes in November 2026 and the district's estimate of what it means for the FY2028 budget, the $38 million wastewater bond, the June 2026 special meeting over the unsigned budget form, the new separate SAU budget article, what happens to year-end surpluses, the 2025 roof, boiler and air handler project and what the capital plan shows next, the FY2027 budget by function, work-based credit and financial literacy at Epping High, the 2026-27 calendar, Fremont's high school students, and the population and enrollment outlook.
- **Taxes.** Epping's 2026 rate is $16.50 per $1,000: local education $10.86, state education $1.01, municipal $4.01, county $0.62. From 2025 the school part rose $0.68 and the municipal part $0.78 (**derived**). The district's own rule of thumb is "$1,745,000.00 = $1.00 on the tax rate."
- **Question 2.** Epping voted 2,280 to 1,511 (60.14%), about 5 votes over the 60% needed (**derived**), so both the school tax cap and the SAU cap apply from the FY2028 budget (unofficial count). On November 5 the superintendent described an anticipated shortfall of almost $888,000, about $0.51 per $1,000 (**derived**), and a plan to cut that amount and offer voters a separate article to restore it. The district's flyer shows the levy would have exceeded the cap in three of the last five years.
- **Wastewater.** Voters approved the $38 million plant bond 905 to 556 (61.9%, **derived**), to be repaid by general taxation; the town estimated about $1 per $1,000. It is a town cost, outside the school cap.
- **Special meeting.** NH DRA found that the MS-27 budget form was posted without the budget committee's certification signatures. The board held a hearing on June 11 and a special meeting on June 18, 2026 to ratify the February 5 session under RSA 31:5-b and 40:16. The district says no figures changed. The outcome was not posted.
- **Governance.** Since August 18, 2026, single-district SAUs like SAU 14 fall under the separate SAU budget vote (HB 564), so the March 2027 ballot is expected to carry one (**derived**). A 2024 petition seeking the same thing locally was turned into an advisory study and passed 1,105 to 417.
- **Surpluses and buildings.** The FY2026 year-end fund balance of $1,981,919.45 went 44.5% to lower taxes, 24.5% to the 2.5% voters let the district keep, 18.9% to the capital reserve voters approved and 12.0% to year-end expenses (**derived**). The 2025 project replaced the high school roof and air handlers and the SAU building's roof and boiler plant, financed by a $6,945,718 note to 2042. The capital plan lists a $250,000 fire panel, $450,000 of elementary HVAC and exhaust fans, and long-range ideas of a $4 million field and track and a $7 million elementary addition.
- **After high school and the calendar.** ELOs, including internships, can earn credit with prior approval. Economics & Personal Finance is required. The four "Work Study Practices" are graded habits, not a job program. Vacation weeks are December 23–January 1, February 22–26 and April 26–30. The only half day is June 18.
- **Enrollment outlook.** The state projects Epping's population to grow 3.5% from 2025 to 2030 (**derived**). The district's capital plan still uses 2018 projections that ran 73 to 92 students high (**derived**). Fremont's high schoolers are now coming to Epping High, and estimated tuition revenue rises from $20,500 to $316,968.

**Main sources:** SAU 14's Business Office page (MS-27 forms, warrant, proposed budget book, tax impact sheet, Mandatory Report to Voters, capital improvement plans, the FY2025 audit), the HB 1300 page with the district's flyer and slides, the special-meeting notice and warrant, School Board minutes from December 2024 to November 2026, the 2024 and 2025 annual reports, the 2026-27 calendar, the Epping High Program of Studies and School Profile for 2026-27; the Town of Epping's tax rate, elderly exemption, voting results, sample ballot, warrant overview, wastewater FAQ and 2025 Town Report; NH OPD's 2022 population projections; RSA 31:5-b, 32:5, 40:16, 194-C:3 and 194-C:9; and the site's database for derived comparisons.

## budget-official-ballot-sb2: Official Ballot Voting (SB2)

- kind: how_it_works
- question_type: governance_money
- description: How the two-session "SB2" form of town and school district meeting works.

1. Under RSA 31:5-b, when a New Hampshire town or district vote is later found to be procedurally defective (a minor irregularity such as the time or place of notice, a hearing or wording), the governing body may call a special meeting to ratify it; the meeting must be at least 21 days after the original vote, a public hearing explaining the reasons must be held at least 7 days before it, and ratification needs the same majority as the original article, after which the action is valid as if every required step had been followed.
   - citation: RSA 31:5-b, II–III
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/III/31/31-5-b.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-21
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text: "Procedurally defective actions shall mean minor procedural irregularities such as failure to comply with statutory requirements regarding time or place of notice, vote, hearing, or wording, or with any procedural act not contrary to the spirit or intent of the law ... (b) The special town meeting called for that purpose may not take place less than 21 calendar days after the original vote. (c) Not less than 7 calendar days prior to the special town meeting ... the governing body shall conduct a public hearing ... (e) The necessary majority required to cure the defects shall be the same as the majority as required for passage of the original article." Raw: raw/money/rsa-31-5-b.html/.txt
