## Summary

**What's covered:** 6 topics, 51 facts and 4 number tables on the Northwood School District's (SAU 44's) money and governance. Updated November 22, 2026 with SAU 44's document library (minutes, the FY2028 budget drafts, the FY2025 audit, policies and newsletters), which a bot check had blocked earlier in the day.
- **Taxes:** the district's own February 2026 estimate for the 2026-27 (FY27) school tax rate ($10.36 per $1,000, up $0.98, plus $0.23 for the teachers' contract); how NH DRA built the tax year 2025 local school rate ($8.46) from the budget, state aid and a 33.9% rise in assessed value (**derived**); the levy's growth against falling resident enrollment (**derived**, site data); what a change in state aid does to the rate, and the hold-harmless grant's $83,820.50 drop for FY2028, which the district's business administrator confirmed; why the November 2026 school tax cap doesn't bind Northwood; a per-student comparison with Epsom and Pittsfield (**derived** table); and the 2025-26 surplus ($375,000 returned to reduce taxes, and what the board spent before year end).
- **Budget:** the $16,205,552 FY27 budget and the teachers' contract, both approved March 10, 2026 ($16,502,333 together, **derived**); FY27 drivers; the high school share (about 30%, **derived** table, FY2024–FY2028); Coe-Brown tuition and special education at Coe-Brown; cost per pupil; administrator salaries; the 2026–2029 salary schedule and the board's comparison with 10 nearby districts (tables); and the FY2028 proposal: $17,720,415 on November 4, cut to $17,418,522 (+5.55%) by November 18, with health insurance and special education accounting for $797,818 of the increase.
