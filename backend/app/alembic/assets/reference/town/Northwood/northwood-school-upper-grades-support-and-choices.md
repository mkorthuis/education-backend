# Northwood School's Upper Grades, Support and Choices

Part of the Northwood research (town/Northwood). Gathered November 22, 2026; updated the same day from the district's document libraries.

## Summary

This file covers Northwood School's middle grades and the support and choices around them: accelerated math, band, clubs and trips; Chromebooks and phones; school counselors and mental health help; the bullying policy, bus conduct and how to raise a concern; speech, occupational therapy and special education, and whom to contact in SAU 44; homeschooled and EFA students' access and the home education notice; building security and drills; middle school sports, tryouts and cuts; volunteering, chaperoning and background checks; and private, charter and EFA options near Northwood.

**Sources.** The district's websites (sau44.org, northwoodschoolnh.gov) show a bot check to automated requests, but the main session listed their document libraries (town/Northwood/raw/browser/), whose files download directly. This file now rests on: Northwood School's **2026–2027 Student & Family Handbook** and athletics forms; current Northwood School Board policies (JJJ, JJJ-R, JFBC, JFBC-R, IHBG, IJOC, KI, JICK, JICJ, JJH, JJIC, IHBB, JLD, EBCA); approved board minutes from August 2025 to October 2026 (principal, technology, student services and superintendent reports); the 2026 Deliberative Session Guide and the November 16, 2026 draft 2027–28 budget (whose "SY27 Approved" column gives the adopted 2026–27 amounts); SAU 44's job board; the LBA's SAFE grant list; Epsom Central's 2026 league soccer schedule; New Hampshire law; and the NEASC visiting report of October 2020. Two derived pieces use the site's data and the library's saved EFA feeds: drive times to nearby private and charter schools, and EFA providers within 10 miles. The 2022–23 handbook used in the first draft has been replaced.

**Counts:** 12 topics (11 shared district and town slugs plus the library's `home-education-rules`), 70 facts and 1 number table (drive times from Northwood School to nearby private and charter schools, derived).

**New data concepts:** a K–8 district's own special education monitoring results (timely evaluations, the yearly LEA determination, ESY counts, IEP meeting and referral counts); contracted therapy providers and costs; an EFA participation policy (half-time classes, no special education, full activity costs); a sports program's roster sizes, cut rule and athlete conduct contract; a state security assessment and door system replacement; yearly volunteer fingerprinting and a paid volunteer coordinator; drive times to private and charter schools; EFA providers near the school.

**Main findings:**
- Accelerated math is offered to 7th and 8th graders who show sufficient progress (2026–27 handbook), and the draft 2027–28 budget buys "updated Algebra materials for grade 8."
- Grades 6–8 may take their Chromebooks home in a voluntary program with a $25 insurance cost; in March 2026 the technology director reported more damaged equipment.
- Speech services are contracted: two speech-language pathologist positions are budgeted but "Currently positions are outsourced" (a speech pathologist and an assistant from Dunstan Pediatrics, $248,710 in 2025–26). The state found Northwood 100% compliant on timely initial evaluations (November 2025), and its 2025–26 special education rating was "Meets Requirements."
- Policy JJJ (revised September 2, 2026) gives home-educated, EFA, charter and private school residents the same access to classes, sports, band and clubs, by written request to the principal; JFBC limits EFA students to half the school day, no special education services and the full cost of club activities with fees.
- Policy IHBG, still posted, describes the pre-2026 homeschool notice (to a "participating agency" of the family's choice, never yearly); since July 1, 2026 state law requires none except to use district programs or on withdrawal.
- Sports: soccer, basketball (A and B), cheerleading, baseball and softball for grades 6–8 and cross country and track for 5–8; recommended roster sizes, and "If a team must make cuts during try-outs." Cross country is unlimited.
- Since September 2025 volunteers are fingerprinted every year; overnight trip chaperones must be district employees, which the superintendent cited when the 2026–27 eighth grade Washington trip was not scheduled.
- Security: a new door security system (August 2025), $98,545 in SAFE grant money, and a voluntary state security assessment in June 2026. No source names a school resource officer at Northwood School.

**Main gaps:** what the $25 device insurance covers; the 2026–27 middle school club list; the yearly gifted-and-talented report; whether the Drama Club (student-led, grades 2–4 in 2025–26) includes middle schoolers; any school resource officer arrangement; the SAU's two Home Education forms (HTTP 403). No source measures how many Northwood families choose private or charter schools.
