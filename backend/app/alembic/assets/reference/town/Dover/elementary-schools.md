# Dover Elementary Schools and the Early Years

Part of the Dover research (town/Dover). Gathered November 15, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers Dover's three elementary schools (Frances G. Hopkins Elementary School at Horne Street, Garrison and Woodman Park, kindergarten to grade 4) and the years around them: class sizes and room to grow, the class-size policies, how the FY2027 budget scenarios would have touched elementary staff, English learner services at Horne Street, how the three schools' staff, schedules and budgets differ, the district preschool, summer reading help, behavior supports and paraeducators, STEM classes and clubs, home prices next to Durham and Portsmouth, and the move to grade 5 at Dover Middle School.

- **8 topics, 62 facts, 4 number tables.** All topics use shared slugs from `town/shared-topics.md`; no new topic is proposed. Raw copies are in `raw/elementary/` (about 41 MB, 110 files), with School Board files in `raw/elementary/board/` and page images in `raw/elementary/img/`.
- **Sources:** SAU 11's policies (IIB, IIBA, JF, JF-R1, FBB, IHCA, IHBBA, IKA, IKB), the FY27 Enrollment Capacity Report (June 2026), the FY2027 budget book and the December 2025 and January 2026 budget workshops, School Board minutes and agenda packets (February–November 2026, from the city's public records site), the three elementary family handbooks and the Dover Middle School handbook, the Lau (English learner) plan and the yearly EL parent notice, the Title I and Title III pages and resolutions, Woodman Park's 2024-25 Title I plan, the 2026 Spring Databook, the MTSS pages, the school web pages, the City of Dover's budget announcement, NH REALTORS® market updates and the site's own database (for one derived comparison).
- **Main gaps:** how long ESOL pull-out sessions last at Horne Street (the Lau plan's service-time table is blank in the posted PDF); the number of paraeducators at each school; whether the summer tutoring that replaced summer school ran in 2026; waitlist lengths at Dover child care programs (two web searches found only third-party directories); any after-school STEM or coding club at Garrison; the 2026-27 Title I plans, which are on Google Drive behind a sign-in; and School Board records before February 2026.
- **Spot-checked against raw copies:** Policy IIB's "Kindergarten - Grade 2 : Maximum class size is limited to 25 students per educator" (`policy-IIB.txt`); the capacity report's "Elementary schools do not have capacity to add additional classroom spaces" (`img/cap-p7.png`, transcribed in `board/capacity-report-2026-06-08.transcribed.txt`); and the Title III page's "support around 160 EL students" (`title3.txt`, line 138).

**Premises checked**

| Question | Premise | What the sources show |
|---|---|---|
| P1.1 | Garrison is getting crowded "with all the new housing going up on the north side" | Not borne out by the enrollment figures. The site's data shows Garrison's enrollment falling from 476 in 2015-16 to 353 in 2024-25, and the district's June 2026 capacity report projected 309 students for 2026-27, about 16.3 per classroom teacher, the lowest of the three schools. | P11.3 | The district may offer "free summer school" | No elementary summer school was found. Woodman Park's 2024-25 Title I plan says the district replaced traditional summer school with one-on-one tutoring and summer learning kits. Title III money pays for a summer program for English learners, the Title I "Traveling Tales" book van ran in summer 2026, and the budget has a summer program line under special education. |
| P24.3 | K–4 class sizes may be "capped by policy" | Correct. Policy IIB (revised February 9, 2026) caps K–2 at 25 and grades 3–5 at 30 students per educator, with targets of 20 and 25. A separate 20-student cap for first grade (Policy IIBA) was withdrawn on April 13, 2026. |
| P29.1 | At grade 5 "all three elementary schools merge together" at Dover Middle School | Correct. Dover Middle School serves grades 5–8 for the whole city; its 2026-27 grade 5 was projected at 254 students. |

1. Dover School Board Policy IIB, revised February 9, 2026, limits classes to 25 students per educator in kindergarten through grade 2 and 30 in grades 3 through 5, middle school and high school, and says that whenever possible classes should be 20 or fewer in K–2 and 25 or fewer in grades 3 and up.
   - citation: Dover School District Policy IIB, Class Sizes and Student-Educator Ratios (adopted May 12, 2014; revised February 9, 2026)
   - source: https://4.files.edl.io/a0c0/02/10/26/180117-a6104754-7a5a-4c04-ad3c-8429842d4e51.pdf
   - as_of: 2026-11-15
   - review_by: 2027-11-15
   - place: district Dover
   - note: Page text: "1. Kindergarten - Grade 2 : Maximum class size is limited to 25 students per educator. However, it is the policy of the School Board that class sizes for Kindergarten through grade 2 should, whenever possible, be limited to 20 students or fewer per educator. 2. Grade 3 - Grade 5: Maximum class size is limited to 30 students per educator ... whenever possible, be limited to 25 students or fewer ... 3. Middle School - High School: Maximum class size is limited to 30 students per educator ... 25 students or fewer". The Board approved the revision 7–0 on February 9, 2026 (raw/elementary/board/min-2026-02-09-130079.txt, item L.2). The state rule, Ed 306.14, sets the same limits; see library topic school-staffing-and-class-size, facts 4–5. Raw: raw/elementary/policy-IIB.txt

   - citation: Dover School District Policy IIB, sections A and B (revised February 9, 2026)
