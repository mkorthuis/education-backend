# Student Support and School Life in Dover: Translation, Special Education Follow-Through, Counseling, Phones, Conduct, Lunch, Meals, Holidays and Military Families

Part of the Dover research (town/Dover). Gathered November 15, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers student support and daily school life in the Dover School District (SAU 11): how schools reach families and handle other languages, how families stay informed (portal, weekly emails, school board records and video), how Woodman Park and the other schools carry out IEPs and 504 plans, counselors and mental health supports with students per counselor at each school, the bell-to-bell phone rule and how Dover High and Dover Middle enforce it, the bullying and conduct policies and what parents are told after bullying or fights, Dover High's lunch periods and closed campus, school meals (Meatless Monday, special diets, halal), religious holidays and absences, placing transfer students, and support for military-connected students.

- **8 topics, 98 facts and 1 number table.** Seven topics are shared district slugs (`district-family-communication`, `district-special-education-services`, `district-student-support`, `district-technology-and-phones`, `district-conduct-policies`, `district-school-day-and-calendar`, `district-meals`), all with `place: district Dover`; one adds federal facts with no place to the library topic `military-families-nh` (programs, help). No new topic is proposed. State and federal rules are left to the library slugs named in the question table (`interpreters-and-translation-for-parents`, `special-ed-iep-meetings-and-consent`, `special-ed-who-is-parent`, `special-ed-moving-with-iep`, `section-504-plans`, `special-ed-disputes`, `parent-communication-rights`, `climate-phone-free-school-day`, `bullying-school-duties`, `compulsory-attendance`, `measure-truancy-vs-chronic-absence`, `military-children-compact`, `school-staffing-and-class-size`), Nashua's `military-children-compact` facts and Concord's proposed `school-meal-modifications` topic (`town/Concord/student-life-and-climate.md`).
- **New data concepts:** listed school counselors at each Dover school for 2026-27 with derived students per counselor; Dover High's caseload split by last name; special education case managers and paraprofessionals listed at Woodman Park, Dover Middle and Dover High; Dover's IEP implementation and meeting-request procedures; the bell-to-bell phone policy's tiered consequences at each level; bullying investigation and parent notice timelines in Dover's September 2026 policy; Dover High's behavior matrix for fights; the district's Lau (English learner) plan commitments on translation; the TRAILS early-intervention grant; the federal Military and Family Life Counseling program.
- **Main sources:** Dover School Board policies JICJ, JICK, JIC, JICD, JH, IC, JG, IK, JFAM, JLD, KA, EG, IHBA and IHBA-R; the district's 2023 Lau Compliance Plan, Special Education Procedures Plan (revised May 18, 2021), ESOL, Title III, Student Services, Food Services, MTSS, Mental Health and Central Office pages; the 2026-27 Dover High and Dover Middle handbooks and the 2025-26 Woodman Park handbook; school counseling, student services and military family pages; the 2026-27 academic calendar; school board agendas and minutes (City of Dover public records); the City's school board and video pages; and the U.S. Defense Department's MFLC Program Guide (April 2025).
- **Main findings:**
  - Dover lists 16 school counselors across its five schools for 2026-27. **Derived** students per listed counselor run from about 177 (Garrison) to about 235 (Dover Middle, one counselor per grade); Dover High's six counselors serve about 225 each, under the state cap of 300. Dover High also lists two social workers and a school psychologist.
  - Policy JICJ (August 11, 2025) bans personal devices from first bell to dismissal, lunch included. Dover High extends this to headphones and smartwatches; a second phone violation means lunch detention and a parent pickup. No annual phone-policy review report to the board (due April 1) was found.
  - Policy JICK (September 14, 2026) requires parents of both students to be told within 48 hours of a bullying report, a decision within 20 school days, and a follow-up to both families within 10 school days. Policy JIC requires parent notice of simple assaults. At Dover High, fighting is a major or critical behavior with a school resource officer referral column.
  - Dover High has no open campus: no student may leave without permission, and leaving is treated as truancy. The cafeteria is called "Town Square." Lunch is one of four 30-minute periods.
  - Dover policy says "reasonable effort will be made" to communicate with parents in their primary language, and the 2023 Lau plan commits staff to translating documents and interpreting meetings and planned a cultural liaison. No posted way to request an interpreter, and no cultural liaison on current staff lists, was found.
  - Religious holidays are excused absences in policy JH, but Dover counts excused absences toward chronic absenteeism (10% of the year).
  - Policy JFAM promises military-connected students access to licensed counseling services. No Dover source names a Military and Family Life Counselor; Dover High lists a regional military school liaison.
- **Main gaps:** any measure of how well IEPs or 504 plans are carried out at Woodman Park or elsewhere (NH DOE's special education reporting page refused automated requests); how strictly teachers enforce the phone rule in class; menus and whether halal meals are offered (the menu site is a JavaScript app); how families request interpreters or translated newsletters; the district communication plan required by policy EG; whether any Dover school has an MFLC.

## military-families-nh: Support for Military Families

- kind: programs
- question_type: help
- description: Who to call about school and family support for National Guard and military families in NH.

1. The Defense Department's Military and Family Life Counseling (MFLC) program provides up to 12 sessions per issue of free, short-term, confidential non-medical counseling on and off military installations, for active-duty, National Guard and reserve members (regardless of activation status) and their families, among others.
   - citation: U.S. Department of Defense, Military and Family Life Counseling Program Guide (April 2025), Section 2, "Eligibility" and "Mission"
   - source: https://download.militaryonesource.mil/12038/MFLC/MFLC-Program-Guide.pdf
   - as_of: 2026-11-15
   - review_by: 2027-11-15
   - note: Text: "Active-duty service members, National Guard and reserve members (regardless of activation status), Coast Guard members when activated for the Navy ... and their immediate family members are eligible for services" and "Military and family life counselors and child and youth behavioral military and family life counselors provide up to 12 sessions per issue of face-to-face, short-term confidential counseling, consultation, and outreach services both on and off military installations." Linked from Military OneSource's program page (https://www.militaryonesource.mil/resources/benefits/military-and-family-life-counseling-program/). The guide says services are free elsewhere on Military OneSource; the guide itself does not mention cost. Raw copy: raw/support/mflc-program-guide.txt (pages 5–6)
2. Child and youth behavioral MFLCs work alongside child and youth programs, Defense Department schools, camps and local public schools, giving non-medical counseling and coaching on topics such as deployment and reunion, moving, separation from parents and bullying, and a parent must consent in writing before a child under 18 is helped.
   - citation: U.S. Department of Defense, Military and Family Life Counseling Program Guide (April 2025), "Child and Youth Behavioral Military and Family Life Counselors"
   - source: https://download.militaryonesource.mil/12038/MFLC/MFLC-Program-Guide.pdf
   - as_of: 2026-11-15
   - review_by: 2027-11-15
   - note: Text: "CYB-MFLCs provide support to families and augment child and youth programs, Department of Defense Education Activity schools, local education agencies, and camps designed for military-connected children and youth ... Parents must acknowledge, in writing, their consent to CYB-MFLC support and whether their child under the age of 18 may receive assistance from a CYB-MFLC." Topics listed include "Bullying ... Separation from parents ... Coping with deployment and reunification; Transition and moving". "Local education agencies" means public school districts. Raw copy: raw/support/mflc-program-guide.txt (page 6)
3. MFLC services are requested by installation points of contact and approved by each military service's headquarters, and child and youth counselors placed in a school serve the whole academic year, with counselors allowed to support activities within 50 miles of their assignment.
   - citation: U.S. Department of Defense, Military and Family Life Counseling Program Guide (April 2025), "Scope of Practice," "Counselor Work Schedules" and "Counselor Travel"
   - source: https://download.militaryonesource.mil/12038/MFLC/MFLC-Program-Guide.pdf
   - as_of: 2026-11-15
   - review_by: 2027-11-15
   - note: Text: "MFLC services are requested directly from installation points of contact, and the requirement is generated and approved by the service headquarters points of contact, with the MFLC Program Manager as the final approval authority." and "School rotations for CYB-MFLCs cover the entire academic year, which may range from nine to 12 months." and "Both MFLCs and CYB-MFLCs are authorized to provide support for activities within a 50-mile radius of their assignment location." So a school district does not hire an MFLC itself; a nearby installation requests one. Raw copy: raw/support/mflc-program-guide.txt (pages 5–7)
4. Military OneSource's online MFLC Locator lists contact information for counselors assigned to specific installations, units, schools and other locations; the April 2025 guide says more than 2,400 counselors work at nearly 5,000 secondary locations.
   - citation: U.S. Department of Defense, Military and Family Life Counseling Program Guide (April 2025), "MFLC Locator"
   - source: https://download.militaryonesource.mil/12038/MFLC/MFLC-Program-Guide.pdf

   - review_by: 2027-11-15
   - note: Text: "The MFLC Locator is an online directory on MilitaryOneSource.mil that provides easy-to-find contact information for an MFLC assigned to specific installations, units, schools and other locations. With over 2,400 counselors on the ground and nearly 5,000 secondary locations, the MFLC Locator is a dynamic tool".
