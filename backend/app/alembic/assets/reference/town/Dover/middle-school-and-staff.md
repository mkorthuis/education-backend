# Dover Middle School and Staff

Part of the Dover research (town/Dover). Gathered November 15, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers Dover Middle School (DMS, grades 5–8) and the district's staff: how DMS groups students into grade-level teams, its counselors, deans and special education staff, its curriculum, grading, homework rules and screening tests, accelerated math and high school credit, world languages, extra help and tutoring, the move to Dover High School, and the district's substitutes, paraeducators and new teachers (pay, vacancies, contracts, induction and coaching).

- **9 topics, 59 facts and 2 tables.** Raw copies are in `raw/middle/` (school and district pages, policies, contracts, and School Board files from the city's public records system in `raw/middle/board/`), about 23 MB.
- **Main findings:**
  - **Teams in every grade.** The 2026-27 staff directory lists five two-teacher teams in grade 5, two four-teacher teams and one two-teacher team in grade 6, and three four-teacher teams each in grades 7 and 8. A January 2026 budget slide warned that team positions "need to be cut in multiples of 4 FTE's to maintain middle school structure."
  - **Accelerated math is growing.** The superintendent's October 2026 report says DMS "expanded accelerated math to grades 6 and 7." Policy IMBD (revised December 2025) lets 7th and 8th graders earn high school credit for advanced courses if the high school principal approves first. No posted DMS course list says whether Algebra I is offered in grade 8.
  - **Substitutes are short.** A School Board agreement with the teachers' union (October 2025) begins "the District does not always have enough substitutes to cover teacher absences" and pays teachers who absorb five or more students from an uncovered class. In September 2025 the district had 47 vacant positions (44.29 FTE), 20 of them filled through staffing agencies. A posting for 50 substitute teachers has stayed open since October 2024. The Board added 11 paraeducator positions in October 2025.
  - **New paraeducator contract.** The 2026-2029 contract, approved September 14, 2026, raises the hourly scale (a new instructional paraeducator moves from $16.73 to $17.76 an hour in 2026-27), then 4% in each of the next two years. Substitute teachers earn $80 a day, or $100 with a NH teaching license (policy GCG).
  - **New teachers.** The teachers' contract requires an orientation in the first three weeks and spares new teachers from covering classes during their planning periods for three years, but it doesn't mention mentors. The district's 2024-2029 professional development plan describes a first-year New Teacher Induction program. In the district's 2026 staff survey, "Feedback & Coaching" was the lowest-rated area (27% favorable).
  - **Reading help.** DMS uses HMH Into Reading and Into Literature in grades 5–8 and began a Title I UFLI program in 2026-27; UFLI's developer calls it "an explicit and systematic program" for foundational reading skills, usable for intervention "in any grade." No source names Orton-Gillingham training for DMS staff.
- **Main gaps:** which world language DMS teaches; how students are chosen for accelerated math; whether DMS offers Algebra I in grade 8; staff turnover and vacancy counts for paraeducators and substitutes by year; any formal mentor assignment for new teachers; Orton-Gillingham or structured literacy training for middle school staff; any homework-load or peer-conflict data by grade; the DMS Title I plan (behind a Google sign-in); whether the state's free Tutor.com program continues in 2026-27; and any comparison of DMS curriculum or special education with Massachusetts schools.
