# Kennett Middle and High School for Madison Students

Part of the Madison research (town/Madison). Gathered November 13, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers what happens to Madison students after Madison Elementary School (K–6, SAU 13): the move to A. Crosby Kennett Middle School (KMS) in grade 7 and on to Kennett High School and the Mount Washington Valley Career and Technical Center (MWVCTC), all run by the Conway School District in SAU 9. Most Kennett and KMS questions were already answered by the Conway, SAU 9 and Jackson research, so this file maps each question to those facts first. It adds Madison's own side: how many Madison students are in each grade at KMS and Kennett, the steps Madison and KMS take before grade 7, Madison's 2003 contract terms on IEPs, 504 plans and reports, Madison's special education counts and service hours at KMS and Kennett, and Madison's policy on homeschooled students. It also adds the statewide rules on IEP responsibility and career-center access, and checks the questions' premises.

- **12 fact topics, 61 facts and 3 number tables.** Places: 17 facts use `district Madison`, 25 `district Conway`, 3 `sau 13`, 4 `town Madison`, and 12 have no place (state law and rules, NH Transfer, NH DOE's statewide grid and one national survey). Two library topics get added facts: special-ed-moving-with-iep (6) and dual-enrollment-rules (1).
- **Main sources:** Madison School Board minutes (September 2024 to October 2026) and the October and November 2026 board packets, which include Madison's 2026-27 enrollment by grade and school, the KMS and KHS principals' reports and SAU 13's special education quarterly update. Also Madison's 2003 grades 7–8 and 9–12 tuition agreements with Conway, Madison policies JJJ and IHBA, the KMS home page, handbook and band materials, Kennett's 2026-27 Program of Studies, handbook, counseling and Boot Camp pages, the ELO handbook, RSA 186-C, RSA 188-E and Ed 1100, NH DOE's 2025 CTE program grid, CCSNH's Early College contacts, the site's database (derived comparisons), and The White Mountain School's own 2024 home page.
- **New data concepts:** Madison students by grade and school for grades 7–12 (including charters, a private school and homeschoolers); Madison's weekly IEP service hours at KMS and Kennett; Madison's contract right to quarterly reports on its students; a derived comparison of Kennett's SAT results and graduates' plans with five southern NH high schools, Kingswood and the state; the statewide rule that makes a homeschooler's home district the "sending district" for career-center purposes.
- **Premises checked:**
  - "Merge into the 7th-grade class at KMS": confirmed. In October 2026, 21 Madison seventh graders were among 141 at KMS. Madison chose in 2024 not to send its sixth graders.
  - "Honors tracks": Kennett calls its upper level "Advanced", not honors. KMS has no honors track; its only accelerated course is grade 8 Algebra 1a.
  - "Band/orchestra": KMS has band, a revived choir and a new jazz ensemble. No orchestra or string program was found at KMS or Kennett.
  - A 13-year-old taking career-center courses part-time: every career-center course is for grades 9–12, and the center serves Kennett students only. State law's enrollment rule speaks of students who have finished a year of high school. No written route exists for a Madison homeschooler.
  - "Forestry/natural resources": the career center has no such program. Automotive, construction trades and welding (within Advanced Manufacturing) exist.
  - "Lakes Region Community College" Running Start: Kennett's Running Start courses are all with White Mountains Community College (WMCC). Lakes Region credit comes only through articulation in two visual media courses and as a dual-enrollment option in advanced automotive.
  - "The White Mountain School": it closed after the 2023-24 school year.
  - "Work-study credits": Kennett has no work-study program. It has Extended Learning Opportunities (ELOs) and paid co-ops for career-center students.
  - "College matriculation rates" for the comparison: the site has graduates' plans, not college enrollment. Actual enrollment figures exist only for Kennett.
- **Proposed new shared topics:** `cte-admission-and-cost-rules` (rule, rules) and `research-workforce-readiness` (research, research). They are described in their sections.
- **Main gaps:**
  - No written SAU 13 or SAU 9 procedure for moving an IEP or 504 plan from Madison Elementary to KMS.
  - No posted quarterly report on Madison students' achievement, although the contracts call for one.
  - No public results for Madison students at KMS or Kennett.
  - No count of Madison students in career-center courses.
  - No AP course counts for the comparison schools.
  - No local employer survey of graduates' skills.
- **Questions answered wholly by earlier files:** none. P8.1 and P17.1 were mostly answered already; this file adds the forestry check and the work-study gap.

## special-ed-moving-with-iep: Moving to a New District or State With an IEP

## cte-admission-and-cost-rules: Who May Enroll at a Career and Technical Education Center

- kind: rule
- question_type: rules
- description: State rules on who may enroll in a regional career and technical education center (including home-educated, charter and private school students), space, prerequisites and age, and who pays tuition and transportation

1. For career and technical education, New Hampshire law says that if a student attends a chartered public school or a private school, or is home schooled, the "sending district" is the school district where the student lives.
   - citation: RSA 188-E:2, VIII(b)
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XV/188-E/188-E-2.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-13
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Statute text: "VIII. 'Sending district' means: (a) A school district where students reside who attend a regional center, regional program, or alternative education program other than within the district itself; or (b) If a student attends a chartered public school, private school, or is home schooled, the sending district shall be the school district in which the student resides." Under RSA 188-E:6, II a sending district pays 25 percent of the career and technical portion of the receiving district's cost per pupil for state-approved programs. Raw copy: raw/kennett/rsa-188-E-mrg.txt
2. New Hampshire law requires regional career and technical education centers to enroll, on a space-available basis, any student "who has attended one year of high school," once course prerequisites are completed or waived, and leaves the receiving district to decide qualifications and space.
   - citation: RSA 188-E:5, V–VI
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XV/188-E/188-E-5.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-13
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Statute text: "V. Regional career and technical education centers shall, on a space available basis, enroll any student requesting enrollment who has attended one year of high school regardless of the number of academic credits earned ... provided that in either case: (a) The student has successfully completed any courses required as a prerequisite ...; or (b) The prerequisites have been waived by the regional career and technical education center director. Such a waiver shall not be unreasonably withheld. VI. The receiving district shall be responsible for determining the student's qualifications for admission and space availability." No section sets a separate age or grade for home-educated students. The Mount Washington Valley center serves Kennett students only (sau9 file cte-center-programs 3). The words left out at the first ellipsis are an exception for Manchester: its district must enroll and pay for its own resident students in grades 9–12 at a center within the district (Manchester/mst-and-pathways.md, cte-admission-and-cost-rules 1).
