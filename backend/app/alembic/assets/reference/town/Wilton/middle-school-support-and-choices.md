# WLC Middle School, Student Support and School Choices in Wilton

Part of the Wilton research (town/Wilton). Gathered November 22, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers the Wilton-Lyndeborough Cooperative (WLC) Middle School (grades 6–8, in the shared middle/high school building at 57 School Road, Wilton) and the support and choices around it: the middle school day, WIN periods, the homework club, Title I tutoring and the remediation process; the move from 8th to 9th grade and coming in from High Mowing School; access to WLC classes and teams for home-educated, private school and EFA students under the district's November 2026 policy JJJ; home education notice; VLACS and the Milford Applied Technology Center; bullying and discipline; counseling, the contracted psychologist and mental health clinician, nurses and suicide prevention; report cards and PowerSchool; Section 504 practice; the phone policy and how it has been enforced; the 2026-27 bus routes and ride times; High Mowing's tuition, aid and scholarships; immunization rules and the only vaccination rate data found (statewide).

- **Main findings:**
  - **Private school, home-educated and EFA residents can take WLC classes and join teams and clubs.** Policy JJJ, readopted November 3, 2026, adds EFA students and sets the steps: a written request to the principal, the same eligibility rules except attendance, the same tryouts and fees, and an appeal path. High school teams also follow the NHIAA by-law for non-public students (cited from the Nashua file). One open question: the handbooks require four courses for athletic eligibility, and no document says how that applies to a non-enrolled student.
  - **No annual homeschool notification exists.** Since July 1, 2026 New Hampshire requires no declaration except to use public school programs or after withdrawing. SAU 63's policy IHBG (revised 2020) still describes the old five-business-day notice, and the district posts no home education form.
  - **The 8th-to-9th move:** 8th graders already follow the high school's block schedule on Wednesdays and Thursdays; course selection and an 8th grade transition night are in February; placement is by teacher recommendation; and the class of 2030 (this year's 9th graders) has its own Program of Studies under the state's new minimum standards.
  - **Phones:** a bell-to-bell policy (adopted September 9, 2025) plus a 4-step handbook ladder. Phones have been the top high school infraction in board reports through 2026; the assistant principal said a "3-strike" system was added in fall 2025 because "every class gave the students a clean slate" before.
  - **Bus rides:** on the four 2026-27 routes, the first morning pickups are 6:25–6:44 and the longest scheduled rides are 46–64 minutes in the morning and 56–73 minutes in the afternoon (derived), with WLC and FRES students on the same buses.
  - **Support staff:** two WLC counselors (one for grades 6–8, about 142 students each way, derived), a contracted district school psychologist (1.0) and a licensed mental health clinician (0.8, new since 2019), a nurse at WLC and FRES, and a part-time LCS nurse hired October 6, 2026 after a contracted nurse filled in.
