# High School Choice and the CRTC for Deerfield Students

Part of the Deerfield research (town/Deerfield). Gathered November 21, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers how Deerfield's choice of Concord High School, Coe-Brown Northwood Academy and Pembroke Academy works from Deerfield's side: what the three agreements say about who may enroll, seats, switching, special education, 504 plans, career programs and buses; the two Deerfield high school buses and the lack of a bus to Pembroke Academy or any late bus; how many Deerfield students are at Concord High and how they do there (grades, AP, SAT, CRTC programs and plans after graduation, 2015–2024); counselors, financial aid and career advising at each school; sports divisions; agriculture and outdoor programs; and the federal counts of students by race and disability at the three schools.

- **8 fact topics, 65 facts and 6 number tables.** Raw copies are in `raw/high/` (about 36 MB): the Coe-Brown and Concord agreements with Deerfield (OCR text from scans) and the Pembroke agreement's OCR text (from the Pembroke research); the new Concord agreement for 2027-28 and Concord's November 2026 enrollment report (Concord board packet, November 2, 2026); SAU 53's Deerfield page and High School Assignment Request form; 18 sets of Deerfield School Board minutes, October 2023 to November 2026, and two board packet items (`minutes/`, four scanned sets read by OCR); Concord High's 2023-24 report to the Deerfield board; Concord High's 2026-27 Program of Studies and career resources page (`concord/`); eight CRTC pages (`crtc/`); Coe-Brown's pages, handbook, catalog, profile and club list as saved by the Barrington research (`coebrown/`; coebrown.org refused this agent's requests); the NHIAA's 2026-27 divisional alignments as saved by the Northfield research (`nhiaa/`); derived CSVs of drive times and federal civil rights counts; and the Nominatim and OSRM responses (`geo/`).
- **Places:** 51 facts use `district Deerfield` (Deerfield's agreements, buses, students and comparisons of its three schools), 8 `district Coe-Brown Northwood Academy`, 5 `district Concord` and 1 `district Pembroke`. No new statewide rules were needed.
- **New data concepts:** the three agreements' rules side by side (admission, denial reasons, IEP decisions, 504 duties, vocational access, transport); Deerfield's high school buses, their 51-rider limit, rider priority and wait list; Deerfield students at Concord High by month (2024 and 2026); Deerfield graduates' plans from Concord High, 2015–2024; Deerfield students in CRTC programs by year; the 2026-27 NHIAA division of each school by sport; drive times from Deerfield Community School to each school; each school's 2023-24 students by race and with IEPs or 504 plans.

**Premises checked**

| Question | Premise | What the sources show |
|---|---|---|
| P3.1 | Deerfield buses students to all three schools | **Partly wrong.** For 2026-27 Deerfield runs two high school buses, one to Concord High and one to Coe-Brown, and no bus to Pembroke Academy. All three agreements make daily transport Deerfield's job, not the school's, and the January 2026 cut list included removing all regular high school transportation. |
| P3.1 (brief) | The town pays the same for each school | **No.** Each agreement has its own tuition formula (Concord: a base of $18,557.59 or a cost-based rate if higher; Coe-Brown: no more than its lowest rate to other sending towns; Pembroke: no more than the AREA towns' rate), special education is billed differently (Concord adds 3.5%), and only Concord and Coe-Brown get a Deerfield bus. Dollar amounts are in the money file (agent 4). |
| P3.2 | A cap on how many Deerfield students Coe-Brown takes | **No numeric cap** in the 2024–2027 agreement: any Deerfield resident "in good standing" may enroll, though Coe-Brown may deny enrollment for the child's best interest, safety, late or mid-year registration, or other agreed reasons. The "cap" in board minutes is the Coe-Brown bus's 51-rider limit (57 riders expected for 2026-27, "six over cap"), and the bus had a wait list in September 2026. |
| P5.3 | IEPs may be handled differently at the academies | **Partly true.** All three agreements use the same core terms, but at Coe-Brown and Pembroke Academy Deerfield "shall have final say" on program and placement, while at Concord High the Concord team makes the "ultimate decision" on whether it can meet a student's needs. Every agreement requires an agreed IEP at registration. |
| P6.2 | SNHU courses at Coe-Brown cost less than full college tuition | **Correct.** $100 per 3-credit course, which Coe-Brown calls "a savings of more than 90 percent"; Early College courses are $150. AP: Concord High's catalog lists 17 courses (not all run each year), Coe-Brown's 13, Pembroke Academy's 7. |
| P11.1 | A late bus from Concord High or Coe-Brown, or a 30-minute drive | **No late bus found** from any of the three, and Concord High itself has none at present. Free-flow drive estimates from Deerfield Community School: 19 minutes to Coe-Brown, 35 to Pembroke Academy and 41 to Concord High (derived). |
| P11.2 | Concord plays in Division I and Coe-Brown in Division II | **Correct** for 2026-27 in nearly every sport; Pembroke Academy is also Division II. Coe-Brown has no football or field hockey team. |
| P15.3 | Switching schools after 9th grade is hard | **Possible but not automatic.** Families go through the superintendent with SAU 53's assignment request form; a switch is "typically" approved before the second trimester cutoff. Coe-Brown may refuse "late or mid-year" registration; Concord bills a full year for a student who leaves during the second semester. |
| P19.2 | FFA or outdoor programs at the three schools | **Only Coe-Brown has FFA:** an FFA chapter and 10 agriculture courses. Pembroke Academy has a greenhouse and outdoor ecology, botany and wildlife courses; Concord High has an agriculture and natural resources pathway, a Grow Dome ELO and an EnviroCorps club. The CRTC has no agriculture program. |
