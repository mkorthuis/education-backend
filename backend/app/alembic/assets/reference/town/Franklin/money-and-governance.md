# Franklin: Money, Taxes, Governance, Buildings and Staffing

Part of the Franklin research (town/Franklin). Gathered November 21, 2026.

## Summary

**What's covered:** 11 topics, 83 facts and 4 number tables on how Franklin's school budget is set inside the city budget under the city charter's tax cap, the 2026-27 (FY2027) budget fight from the School Board's $21.55 million request to the $19.35 million the City Council adopted on August 14, 2026, the council votes that failed to raise school money above the cap, the fall 2026 adequacy aid shortfall and Resolution 11-27, what drives school costs (special education, contracts, contracted services, the loss of one-time and state money), positions cut and layoffs, teacher retention and the 2026-27 teachers' contract, the school buildings and the city's capital plan, the federal grants that were cancelled or paused and what replaced them, the Joint Finance Committee and talk of sharing services (the Lakes Region special education consortium, the "Pathfinder" committee), the School Board's meetings and public comment rules, Franklin's open enrollment plan and nearby charter schools, and how Question 2 (the state school tax cap) applies to a city whose school budget is part of the city budget.
- **New data concepts:** the charter tax cap's text and FY2027 arithmetic; the city/school property tax split (35% school); the city's own "tax impact" method (assessed valuation $1,146,617,226) and **derived** costs on a $260,000 home; the School Board's budget versions and position counts; the share of the budget that is special education; school roof replacement dates from the capital plan; teacher retention rates the superintendent reported; federal grant terminations and replacements; the open enrollment plan's terms.
- **Places:** `district Franklin` for the school district, its budget, board and staff; `town Franklin` for the city charter, the council and the tax cap; one general fact (no place) under the library's `budget-hb-1300-school-tax-cap`.

**Main sources:** the City of Franklin's FY2027 Adopted Budget (August 14, 2026), which prints the charter's tax cap section, the tax cap calculation, the debt schedule, the School District's FY2025 audited statements and its revenue projection; the School District's 2026-27 budget presentation (June 8, 2026) and its Mandatory Report to Voters; City Council, Joint Finance Committee and City Finance Committee minutes from June 2025 to November 2026 (the city's CivicClerk portal); the School Board's November 16, 2026 packet (October 19 minutes, superintendent's update, open enrollment plan, RSA 32:5-i guidance, wellness report); the city's draft FY25–FY40 Capital Improvements Plan; the New Hampshire congressional delegation's September 12, 2025 letter to the Department of Homeland Security; and the site's database (enrollment, proficiency, salaries, adequacy aid, Question 2).

## district-tax-and-state-aid: School Taxes and State Aid

- kind: local
- question_type: governance_money
- description: What drives a town's school taxes, beyond the site's tax rate and adequacy aid data: how a district's budget, its state aid and the tax rate fit together locally, its own projected rates, revaluations and their reasons, and village precinct rates

1. Franklin's city charter (§ C-32 A) limits the combined city, school and county budget: the council may assume a tax rate no higher than the prior year's rate increased by the change in the national cost of living index for the prior calendar year, and total spending may not exceed what that rate plus other revenue would raise; by a two-thirds vote the council may exceed the limit, but only by the amount of lost non-property-tax revenue, when revenue from one source falls more than 2% (or several sources more than 4%) of the prior year's revenue, and the limit "shall not limit the Council from appropriately funding any programs or accounts mandated to be paid from municipal funds by state or federal law."
   - citation: Franklin City Charter § C-32 A (added 1989), as printed in the FY2027 Adopted Budget, Section 3, page 121
   - source: https://www.franklinnh.gov/DocumentCenter/View/3116/FY27-Adopted-Budget
   - as_of: 2026-11-21
   - review_by: 2027-11-21
   - place: town Franklin
   - note: Text: "In establishing a combined municipal budget, the City Council shall be allowed to assume an estimated property tax rate only in an amount equal to the tax rate established during the prior fiscal year increased by a factor equal to the change in the National Cost of Living Index ... In the event of the loss of other revenues other than from property taxes from any single source in excess of 2% of the prior year's revenues or a combination of sources in excess of 4% of the prior year's revenues, the Franklin City Council may, by a two-thirds vote, exceed the above limitation but not in excess of the amount of the lost revenue. This provision shall not limit the Council from appropriately funding any programs or accounts mandated to be paid from municipal funds by state or federal law."
