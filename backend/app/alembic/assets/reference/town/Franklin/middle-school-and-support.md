# Franklin Middle School and Student Support

Part of the Franklin research (town/Franklin). Gathered November 21, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers Franklin Middle School (FMS, grades 5–8, 200 Sanborn Street) and student support in the Franklin School District (SAU 18): its staff and class sizes, special education staffing for students with IEPs, counselors and mental health help, the bullying and conduct policies, attendance and truancy, who to contact and the parent portal, homeschooled and EFA students in district sports, band and classes, coming back for 9th grade, enrolling a child as a relative, and food help for families.

- **12 topics, 76 facts, 3 tables.** Nine topics are shared district slugs with `place: district Franklin`; one fact in `district-student-support` (Riverbend's Franklin office) has `place: town Franklin`. Two are shared town slugs with `place: town Franklin` (`town-family-help`, `town-school-options-nearby`). One adds state law (no place) to the shared `school-residency-rules` topic: RSA 193:12, V and V-a, which let a child placed with a relative by DHHS or a court, or placed voluntarily at DHHS's request, attend school where the relative lives.
- **Main sources:** the FMS 2026-27 Student & Family Handbook and the FMS counselors page (read in the main session's browser pass, since sau18.nh.gov blocks automated requests); the district's 2026-27 budget presentation of June 8, 2026 (staffing by school, the two competing 2026-27 budgets, enrollment and students per teacher by grade, special education case managers, therapists and behavior support); Franklin School Board policies JH, JICK, JIC, JLDBA, JLD, JLCK, JLDBB, JG, JFAA, JFABD, JRA, JJJ, JJJ-R, JJA-R and JJIB, read from the district's file server; the district's registration and food services pages (browser pass); the federal Common Core of Data record for FMS; RSA 193:12; Riverbend Community Mental Health's pages; Twin Rivers Food Pantry's pages; the City of Franklin's welfare and food pantry pages; Archways' Franklin pages; a 2020 NH Charitable Foundation story; and two Concord Monitor articles (2025 and 2026). Staff names are left out of the facts.
- **Main findings:**
  - **Staff at FMS (2025-26):** 12 classroom teachers, 5 special education teachers, 13.65 special education paraprofessionals, 1 school counselor, 1 grant-funded social worker, 1 grant-funded climate specialist, an in-school suspension paraprofessional and a nurse, among 48.73 positions. Speech, occupational and physical therapy are part-time at FMS (0.75, 0.75 and 0.10 of a therapist, each with an assistant). A behavior analyst consults as needed, and a full-time behavior specialist works in a program the district calls the Odell Program.
  - **2026-27:** the School Board budget kept FMS's 12 teachers, counselor, social worker and music; the tax-cap budget cut FMS to 39.65 positions. The City Council adopted a school budget close to the tax-cap figure on August 14, 2026 (agent 4's file). FMS's 2026-27 handbook matches that plan's 10 homeroom teachers (2 each in grades 5 and 7, 3 each in grades 6 and 8), with no social worker or assistant principal listed. It does list a band and chorus teacher, a counselor, a Student Assistance Program coordinator (at FMS Tuesdays and Thursdays), a culture and climate specialist and a school resource officer.
  - **Class sizes:** with 2 teachers in grades 5 and 7, the district's projections give 23.5 and 26.5 students per teacher in those grades, against 21 and 19.3 in grades 6 and 8.
  - **Behavior:** FMS's code of conduct is built on restorative practices and a tiered behavior support system (MTSS-B), with graduated consequences. Out-of-school suspension is possible for aggression, weapons or substantiated bullying.
  - **Parents:** Alma shows grades, daily homework, missing work, recent scores and attendance, and calls home on unverified absences. The attendance liaison sends letters at 5, 7 and 10 unexcused absences and visits after 7. The handbook lays out who to contact, from teacher to counselor, principal, superintendent and board.
  - **Retention:** it rests on mastering competencies, with a parent meeting; absences aren't listed as a reason. After 10 excused absences, further absences need documentation.
  - **Homeschool and EFA access:** policy JJJ, adopted September 21, 2026, gives resident home-educated, EFA, charter and private school students the same access as enrolled students to classes, sports and activities. Its regulation names band and athletics, and a physical may be required.
  - **Food:** Twin Rivers Food Pantry's "Feed the Need!" program gives weekend food to Franklin and Andover school children facing food insecurity. Lunch is $3.25, the meal application is yearly, and there are free summer meals for anyone 18 or younger.
- **Main gaps:** the FMS special education and MTSS-B pages, the staff directory, board minutes, summer school notices and the Section I and K policies themselves (IK, IHBG, KE) were not read. No FMS summer school or math intervention or screening details were found. Athletics funding for 2026-27 was still unsettled in October 2026 (agent 4's file).

Neither the FMS handbook nor any page read mentions a summer school for other students. |
| P17.1 | Not being on the birth certificate may block enrolling a nephew | **Not by itself.** Policy JFAA asks for a copy of the child's birth certificate, proof of a physical and immunizations, and proof of residency. School residency follows the parent, a court-appointed guardian, or a court or DHHS placement, and a relative with a guardianship petition pending may enroll the child. The FMS handbook adds that a child "living without a parent or legal guardian" or living with others after losing housing may qualify to enroll at once, without the usual documents, under the McKinney-Vento Act. |
| P17.2 | The school may "test him quietly" and put him in a smaller group | **Partly supported.** Policy JG allows assessment testing for placement. FMS had a grant-funded Title I teacher in 2025-26, listed in the 2026-27 handbook as "Title I ELA Support" (reading, not math). A special education evaluation is a separate path, and the handbook sends testing questions to the special education office. No FMS math intervention or small-group description was found. |
| P17.3 | The school may have a food pantry or weekend backpack program | **Weekend food yes, through the local pantry.** Twin Rivers Food Pantry's "Feed the Need!" sends weekend food to Franklin school children experiencing food insecurity. Neither the FMS handbook nor the district's food services page mentions a school-run pantry. Families apply each year for free or reduced-price meals, and free summer meals serve anyone 18 or younger. |

## school-residency-rules: Children Placed With Relatives

- kind: rule
- question_type: rules
- description: Which district is legally responsible for a student under New Hampshire law: domicile, guardians, divided homes and parenting plans, moves between districts, attending a district where a student doesn't live, and who decides disputes (added from the November 13, 2026 towns)

1. Under New Hampshire's residency law, a child whom the Department of Health and Human Services has placed in a relative's home, or whom a court has placed in the home of a relative or friend under the child protection, delinquency or guardianship laws, may attend the public schools of the district where that home is.
   - citation: RSA 193:12, V
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XV/193/193-12.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-21
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text: "nothing in this section shall limit or abridge the right ... of any child placed in the home of a relative of that child by the department of health and human services, or placed in the home of a relative or friend by a court pursuant to RSA 169-B, RSA 169-C, RSA 169-D, RSA 170-C, or RSA 463, to attend the public schools of the school district in which the home ... is located, as provided in RSA 193:28." The rule for a relative whose guardianship petition is still before the court is in library special-ed-who-is-parent 6. Raw: raw/middle/rsa-193-12.txt
2. Under New Hampshire's residency law, when a parent or guardian voluntarily places a child with a relative at the recommendation or request of the Department of Health and Human Services, the child may attend school in the relative's district; the district may ask DHHS to confirm the placement was for the child's well-being and not for school choice, and may ask the relative to take reasonable steps toward guardianship, with the child attending school meanwhile.
   - citation: RSA 193:12, V-a
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XV/193/193-12.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-21
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text: "Whenever a parent or guardian voluntarily places a child with a relative at the recommendation or request of the department of health and human services, that child shall be permitted to attend the public schools of the school district in which that relative resides provided that: (a) Upon request of the school district, the department of health and human services shall confirm that the department recommended or requested that the child be placed with the relative to promote the child's well being, and not for the purpose of allowing the child to attend school in the district where the relative resides; and (b) Upon request of the school district, the relative shall take reasonable steps to secure a court award of guardianship over the child, the child being allowed to attend school in that district while the relative seeks guardianship."
