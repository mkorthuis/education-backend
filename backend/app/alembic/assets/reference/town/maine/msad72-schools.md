# MSAD 72 Schools (Grades K–8) for Chatham Students

Part of the Chatham research (town/Chatham), shared in town/maine/ because MSAD 72 matters to other SAU 9 towns too. Gathered November 13, 2026.

## Summary

What's covered: which MSAD 72 schools and grades Chatham children attend, and how many; registration and enrollment papers; public pre-K; counseling, mental health and substance prevention; special education and related services; curriculum, Maine state tests and MSAD 72's results; gifted and talented; outdoor and place-based learning; the Brownfield-Denmark Elementary (BDES) consolidation study; the MSAD 72 board; Maine rules that differ from New Hampshire's; and derived comparisons with Jackson and Conway schools.

- **11 fact topics, 100 facts and 8 number tables.** Raw copies are in `raw/msad72/` (about 29 MB, mostly MSAD 72 web pages, Google Docs and Drive files, Maine DOE pages and files, NCES and GreatSchools pages, and Chatham School District annual reports).
- **Main findings:**
  - MSAD 72 runs three schools: the Molly Ockett School in Fryeburg (pre-K to grade 8), New Suncook School in Lovell (K–4) and Brownfield-Denmark Elementary in Denmark (K–4). The C.A. Snow School in Fryeburg closed in 2017, and Molly Ockett became a K–8 school.
  - In MSAD 72's October 1, 2026 count, 22 Chatham children were in grades K–8: 10 at New Suncook (9 of the 10 Chatham K–4 children) and 12 at Molly Ockett (all 11 in grades 5–8 and one fourth grader). Three more were in the Head Start pre-K class at Molly Ockett.
  - An MSAD 72 board committee is studying the future of BDES and is due to recommend by December 2026. Options on the table include sending all district K–2 children to New Suncook in Lovell and grades 3–8 to Molly Ockett. Under Maine law, closing an elementary school in an SAD requires a local referendum.
  - MSAD 72 employs its own speech-language pathologists (3), occupational therapists (2), a physical therapist, psychologists and a BCBA (2026-27). Chatham's own budget also pays for speech, OT/PT and psychological testing lines; the report doesn't say who delivers them.
  - Maine and New Hampshire both base their science standards on the NGSS (NH 2016, Maine 2019), and both test science in grades 5, 8 and the third year of high school. Their reading and math tests differ, so percent-proficient figures can't be compared directly.
- **Main gaps:** Maine DOE's school-level test results for 2024 and 2025 (the ESSA dashboard is interactive and refused export); MSAD 72's Chapter 104 gifted-and-talented plan and approval; pre-K eligibility and registration rules for non-resident (Chatham) children; any written MSAD 72 procedure specific to Chatham families; and any data on how Chatham children feel at school.
- **New shared topic proposed:** `maine-school-rules` (kind rule, question type rules, no place): "Maine laws, rules and state programs that apply to Maine schools attended by New Hampshire students (state tests, standards, gifted programs, school closures, pre-K), and how they compare with New Hampshire's." Other agents writing Maine rules (truancy, diplomas) may want to use the same slug.
