Part of the Hudson research (town/Hudson). Gathered 2026-11-16.

## Summary

16 topics, 100 facts and 1 number table. Facts are from Hudson School District (SAU 81) policies, 2026-27 handbooks, school and district pages, school board minutes (July 2025 to September 2026), Champions' and Bishop Guertin's own pages, the Rodgers Memorial Library, and two state statutes. ## school-residency-rules: Temporary Agents for a Minor in New Hampshire

- kind: rule
- question_type: rules
- description: Which district is legally responsible for a student under New Hampshire law: domicile, guardians, divided homes and parenting plans, moves between districts, attending a district where a student doesn't live, and who decides disputes (added from the November 13, 2026 towns)

1. Since January 1, 2025, New Hampshire law has let a parent or guardian appoint a temporary agent, such as a grandparent, for up to 60 days and hand that person any of the parent's powers over the child's care and custody except consent to marriage or adoption; the appointment must be in writing, signed by the parent, witnessed by two adults who are not the agent, and accepted in writing by the agent, and it can be revoked or changed.
   - citation: RSA 461-A:19-a (2024, 309:1, eff. Jan. 1, 2025)
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XLIII/461-A/461-A-19-a.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-16
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Statewide rule, so no place. Text: "a parent or parents of a minor or a guardian or guardians of a minor ... may appoint a temporary agent for a period not exceeding 60 days and may delegate to such agent any power that the parent or guardian has regarding the care, custody, or property of the minor child ... except the power to consent to marriage or the adoption of a minor. II. Any delegation under this section shall be by a writing signed by, or at the direction of, the parent ... attested by at least 2 witnesses 18 years of age or older, neither of whom is the temporary agent, together with the written acceptance of the temporary agent." Limits in paragraph III: where there is no parenting plan, a parent may not appoint an agent if the other parent is known, willing and able to care for the child, unless that parent agrees in writing; no appointment after parental rights are terminated or when a court has placed the child with someone else.
