# Alvirne High School and the Palmer CTE Center

Part of the Hudson research (town/Hudson). Gathered November 16, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers Alvirne High School and the Wilbur H. Palmer Career and Technical Education Center at Alvirne: the 2026-27 Program of Studies (three diploma tracks, competency-based grading and the GPA scale, AP, Early College at Your High School, SNHU dual enrollment, VLACS, extended learning opportunities and internships, the minimum class-size rule), the A/B ("Maroon/Gold") block schedule, the Palmer Center's programs, credentials, college credit, renovation, enrollment, sending districts and advisory committees, employer partners and gifts, the Alvirne Farm, Alvirne's enrollment since 1986 (NCES), its NHIAA divisions, clubs and co-curricular stipends, how athletics are organized, working papers, and AP results for 2022–2026.

### Premises checked

| P3.1 | "Now that the Palmer CTE Center renovations are complete" | **Correct.** The district's history says voters approved Palmer improvements in 2018 with $17 million from the state, and the center "completes a $25 million renovation" in 2021. Follow-on work continued: in January 2023 the board approved a $912,914 kitchen ventilation project, 73% state-funded (cte-center-programs 1–2). |
| P3.2 | Alvirne uses block scheduling | **Correct.** Since 2022-23 Alvirne has run an A/B ("Maroon/Gold") block schedule: four periods of about 80 minutes a day plus a 35-minute flex period. It replaced a "modified block" with two block days a week (school-programs-after-grade-6 1–2). |
| P5.1 | "dual-enrollment Running Start courses" | **Renamed.** The community colleges' program is now "Early College at Your High School (Formerly Running Start)." Alvirne offers 13 of these course pairings, all in CTE courses, plus SNHU dual enrollment in French and Spanish (school-programs-after-grade-6 15–16). |
| P5.2 | Alvirne has a "competency-based grading system" | **Correct, with a GPA alongside it.** To earn credit, a student must meet or exceed expectations in every course competency. Courses also get letter grades on a 4.3 scale (A+), with honors and AP weighting. Transcripts show only final grades and the career GPA (school-programs-after-grade-6 9–11). |
| P5.3 | "so many electives or upper-level STEM classes get canceled … due to low enrollment" | **Partly checkable.** Since 2024, a written rule has governed small classes. A course with fewer than 5 students isn't offered unless special circumstances exist (an AP class, for example), and courses with 5–9 need School Board review. AP Biology, Chemistry, Physics and Environmental Science each run every other year. No count or list of canceled courses was found (school-programs-after-grade-6 13, 17–19). |
| P12.1 | Alvirne had "over 1,500 students" in the late 1990s and is "under 1,000 today" | **Correct.** NCES counts 1,550 students in 1997-98 and 1,566 in 1998-99. The site counts 976 in 2024-25, and NHIAA lists 955 as of October 1, 2025. Enrollment also passed 1,500 again in 2005-06 and 2006-07 (table hudson_alvirne_enrollment_history). |
| P3.3 | Employers offer "paid co-ops" | **Not found.** Alvirne's Career Focus Internship says internships "may be paid" but "students should expect an internship that is unpaid." No paid co-op program was found (district-volunteers-and-partners 6). |
| P11.1 | Recent graduates are "struggling with basic workplace math, financial literacy, and soft skills" | **Not measured by any source found.** The facts show what Alvirne requires and teaches (school-programs-after-grade-6 25–27). |
| P18.2 | Clubs are "underfunded compared to other districts" | **No comparison found.** Alvirne's 2025-26 co-curricular stipends total $76,750.03 (derived). An amendment adding $75,000 for extracurriculars failed at the 2025 deliberative session (school-programs-after-grade-6 31, 33). |

## youth-employment-rules: New Hampshire's Youth Employment Certificate ("Working Papers") and Parent Permission

- kind: rule
- question_type: rules
- description: Federal and state rules on the ages, hours and kinds of work teenagers may do, including in a parent's business and in work-based learning

1. Under New Hampshire law, a youth under 16 may not work without a youth employment certificate except for a parent, grandparent or guardian, in casual work or as farm labor, and a 16- or 17-year-old who hasn't graduated or earned an equivalency diploma may not be employed unless the employer keeps on file a signed document from a parent or guardian permitting the job.
   - citation: RSA 276-A:4, II and VIII
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XXIII/276-A/276-A-4.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-16
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Text: "II. No youth under 16 years of age shall be employed or permitted to work without a certificate except: (a) For his or her parents, grandparents, or guardian; (b) At work defined in this chapter as casual; or (c) As farm labor."; "VIII. No youth 16 or 17 years of age, except a youth 16 or 17 years of age who has graduated from high school or obtained a general equivalency diploma, shall be employed by an employer unless the employer obtains and maintains on file a signed written document from the youth's parent or legal guardian permitting the youth's employment." Paragraph IV limits under-16 hours (3 hours on school days, 23 hours in a school week). Hour limits at 16 and 17: Rochester `high-school-and-creteau.md` youth-employment-rules 1–2. Raw: raw/high/rules/rsa-276-A-4.txt

Under New Hampshire law, youth employment certificates are issued by school principals (or people they authorize) or by a parent or guardian, only after a determination that the student's academic performance is satisfactory; the issuer may revoke a certificate if performance slips, certificates must show proof of age and adequate health, and the employer must obtain the certificate within 3 business days of the first day of work.
   - citation: RSA 276-A:5, I–IV
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XXIII/276-A/276-A-5.htm
