# Special Education, Student Support and Support Staff in Hudson

Part of the Hudson research (town/Hudson). Gathered November 16, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers how the Hudson School District (SAU 81) serves students with disabilities and English learners, its counselors and mental health staff, school meals, and the pay, vacancies and training of its paraprofessionals, custodians and other support staff. It answers questions P2.1–P2.3, P6.2, P8.1–P8.3, P13.1, P15.1–P15.3, P16.1, P16.2 and P20.3.

- **6 topics, 94 facts and 7 number tables.** All six topics are shared district slugs with `place: district Hudson` (`district-special-education-services`, `district-classrooms-and-staff`, `district-student-support`, `district-family-communication`, `district-meals`, `school-programs-after-grade-6`). No statewide rule was written; the library's and earlier towns' rules are cited by slug and fact number.
- **New data concepts:** the district's own count of students with IEPs and out-of-district placements by year (2017-18 to 2026-27), IEPs by disability category, paraprofessional positions filled by year, reported para vacancies, staff and teacher turnover rates, support-staff wage schedules set against Manchester–Nashua area wages, and counselors and support staff listed at each school with derived students per counselor.
- **Main sources:** the district's Special Services, ESOL, Food Services, Human Resources, Title I, Section 504 and Suicide Prevention pages; its Special Education Procedures Plan (September 2026); the LAU (ESOL) Compliance Plan (2026); the PSRP (2026–28), Teamsters (2026–29) and teachers' (HFT, 2025–27) contracts; board policies IHBBA, JKAA, JLD, JLDBA, EFAA and GCI; the 2026 Strategic Plan; school board minutes from August 2024 to September 2026; the February 7, 2026 deliberative session minutes; and the October 2026 agenda packets (the Special Education Statistical Update of October 19, 2026 and the FY28 Executive Summary of October 8, 2026). Scanned contracts and charts were read with on-device text recognition and checked against the page images.
- **Main findings:**
  - Hudson counted 480 students with IEPs on October 1, 2026 (15.6% of students), down from 599 (18.7%) in 2019-20. Out-of-district placements fell from 26 in 2023-24 to 16 in 2026-27 (3.3% of students with IEPs, **derived**). The district puts typical out-of-district tuition at about $170,000 a year before transportation, and names special education tuition, transportation and related services among the main FY28 cost drivers.
  - A new middle school placement program at Hudson Memorial was put in the FY27 budget to serve students who would otherwise go out of district; in July 2026 the director said eight students were anticipated and documents were sent to the state.
  - Para staffing: 95 paraprofessional positions were filled at the start of 2026-27, against 108–111 in 2017–2019. Reported para vacancies fell from 37 (August 2024) to 31, 22 and 19 over the next year. The new PSRP contract raised the starting rate for part-time paras from $18.25 to $20.00 an hour (2026-27) and $21.50 (2027-28); full-time paras on the teachers' contract start at $17.30.
  - The Director of Special Services told the board in October 2024 that para services written into IEPs "cannot be removed from role to substitute."
  - Counselors: the district reports about 240 students per counselor (2024) and says it is under the 1:250 ratio the ASCA model recommends (2026). Each school lists one school psychologist except the Early Learning Center; no school lists a "social worker" title, but each has a student and family interventionist and the middle and high schools have mental health counselors.
  - English learners: the ESOL program uses push-in help in elementary classrooms and pull-out lessons, with a coordinator and three teachers listed; a district-wide ESOL teaching job was vacant in August 2026. The ESOL plan promises interpreters and translations of "all essential information" for families who ask, but no district statement covers translating report cards or IEP documents.
- **Main gaps:** any record of missed or canceled IEP service minutes; actual out-of-district tuition dollars for a year; the name of the de-escalation program staff are trained in; any quiet or sensory room at Alvirne; how fast a student in crisis sees a psychologist; Hudson bus drivers' pay (drivers work for First Student, the bus contractor); minutes for October–November 2026 meetings, not yet posted.
