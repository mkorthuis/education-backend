# Hudson: Money, Buildings and Governance

Part of the Hudson research (town/Hudson). Gathered November 16, 2026.

## Summary

**What's covered:** 8 topics (two proposed), 78 facts and 9 number tables on how the Hudson School District (SAU 81) builds, votes and pays for its budget, what it spends on administration and teaching, its contracts and teacher pay, the default budget, taxes and fund balance, the November 2026 tax cap vote, its buildings, capital reserves and debt, and where residents can watch and take part.
- **Budget and enrollment.** Total appropriations rose every year from $55.1 million (2019-20) to $74.2 million (2026-27) while district enrollment fell from 3,195 to 2,845; the superintendent's stated drivers (contracts, health insurance up 21.37% for 2026-27, special education tuition and transportation for 2027-28), the unbudgeted $1,470,579 SchoolCare assessment, and the district's own inflation-adjusted Report to Voters (cost per pupil up 23.9% after inflation over ten years, average teacher salary down 6.6%).
- **Administration against instruction.** The district's 2026-27 function budget (59.1% on instruction lines), a **derived** DOE-25 comparison with the state for 2024-25 (instruction 60.9% against 63.2%; all administration lines 10.9% against 10.8%), the 2027-28 SAU budget ($3.06 million, about 4% of the total, under Question 2's 6% cap), and the three top salaries.
- **Votes and the default budget.** Every operating budget and contract vote 2011–2026 (budgets failed in 2013, 2015 and 2022; the teachers' contract last failed in 2013), how the 2026 budget went from the board's $74.4 million to the Budget Committee's $72.2 million to $72.9 million at deliberative session, the 2026 default ($73.25 million, higher than the budget voters passed), the 2027-28 timeline and working warrant.
- **Teacher pay and hiring.** The 2025–2027 teachers' contract (salary schedules and cost items), its June 30, 2027 expiration with no raises in the 2027-28 budget, turnover rates, alternative-licensure hires, vacancies and time to hire, and a comparison with northern Massachusetts districts' average salaries.
- **Taxes and fund balance.** Which part of the tax rate drove each year's change (in 2025 the town rate rose 60 cents while the local school rate fell 13 cents), the district's estimated rates, revenue mix and state aid, the June 30, 2025 fund balance, eight years of budget surpluses and fund balance used to cut taxes, and the 2021-22 year-end projections against the audited result.
- **Tax caps.** Hudson's Question 2 vote (6,639 to 3,382, 66.25%), and the separate local options in state law (RSA 32:5-b, 32:5-e) for a town or district tax or budget cap tied to inflation and enrollment.
- **Buildings.** School capital reserve balances ($2.19 million on September 30, 2026), the Palmer CTE bond, six Alvirne renovation bond votes since 2018 (all but the CTE bond short of 60%), Alvirne's track, fields and gym work, the 2021 capital committee report, the 2023 preK–8 facilities study, and recent roof, window, lab and fire alarm articles.

**Main sources:** the district's Business Office pages (budget books and documents for FY2015–FY2028, audits FY2014–FY2025, annual reports, Budget Committee information, NESDEC report, facilities page), the FY28 Preliminary Budget Book (October 8, 2026) and change order (October 29, 2026), the 2025 Annual Report (with the FY2025 audit and March 2026 warrant), school election results 2011–2026 (district and town pages), School Board minutes and packets (October 20, 2025; September 8 and November 9, 2026), the Town of Hudson's assessing, Budget Committee, HCTV, Trustees of Trust Funds and election pages, RSA 32:5-b, 32:5-c, 32:5-e, 32:5-f and 198:4-b, the NH Constitution (Part II, Art. 83), Massachusetts DESE's teacher salary report, and the site's database (enrollment, teacher salaries, DOE-25, Question 2).

## local-tax-and-budget-caps: Local Tax Caps and School Budget Caps in New Hampshire Law

- kind: rule
- question_type: rules
- description: New Hampshire's local-option tax caps and school budget caps (RSA 32:5-b to 32:5-f): how a town or school district adopts one, the formulas allowed, overrides and repeal.

1. A New Hampshire town or school district that votes at an annual meeting, including an SB2 district, may adopt a local tax cap under RSA 32:5-b by a three-fifths vote on a ballot question placed by the governing body or by petition, after a public hearing 15 to 30 days before the vote; the cap then applies from the next fiscal year until changed or repealed, each also by three-fifths.
   - citation: RSA 32:5-c, I–III, V–VI
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/III/32/32-5-c.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-16
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Statute text: "The provisions of RSA 32:5-b may be adopted by any local political subdivision of the state whose legislative body raises and appropriates funds through an annual meeting. A 3/5 majority of those voting on the question shall be required to adopt"; "A public hearing shall be held by the local governing body on the question at least 15 days, but not more than 30 days, before the question is to be voted on"; "If a 3/5 majority ... vote 'yes,' RSA 32:5-b shall apply ... beginning with the following fiscal year and for all subsequent years until it is changed ... or rescinded". This local cap is separate from the state's 2026 school tax cap (RSA 32:5-i; library budget-hb-1300-school-tax-cap). Raw: raw/money/rsa-32-5-c.txt
2. Under New Hampshire's local tax cap (RSA 32:5-b), the cap may be a fixed dollar amount, a fixed percentage or a formula, and a school district may use last year's local tax base times one plus inflation (a federal price index or the Municipal Cost Index) times the change in its average daily membership in residence, so the allowed taxes move with inflation and enrollment.
   - citation: RSA 32:5-b, I-b and II
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/III/32/32-5-b.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-16
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Statute text: "a tax cap limiting the base amount to be raised by property taxes to last year's base shall be adjusted to account for inflation and change in population for towns or village districts, or attendance for school districts, according to the following formula: This year's base = (Last year's base) x (1 + CPI) x (This year's population or attendance / Last year's population or attendance)"; "'Attendance' shall mean the average daily membership in residence (ADMR)"; "The tax cap shall be either a fixed dollar amount, a fixed percentage, or a multiplication factor". Raw: raw/money/rsa-32-5-b.txt
3. Under New Hampshire's local tax cap (RSA 32:5-b), voters can exceed the cap only with a three-fifths "yes" vote on the appropriation; in an SB2 district, if the operating budget would exceed the cap and gets less than three-fifths, the adopted budget is cut to fit, and fund balance used to lower the prior year's taxes is added back when the cap is figured.
   - citation: RSA 32:5-b, I-a and III
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/III/32/32-5-b.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-16
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Statute text: "If a 3/5 majority ... of those voting on the question vote 'yes,' the appropriation is approved"; "(a) When using the official ballot form of meeting under RSA 40:13, if the warrant article for the operating budget results in appropriations exceeding the tax cap under this section and receives less than 3/5 majority 'yes' vote, the adopted operating budget shall be reduced by appropriations already raised to remain compliant"; "I-a. If the local taxes raised for the prior year were reduced by any fund balance brought forward from previous years, the amount of such reduction shall be added back". Raw: raw/money/rsa-32-5-b.txt
4. A New Hampshire school district may instead adopt a school budget cap under RSA 32:5-e, which limits the total raised and appropriated (operating budget and warrant articles with a tax impact) to a set cost per pupil times its October 1 average daily membership in residence, increased each year for inflation by a fixed percentage or a price index.
   - citation: RSA 32:5-e, I–II
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/III/32/32-5-e.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-16
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Statute text: "the total amount raised and appropriated for the fiscal year, including the operating budget and all other warrant articles with a tax impact ... shall not exceed the current per pupil cost, as defined herein, times the average daily membership in residence (ADMR) ... as of October 1 of the year immediately preceding the proposed budget year ... times (1+ IF), where IF is an amount for an annual increase for inflation"; "IF shall be either a fixed percentage, annual percentage change of an inflation index published by the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics ... or ... the Municipal Cost Index". Unlike the tax cap, this caps spending, not just the tax levy. Raw: raw/money/rsa-32-5-e.txt
5. A New Hampshire school budget cap under RSA 32:5-e is adopted, changed or repealed by a three-fifths ballot vote after a hearing held 15 to 30 days before, and voters can exceed it only by a three-fifths "yes" vote; in an SB2 district an operating budget over the cap that gets less than three-fifths is reduced to fit.
   - citation: RSA 32:5-e, III–IV; RSA 32:5-f, I–VI
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/III/32/32-5-f.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-16
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Statute text (32:5-f): "A 3/5 majority of those voting on the question shall be required to adopt the provisions of RSA 32:5-e"; "A public hearing shall be held by the school board on the question at least 15 days, but not more than 30 days, before"; "A 3/5 majority of those voting on the question shall be required to rescind". (32:5-e, IV): "if the warrant article for the operating budget results in appropriations exceeding the budget cap and receives less than 3/5 majority 'yes' vote, the adopted operating budget shall be reduced". A Salem 2025 article was a budget cap of this kind (town/Salem/money-buildings-and-governance.md, district-board-and-public-input fact 6). Raw: raw/money/rsa-32-5-e.txt; raw/money/rsa-32-5-f.txt
