Part of the Keene research (town/Keene). Gathered November 16, 2026.

- **Premises checked:**
  - "Benjamin Franklin Elementary" (P3.1): correct either way. The school's budget sheet and SAU 29's 2026-27 hours list it as "Benjamin Franklin"; its 2026-27 handbook uses both names; NH DOE (and the site) list it as Franklin Elementary School.
  - "The 2:40 p.m. pickup" (P3.1): correct. All four Keene elementary schools run 8:00 a.m. to 2:40 p.m. in 2026-27; on the six early release days they let out at 12:10 p.m.
  - "The SAU 29 preschool waitlist" (P1.3): the public preschool is the Keene School District's own Jonathan Daniels Preschool (Marlborough runs a separate one). It enrolls on a rolling basis once a child turns 3, "depending on availability"; no waitlist procedure or sign-up date is posted.
  - "Cheshire Center Preschool" (P1.3): it exists. It is a half-day laboratory preschool inside Keene High School, run by the Cheshire Career Center's Careers in Education program ($950 a year), and it isn't in the state's licensed child care list. The Children's Learning Center at Cheshire Medical Center, a different program, is for the children of Dartmouth Health employees only. (Corrected by the main session, November 16, 2026.)
  - "Full-day pre-K" (P16.3): Jonathan Daniels runs only half-day sessions (7:45–10:30 and 11:15–2:00). The YMCA runs a separate full-day preschool in the same building at $275 a week. No plan to add a full-day public program was found in the 2026-27 budget, board minutes or superintendent's reports. New Hampshire has no state-funded preschool program (library).
  - "One teacher for 400 kids" in elementary Spanish (P10.2): partly. In October 2026 a board committee raised concern that "one teacher is covering so many schools and students" and that "one teacher cannot do 400 report cards." The district had two elementary Spanish teachers in 2024-25 (loads of 520 and 478 students); in fall 2026 the Franklin and Symonds position was vacant, and the staff directory lists one elementary Spanish teacher, for Fuller and Wheelock (449 students, **derived**). The 2026-27 budget lists no cut to elementary Spanish; it cut 0.6 of a world language position at Keene Middle School.
  - "A walking-distance cutoff for buses" (P3.3): correct. Children in K–3 ride if they live more than half a mile from school, grades 4–5 more than a mile. Bus eligibility depends on distance, not income; meal benefits depend on income.
  - "Different early-release and snow-day logistics for elementary and middle school" (P16.2): partly. All Keene schools share one calendar, the same six early release dates and the same closing decisions. What differs is the clock: elementary schools run 8:00–2:40 and let out at 12:10 on early release days; Keene Middle runs 8:50–3:22 and lets out at 12:57; on a two-hour delay elementary school starts at 10:00 and middle school homeroom at 10:50; the preschool drops its afternoon session on early release days and its morning session on delay days.
  - "Lower ratings on GreatSchools" (P7.2): GreatSchools gives Symonds 8, Franklin 5, Fuller 5, Wheelock 4, Keene Middle 4 and Keene High 6 (of 10). Its pages say the ratings were calculated in 2025 from spring 2024 test and growth data, so they don't reflect the 2025 or 2026 state results, which rose at several schools. Whether ratings match what people "say in real life" is not something the data can show.
