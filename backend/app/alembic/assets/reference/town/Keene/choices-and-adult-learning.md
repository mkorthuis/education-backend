# Charters, Private Schools, Home Education and Adult Learning in Keene

Part of the Keene research (town/Keene). Gathered November 16, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers the choice side of the Keene residents' questions: special education, admission lotteries, sibling priority and buses at Gathering Waters Chartered Public School; how Making Community Connections (MC2) works and whether its students can play for Keene High or take Cheshire Career Center programs; Granite Valley Prep, a newer charter high school; Saint Joseph Regional School (SJRS) and Our Lady of Mercy Academy (OLMA), their tuition and Education Freedom Accounts (EFAs); moving from a Waldorf, Catholic or home school to Keene Middle or Keene High; what Keene's policies let home-educated, charter and private school students take or join; and Keene Community Education (KCE), the district's adult education division, with the library and River Valley Community College for computer, English and bookkeeping classes.

- **Places used:** `district Keene` (the district, its policies, KMS, KHS and Keene Community Education, which calls itself "the adult education division of the Keene School District"); `sau 29` (the SAU's child find notice); `district Gathering Waters Charter School`; `district Making Community Connections Charter School`; `district Granite Valley Preparatory Chartered Public School`; `town Keene` (SJRS, OLMA, EFA counts, River Valley's Keene center and the public library); no place for the NHIAA by-law.
- **Main findings:**
  - **A Keene child with an IEP at Gathering Waters stays Keene's responsibility.** Gathering Waters' 2026-27 admissions guidelines say the home district provides IEP services and families must set up a meeting of the sending district, the school and the parent. Keene's policy JJJ sends charter, private and home-educated students' requests for speech and other related services to the Director of Student Services.
  - **Gathering Waters gives siblings of current students third priority** in its lottery, after children of founding board members and of full-time staff. For 2026-27 the deadline was February 9, 2026 and the lottery February 16. Its posted priorities don't mention the preference state law gives Keene residents. Its classes are capped at 25, and site data shows 23 to 25 students in every grade from K to 8 in 2024-25.
  - **No source shows Keene buses for charter students.** State law requires the district to carry Keene residents to a charter school in Keene on its regular routes (library `charter-schools` fact 5). Keene's posted route documents cover only its own schools and the sending towns, and Gathering Waters' pages describe only car drop-off.
  - **MC2 (310 Marlboro Street) has no grades, GPA or class rank.** Students move through four competency-based "Phases" and need 24 credits. Internships take up 40% of the program, on Tuesdays and Thursdays. Its FAQ says a one- or two-year stay makes it hard to meet graduation requirements, and moving back "takes some translation."
  - **Keene's policies JJJ and JJJ-R (May 16, 2023) let resident charter, private and home-educated students take KHS classes and play sports** on the same terms as enrolled students, by written request to the principal. Career Center seats follow the Region 13 Agreement, and non-district students count against their sending high school's share. KHS athletics need prior NHIAA approval. JJJ doesn't yet name EFA students.
  - **Keene's home education policy IHBG (May 16, 2023) still describes the annual evaluation that state law repealed on July 1, 2026.** A child returning to a Keene school is placed after an evaluation by the principal, with an appeal.
  - **SJRS's financial aid page still gives a 350%-of-poverty income limit for EFAs.** State law dropped it on June 10, 2025. For 2026-27, SJRS charges $7,750 (K–8) and OLMA $9,500 (9–12). Both are on the EFA marketplace, and OLMA's profile says about 55% of its students get tuition help.
  - **Keene Community Education lists no medical billing or bookkeeping course.** Its career offerings are apprenticeship classes for electricians and plumbers ($1,600 a year), a Saturday gas technician course ($2,450), plumbers' license renewal seminars, support for nursing assistant students and paraprofessional training. River Valley Community College's Keene center lists an 18-credit Accounting Certificate (graduates' roles include bookkeeper) at $238 a credit; its 2026-27 catalog lists no medical billing or coding program.
  - **Free English classes, basic skills and HiSET preparation are free at Keene Community Education.** Its evening adult diploma classes run 5:30–8:30 p.m., and its fall 2026 form lists no fee for adults over 18. The public library gives free one-on-one computer help and free online Universal Class courses.
