# Maple Street, Hopkinton Middle School, Support and Choices

Part of the Hopkinton research (town/Hopkinton). Gathered November 22, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers the move from Harold Martin School to Maple Street School (MSS, grades 4–6), after-school transport at MSS, special education and 504 plans, instructional assistants (paraprofessionals), moving up with an IEP, EFAs and access for home-educated students, middle school sports, how grades 7–8 share Hopkinton Middle High School (HMHS) with grades 9–12, bullying and inclusion, the 6th-grade transition, phones, English learner (ESOL) services, translation, fee help, and high school math for 8th graders.

- **Places used:** `district Hopkinton` (the four schools, Student Services, the board and policies) and `town Hopkinton` (2020 Census counts, Parks & Recreation). No general rules were written; state and federal rules are cited from the library and earlier towns.
- **Main findings:**
  - **Late bus:** none. The 2026-27 route sheets list seven routes (#91–#97), each with one morning and one afternoon run. MSS band and chorus meet during the school day. For MSS after-school Title I tutoring (3:00–4:30 p.m.), families must provide the ride home.
  - **Grades 7–12 in one building:** the middle school has its own bell schedule and a lunch (12:30–12:55) that doesn't overlap the two high school lunches. Middle schoolers must use set corridors "to minimize disruption to high school classes," get their own lockers and Hawk Time, and have separate dance hours. Buses, some clubs and the building are shared.
  - **6th-grade transition:** a late-March visit to HMHS with an assembly and tour, one-on-one meetings with the middle school counselor, meetings for 6th graders with IEPs and 504 plans, world language mini-lessons, a May family night and a June CRTC Middle School Day.
  - **Grade 3 to 4:** MSS ran "transition activities for rising fourth" graders in spring 2026, including a transition day and an incoming 4th-grade information night (May 27, 2026).
  - **Special education:** 19.2% of students in October 2026 (21% a year earlier; state 20.1%). Grades 4–6 had 45 of 192 (23%). Student Services reported being "fully staffed," with a new out-of-district case manager and LEA role. On November 22, 2026 the job board listed a middle school special education teacher opening and no general instructional assistant openings.
  - **Homeschool and EFA access:** policy JJJ, revised November 5, 2026, covers home-educated, EFA, charter and private school residents. They have the same access as enrolled students, apply in writing to the principal, pay the same fees and meet the same tryout and physical-exam rules. Policy IHBG still describes home education rules the state repealed on July 1, 2026.
  - **ESOL:** the September 2025 Lau plan describes a pull-out program with one district ESOL teacher; the district gets no Title III money. It commits to interpreters and translated information "in a language they can understand," including report cards and permission slips. The MSS staff list names no ESOL teacher.
  - **Fee help:** the sports fee ($100 a season, $200 family cap) and field trip costs can be waived or helped through the school nurse. The PTA helps pay for field trips. Renting an MSS instrument costs a one-time $75 for the school year.
  - **Math:** the 8th grade has Algebra 1 and Pre-Algebra classes. Policy IMBD lets 7th and 8th graders earn high school credit if the high school principal approves before the student enrolls.
- **Main gaps:** how quickly special education referrals and 504 plans are handled in practice (no timeliness data found); whether Harold Martin and MSS hold IEP transition meetings; how often the ESOL teacher is at MSS; whether ParentSquare translation is turned on; whether 8th-grade Algebra 1 earns high school credit; counts of phone-rule violations; the town's current recreation scholarship page (blocked); any program aimed at the experience of Black or biracial students.
