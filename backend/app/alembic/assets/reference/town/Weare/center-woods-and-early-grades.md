# Center Woods, Weare Middle's Younger Grades and Young Families in Weare

Part of the Weare research (town/Weare). Gathered November 21, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers Weare's youngest students and their families: school hours at Center Woods (preschool to grade 3) and Weare Middle (grades 4–8), before- and after-school care, the 2026-27 bus routes and how long the morning rides are, unpaved roads and winter bus stops, storm delays and closings, class sizes and staffing, why 4th graders are at Weare Middle, reading help and intervention, the public preschool, full-day kindergarten and the entry cutoff, enrichment and advanced learners, homework, school meals and prices, the nurse and medication rules, and the school calendars.

- **Main sources:** the Weare School District 2026-27 Student and Parent Handbook (Center Woods and Weare Middle); SAU 24's 2026-27 route sheets (September update), transportation, cancellation, curriculum, assessment, Title I and dyslexia plan pages; the Weare School Board's public Google Drive folder (linked as "School Board Policies" from the Center Woods site), which holds the district's policies, board minutes through June 2026, the SAU 24 annual reports for 2018–2025 and the fall 2022 bus study; the 2026-27 district and John Stark calendars; the staff directories; New Morning Schools' and the Boys & Girls Clubs' own pages; NHDOT's paved and unpaved roads map service; the federal Civil Rights Data Collection; Duke University's summary of the 2006 homework research synthesis.
- **Main findings:**
  - Weare Middle is grades 4–8. For many years 4th and 5th graders attended a separate school, Center Woods Upper Elementary, in the middle school building; in 2022-23 the two were combined into one Weare Middle School. No source found says why grades 4 and 5 were first placed in that building.
  - Center Woods runs 8:30 a.m. to 3:05 p.m. (drop-off from 8:15); Weare Middle runs 7:20 a.m. to 2:10 p.m. The buses are tiered: the same buses run the middle school routes first, then Center Woods or John Stark. Morning Weare Middle pickups start at 6:33–6:47; Center Woods pickups start at 7:12–7:47. No district document found states why the schools start at different times.
  - On the six Center Woods morning routes, the scheduled time from the first to the last active stop runs from 24 to 52 minutes; the Blue route is shared with John Stark's Bus 3 and drops at John Stark first. The Red route runs along Route 77 (Concord Stage Road) and stops on Fox Hollow Drive, an unpaved town road. NHDOT lists 44.08 miles of Weare's town-maintained roads as unpaved and 68.75 as paved.
  - Two private programs offer care: New Morning at Center Woods (drop-off after 6:45 a.m., pick-up by 6 p.m., $11 a day before school, $22 a day after) and the Boys & Girls Club at Weare Middle (K–8, opens 6:30 a.m., closes 6 p.m., $182.90 a week with lower member rates).
  - Preschool is half-day (8:30–10:55 or 12:15–2:40), was created for children with special needs ages 3 to 5, and takes typically developing children for $200 a month (2026-27). It had 28 children on opening day 2025 and was cut from three sessions to two in 2023 because of special education demands. On a delayed opening, half-day pre-K is cancelled.
  - Kindergarten has been full-day since fall 2022 (voters approved it 940 to 714 in March 2022). Policy JEB sets entry at age five before September 30, with early entry considered for October–December birthdays after a district evaluation the parents pay for.
  - On opening day 2025, Center Woods had 86 kindergartners in 5 classes and 86 second graders in 5 classes (about 17 per class, derived). The November 2026 staff directory lists 5 kindergarten teachers and 4 grade 2 teachers; 2026-27 counts by grade were not found. Policy IIB says the district strives for no more than 20 students per class in K–2.
  - Neither Weare school reports a gifted and talented program (federal civil rights survey, 2021-22 and 2023-24), and no gifted policy is posted. Enrichment and acceleration are offered through the daily WIN ("What I Need") block, i-Ready's personalized lessons and Policy IHCD on advanced coursework.
  - Reading help runs through the RtI/MTSS team, Title I (targeted assistance, referral through the classroom teacher), interventionists in WIN time and, in grades K–3, the dyslexia screening plan. Weare Middle had no math or English intervention in 2023-24 because of staffing; it was restored once the school was fully staffed.
  - Meals are not free for everyone: 2026-27 lunch is $3.35 at Center Woods, and families must file a new free or reduced-price application each year (any time, ideally before October 15). A doctor's note alone isn't enough for medication: the school needs a written prescriber's order, a signed parent request with a "hold harmless" release, and the medicine in its pharmacy-labeled container, delivered by an adult.

## research-homework: What Research Says About Homework

- kind: research
- question_type: research
- description: What research syntheses find about homework and achievement at different grade levels, and the "10-minute rule" for how much homework to assign

1. A 2006 synthesis led by Harris Cooper of Duke University, which reviewed more than 60 U.S. studies of homework from 1987 to 2003, found that homework was generally linked to higher achievement, but that the link was much stronger for students in grades 7–12 than for elementary students.
   - citation: Cooper, H., Robinson, J. C., & Patall, E. A. (2006), "Does Homework Improve Academic Achievement? A Synthesis of Research, 1987–2003," Review of Educational Research 76(1), 1–62; Duke University news release, March 2006
   - source: https://today.duke.edu/2006/03/homework.html
   - as_of: 2026-11-21
   - review_by: 2027-11-21
   - note: Release: "Duke University researchers have reviewed more than 60 research studies on homework between 1987 and 2003 and concluded that homework does have a positive effect on student achievement." "the positive correlation was much stronger for secondary students --- those in grades 7 through 12 --- than those in elementary school." Abstract (Duke Scholars, https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/688435): "all studies, regardless of type, had design flaws." Raw: raw/elementary/duke-homework-2006.txt; raw/elementary/duke-scholars-cooper-2006.txt
2. Cooper said the 2006 findings are consistent with the "10-minute rule," a common practice of adding 10 minutes of nightly homework per grade (about 40 minutes in grade 4 and about two hours in grade 12, which works out to about 50 minutes in grade 5, derived), and that for upper high school students more than about two hours a night was not linked to higher achievement.
   - citation: Duke University news release, "Duke Study: Homework Helps Students Succeed in School, As Long as There Isn't Too Much," March 2006
   - source: https://today.duke.edu/2006/03/homework.html
   - as_of: 2026-11-21
   - review_by: 2027-11-21
   - note: Text: "Cooper said the research is consistent with the '10-minute rule' suggesting the optimum amount of homework that teachers ought to assign. The '10-minute rule,' Cooper said, is a commonly accepted practice in which teachers add 10 minutes of homework as students progress one grade. In other words, a fourth-grader would be assigned 40 minutes of homework a night, while a high school senior would be assigned about two hours. For upper high school students, after about two hours' worth, more homework was not associated with higher achievement." Grade 5 figure derived (5 × 10 minutes).
