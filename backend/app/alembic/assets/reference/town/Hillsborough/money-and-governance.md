# Money and Governance in Hillsborough: The Hillsboro-Deering Cooperative, Deering, Windsor and SAU 34

Part of the Hillsborough research (town/Hillsborough). Gathered November 22, 2026.

## Summary

**What's covered.** The Hillsboro-Deering Cooperative School District's 2026-27 budget ($23,792,673, down 3.72%; $24,782,688 with its SAU 34 share), what drives it (salaries and benefits 78.1%, a 12.8% health premium rise, special education at about $5.9 million net, staff cuts as enrollment fell from 1,050 to 969), the 2025 default budget, the two staff contracts and the teachers' contract that failed in March and passed in a July 2026 special election; how costs are split between Hillsborough and Deering (50% pupils, 50% property value since 1998, and since March 2026 all state adequacy aid applied before the split, which the district's own committee said shifts taxes toward Hillsborough), each town's share from 2020-21 to 2026-27 and a worked 2024-25 comparison of the two methods; Deering's 2025-26 withdrawal study and its 5-0 vote not to recommend leaving; the 2026 bylaws that stand in for articles of agreement the district never had; Windsor's place (not a member: it pays per-pupil tuition for about 13 students, at rates set from the district's cost per pupil, plus 1.36% of SAU costs) and Washington's (grades 6–12 tuition); every 2026 ballot result in a table; the March 2026 open enrollment vote (1% in, 0% out) and the board's cost estimate; SAU 34's makeup, budget, split and vote, its office rent, and its 2026 exploration of shared services with SAU 24; school board seats, meetings, YouTube streams, audio and packets, and the 2027 calendar; buildings: no bond debt since 2023, a $13.98 million capital list with $3.6 million of roofs, trust balances, the 2026 vote that took trust-spending power from the board, an energy lease and a $150,000 state grant; community use of the buildings (policy, fees, exemptions, waivers); the partnership policy and donations; GreatSchools' ratings and what they're built from; how the 2025 revaluations changed the tax rates, and how Hillsborough's school tax compares with its neighbors on market value.

**Counts.** 11 topics (all shared slugs), 67 facts, 3 number tables.

**Main sources.** SAU 34's site (sau34.org) and the Hillsboro-Deering board's public Google Drive folders: the FY27 budget book (scanned; read by OCR and checked against page images), deliberative session slides, budget brochure, capital plan, Mandatory Report to Voters, election results for March 2025, March 2026 and July 2026, the 2025 annual report, board minutes (September and November 2026), the November 30, 2026 packet (financial report and the 2026 bylaws), the facilities committee's minutes, policies KF, KF-R and KA/IJO and the 1954 cooperative records; the Deering Withdrawal Feasibility Study Committee's files; SAU 34's FY27 and FY28 budgets, board minutes and the SAU 24–SAU 34 joint committee minutes and memorandum; Windsor's FY27 budget book; RSA 195:14 and 195:14-a; GreatSchools; and the site's database for tax rates, valuations and test results.

**Main gaps.** Building ages: no source giving when the three schools were built was found. Hillsborough's town website (hillsboroughnh.org) and NH DRA refused automated requests, so the 2026 tax rate, the 2025 revaluation's details, exemptions and the town's own budget weren't read. The FY2028 district budget (given to the board November 20, 2026) wasn't posted yet. The district's "Funding Formula" and "Formation History" files need a Google sign-in. Niche refused automated requests. No backpack food, coat drive or mentoring program was found on the district's sites.
