# Winnacunnet and the Seacoast School of Technology From North Hampton

Part of the North Hampton research (town/North Hampton). Gathered November 21, 2026.

## Summary

This file adds what North Hampton families need about Winnacunnet High School (WHS, Hampton) and the Seacoast School of Technology (SST, Exeter) beyond the shared research in `town/winnacunnet/winnacunnet-high-school.md`, Seabrook's `town/Seabrook/winnacunnet-and-sst-for-seabrook.md` and Exeter's `town/Exeter/cms-sst-and-getting-to-school.md`. It covers how North Hampton School's eighth graders compare on state tests with the other schools that feed WHS, North Hampton's share of the WHS student body, the 2026-27 WHS bus runs on North Hampton streets and the timing of the SST bus, state rules on scheduling CTE students, federal refrigerant certification for HVAC work, scholarships for North Hampton residents, WHS's special education programs for students up to age 21 and who coordinates with vocational rehabilitation, the athletic trainer, AP exam results and college applications, how North Hampton School's and WHS's reading and math programs have changed since 2020, and what WHS teaches about money and artificial intelligence.

- **13 fact topics, 41 facts and 2 number tables.** Places: 26 facts use `district Winnacunnet Cooperative`, 8 `district North Hampton`, 2 `sau 21`, 2 `town North Hampton`, and 3 rule facts have no place. Raw copies are in `raw/high/` (about 65 MB).
- **Main sources:** SAU 21's Special Education Policies and Procedures Manual (adopted October 19, 2021) and Special Education Resources page; the WHS 2026-27 Program of Studies and Student/Parent Handbook and the 2021-22 Student Athlete and Parent Handbook (copied from the Winnacunnet and Seabrook research); SAU 21's K-12 School Counseling Plan (2026); Winnacunnet annual reports for 2019-20, 2021-22, 2023-24, 2024-25 and 2025-26; North Hampton School District annual reports for 2019-20, 2020-21, 2023-24, 2024-25 and 2025-26; North Hampton School's "On to High School" page and its August and November 2024 curriculum reports to the board; the WHS 2026-27 bus route sheet (street names only) matched against OpenStreetMap street lists; the College Board's 2025 AP School Honor Roll for New Hampshire; RSA 188-E; 40 CFR 82.161; the WHS 2024-25 scholarship list; the library's VR and area agency lookup files; and read-only queries of the site's database.
- **New data concepts:** grade 8 results at each school that feeds WHS side by side (table north_hampton_feeder_grade8_results_2024_2026, **derived**); North Hampton's share of WHS's ninth graders (**derived**); WHS bus runs on North Hampton streets (table winnacunnet_north_hampton_bus_runs_2026_27, **derived**); WHS's AP exam takers and pass rates in 2019 and 2024; WHS's Transitions Program and its Transition & Out of District Coordinator; WHS special education tuition and transportation spending; North Hampton-only scholarships; WHS's rule on disclosing AI use; a timeline of North Hampton School's curriculum work since 2020.

## occupational-licensing-rules: Refrigerant Certification for HVAC Work

- kind: rule
- question_type: rules
- description: New Hampshire licensing rules for trades and health careers that career programs prepare students for: plumbing and fuel gas licenses, apprentice and trainee licenses, required training and work hours, exams and minimum ages, and nursing assistant (LNA) licensing

1. Under federal Clean Air Act rules, anyone who services, repairs or disposes of air conditioning or refrigeration equipment in a way that could release refrigerant must pass an EPA-approved technician certification exam (Section 608): Type I for small appliances, Type II for high-pressure equipment such as most home and commercial air conditioners, Type III for low-pressure equipment, or Universal for all; an apprentice is exempt while closely and continually supervised by a certified technician.
   - citation: 40 CFR 82.161(a)
   - source: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-40/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-82/subpart-F/section-82.161
   - as_of: 2026-11-21
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: Rule text: "Any person who could be reasonably expected to violate the integrity of the refrigerant circuit during the maintenance, service, repair, or disposal of appliances ... must pass a certification exam offered by an approved technician certification program. (i) Persons who maintain, service, or repair small appliances must be certified as Type I technicians. (ii) Persons who maintain, service, repair, or dispose of medium-, high-, or very high-pressure appliances (except small appliances, MVACs, and MVAC-like appliances) must be certified as Type II technicians. (iii) ... low-pressure appliances must be certified as Type III technicians. (iv) ... must be certified as Universal technicians." "(2) Apprentices are exempt ... provided the apprentice is closely and continually supervised by a certified technician". Vehicle air conditioning is covered separately (subpart B; SST's Automotive program lists the EPA 609 certificate, Exeter file cte-center-programs 13). The rule names "small appliances" and "high-pressure appliances"; that most home and commercial air conditioners are high-pressure is the plain reading of those categories.
