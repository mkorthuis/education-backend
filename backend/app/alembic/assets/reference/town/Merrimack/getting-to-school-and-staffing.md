# Getting to School, Safety and Staffing in Merrimack

Part of the Merrimack research (town/Merrimack). Gathered November 15, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers how Merrimack students get to school and how the school day, safety and staffing work: the Student Transportation of America (STA) bus contract and its 2027 expiration, who to call about a late bus, the 2026-27 morning routes and ride times by school, bell times and why K–4 and JMUES differ, snow-day and delay alerts, emergency plans, drills, cameras, locks and school resource officers, how K–4 students are assigned to schools by street, the 2023 space concerns at Thorntons Ferry and Reeds Ferry, New Hampshire Housing's Merrimack figures on students per housing unit, mid-year teacher vacancies and how classes are covered, paraeducator pay, benefits and staffing, and support for new teachers.

- **Premises checked:**
  - "Bus route canceled or delayed because of driver shortages" (P5.2) and "routes getting combined because they can't hire drivers" (P26.3): not found. Board minutes from July 2025 through October 19, 2026 record no canceled or combined routes and no driver shortage; the last mention found is December 2024, when athletic practice buses "changed based on bus driver availability." STA was still advertising for drivers ($25–$29 an hour). The board's 2026 budget guidance asks for route optimization based on actual ridership, a cost question. No on-time data are published.
  - "Merrimack keeps having trouble keeping paraeducators and bus drivers" (P17.1): partly. Four paraeducator jobs and two long-term substitute paraeducator jobs were open on November 15, 2026; the board chair called support staff "in such a critical shortage across the board" in May 2023; the site's data show instruction support staff falling from 196.5 FTE (2019-20) to 162.0 (2022-23) and 174.0 (2025-26). No turnover rates or exit survey results are published, so whether pay, benefits or workload drive departures can't be told from the sources. Bus drivers work for STA, not the district.
  - "Locked vestibules, cameras and resource officers at Thorntons Ferry" (P18.1): district-wide, not school by school. The district added cameras and lock sets with state grants (2022–2024) and requires visitors to sign in at the main office; the police MOU assigns two full-time SROs to the district (an SRO is mentioned at the middle school). No public document describes Thorntons Ferry's entry or assigns an SRO to it; the board discusses security specifics only in non-public session.
  - "A new apartment complex near Route 3" (P20.3): none found. The Planning Board's 2026 agendas list a 20-unit elderly housing project on Loop Road and a planned unit development change at 725 DW Highway, not a large apartment complex. In 2023 the district counted about 70, 161 and 110 planned units in the Mastricola, Thorntons Ferry and Reeds Ferry zones, said TFS and RFS were "stressed" for space, and proposed studying grade reorganization rather than redistricting. New Hampshire Housing found Merrimack apartments built 2014–2023 had 0.057 public school students per unit.
  - "Two different drop-off schedules" at Reeds Ferry and JMUES (P30.1): confirmed. Arrival opens at 8:15 and 8:25, classes start at 8:30 and 8:40, and dismissal is at 2:45 and 2:55. No document explains the offset.
  - "Do kindergartners ride with older kids?" (P21.1): only with grades 1–4. Each 2026-27 route sheet serves one school, and K–4, JMUES and middle/high school routes are separate.
  - "How early does the superintendent send the alert?" (P18.3): the handbooks set a 6:30 a.m. deadline for announcing a cancellation or delay.
  - "Right near the Nashua line" (P11.1): K–4 schools are assigned by street lists (dated April 2020), with some long roads split by house number; there is no zone map.
