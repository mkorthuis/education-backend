# JMUES, Merrimack Middle School and Curriculum

Part of the Merrimack research (town/Merrimack). Gathered November 15, 2026.

## Summary

This file covers James Mastricola Upper Elementary School (JMUES, grades 5–6), Merrimack Middle School (MMS, grades 7–8) and district-wide curriculum questions: the math path from MMS into honors math at Merrimack High School (MHS), bullying rules and support around the move to MMS, after-school clubs, intramurals and sports, Chromebook repair costs and band instrument rentals, grading, competencies and retakes, the K–6 health curriculum, the new Personal Finance and New Hampshire History requirements, parents' say over materials, the AI and phone policies, gifted services, civics teaching, grade 7 class sizes, and math results since the pandemic. Sources are SAU 26's posted board policies (Google Drive copies), the 2026-27 JMUES, MMS and MHS parent-student handbooks, the MHS 2026-27 Program of Studies and school profile, the district's curriculum site, school web pages, the band director's site and the rental vendors' flyers, the district's Chromebook coverage documents, school board minutes from February 2025 to October 19, 2026, the federal Civil Rights Data Collection (CRDC) for 2023-24, and the site's own database (test scores, enrollment, class sizes, bullying counts).

**Counts:** 11 topics, 95 facts, 1 table.

**Main findings:**
- **Honors math.** MMS teaches Math 7 or an accelerated Grade 7 Pre-Algebra (two years of pre-algebra in one), then Pre-Algebra or Algebra I in grade 8. In 2023-24, 61 eighth graders (about 23%) took Algebra I. MHS's chart sends grade 8 Algebra I students to Geometry Honors in grade 9, which needs a middle school recommendation, and on to AP Calculus by grade 12. Other students can petition into honors each spring.
- **Bullying.** Policy JICK was rewritten on October 5, 2026 (adding doxxing, catfishing and deepfakes). It follows the state timelines: parents told within 48 hours, an investigation started within 5 school days. MMS gives every student a daily advisory group and has one counselor per grade. JMUES counselors teach every class what bullying legally is.
- **Costs.** Chromebook coverage costs $10 a year and pays for the first repair in full and half of the second. Replacing a lost device costs $68.75 to $358 depending on its age. Band instruments are rented from private vendors (about $25–$42 a month), and the band director asks families to contact him if renting is a hardship.
- **AI and phones.** AI is neither banned nor freely allowed. Policy EHAGA (October 2025) lets students use AI only when a teacher permits it, and they must disclose it. Student use of approved tools begins under the district's plan in 2026-27, and MHS English competencies include "responsibly use artificial intelligence." Phones are banned from bell to bell under policy JICJ. In September 2025 administrators reported little to no impact outside the high school, and in November 2025 the board extended the ban to cover the end-of-day wait for buses.
- **Math recovery.** MMS math proficiency was 50% in 2019, 28% in 2022 and 45% in 2026, still below 2019. MMS reading (57% in 2026) is above its 2019 level. The district's March 2026 review found that math lagged reading in grades K–8. The board made math its budget priority in October 2026, and a K–6 math pilot is being planned.
- **Gifted services.** All six schools reported a gifted program to the CRDC in 2023-24 (24 students at JMUES, 15 at MMS). The shared MMS–MHS gifted facilitator had already been cut, and the FY2027 budget proposal cut the JMUES gifted teacher. Minutes through October 2026 show no reinstatement. There is no posted gifted policy.
