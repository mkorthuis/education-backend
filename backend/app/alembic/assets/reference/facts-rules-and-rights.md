# Rules and Rights: Cited Facts (Strategy Type 3)

**Summary**

- **What this file is:** plain statements of what New Hampshire and federal law require of public schools, and what families can do. Each statement cites the exact section of the statute, rule or federal guidance it comes from. Every source was opened on 2026-11-08 (or, where noted, read from a Wayback snapshot). Raw copies are in `raw/rules/`.
- **Size:** 42 topics, 320 facts (plus 17 unresolved items listed at the end).
- **Sources:** NH statutes (RSA) and NH Department of Education and DHHS rules on gc.nh.gov; federal regulations from the eCFR (titles 7, 34 and 45, up to date as of 2026-11-05); the U.S. Code via Cornell LII; the Federal Register; ed.gov (read in a browser); General Court bill-status pages; and two program operators, the Community College System of NH (CCSNH) and Children's Scholarship Fund NH (CSF NH).
- **Review dates:** facts from NH law and rules: 2027-08-15. Federal facts: 2027-11-08. The 2026–27 school meal income limits expire June 30, 2027, so their review date is 2027-06-30.
- **Changed in 2025–26 (all reflected below):**
  - EFAs: SB 295 (2025 ch. 75, effective June 10, 2025) removed the income limit. It also set an enrollment cap (10,000 in 2025–26; 12,500 in 2026–27, now reached) with priority groups that are not held to the cap.
  - The Parental Bill of Rights, RSA 189-B (2025 ch. 74), took effect July 1, 2025 and was amended in 2026.
  - Home education: RSA 193-A was rewritten by HB 1268 (2026 ch. 297), effective July 1, 2026. A declaration is now needed only to use public school programs or when a child leaves a public school, and the annual evaluation section was repealed.
  - Bullying: the "imbalance of power … personal characteristics" part of the definition (RSA 193-F:3, I(b)) was repealed effective August 1, 2026 (2026 ch. 205). The policy and reporting sections were also amended in 2025 and 2026.
  - School transfers: in-district transfers, RSA 193:3, were changed by SB 97 (2025 ch. 293), effective July 1, 2025. Open enrollment funding, RSA 194-D, changed effective September 13, 2025. Both 2026 bills for statewide open enrollment (SB 101 and HB 751) died.
  - New excused absences for civic and career-tech events (RSA 193:1-d), effective July 10, 2026.
  - The dual enrollment funding section (RSA 188-E:27) was amended effective June 21, 2026.
  - Phone-free school day policies (RSA 189:1-a, V) were added in 2025.
  - Coming January 1, 2027: teachers must answer parents' written questions within 10 business days (RSA 189:13-e), and districts need a policy on questioning students (RSA 189:1-a, VI).
  - Special education: the state complaint tracking and publishing law (RSA 186-C:5-a) arrived in 2025, and the due process and dispute resolution sections were amended in 2025.
  - Federal changes:
    - The 2015 ED/DOJ letter on English learners and parents with limited English is marked "formally rescinded."
    - Executive Order 14224 (March 2025) revoked the federal order on access to services for people with limited English (EO 13166).
    - ED replaced its school prayer guidance on February 5, 2026.
    - New school meal income limits apply for July 2026 through June 2027.
- **Unresolved (details at the end):**
  - No current RSA section was found that expressly requires every district to offer kindergarten.
  - The Ed 315 home education rules still describe the pre-2026 law.
  - It is unclear how CCSNH's "two free courses a year" fits with the statute's "up to 4 per grade."
  - Not retrieved: which option New Hampshire uses for testing recently arrived English learners, whether any NH schools serve universal free meals, and federal guidance replacing the 2015 English learner letter.
  - NH's statutory definition of "parent" for special education is narrower than the federal one for a grandparent who is not a guardian.
  - Three different deadlines for parents to see school records are in force (state 10 business days, state 14 days, federal 45 days).

## special-ed-iep-basics: Special Education and IEPs: What They Are and Who Qualifies

- kind: rule
- question_type: rules
- description: What an IEP and special education are, which children qualify in New Hampshire, and that services are free.

1. Under New Hampshire law, an individualized education program (IEP) is a written plan for the education of a child with a disability, developed by the school district, that provides the special education and related services the child needs.
   - citation: RSA 186-C:2, III
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XV/186-C/186-C-2.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-08
   - review_by: 2027-08-15

2. New Hampshire law defines "special education" as specially designed instruction to meet the unique needs of a child with a disability. "Related services" (such as speech or occupational therapy) are defined by the federal special education law, IDEA.
   - citation: RSA 186-C:2, IV and VII
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XV/186-C/186-C-2.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-08
   - review_by: 2027-08-15

3. In New Hampshire, a "child with a disability" is a person aged 3 through 21 who has been evaluated by the school district and found to have a listed disability and who, because of it, needs special education. The listed disabilities include intellectual disability, hearing or vision impairment, speech or language impairment, emotional disturbance, orthopedic impairment, autism, brain injury, other health impairment, specific learning disability, deaf-blindness and multiple disabilities.
   - citation: RSA 186-C:2, I
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XV/186-C/186-C-2.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-08
   - review_by: 2027-08-15

4. New Hampshire law also counts as a "child with a disability" a child aged 3 to 9 who has developmental delays and, because of them, needs special education, if the child meets the state board's criteria.
   - citation: RSA 186-C:2, I and I-a
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XV/186-C/186-C-2.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-08
   - review_by: 2027-08-15

5. Under federal special education rules, a child who has a listed disability but needs only a related service, not special education, is generally not a "child with a disability" under IDEA. The exception is where the state treats that service as special education.
   - citation: 34 CFR 300.8(a)(2)
   - source: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-300.8
   - as_of: 2026-11-08
   - review_by: 2027-11-08

6. Under federal law, a "free appropriate public education" means special education and related services provided at public expense and without charge, including preschool, elementary and secondary education, in line with the child's IEP.
   - citation: 34 CFR 300.17
   - source: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-300.17
   - as_of: 2026-11-08
   - review_by: 2027-11-08

7. New Hampshire law gives the parents of a child with a disability the right to take part in developing the child's IEP and to appeal the school district's decisions about it.
   - citation: RSA 186-C:7, II
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XV/186-C/186-C-7.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-08
   - review_by: 2027-08-15

8. New Hampshire requires every IEP to include short-term objectives or benchmarks, unless the parent agrees they are not needed for some or all of the child's annual goals.
   - citation: RSA 186-C:7, III; Ed 1109.01(a)(6)
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XV/186-C/186-C-7.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-08
   - review_by: 2027-08-15

9. New Hampshire rules require each IEP to be reviewed at least once a year, and an IEP may not run longer than 12 months without review and approval by the IEP team.
   - citation: Ed 1109.03(d)-(e)
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rules/state_agencies/ed1100.html
   - as_of: 2026-11-08
   - review_by: 2027-08-15

10. New Hampshire has an Office of the Advocate for Special Education, independent of the Department of Education. It serves as an advocate and point of contact for parents and students with disabilities in dealing with school districts about IEPs and 504 plans.
   - citation: RSA 186-C:36, I-II; RSA 186-C:38, I
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XV/186-C/186-C-36.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-08
   - review_by: 2027-08-15

## special-ed-evaluation-request: Asking for a Special Education Evaluation, and the Timelines

- kind: rule
- question_type: rules
- description: How a parent asks the school to evaluate a child for special education, and the deadlines New Hampshire sets.

1. Under federal special education rules, either a parent or the school may ask for an initial evaluation to find out whether a child has a disability and needs special education.
   - citation: 34 CFR 300.301(b)
   - source: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-300.301
   - as_of: 2026-11-08
   - review_by: 2027-11-08

2. In New Hampshire, when a school district receives a referral for special education from any source (a parent, a teacher, a doctor), it must immediately tell the parent in writing.
   - citation: Ed 1106.01(c)
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rules/state_agencies/ed1100.html
   - as_of: 2026-11-08
   - review_by: 2027-08-15

3. In New Hampshire, within 15 business days of a referral the IEP team must decide how to address the concerns (through supports available to all students, more information, or an evaluation) and give the parent written notice of that decision.
   - citation: Ed 1106.01(d)-(e)
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rules/state_agencies/ed1100.html
   - as_of: 2026-11-08
   - review_by: 2027-08-15

4. When the IEP team decides testing is needed, New Hampshire rules require the written notice to include a request for the parent's written consent to the evaluations, and a school district must get informed written consent before an initial evaluation.
   - citation: Ed 1106.01(g); Ed 1120.04(a)(1)
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rules/state_agencies/ed1100.html
   - as_of: 2026-11-08
   - review_by: 2027-08-15

5. In New Hampshire, an initial special education evaluation must be completed within 60 days after the school district receives the parent's consent.
   - citation: Ed 1107.01(c)
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rules/state_agencies/ed1100.html
   - as_of: 2026-11-08
   - review_by: 2027-08-15

6. In New Hampshire, a reevaluation must also be completed within 60 days after consent. With the written consent of the parent and the district, that time may be extended by a set number of days, up to 30.
   - citation: Ed 1107.01(d)-(e)
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rules/state_agencies/ed1100.html
   - as_of: 2026-11-08
   - review_by: 2027-08-15

7. A New Hampshire parent who disagrees with the IEP team's decision on a referral may ask for alternative dispute resolution (such as mediation) or a due process hearing.
   - citation: Ed 1106.01(h)
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rules/state_agencies/ed1100.html
   - as_of: 2026-11-08
   - review_by: 2027-08-15

8. Under federal rules, once a child is found to need special education, a meeting to write the IEP must be held within 30 days, and services must start as soon as possible after the IEP is written.
   - citation: 34 CFR 300.323(c)
   - source: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-300.323
   - as_of: 2026-11-08
   - review_by: 2027-11-08

## special-ed-who-is-parent: Who Counts as a "Parent" in Special Education (Guardians, Grandparents, Foster Parents)

- kind: rule
- question_type: rules
- description: Who can act as the parent for a child's special education under NH and federal law, including guardians and relatives.

1. Under New Hampshire special education law, a "parent" includes a natural or adoptive parent with legal custody, and a guardian of the child, but not the state when the state is the child's guardian.
   - citation: RSA 186-C:2, VIII(a)-(b)
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XV/186-C/186-C-2.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-08
   - review_by: 2027-08-15

2. New Hampshire special education law also counts as a "parent" a person acting in place of the custodial parent or guardian when none is available, if the parent or guardian has designated that person in writing to make educational decisions. Surrogate parents and foster parents appointed under state law also count.
   - citation: RSA 186-C:2, VIII(c)-(e)
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XV/186-C/186-C-2.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-08
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: The NH statute's wording (written designation, no custodial parent available) is narrower than the federal definition in fact 3 for a relative who is not a guardian. The rule in fact 4 refers to the federal definition. Do not state which governs for a grandparent without guardianship; see Not retrieved.

3. Under federal special education rules, "parent" includes a guardian and a person acting in place of a parent, such as a grandparent, stepparent or other relative with whom the child lives, or a person legally responsible for the child's welfare.
   - citation: 34 CFR 300.30(a)(3)-(4)
   - source: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-300.30
   - as_of: 2026-11-08
   - review_by: 2027-11-08

4. New Hampshire's special education rules define "parent" as a biological or adoptive parent, a surrogate parent, or a guardian as described in the federal definition (34 CFR 300.30), and not the state when the state is the guardian.
   - citation: Ed 1102.04(h)
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rules/state_agencies/ed1100.html
   - as_of: 2026-11-08
   - review_by: 2027-08-15

5. Under federal rules, when more than one person could act as the parent, the biological or adoptive parent is presumed to be the parent unless that parent lacks legal authority to make educational decisions. A court order naming a specific person controls.
   - citation: 34 CFR 300.30(b)
   - source: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-300.30
   - as_of: 2026-11-08
   - review_by: 2027-11-08

6. Under New Hampshire residency law, a child in the custody of a court-appointed legal guardian lives, for school purposes, where the guardian lives. While a relative's petition for guardianship or custody is pending in court, the child may attend school in the district where that relative lives.
   - citation: RSA 193:12, II(a)(3)
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XV/193/193-12.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-08
   - review_by: 2027-08-15

## special-ed-iep-meetings-and-consent: IEP Meetings, Notices and Parent Consent

- kind: rule
- question_type: rules
- description: Notice before IEP meetings, who is on the IEP team, written notice of changes, and when parent consent is required in New Hampshire.

1. In New Hampshire, the school must give parents written notice at least 10 days before an IEP meeting, stating its purpose, time, place and who will attend. A notice mailed 12 days ahead counts as received 10 days ahead, and a parent may waive the notice in writing.
   - citation: Ed 1103.02(a), (c)-(d)
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rules/state_agencies/ed1100.html
   - as_of: 2026-11-08
   - review_by: 2027-08-15

2. Under federal rules, the IEP team includes the parents, at least one regular education teacher and one special education teacher or provider of the child, and a district representative. At the discretion of the parent or the school, it can also include other people with knowledge or special expertise about the child.
   - citation: 34 CFR 300.321(a)
   - source: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-300.321
   - as_of: 2026-11-08
   - review_by: 2027-11-08

3. Under federal rules, the school must give parents a copy of their child's IEP at no cost.
   - citation: 34 CFR 300.322(f)
   - source: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-300.322
   - as_of: 2026-11-08
   - review_by: 2027-11-08

4. In New Hampshire, parents must get written notice at least 14 days before the school proposes, or refuses, to start or change a child's evaluation, eligibility, IEP, placement or services.
   - citation: Ed 1120.03(a)
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rules/state_agencies/ed1100.html
   - as_of: 2026-11-08
   - review_by: 2027-08-15

5. In New Hampshire, a school district must get the parent's informed written consent before an initial evaluation or a reevaluation, before first providing special education, before each annual renewal of the IEP and placement, and before setting or changing the disability classification or the nature or extent of services.
   - citation: Ed 1120.04(a)
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rules/state_agencies/ed1100.html
   - as_of: 2026-11-08
   - review_by: 2027-08-15

6. New Hampshire law lets a school district send IEP notices and records to parents by email, unless the parent chooses to get them by U.S. mail.
   - citation: RSA 186-C:7, V
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XV/186-C/186-C-7.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-08
   - review_by: 2027-08-15

## special-ed-moving-with-iep: Moving to a New District or State With an IEP

- kind: rule
- question_type: rules
- description: What a new school must do when a child with an IEP transfers within New Hampshire or from another state.

1. Under federal rules, when a child with an IEP moves to a new district in the same state and enrolls during the same school year, the new district (in consultation with the parents) must provide services comparable to those in the old IEP. That lasts until it either adopts the old IEP or develops and implements a new one.
   - citation: 34 CFR 300.323(e)
   - source: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-300.323
   - as_of: 2026-11-08
   - review_by: 2027-11-08
   - note: "Comparable" is the regulation's word; it does not mean identical. The rule sets no number of days.

2. Under federal rules, when a child with an IEP moves from another state and enrolls during the same school year, the new district must provide services comparable to the old IEP. That lasts until it conducts an evaluation, if it decides one is needed, and develops a new IEP if appropriate.
   - citation: 34 CFR 300.323(f)
   - source: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-300.323
   - as_of: 2026-11-08
   - review_by: 2027-11-08

3. Under federal rules, the new school must take reasonable steps to promptly get the child's records, including the IEP, and the previous school must take reasonable steps to respond promptly.
   - citation: 34 CFR 300.323(g)
   - source: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-300.323
   - as_of: 2026-11-08
   - review_by: 2027-11-08

4. Under federal rules, at the beginning of each school year every district must have an IEP in effect for each child with a disability it serves. This covers families who move over the summer.
   - citation: 34 CFR 300.323(a)
   - source: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-300.323
   - as_of: 2026-11-08
   - review_by: 2027-11-08

5. New Hampshire's special education rules require IEPs to be in effect as the federal rule (34 CFR 300.323) describes.
   - citation: Ed 1109.03(a)
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rules/state_agencies/ed1100.html
   - as_of: 2026-11-08
   - review_by: 2027-08-15

6. Under New Hampshire residency law, while a dispute over which district a student lives in is being decided, the student stays in their school of origin. No school district may deny the student attendance or the carrying out of an existing IEP.
   - citation: RSA 193:12, VI(a)-(b)
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XV/193/193-12.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-08
   - review_by: 2027-08-15

## military-children-compact: Military Families: The Interstate Compact and Residency Rules

- kind: rule
- question_type: rules
- description: What the Interstate Compact on Educational Opportunity for Military Children (RSA 110-D) and NH residency law provide for military-connected students.

1. New Hampshire's military children's compact applies to children of active-duty members of the uniformed services, including National Guard and Reserve members on active duty orders under chapters 1209 and 1211 of Title 10 of the U.S. Code. It also applies for one year to children of members who were severely injured and medically discharged or retired, or who died on active duty.
   - citation: RSA 110-D:4, I (Article III), as amended effective 2024-08-13
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/VIII/110-D/110-D-4.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-08
   - review_by: 2027-08-15

2. The compact does not apply to children of inactive Guard and Reserve members, most retirees and veterans, or Defense Department civilian and contract employees.
   - citation: RSA 110-D:4, III
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/VIII/110-D/110-D-4.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-08
   - review_by: 2027-08-15

3. Under the compact, when a student moves from another state, the receiving state must at first provide comparable services based on the student's current IEP, or reasonable accommodations and modifications for a student with a 504 plan. The new school may evaluate the student later.
   - citation: RSA 110-D:6, III (Article V)
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/VIII/110-D/110-D-6.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-08
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: The compact is written for moves between a "sending state" and a "receiving state." A move between two NH districts is covered by 34 CFR 300.323(e) instead.

4. Under the compact, the receiving school must enroll and place a student using hand-carried unofficial records while it waits for official records. The sending state's school must send the official records within 10 days of the request, or within the time set by the compact's commission.
   - citation: RSA 110-D:5, I-II (Article IV)
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/VIII/110-D/110-D-5.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-08
   - review_by: 2027-08-15

5. Under the compact, a military child may continue in the same grade, including kindergarten, after moving to New Hampshire, regardless of age. The child also has 30 days from enrollment to get required immunizations.
   - citation: RSA 110-D:5, III-IV
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/VIII/110-D/110-D-5.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-08
   - review_by: 2027-08-15

6. Under the compact, the receiving school must at first honor the student's placement in courses (such as honors or AP) and in programs (such as gifted, remedial or English language learner services) based on the sending school's placement or assessments.
   - citation: RSA 110-D:6, I-II
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/VIII/110-D/110-D-6.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-08
   - review_by: 2027-08-15

7. Under the compact, a local district must waive specific courses required for graduation if similar coursework was completed elsewhere, or give a reasonable justification for denial. A senior who transfers and cannot graduate from the new school must get a diploma from the sending school if they meet its requirements.
   - citation: RSA 110-D:8, I and III (Article VII)
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/VIII/110-D/110-D-8.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-08
   - review_by: 2027-08-15

8. Under New Hampshire residency law, a military-connected student counts as a resident of a school district if a parent is transferred, or pending transfer, to a military installation in the state under official orders. The parent must show proof of residence within 10 days after the arrival date, and districts must accept electronic enrollment applications.
   - citation: RSA 193:12, III-a
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XV/193/193-12.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-08
   - review_by: 2027-08-15

9. New Hampshire's open enrollment chapter makes some military-connected students eligible for admission to the school district of their choice: children of active-duty members, or of reservists on active duty orders for more than 30 consecutive days, where a parent has a Defense Department ID. A district of residence may not block such a transfer if the receiving district approves it.
   - citation: RSA 194-D:2, XI-XII
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XV/194-D/194-D-2.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-08
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: The section does not say who pays tuition in this case; do not state it.

## special-ed-transition-and-age-limit: Transition Planning and How Long Special Education Lasts

- kind: rule
- question_type: rules
- description: When IEPs must plan for life after high school (age 14 in NH, 16 federally), and the age limit for services (through age 21, "21 inclusive").

1. Federal rules require the IEP that is in effect when a student turns 16, or an earlier one if the IEP team decides, to include measurable goals for after high school and the transition services (including courses of study) to reach them. These are updated every year.
   - citation: 34 CFR 300.320(b)
   - source: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-300.320
   - as_of: 2026-11-08
   - review_by: 2027-11-08

2. New Hampshire goes further than federal law: starting at age 14, or younger if the IEP team decides, the IEP must include a statement of the student's transition service needs. That statement focuses on courses of study such as advanced placement, vocational or career and technical education.
   - citation: Ed 1109.01(a)(10), as amended effective 2023-10-12
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rules/state_agencies/ed1100.html
   - as_of: 2026-11-08
   - review_by: 2027-08-15

3. Under federal rules, when transition planning will be discussed at an IEP meeting, the meeting notice must say so and say that the student will be invited.
   - citation: 34 CFR 300.322(b)(2); 34 CFR 300.321(b)(1)
   - source: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-300.322
   - as_of: 2026-11-08
   - review_by: 2027-11-08

4. Since a 2022 change, New Hampshire law defines a "child with a disability" as a person between the ages of 3 and 21, inclusive. "Inclusive" means services can continue through age 21, that is, until the 22nd birthday.
   - citation: RSA 186-C:2, I, amended by 2022 ch. 230:1, eff. 2022-06-17
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XV/186-C/186-C-2.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-08
   - review_by: 2027-08-15
   - note: The statute does not say whether services run to the end of the school year in which a student turns 22; do not state either way.

5. In New Hampshire, a student with a disability is entitled to stay in an approved special education program until the student earns a regular high school diploma or reaches the age of 21 inclusive, whichever comes first, or until the IEP team decides the student no longer needs special education.
   - citation: RSA 186-C:9
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XV/186-C/186-C-9.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-08
   - review_by: 2027-08-15

6. New Hampshire school boards must provide education at district expense until a student earns a high school diploma or reaches age 21. For a child with a disability, the limit is a diploma or age 21 inclusive.
   - citation: RSA 189:1-a, I
   - source: https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XV/189/189-1-a.htm
   - as_of: 2026-11-08

## student-records-ferpa: Seeing and Correcting School Records (Federal FERPA)

   - as_of: 2026-11-08
   - review_by: 2027-11-08

3. Under FERPA, a parent may ask the school to amend a record they believe is inaccurate, misleading or violates the student's privacy. If the school refuses, it must tell the parent of the right to a hearing.

   - as_of: 2026-11-08
   - review_by: 2027-11-08

4. Under FERPA, if a hearing ends without the record being changed, the parent may put a statement in the record explaining their disagreement. The school must keep it with the contested record and disclose it whenever that part of the record is disclosed.

   - as_of: 2026-11-08
   - review_by: 2027-11-08

   - source: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-99.30
   - as_of: 2026-11-08
   - review_by: 2027-11-08

6. FERPA allows disclosure without consent in listed cases. Two of them are disclosure to school officials, including teachers, with a legitimate educational interest, and to officials of another school where the student seeks or intends to enroll.
